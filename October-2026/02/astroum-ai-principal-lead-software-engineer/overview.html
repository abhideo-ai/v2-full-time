The company and its products

Astroum AI Private Limited
The employer: a private limited company registered in Hyderabad on 10 July 2026. “Private limited” is the usual legal form for an Indian start-up; its shares are not traded publicly.
BRAHMO
Astroum AI’s “knowledge layer for AI”: it collects an organisation’s notes, documents, audio and decisions and hands the relevant context to whichever AI tool a person uses. It is pitched at law firms and healthcare practices, so it is the most likely home of the post’s “knowledge systems” and “legal” work (our inference).
Einstein IQ
Astroum AI’s operations-intelligence product for restaurant, hotel, salon and diagnostic chains, including an AI voice agent that returns missed calls. It is the most likely home of the post’s “voice” work (our inference). IQ here is a brand name, not a measurement.
Netra AI
Astroum AI’s camera-monitoring product: a small computer on the customer’s premises analyses CCTV (closed-circuit television) video and sends alerts. Aimed at hospitals and jewellery chains. If this seat owns it, computer vision on edge devices would be in scope.
Edge device; Jetson Orin NX
An edge device is a computer placed where the data is produced, here on the customer’s premises, instead of in a cloud data centre. The Jetson Orin NX is an NVIDIA module built to run AI models on such devices. Netra AI uses one.
QSR (quick-service restaurant)
The trade term for fast-food and quick-dining chains. Einstein IQ’s main market.
Zomato and Swiggy
India’s two big food-delivery platforms, whose restaurant ratings Einstein IQ watches.
Paid pilot
A short trial a customer pays for, with success measures agreed beforehand. The website offers 45-day paid pilots; whether any are running is an open question.

The Indian government ministry that keeps the register of companies. Websites such as Falcon eBiz, Planetexim, Tracxn, Tofler and ZaubaCorp copy its data.
ROC (Registrar of Companies)
The MCA office for a region, where a company is registered; here, ROC Hyderabad.
CIN (corporate identification number)
The unique number an Indian company gets at registration. Astroum AI’s is U62011TS2026PTC219095: “TS” is Telangana, “2026” the year, “PTC” a private company.
Authorised and paid-up capital
Authorised capital is the most share capital a company may issue; paid-up capital is what shareholders have actually paid in. A low paid-up figure and no announced funding suggest a company funded by its founders so far (our inference).
Runway
How many months a company can keep paying its costs with the money it has. The main risk of any early-stage seat.
WHOIS; domain registration

Applied AI and LLM engineering

LLM (large language model)
An AI model trained on very large amounts of text that writes and reads language, such as Claude, GPT or Gemini. The post’s “LLM-powered features” are built on these, usually through a provider’s API.
LLM API
Calling a hosted model over the internet through its API (application programming interface), instead of running the model yourself. The post lists “LLM APIs” in its stack.
RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)
Before the model answers, the system first searches the organisation’s own documents and puts the most relevant passages into the request, so the answer rests on real sources. The post’s “retrieval/RAG” and “knowledge workflows”.
Vector search; embeddings
An embedding turns a piece of text into a list of numbers so that similar meanings sit close together; vector search finds the closest ones. It is how RAG usually finds relevant passages. The post lists “vector search”; pgvector adds it to PostgreSQL.
Context assembly; token budget
Choosing what to put into a model’s request when only a limited amount fits. A token is a small piece of text, about three-quarters of an English word. An earlier Astroum AI advertisement tested candidates on exactly this.
Structured outputs
Making a model answer in a fixed, machine-readable shape (for example JSON that follows a schema) so that software can check and use it. The post names it.
Evaluation suite (evals); golden set
A repeatable set of test cases with agreed good answers, run against an AI feature to measure its quality. The post asks for “AI evaluation suites”, and its second application question is about a test or evaluation harness.
Regression check
A test that re-runs after every change to make sure something that worked before still works. The post asks for “regression checks”.
End-to-end acceptance test
A test that runs a whole user journey through the real system, from the screen to the database and back. The post asks for these.
Deterministic validation
Checking an AI’s output with ordinary rules that always give the same result, such as a schema check or a calculation, instead of trusting the model. The post: “Know when to use a model, conventional software, deterministic validation, or human review.”
Human review (human-in-the-loop)
A person checks or approves the AI’s output before it takes effect. Important wherever errors are costly, as in healthcare and legal work.
Hallucination
When a model states something false with confidence. Evaluation suites and deterministic checks exist to catch it.
Build versus buy
Deciding whether to write a capability yourself or use an existing product or service. The post asks for “practical build-versus-buy decisions”.

The stack

Python
The main programming language of the post (“Expert-level Python”) and of most AI work.
FastAPI
A Python framework for building web APIs quickly, with request checking built in. Named in the post and in every earlier Astroum AI advertisement.
PostgreSQL; Supabase
PostgreSQL is a widely used open-source relational database. Supabase is a hosted service built around PostgreSQL, named in an earlier advertisement.
RLS (row-level security)
A PostgreSQL feature that limits which rows each user may see, inside the database itself. An earlier Astroum AI advertisement made it a core skill; it matters for keeping clients’ data apart.
Redis
A very fast in-memory data store, used for caching, queues and sessions. Named in the post.
React; TypeScript
React is a library for building web user interfaces; TypeScript is JavaScript with types. The post asks for “strong React/TypeScript”.
Docker
A tool that packages an application with everything it needs into a container that runs the same way everywhere. Named in the post.
CI/CD (continuous integration and continuous delivery)
Automatically building and testing every change, then releasing it through an automated pipeline. Named in the post.
Observability; monitoring
Seeing what a running system is doing through logs, metrics and traces, so that problems are found before users report them. The post asks him to own “deployment, observability, error handling, recovery, latency, and operating costs”.
OAuth2; JWT (JSON Web Token)
OAuth2 is the standard way to let a user sign in with, or grant access to, another service. A JWT is a signed token that carries who the user is. Both appear in earlier Astroum AI advertisements and in the scoring evidence.
RBAC (role-based access control)
Giving permissions by role (for example doctor, admin, paralegal) rather than person by person. Part of the post’s “access controls”.
Audit trail
A record, which cannot be quietly changed, of who did or saw what and when. The post asks for audit trails; healthcare and legal customers expect them.
SQL (Structured Query Language); data modelling
SQL is the language for querying relational databases; data modelling is designing the tables and their relationships. The post asks for “solid SQL and data-modeling skills”.
AWS terms in the scoring evidence
AWS is Amazon Web Services. EC2 (Elastic Compute Cloud) is its virtual servers; ECS (Elastic Container Service) runs containers; RDS (Relational Database Service) is managed databases; S3 (Simple Storage Service) stores files; SQS (Simple Queue Service) is a message queue; a VPC (virtual private cloud) is a private network inside AWS.
P95, P99
The 95th- and 99th-percentile response times: 95 or 99 of every 100 requests are faster than this. A common way to state latency.

Speech and voice

ASR (automatic speech recognition)
Software that turns speech into text. The base of every voice product.
Streaming transcription
Transcribing audio while it is still being spoken, with results arriving in real time. Needed for live calls and voice agents, such as Einstein IQ’s.
Speaker separation (diarization)
Working out who spoke when, so a transcript of a call or meeting is labelled by speaker. The post names “speaker separation”.
Multilingual audio
Audio in many languages. India has 22 languages in the Constitution’s Eighth Schedule, and an Indian product will meet several.
Code-switching
Moving between languages within one conversation or sentence, such as Hindi and English (“Hinglish”). Speech models trained on one language often handle it poorly, which is why the post lists it.
Voice agent
Software that holds a spoken conversation: speech recognition, a language model to decide what to say, and speech synthesis to say it. Latency matters, because people notice pauses.

Healthcare

EHR / EMR (electronic health record / electronic medical record)
The digital patient chart. An EMR is usually one provider’s record; an EHR is meant to follow the patient across providers. The post lists “EHR/EMR integrations” as helpful experience.
HL7 FHIR (Health Level Seven, Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources)
A standard for exchanging health data as “resources” such as a patient or an observation. It is widely used for modern EHR integrations.
ABDM (Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission)
India’s national digital-health programme, run by the National Health Authority (NHA), which links health records across providers.
PHI (protected health information)
A United States legal term: health information that can identify a person, as defined under HIPAA. A product holding PHI needs strict access control, encryption and audit trails.
HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act)
The United States law, and its rules, that protect health information. It matters only if the products serve United States customers.
DPDP (Digital Personal Data Protection) Act, 2023, and Rules, 2025
India’s data-protection law and its rules, notified on 14 November 2025, with most duties for organisations applying from 13 May 2027. Health and client data in India fall under it. MeitY (Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology) wrote the Rules; PIB (Press Information Bureau) is the government’s press office.
NABH (National Accreditation Board for Hospitals & Healthcare Providers)
The body that accredits Indian hospitals. Netra AI is pitched at recording “NABH compliance events”.
Clinical workflow
The sequence of steps clinicians follow in their work, such as admission, examination, ordering tests and prescribing. Software has to fit into it, not add steps.

Legal technology

Legal technology (legal tech)
Software for lawyers and law firms: research, contract review, document management, matter management.
Document-heavy workflows
Work that runs on long documents such as contracts, filings and correspondence. The post’s “document workflows”.
Matter
A law firm’s word for one case or piece of work for a client. BRAHMO promises “Client matters isolated”.
Confidential client information; privilege
Lawyers must protect what clients tell them, and some communications are “privileged”, protected from disclosure. A legal AI product must never leak one client’s material into another’s work or into model training.
ABA (American Bar Association) Formal Opinion 512
The ABA’s July 2024 guidance on lawyers’ use of generative AI, cited in the research as general context about client confidentiality.

Collisions to watch

  • RAG: here, retrieval-augmented generation (AI). In project status reports, RAG means Red/Amber/Green. Never mix them in one document.
  • LLM: here, large language model. In legal work, an LL.M. is a Master of Laws degree, a real risk of confusion on a legal-technology product.
  • ROC: here, the Registrar of Companies. In AI evaluation, a ROC curve (receiver operating characteristic) plots a classifier’s true-positive rate against its false-positive rate.
  • CV: computer vision (Netra AI’s camera models) or curriculum vitae, which in India means a résumé.
  • MCA: here, the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. In Indian education, an MCA is a Master of Computer Applications degree.
  • PR: here, pull request. Elsewhere, public relations.
  • CI: here, continuous integration. In evaluation results, a CI is a confidence interval.
  • EHR and EMR are often used as if they were the same; strictly, an EMR is one provider’s record and an EHR follows the patient.
  • IQ in “Einstein IQ” is a brand name, not an intelligence quotient; and Einstein IQ is unrelated to Salesforce Einstein, Salesforce’s AI product.
