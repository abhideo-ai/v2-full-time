6. What the post’s work means in practice (general context)

General context, not about Astroum AI. This explains what the post’s domains usually involve, from reliable sources, so that the job’s words are easy to read. Nothing here says what Astroum AI does or needs.

Healthcare workflows

  • Electronic health records and integrations. An electronic health record (EHR) or electronic medical record (EMR) is the digital patient chart. Integrating with one usually means exchanging data in a standard format; a widely used modern standard is HL7 FHIR (Health Level Seven, Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources), whose “basic building block … is a Resource” such as a patient or an observation (HL7 FHIR overview). In India, the national framework for digital health records is the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission (ABDM), run by the National Health Authority (abdm.gov.in).
  • Sensitive health data. In the United States, “Protected health information means individually identifiable health information” held or sent in any form, under the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) rules (45 CFR 160.103). In India, health data is personal data under the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act, 2023. Its Rules were notified on 14 November 2025 (Press Information Bureau, 17 Nov 2025), and organisations have 18 months, to 13 May 2027, to meet the core duties, including “reasonable security safeguards” (Bar & Bench). The press release lists penalties of up to ₹250 crore for failing to keep reasonable security safeguards.
  • Hospital accreditation. NABH, the National Accreditation Board for Hospitals & Healthcare Providers, accredits Indian hospitals against its standards (nabh.co). The website pitches Netra AI at “NABH compliance events”.
  • Inference For an engineer this means access control by role, audit trails of who saw what, encryption, care about which data may leave the premises or the country, and testing against real record formats. The post’s “Security and responsible implementation” section asks for exactly these.

Legal workflows

  • Document-heavy work. Legal work runs on contracts, filings, correspondence and research; AI tools are used for “legal research, contract review, due diligence, document review, regulatory compliance, and drafting” (American Bar Association (ABA) Formal Opinion 512, 29 July 2024).
  • Confidential client data. The same opinion warns that “self-learning” generative AI tools “raise the risk that information relating to one client’s representation may be disclosed improperly, even if the tool is used exclusively by lawyers at the same firm”, and says a client’s informed consent is required before such information goes into a tool of that kind. This is a United States source; it shows the kind of duty a legal product must respect, not Indian law.
  • Inference For an engineer this means strict separation between clients’ matters, no training on client data, permission checks before retrieval, and an audit trail. BRAHMO’s own pitch (“Client matters isolated”, “No training on your data”) and the earlier advertisement’s focus on row-level security point the same way.

Speech and voice products

  • Streaming transcription. Automatic speech recognition (ASR) turns audio into text. In streaming mode, Google’s documentation says, you “stream audio to Cloud Speech-to-Text and receive a stream speech recognition results in real time as the audio is processed”, which voice agents and live captions need (Google Cloud, streaming recognition).
  • Speaker separation (diarization). Google’s documentation defines speaker diarization as a feature that “detects when speakers change and labels by number the individual voices detected in the audio”, so that a call’s transcript shows who spoke when (Google Cloud, multiple voices). An open-source toolkit for it is pyannote (pyannote-audio).
  • Multilingual audio and code-switching. Code-switching is moving between languages inside one conversation or even one sentence, such as Hindi and English (“Hinglish”); a 2025 systematic review collects the research on handling it in end-to-end speech recognition (Agro et al., arXiv 2507.07741). For India’s languages, the IndicVoices dataset holds 23.7 thousand hours of read, extempore and conversational speech from 51,000 speakers in 400+ districts, across 22 languages (AI4Bharat IndicVoices).
  • Inference For products built for the Indian market, as the post’s “Experience That Helps” mentions, this means choosing speech models that handle Indian languages and mixed-language speech, telephone-quality audio, and latency budgets for live calls. Einstein IQ’s “AI voice callback agent” is this kind of product.
