Astroum AI: open questions

What is still open about the seat, and who can settle it

The research could not settle these from public sources. Item 1 he can check himself straight away; the rest need the founder, Dinesh S, who wrote the post and receives the emailed applications. The forcing questions in the next fold turn them into questions he can ask.

  1. Who is the founder, professionally? The website names no previous employer, and his LinkedIn profile (linkedin.com/in/dinesh-sudagoni, linked from the website) could not be read from outside (research section 2). Who can settle it: he can, by reading the profile while signed in to LinkedIn.
  2. Full-time from day one, or a contract first? The post says “Employment: Full-time”; the company’s own website says its hiring is a “3-month contract with full-time conversion for strong performers” (research section 7). Who can settle it: the founder, in writing.
  3. Which products would this seat own? The website shows BRAHMO (a knowledge layer for AI tools), Einstein IQ (operations intelligence and a voice agent for restaurant and other chains) and Netra AI (camera monitoring on edge devices). The post speaks of healthcare and legal and keeps product details for the interview. Who can settle it: the founder.
  4. How big is the team, and who would report to him? An April 2026 advertisement spoke of a “4-person team”; LinkedIn shows four employees; the post says “a small team”. Whether the people are employees or contractors, and where they sit, is not public. Who can settle it: the founder.
  5. Is there a technical lead today? A contract-to-hire “Lead Engineer” was advertised from 26 April to 26 May 2026, and the website still lists one. Whether that person was hired, and how this seat relates to that role, is unknown (research section 4). Who can settle it: the founder.
  6. Are the Java and machine-learning lines firm? The poster added “6+ years of work experience with Machine Learning” and “6+ years of work experience with Java”; the post’s own technical environment names neither. Who can settle it: the founder, as the poster.
  7. How is the company funded, and how long is its runway? No funding is announced; the register shows a paid-up capital of ₹10,000 as of August 2026 (research section 3). Who can settle it: the founder.
  8. Are there paying customers or live pilots? The website calls Einstein IQ “Live” and BRAHMO in “Early Sales” but names no customer. Who can settle it: the founder.
  9. Where is the company actually based, and is there a United States entity? LinkedIn says San Francisco; the website and the register say Hyderabad. Who can settle it: the founder.
  10. Office days, travel and on-call. The post says on-site in Hyderabad, and the website names a Kondapur office. Nothing says how often travel happens (the website mentions “USA, Europe & India”) or whether the lead carries a production pager. Who can settle it: the founder.
  11. Which data rules apply to the healthcare and legal work? India’s Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act and Rules, and, if any customer is in the United States, the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA). Whether the products hold patient or client data at all is not public (research section 6). Who can settle it: the founder.
Forcing questions for the founder

A forcing question asks for a specific answer, so that the answer settles something. There is no recruiter here: the founder wrote the post and reads the applications, so every question goes to him. These come from the research; they are not interview preparation, which waits until he asks for it. Ask them as curiosity, never as a challenge. (Q stands for question.)

If the call runs short, get Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q5: the terms, the products, the team, and the runway.

Q1 Full-time or contract-to-hire?

“Your posting says full-time, and the careers section of your website mentions a three-month contract before conversion. Which applies to this role?”

Good answer: a clear “full-time from day one”, confirmed later in the offer letter. Weak answer: “we start everyone on contract” for a principal seat, or no clear answer.

The company’s own pages disagree (research section 7). The answer changes what he is being offered more than any other single fact.

Q2 Which products, and which come first?

“Which products would I own in the first six months: BRAHMO, Einstein IQ, Netra AI, or the healthcare and legal products the posting mentions? Which one matters most right now?”

Good answer: one or two named products, with a clear first priority. Weak answer: “all of them”, with no order.

The website shows three different kinds of engineering: a knowledge system, a voice agent and camera models on edge devices. A seat that owns all of them with a small team is a very different job from one that owns BRAHMO (research section 2).

Q3 The team today

“How many engineers are on the team today, are they employees or contractors, and would they all report to me? Is there a lead engineer in place now?”

Good answer: named roles, a clear reporting line, and what happened to the April lead-engineer search. Weak answer: “we are hiring the team”, meaning he would start alone.

The post asks him to “lead a small engineering team”; the public record shows about four people and a lead search running since April (research section 4).

Q4 What the founder hands over

“Today, who reviews code and decides on architecture? What would you stop doing in my first month?”

Good answer: concrete handovers, such as code review and release decisions. Weak answer: the founder keeps final say on technical choices.

The post promises “You will own engineering execution and technical decisions”. The answer shows whether that ownership is real.

Q5 Funding and runway

“How is the company funded today, and how many months of runway does it have at the planned team size?”

Good answer: a number of months, and the source: founders, customers, or named investors. Weak answer: “we are about to raise”, with no runway figure.

The company was incorporated in July 2026 and has announced no funding (research section 3). Stability is the main risk of an early-stage seat.

Q6 Customers

“Which of the products have paying customers or pilots running today?”

Good answer: named pilots or customers, even if anonymised, and what they use. Weak answer: only projections.

The website calls Einstein IQ “Live” and shows large per-customer estimates but names no customer (research section 5). The post’s “Success” section is about products that work “in realistic user workflows”, so real users matter.

Q7 The Java and machine-learning lines

“The LinkedIn screening asks for six years each of Java and machine learning, but the posting describes a Python and TypeScript stack. Are those two lines firm requirements?”

Good answer: “No, the posting’s stack is what matters.” Weak answer: Java is needed for a product not mentioned in the post.

If they are firm, they change how the application should be framed; if they are LinkedIn defaults, they can be set aside (research section 4).

Q8 Healthcare and legal data

“Do the healthcare and legal products hold patient records or client documents today, and which rules do you build to: India’s DPDP Act, or HIPAA for United States customers?”

Good answer: a clear statement of what data is held and where, and who owns compliance. Weak answer: “we will look at that later”.

The post makes him responsible for “access controls, sensitive-data handling, audit trails, and safeguards”. India’s DPDP duties, including security safeguards, apply from 13 May 2027 (research section 6).

Q9 Office, travel and on-call

“Is the role five days a week at the Kondapur office? How often would I travel, and would I be on call for production issues?”

Good answer: clear days, a travel estimate, and an on-call rota shared with the team. Weak answer: “whenever needed” for both.

No source mentions travel or on-call; the website mentions “USA, Europe & India” (research section 4).

Q10 How AI-assisted work is reviewed

“Your earlier posting said AI coding tools generate most of the implementation. What review and testing gates do you have today, and which would I be expected to build?”

Good answer: what exists now (tests, evaluation suites, review rules) and what is missing. Weak answer: nothing exists yet and it is all expected at once.

The post asks him to “Establish automated tests, AI evaluation suites, regression checks, and end-to-end acceptance tests”; the answer shows the starting point (research section 2).
