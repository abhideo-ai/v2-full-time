1. A production system I owned end to end

At VoltusWave I was embedded with a chat-based care platform, and I owned two systems on it from design through production.

The first was the chat service itself. A single patient message fanned out into about 50 downstream calls. I put in Datadog first, to see what was really failing, and brought production errors under 3%. Then I rewrote the core chat service from Node.js to Go, proved 100,000 concurrent connections in a burst test, and designed a two-stream Amazon Kinesis architecture for headroom against a real spike. More than 10,000 patients moved onto the platform’s own app from a third-party messaging tool.

The second was clinical precedent search on the same platform. A doctor on a call asks: have we treated someone like this before, and what happened? Nine years of history went into an append-only event spine on DynamoDB; precedent cards are served from Elasticsearch dense-vector search through FastAPI with a two-tier Redis cache, and the models are self-hosted inside the client’s private cloud, so patient data never leaves it. Three decisions mattered: the graph database stays off the serving path, no language-model call sits in the ranking path, and a case missing any of seven required clinical fields gets a plain cohort count, not a confident-looking match.

One decision I would change today: in the chat service, room membership lived in a search index, and every message updated each member’s unread state. I would move that relational, read-heavy part to PostgreSQL behind read replicas, connection pooling and a cache, and keep the append-only message stream on DynamoDB and Kinesis. OpenAI’s account of scaling PostgreSQL for ChatGPT, one primary with nearly 50 read replicas and the write-heavy work moved to sharded stores, is the pattern I would follow.

2. How I verify my work

Before launch, I wrote an evaluation contract for that service: a stratified set of 100+ golden queries with a labelling and adjudication rule, and floors for ranking quality, recall per programme, warm-path latency and zero cross-tenant results, wired into an offline harness and version-pinned to the exact dataset, model and index behind every score. Continuous integration (CI) runs the exact production call path against a mock model, with no keys and no network, and re-running the data load over the same records must give an identical event spine and snapshot-manifest hash.

On top of that I run structured adversarial reviews of the design and of the runnable prototype. One review of the prototype caught a real gap between design and code: the cold-start quality gate checked only 2 of its 7 required clinical fields. I aligned it to all 7, and the next review round confirmed the fix and approved it. On another service, review caught a real sign flip between two versions of a pipeline; it became a parity fixture that the downstream consumer must reproduce exactly.
