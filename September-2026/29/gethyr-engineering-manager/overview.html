Glossary: every acronym and unfamiliar term, expanded

This is a recruiter-led search for an engineering leader in India, so two vocabularies meet: engineering management, and how recruitment agencies work. Each entry says what the thing is and why it matters for this seat. The full list of acronyms, on every page of this workspace, is at the end.

Manager and individual-contributor tracks

Engineering Manager (EM)
A manager who leads one team of engineers: hires them, helps them grow, handles performance, and is answerable for what the team delivers. Usually writes less code than the engineers. Why it matters: it is the first title in this post, and a management track rather than the individual-contributor seats this workspace usually looks for.
Senior Engineering Manager
A more senior manager, usually with a larger scope: a bigger team, several teams, or managers reporting to them. Why it matters: the post offers both titles, so the level will be decided by the client, probably from his experience and the interviews.
Individual contributor (IC)
An engineer whose job is the technical work itself rather than managing people: senior engineer, staff, principal, architect. Why it matters: this workspace is built for IC seats; this post is a manager title, so the questions are how many people report to it and how hands-on it is.
Hands-on technical leader
A leader who still does technical work: designs systems, reviews code, and sometimes writes it, rather than only managing. Why it matters: it is one of the post’s four must-haves, and the part of the seat closest to IC work.
Direct reports
The people who report to a manager. Why it matters: the number tells how much of the week goes to people management.
Performance management
Setting goals, giving feedback and running reviews for the people on a team. Why it matters: GetHyr’s earlier post for a similar seat listed it as part of the job.
Startup mindset
Recruiter shorthand for working with few resources, deciding fast and doing whatever the company needs. Why it matters: it is a must-have in the post; it is a description, not something that can be checked.

The technical must-haves

Backend engineering
Building the server side of software: the services, application programming interfaces (APIs), data stores and processing that users do not see. Why it matters: the first must-have, “Strong Backend Engineering experience”.
Java
A widely used programming language for backend systems. Why it matters: the post says “Java preferred”, which reads as a preference rather than a gate.
Spring Boot
A popular framework for building Java services. Why it matters: named in GetHyr’s July post for a similar seat, not in this one.
System design
Deciding how a software system is put together: its parts, how they talk, how data flows, and how it copes with growth and failure. Why it matters: the second must-have, and a usual interview topic for this level.
Distributed systems
Software that runs across many machines that must work together, such as services, queues and databases spread over a network. It brings problems such as partial failure, consistency and scale. Why it matters: named with system design in the second must-have.
Microservices
A way of building a system as many small services, each doing one job and deployed on its own. Why it matters: a common shape of the backend systems this post describes.

AI product and platform

AI product
A product whose features use artificial intelligence (AI), for example a large language model (LLM) answering questions or an agent carrying out tasks. Why it matters: “AI product/platform experience is a strong plus”.
AI platform
The internal systems that let a company build and run AI features: model serving, data pipelines, evaluation, monitoring. Why it matters: the other half of the same line; which half the client means is an open question.
Agent, agentic
Software in which an AI model plans and takes steps, such as calling tools or other services, rather than only answering. Why it matters: much of GetHyr’s recent AI hiring is for “agentic” systems.
Spec-driven development (SDD)
Writing a precise specification first and having engineers, and increasingly AI coding tools, build from it. Why it matters: GetHyr’s July post for a similar seat asked for “Real experience driving Spec Driven Development”.

Recruiters and agencies

Recruitment agency
A firm that finds candidates for other companies and is paid by those companies, not by the candidate. Why it matters: GetHyr is the agency here; the employer is someone else.
Client (of a recruiter)
The company that pays the agency to fill the seat: the actual employer. Why it matters: this post does not name it.
Contingent search
The agency is paid only if its candidate is hired; the client may use several agencies at once. Why it matters: if this search is contingent, other agencies may be sending candidates to the same client.
Retained search
The client pays one agency to run the search exclusively, usually for director-level seats and above. Why it matters: the other model; which one this is has not been said.
Executive search, headhunting
Recruiting for senior and leadership seats, usually by approaching people directly. Why it matters: GetHyr lists it among its services.
Hiring platform, applicant tracking system (ATS)
Software that companies use to track candidates through hiring. Why it matters: HyrHQ, in the poster’s headline, offers one; it is not the channel for this post.
Talent pool, candidate pool
The set of candidates an agency keeps on file. Why it matters: a résumé sent to an agency may stay in its pool; ask how it will be used.

Submission and consent

Submission
The moment a recruiter sends a candidate’s résumé to the client. Why it matters: it should happen only for a named company and with his go-ahead.
Consent to submit
The candidate’s permission, ideally in writing, for one submission to one client for one role. Why it matters: India’s data-protection law expects consent to be specific and limited to its purpose.
Duplicate submission
The same candidate sent to the same company twice, by two agencies, or by an agency and the candidate himself. Why it matters: it can cause a fee dispute and make the candidate look disorganised.
Right to represent (RTR)
Written permission from a candidate for a recruiter to represent him for one specific role, sometimes exclusively. Why it matters: if GetHyr asks for one, check that it covers one role and one company and has an end date.
Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act)
India’s personal-data law. Why it matters: its section 6(1) describes what valid consent is; how it applies to recruiters in practice is not settled on these pages.

Notice period and the post’s other terms

Notice period
The time an employee must work after resigning before leaving, set by the employment contract; in India often 30 to 90 days. Why it matters: the post asks candidates to state it.
Hybrid
Some days in the office, some at home. Why it matters: how many days, and which Bengaluru office, is an open question.
CTC
Cost to company.

Company-register terms

Private limited company (Pvt Ltd)
The most common form of Indian company: owned privately, shares not traded on a stock exchange. Why it matters: GetHyr’s legal form.
Director
A person legally responsible for running a company, recorded on the register. Why it matters: a Raj Singh is one of GetHyr’s two directors.
Annual general meeting (AGM)
The yearly shareholders’ meeting a company must hold and report. Why it matters: the dates shown by the register mirrors disagree.
Charge
A secured loan or mortgage recorded against a company. Why it matters: none is recorded for GetHyr.
WHOIS
The public record of who registered an internet domain and when (the name comes from “who is”). Why it matters: it dates gethyr.com to June 2019.
