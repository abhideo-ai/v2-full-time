Forcing questions for the recruiter or the hiring manager

A forcing question asks for a specific answer, so that the answer settles something. These come from the research; they are not interview preparation, which waits until he asks for it. Q1 to Q5 suit the recruiter, Raj Singh at GetHyr; Q6 to Q9 suit the client’s hiring manager (Q stands for question). Ask them as curiosity, never as a challenge.

If there is only one message before he sends anything, ask Q1 and Q2 together: which company, and that nothing goes to it without his go-ahead.

Q1 Which company is it?

“Before I send my résumé, could you tell me which company this Engineering Manager role in Bengaluru is for, and a line on the team and product?”

Good answer: a company name and a short description, or a clear reason it is confidential with a promise to name it before any submission. Weak answer: “send your résumé first and we’ll share details”.

Settles open item 1, and with it almost every other question. Without it he cannot check the company or avoid a double submission (research section 6).

Q2 Will you submit me only with my go-ahead?

“Could you confirm that my résumé goes only to this one company for this one role, and only after I say yes?”

Good answer: a plain yes, in writing. Weak answer: vagueness about “similar roles in our network”.

Settles open item 9. India’s data-protection law expects consent to be specific and limited to its purpose, and GetHyr publishes no privacy notice on its website (research section 8).

Q3 Is this the same search as your July post?

“Is this the same role as the Senior Engineering Manager, Java and Spring Boot, in Bengaluru that GetHyr posted in July, or a different company?”

Good answer: a clear yes or no. Weak answer: none; it only tells him less.

Settles open item 2. If it is the same search, the July post’s requirements (a current manager title, spec-driven development) probably apply (research section 3).

Q4 Is a current manager title a must?

“Is the client looking only for people who are Engineering Managers today, or also for senior hands-on engineers and architects who have led teams?”

Good answer: either way, as long as it is clear. Weak answer: “just send it and we’ll see”.

Settles open items 3 and 5: whether there is a gate before the technical fit matters at all.

Q5 Are you the only agency, and how do office days work?

“Are you working on this search alone, or with other agencies? And how many days a week in the office, at which location?”

Good answer: a straight answer on both. Weak answer: not knowing the office pattern for a hybrid role.

Settles open items 8 and 9. With several agencies, the chance of a duplicate submission is higher.

Q6 How big is the team, and how hands-on?

“How many engineers would report to this role, and how much of the week would I spend on design, code review and code?”

Good answer: a number of reports and a rough share of the week. Weak answer: “it depends”, with no example of a week.

Settles open item 4: how far this manager seat is from the individual-contributor seats he is aiming for (research section 5).

Q7 What does “AI product/platform” mean here?

“When the role asks for AI product or platform experience, is that building AI features for customers, building an internal platform for AI, or using AI tools in how the team works?”

Good answer: one of the three, with a current example. Weak answer: a buzzword with no product behind it.

Settles open item 6, and tells agent 2’s score which reading of the must-have applies.

Q8 How strict is Java?

“Is Java the main language of the backend today, and would strong backend work in other languages count?”

Good answer: the actual stack, named. Weak answer: “Java is a must” with no stack detail.

Settles open item 7. “Java preferred” reads as a preference, not a gate, but only the client can confirm.

Q9 Travel and on-call?

“Does the role involve regular travel, or an on-call rotation for the manager?”

Good answer: a plain description. Weak answer: none expected; it is simply unknown today.

Settles open item 10. Neither could be researched, because the client is not named.
