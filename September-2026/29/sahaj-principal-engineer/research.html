1. The verdict
A real, open, hands-on individual-contributor seat at a small, founder-run engineering consultancy whose published work matches the posting; the open questions are which client project he would join, what “on-site” means in Hyderabad, and how a title-free, manager-free company would recognise a principal-level engineer, not whether the job exists

What it is. Sahaj Software calls itself “an artisanal technology services company” that builds “technology-based solutions” for clients, with a focus on software engineering, data engineering and artificial intelligence (AI) Self-claim (Sahaj, Careers). In plain terms it is a consultancy: client companies pay it to design and build their systems, and its engineers work in small teams on one client at a time. Its Indian company was incorporated in Bengaluru on 27 March 2014 Register; its co-founders had just left Thoughtworks, another software consultancy Independent. LinkedIn now lists its headquarters as Austin, Texas, and its size as 201–500 employees, with 234 people linked to the page; Tracxn counts 233 as of 31 August 2026 Checked by us (section 2).

Is it real? Yes Checked by us. LinkedIn’s public page for job 4470146279 was live on 29 September 2026, posted about 25 September 2026 (worked out from LinkedIn’s “3 days ago”; the page gives no date), with 142 applicants, posted from Sahaj’s own LinkedIn company page by a member of its hiring team. Its text matches his paste word for word, apart from line breaks (section 4).

What kind of seat. An individual-contributor (IC) seat. The posting says “You won’t be: A people manager”, and Sahaj’s own book says everyone is “called a Solution Consultant” under a “no roles, no grades” approach, with “no front-line managers” Self-claim. An employee review from 2021 says the same: “everyone has the same title regardless of what work they do” Independent. So “Principal Engineer” is a label for the job market, not a grade inside Sahaj Inference (section 4).

Does the work match? Mostly Checked by us. The ClickHouse example matches a February 2026 post by a Sahaj engineer almost detail for detail, though the post describes 85 billion potential data points and tested on a far smaller sample. The mobile-wallet example matches Sahaj’s published case study for Comviva. The vehicle-telemetry example matches Sahaj’s published connected-vehicle work, but not the “billions of messages a day” or the Kafka detail. For the publisher example, Sahaj publicly names Springer Nature as a client, but nothing published describes a peer-review product (section 3).

The cautions. Career growth is the most common complaint in employee reviews: AmbitionBox rates promotions lowest of its categories (2.8 of 5), and 4 of 10 written Blind reviews raise it, though Blind’s growth rating is 4.1; the flat structure that makes this an IC seat is also why reviewers say growth “becomes stagnant”. Sahaj’s own history includes laying off its whole ten-person United States (US) team in 2018, an aggressive hiring push for a planned stock-market listing in 2022–23 that it later paused, and a hiring pause in Hyderabad. The Hyderabad office is young and sits in a co-working space. “On-site” on LinkedIn does not match what employees report (two or three office days a week) (section 6).

This describes the company and the seat. It is not a recommendation about whether to apply; that decision is his.

  • Inference Hyderabad is Sahaj’s youngest and probably smallest Indian office. A principal-level hire there would be one of a small group, and client projects are likely staffed across offices, as the book describes for Bengaluru and Chennai.

How the consulting model works day to day

  • Client work, not its own product. Sahaj builds for client companies: its client-stories page carries testimonials from, among others, Talon and Clear Channel (outdoor advertising), Springer Nature (academic publishing), Comviva (mobile payments), Axi and the Norwegian Refugee Council Self-claim (Sahaj, Client Stories).
  • Engagements of six to eighteen months. A Sahaj engineer wrote that “Technical consulting involves working with the client for around 6–18 months”; that clients “expect more from consultants in unit time compared to their own employees”; that “We are not always working on projects”, and between projects people sharpen their skills; and that the downside is less “sense of belonging” and not getting to “chisel and refine something overtime” Self-claim, an engineer’s own account (Software Consulting — pros and cons).
  • Small teams. Sahaj’s own “Senior and Lead Engineer – Full Stack” posting describes “small, collaborative teams (2-5 people) where a well-founded argument holds more weight than the years of experience” and “short, iterative sprints” against “client-defined deadlines” Self-claim (Sahaj, Full Stack posting).
  • Who places people on projects. Each office has an “office lead”, a Solution Consultant who also works on projects, who helps with “the aspirations of people in the office, their assignment to projects” Self-claim (book, chapter 11).
  • Offshore first. After the 2018 US setback, the book says, “We would primarily sell offshore rather than on-site services”: most work is delivered from Sahaj’s own offices, not from the client’s Self-claim.
  • A current push into AI-assisted delivery. Sahaj now sells “intelligent Engineering”, a way of wiring AI tools into every stage of a client’s software delivery, and a September 2026 post by a Sahaj consultant reports measured productivity gains in two client teams Self-claim (intelligent Engineering; Building an AI-First Software Engineering Organisation). Inference The posting does not mention AI, but a principal-level consultant is likely to meet this work.

5. The engineering culture, as publicly described

All of this is Sahaj’s own material or its engineers’ own posts Self-claim, except where marked. It shows what Sahaj says and writes about, not how every team works.

  • Test-driven development (TDD). Two Sahaj engineers proposed “Test Driven Generation” in May 2023: write the tests first, let an AI model generate code to pass them, then refactor, a variant of TDD’s “Red-Green-Refactor” cycle (Test Driven Generation). The Full Stack posting asks for an understanding of “practices like Test Driven Development”, and the intelligent Engineering page lists a “test-driven mindset” among its practices. There are also posts on testing data pipelines on Databricks and on why unit tests fail even when they pass.
  • Pairing. Two 2024 posts by Sahaj engineers describe pair programming from practice. One notes, “I couldn’t understand why clients would pay extra for it”, before describing its benefits and its costs (“Pair programming does feel slower at times”) (Pair Programming: Challenges and Rewards; Top 5 Benefits of Pair Programming). Inference Pairing appears to be a normal, client-visible way of working, which fits the posting’s “You mentor through pairing and feedback”.
  • Refactoring. “Creating a habit: Refactoring” (May 2024) builds on Martin Fowler’s book Refactoring (sahaj.ai). The Comviva case study describes a “Strangulation Approach”: replacing a monolith piece by piece while it stays in production. The book describes a mobile-banking rebuild where “We innovated like we do, refactored the whole thing”.
  • Polyglot teams. The same ten languages appear in the Full Stack posting and in this one, and the posting says “Work across languages when needed”. A Blind reviewer (April 2025) wrote: “You can choose to work on frontend, backend, infra, ci/cd. You just have to ask for it” (CI/CD: continuous integration and continuous delivery) Independent.
  • Writing and talks. Engineers publish deep technical posts under their own names: the ClickHouse schema post, a two-part post, “DOOH programmatic bidding: Scaling to ~15K requests per second with Java” (DOOH: digital out-of-home advertising), and a post on multi-tenant Kafka consumers in Spring Boot (June 2025). Sahaj runs a community meetup series, DevDay, in its offices; the Hyderabad chapter reported a community of “4,000+ members” with “monthly DevDays with 90-100 participants” in February 2025.
  • Hiring as a conversation. The engineer who set up Sahaj’s Hyderabad office wrote that he invited candidates “not for an interview, but for a discussion”, looking at “problem-solving, adaptability, and pragmatic thinking” over experience with a specific technology. An Org Builder says Sahaj commits to “giving thoughtful feedback, even when the outcome isn’t a yes”.
