Eli Lilly and Company: Sr. Principal Engineer AI/ML System Integration

What Lilly is, what its engineering centre in Bengaluru does, what this seat is on Lilly’s own ladder, what Lilly has said in public about its artificial intelligence (AI) and commercial technology, and what could not be settled. Everything here was read on 29 September 2026.

The seat is real and open on Lilly’s own hiring system: requisition R-112121, posted on 23 September 2026, open until 28 October 2026, with the same text as his LinkedIn paste. The JD (job description) itself calls it “a senior individual contributor” role. Its team is not named anywhere; the commercial wording and the postings around it point to Lilly’s United States (US) commercial technology teams in Bengaluru, but that is our inference.

How to read the labels. Every finding carries one of these:

  • Register An official filing: here, Lilly’s annual and quarterly reports to the US Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC).
  • Independent A source Lilly does not control: press reports, a vendor’s press release, employee-review sites.
  • Checked by us Read directly on 29 September 2026: Lilly’s careers site and its Workday hiring system through their public data feeds, and the public LinkedIn job page.
  • Self-claim What Lilly says about itself: its website, press releases, job postings, talks by its staff.
  • Inference Our own reading of the evidence. Useful, but not a fact.
1. The verdict
A real, open, individual-contributor architecture seat in Lilly’s Bengaluru technology centre, posted on Lilly’s own system on 23 September 2026; the open questions are which team it serves, how Lilly levels it, and how much of it is hands-on building versus integration governance, not whether the job exists.

What it is. Eli Lilly and Company is a US medicine company, founded in Indianapolis in 1876, with about 50,000 employees at the end of 2025 and revenue of $65.2 billion in 2025 Register (section 2). The seat is “Sr. Principal Engineer AI/ML System Integration” in Bengaluru. The JD calls it “a senior individual contributor and cross-team technical leadership role” that “owns the technical integration strategy” for AI and machine learning (ML) applications across “enterprise platforms, data ecosystems, commercial workflows, and third-party services” Self-claim.

Is it real? Yes. Lilly’s own careers site and its Workday hiring system both carry requisition R-112121 with the same text, posted on 23 September 2026, accepting applications, with an end date of 28 October 2026 Checked by us (section 6).

Where it sits on the ladder. Lilly’s own Bengaluru posting for a “Senior Principal Machine Learning Engineer” says that title is “at level R4 on our engineering ladder”, and other Lilly postings put “Technical Lead Architect” and an “Advisor” role at R5 Self-claim. So a Sr. Principal is probably R4, one step below the R5 roles. Whether this posting is also R4 is Inference, and the same title asks for 11–15 years there but only 8+ years here (section 5).

Which team. The JD names no team. Its preferred background (“commercial analytics, CRM, field engagement, or customer engagement platforms”) and two Bengaluru postings from the same day for Lilly USA commercial and market-access technology (R-112129, R-111955) suggest the Lilly USA commercial technology side of the Bengaluru centre Inference (section 5).

What the work touches. In public, Lilly describes an AI “field assistant” built on Databricks for about 12,000 global sales professionals (Databricks’ write-up of the talk), a global move to Veeva Vault CRM (customer relationship management) announced on 11 August 2026, and a Bengaluru AI engineering team building a commercial “agentic AI application platform” Self-claim (section 8). That is the kind of system an “AI/ML System Integration” architect would join up Inference.

The research describes the company and the seat. It makes no recommendation; the decision is his.

2. Lilly: what it does, its size and where it is based

The JD opens with two claims about Lilly: it is “Headquartered in Indianapolis, Indiana”, and it has “over 50,000 employees around the globe”. We checked both against Lilly’s annual report to the SEC (the Form 10-K for 2025, filed on 12 February 2026). Lilly’s main website (lilly.com) refused our automated fetches for most pages, so the SEC filing is the main source here.

What Lilly does

  • Lilly was “incorporated in 1901 in Indiana to succeed to the drug manufacturing business founded in Indianapolis, Indiana, in 1876 by Colonel Eli Lilly”. It works in “a single business segment—human pharmaceutical products”: it discovers, develops, makes and sells medicines. Register (Form 10-K for 2025)
  • Its head office is “Lilly Corporate Center, Indianapolis, Indiana 46285”. Register (same filing)
  • Revenue was $65.2 billion in 2025, up 45% from $45.0 billion in 2024. Two medicines drove it: Mounjaro ($23.0 billion) and Zepbound ($13.5 billion), both based on the molecule tirzepatide, for diabetes and obesity. Register (same filing)
  • Growth continued in 2026: revenue for April to June 2026 was $23.0 billion, against $15.6 billion a year earlier. Register (Form 10-Q for the quarter to 30 June 2026)

How big it is

  • “At the end of 2025, we employed approximately 50,000 people, including approximately 27,000 employees outside the U.S. Our employees include approximately 12,000 people engaged in research and development activities.” Register (Form 10-K for 2025)
  • One small oddity: another Lilly posting in Bengaluru from the same week (R-112129) still carries an older paragraph that says “Our 35,000 employees around the world”. That is stale boilerplate in Lilly’s own text, not a contradiction of the filing. Checked by us

Who runs technology and AI

  • The annual report says Lilly’s Chief Information and Digital Officer (CIDO) “is a member of our Executive Committee and leads our information technology, cybersecurity, digital health, and advanced analytics and data science functions”, and that the Chief Information Security Officer (CISO) reports to the CIDO. Register
  • The CIDO is Diogo Rau, “executive vice president and chief information and digital officer”. Self-claim (Lilly press release, 28 Oct 2025)
  • Lilly appointed its first Chief AI Officer, Thomas Fuchs, in October 2024, to oversee AI across drug discovery, clinical trials, manufacturing, commercial activities and internal functions. Independent (CIO Dive, 9 Oct 2024). In October 2025 Lilly itself called him “senior vice president and chief AI officer”. Self-claim

4. Lilly in India, and its Bengaluru engineering centre

Three sites, three jobs

  • Gurgaon (Gurugram), since 1993: Lilly’s India page says “Lilly in Gurgaon is present in India since 1993 and serves as a hub for our commercial operations”, meaning selling medicines in India. Self-claim (Lilly in India)
  • Bengaluru, since 2016: the same page says “Lilly in Bengaluru stands as a critical global hub accelerating the development and delivery of innovative medicines through cross-functional collaboration and deep scientific expertise”, and that it brings together “capabilities from Lilly Research Laboratories, Commercial, and Tech@Lilly”. Self-claim. The press reported it as the Lilly Capability Center India (LCCI), launched in Bengaluru in 2016 as Lilly’s first such centre in India. Independent (Medical Dialogues, 12 Jan 2025)
  • Hyderabad, since mid-2025: a second centre focused on “artificial intelligence (AI), automation, cloud computing, and software product engineering”. It was opened by Diogo Rau in August 2025, in about 220,000 square feet in Gachibowli, with 100 people on board and a plan for 1,500. Independent (Medical Dialogues, 5 Aug 2025)

What Lilly calls it now

The name has moved. In September 2026, Lilly’s Hyderabad postings say “Lilly Capability Centre India (LCCI), Hyderabad, is Lilly’s premier Global Technology Hub”, while a Bengaluru posting (R-107037) still speaks of “Indy and LCCI based Leadership and LCCI HR”. The Bengaluru technology postings mostly say “Lilly Bengaluru” or “Tech@Lilly”, and Lilly’s hiring system files this seat under “IN: Lilly Bengaluru”. Checked by us. Inference “LCCI” now seems to be the name for Lilly’s India capability centres as a group, not only the Bengaluru one.

The legal employer for this seat is Eli Lilly Services India Pvt Ltd, the name on the Workday record Checked by us. That company’s registration number begins U72200KA2015, which means it was registered in Karnataka in 2015, the year before the centre opened Inference from the number’s format; the register page itself refused our fetch.

What Bengaluru builds, from Lilly’s open postings

On 29 September 2026 Lilly’s careers feed listed 49 open postings in India: 25 in Bengaluru and 24 in Hyderabad Checked by us. The Bengaluru technology postings describe these teams in Lilly’s own words Self-claim:

  • Customer Insights & Analytics (CI&A): “started in 2017”, “more than 100 staff members”, supporting “Marketing, Sales, Medical Affairs, and other functions”. Inside it, an AI Engineering team “designs, builds, and operates the platforms that take Lilly’s commercial generative-AI and agentic-AI solutions from prototype to enterprise-scale production - including our agentic AI application platform and its transition to a modern, cloud-hosted architecture, and CI&A’s participation in Lilly’s broader enterprise agentic AI platform initiative.” (R-112474) Inference R-110886 names an “AI Engineering team” and asks for “LangGraph or comparable agent frameworks”, but not CI&A; that it is the same team is our inference.
  • Lilly USA Commercial Tech: a hands-on “Systems Architect” seat “Reporting to the Senior Director - Customer Tech Innovation India”, working “alongside global CTI Architecture leaders” (R-112129, posted the same day as this seat).
  • Lilly Value & Access (LVA), Lilly USA Tech: a “Senior Principal- AI / ML Engineer, Market Access Intelligence”: agents, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and “Text2SQL” over payer and real-world data (R-111955, posted the same day).
  • Clinical, regulatory and product research technology (“CTI-MD”): Veeva Vault, semantic layers and knowledge graphs for regulatory and manufacturing data (R-109513, R-109017, R-109473, R-109186).
  • Cybersecurity: a “Cybersecurity AI Platform Advisor (R5)” and a “Cyber Intel Analyst” (R-112358, R-112357).

Inference On Lilly’s ladder a Senior Principal engineer is most likely R4: one level below the R5 “Technical Lead Architect” and “Advisor” roles, and above a Principal (probably R3). Two things are unknown. First, whether this seat is also R4: its experience bar (8+ years) is much lower than the Senior Principal ML posting’s (11–15 years). Second, what sits at the top of the individual-contributor ladder: no open Lilly posting uses the title “Distinguished”, so we could not place a Distinguished Engineer level. One Lilly posting also notes that jobs are sometimes “banded” across levels (“ex: P1-P3, R1-R2, B1-B3”), so a posting’s level can be a range.

Which Lilly organisation it likely sits in

Inference No source names this seat’s team. Four clues point to the Lilly USA commercial technology side of the Bengaluru centre (the part that builds for Lilly’s US sales, marketing and market-access teams):

  1. The JD names “commercial workflows” in its first sentence, and prefers “commercial analytics, CRM, field engagement, or customer engagement platforms”.
  2. It was posted on 23 September 2026, the same day as two Lilly USA technology seats in the same office: the “Systems Architect - Lilly USA Commercial Tech” (R-112129) and the “Senior Principal- AI / ML Engineer, Market Access Intelligence, LVA, Lilly USA Tech” (R-111955).
  3. The JD’s preferred platforms (“Databricks, Azure AI, Azure OpenAI, AWS AI/ML services”) overlap with the tools the commercial AI Engineering team in CI&A lists (Amazon Web Services (AWS), Databricks and Unity Catalog).
  4. Lilly USA’s public AI field assistant is a system that joins “dozens of fragmented systems” (section 8), which is exactly the kind of work an “AI/ML System Integration” architect does.

It could equally sit in a central Tech@Lilly platform or architecture team that serves several functions; the JD’s list of partners (“platform owners, enterprise architects, cybersecurity, privacy, compliance, vendors”) fits either. The recruiter can settle it (open questions).

A named contact on Lilly’s record

Lilly’s careers-site record for R-112121 fills its “hiringManager” field with the name Arunabh Sinha Checked by us. We did not research this person. On careers sites like this one, that field sometimes holds the recruiter rather than the manager, so treat it as a name to confirm, not as the hiring manager.

7. Lilly’s AI and platform work, as Lilly describes it

Most of Lilly’s public AI news is about drug discovery. It shows how much Lilly is spending on AI, but it is not this seat’s work. The commercial side, which is closer to this seat, is in section 8.

  • NVIDIA supercomputer and “AI factory” (28 October 2025). Lilly announced “the most powerful supercomputer owned and operated by a pharmaceutical company”: “the world’s first NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD with DGX B300 systems”, with “more than 1,000 B300 GPUs”. Its subtitle lists “manufacturing, medical imaging and enterprise AI agents” as uses beyond discovery. Self-claim (Lilly press release)
  • NVIDIA co-innovation lab (12 January 2026). NVIDIA and Lilly said they would “Jointly Invest up to $1 Billion Over Five Years in Infrastructure and Research” for drug discovery. Self-claim (NVIDIA newsroom)
  • Lilly TuneLab (9 September 2025). An AI and ML platform that gives biotech companies access to drug-discovery models trained on Lilly’s data, hosted by a third party and using federated learning. Self-claim (Lilly press release)
  • OpenAI (June 2024). Reported as a collaboration to invent new antimicrobial medicines with OpenAI’s generative AI. Independent, but we could not open the article (C&EN), so this rests on search summaries only.
  • Databricks. Lilly’s US commercial AI field assistant is built on Databricks (section 8), and the Bengaluru AI Engineering team lists “Databricks & Unity Catalog” as core infrastructure. Self-claim
  • AWS. The same Bengaluru AI Engineering postings name AWS services (“EC2/ECS, S3, Lambda, IAM, CloudWatch or equivalent”), with Docker, Kubernetes, Prefect and GitHub Actions for deployment, and “Claude or comparable LLMs”, “LangGraph or comparable agent frameworks” and “vector databases (e.g. Pinecone)” for generative AI. Self-claim (R-110886, R-112474)
  • Microsoft Azure and Azure OpenAI. The JD prefers experience with them, but we found no Lilly announcement of an Azure or Azure OpenAI partnership. A third-party summary says Lilly runs Azure alongside AWS and Google Cloud Platform (GCP); we could not trace that to a Lilly source, so it is not used here.
  • How Lilly talks about AI risk. Lilly’s annual report: “We deploy AI and other emerging technologies in various facets of our operations”, with risks “including that AI-generated content, analyses, or recommendations we utilize could be inadequate”. Register (Form 10-K for 2025)
8. Lilly’s commercial and field-engagement technology

“Commercial” in a medicine company means the business of selling medicines: sales representatives visiting doctors, marketing, and market access (getting insurers to pay). The JD’s preferred background is exactly this area. The words are explained in the glossary.

  • How Lilly sells in the US. “Promotion of our major products in the U.S. includes engagement by employee or contracted sales representatives with physicians and other healthcare professionals.” Lilly also runs LillyDirect, “our direct-to-patient digital health care platform”. Register (Form 10-K for 2025)
  • An AI field assistant for about 12,000 global sales professionals (the number is Databricks’ write-up; Lilly does not state it). At Databricks’ Data + AI Summit (the 2026 agenda), Todd Walker, “Executive Director – Lilly USA Technology”, and Thomas Raborn, “Executive Director, Lilly US Field Operations”, presented “Driving Enterprise Transformation With an AI‑Enabled Field Assistant” Self-claim (session page). Databricks’ write-up of the talk says it was “deployed … to 12,000 global sales professionals”, joining data from “dozens of fragmented systems”, using Databricks Genie for questions in plain English, Unity Catalog for role-based access, vector search and RAG for documents, a “supervisor agent calling other data-related agents”, and a move from Llama to Anthropic’s Sonnet models Self-claim, reported by the vendor (Databricks write-up). We did not see the talk itself.
  • Veeva Vault CRM, globally (11 August 2026). Veeva announced that “Eli Lilly and Company (Lilly) has committed to Veeva Vault CRM globally”. Veeva calls Vault CRM “the technology foundation for agentic commercial”. Lilly’s Giuseppe Firenze, “senior vice president, U.S. HCP and field engagement hub”, is quoted: “We are excited to expand our strategic work with Veeva by moving to Vault CRM”. Independent (vendor release) (Veeva press release). Inference A CRM migration in progress means a lot of integration work between the CRM, data platforms and AI services.
  • Salesforce Marketing Cloud. Lilly is hiring a “Technical Lead - SFMC” in Bengaluru, asking for Salesforce Marketing Cloud, “Salesforce Data Cloud” and “Marketing Cloud Connect” integration experience. Self-claim (R-111748)
  • Market access. The LVA posting describes AI for “payer coverage, real-world evidence, pricing and reimbursement, and market access strategy”. Self-claim (R-111955)
9. Compliance that shapes this work, as Lilly’s own sources describe it
  • GxP and data integrity, on the research and manufacturing side. A Bengaluru posting for product research says “The function operates under strict GxP, data integrity, and audit-readiness requirements” (R-109186); a clinical one mentions “CSV readiness”, meaning computer system validation (R-109513). Self-claim. Inference GxP rules apply most to research, clinical and manufacturing systems. Commercial AI is more likely governed by privacy and promotional-compliance rules, but no Lilly source says which rules apply to this seat.
  • Privacy, including HIPAA. Lilly’s February 2025 policy paper, “Lilly AI Policy and Implications for Digital Health and Drug Development”, discusses how AI tools are “covered under existing privacy laws, such as HIPAA”, and says AI algorithms “should be free from bias and avoid exacerbating health inequities”. Self-claim (PDF). The PDF names Lilly as its author; we could not open the lilly.com page that links to it.
  • Security governance. The annual report describes an “Executive Information Security Governance function (EISG)” drawing on “information security, legal, finance, audit, and ethics and compliance”. Register
  • The JD’s own words. It asks the seat to “Promote Responsible AI principles, security-by-design, and privacy-by-design approaches”, to ensure “data quality, security, lineage, and compliance requirements are addressed”, and to put “human-in-the-loop controls” around AI. Self-claim
10. Green flags and red flags

Both lists are ranked by strength of evidence, not by how encouraging or alarming a point sounds.

Green flags

Strongest 1. The job is real, recent and open on Lilly’s own system. Requisition R-112121, posted on 23 September 2026, open until 28 October 2026, with the same text as LinkedIn. Sources: Workday record Checked by us

Strong 2. A company that is growing, with no job-cut programme in its filings. Revenue rose 45% in 2025 and about 48% in April to June 2026. Its restructuring charges in 2025 and the first half of 2026 are explained as litigation and acquisition costs, not workforce reductions. We also found no report of Lilly layoffs in 2025 or 2026. Sources: Form 10-K for 2025 · Form 10-Q, June 2026 Register

Strong 3. An individual-contributor seat, in Lilly’s own words. “This is a senior individual contributor and cross-team technical leadership role.” No direct reports are listed. Source: the JD Self-claim

Moderate 4. The commercial AI work is live, not a plan. Lilly USA Technology has presented an AI field assistant in use by about 12,000 global sales professionals (Databricks’ write-up of the talk), and a Bengaluru AI Engineering team is moving a commercial agentic AI platform to a cloud-hosted architecture. Sources: Data + AI Summit session · Databricks’ write-up · R-112474 Self-claim

Moderate 5. AI has senior sponsorship. A CIDO on the Executive Committee, a Chief AI Officer since October 2024, and large, public investments in AI infrastructure. Sources: Form 10-K for 2025 Register · CIO Dive Independent · Lilly and NVIDIA releases Self-claim

Moderate 6. Bengaluru is hiring senior AI people in the same week. Three senior AI and architecture seats in the same office were posted within a few days: this one, the Lilly USA Commercial Tech Systems Architect and the LVA Senior Principal AI/ML Engineer. Inference This looks like a function building up its AI architecture, not one stray opening. Source: Lilly careers feed Checked by us

Red flags and cautions

Strong evidence 1. The level is not stated, and the experience bar hints it may differ from the title. Lilly’s other Senior Principal posting in Bengaluru is R4 and asks for 11–15 years; this one asks for 8+. Nothing says whether this seat is R4 or banded across levels. Sources: R-110886, R-112121 Checked by us. Inference The recruiter should confirm the grade before an interview.

Moderate, self-reported 2. Middling employee ratings in India; promotions rated lowest. AmbitionBox, which covers all of Lilly in India (field sales included, not only technology), gives 3.2 out of 5 from about 700 reviews. By category: work-life balance 3.4, company culture 3.0, job security 3.0, skill development 3.0, work satisfaction 2.9, promotions 2.6. In its “work policies” panel, 84% of reviewers report hybrid working and 61% report no travel. A search summary gave Glassdoor’s Bengaluru rating as 3.6 from 428 reviews, but Glassdoor refused our fetch, so that figure is unverified. Source: AmbitionBox Independent

Moderate 3. On-site, and some Lilly India technology teams work shifts and weekends. LinkedIn marks this seat “On-site” and Lilly’s record sets no remote option. This posting says nothing about on-call or shifts. Two Hyderabad engineering postings at this seat’s own level, “Senior Principal Agentic Engineer” (R-110132) and “Principal Agentic Engineer” (R-111676), and one production-operations posting (R-110138), however, say “This team supports continuous operations and may require non-standard work hours, including some work on weekends and holidays” and ask candidates to be “open to working different shifts when required”, and a Bengaluru cybersecurity posting requires “Rotational on-call availability”. The seat’s own support duties (“operational support models”) are worth asking about. Sources: R-110132, R-111676, R-110138, R-112357 Self-claim

Moderate 4. The team is not named. The JD has no “about the team” paragraph, and the abbreviation “CTI” is used in Bengaluru postings for both commercial (“Customer Tech Innovation India”) and clinical (“CTI-MD”) groups. Which team this seat serves, and who it reports to, is not public. Sources: R-112121, R-112129, R-109513 Checked by us

Weak 5. Heavy competition, and a governance-heavy description. 106 applicants within about five days, on a promoted posting. Checked by us. The JD leans towards standards, reviews, governance and vendor management. Lilly’s own sibling posting (R-112129) takes care to say “This is not a slide-deck architecture role”; this one does not say how hands-on it is. Inference How much of the week is building versus reviewing is an open question, not a known problem.
