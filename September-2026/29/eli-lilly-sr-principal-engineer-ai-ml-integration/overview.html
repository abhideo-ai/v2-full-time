Glossary: every acronym and unfamiliar term, expanded

Two fields meet in this seat: the commercial side of a medicine company (how medicines are sold, marketed and paid for), and enterprise AI integration (joining AI models to the business systems people already use). Each entry says what the thing is and why it matters for this seat. The full list of acronyms, on every page of this workspace, is at the end.

Lilly, its people and its India centres

Tech@Lilly
Lilly’s name for its technology organisation. Lilly’s India page says the Bengaluru centre brings together “Lilly Research Laboratories, Commercial, and Tech@Lilly”. Why it matters: this seat is a Tech@Lilly engineering seat, whichever business team it serves.
Lilly Capability Center India (LCCI), and Global Capability Centre (GCC)
A Global Capability Centre is a company’s own office in another country that does work for the whole group; its staff are the company’s employees, not an outsourcing firm’s. Lilly’s first one in India opened in Bengaluru in 2016 and was reported as LCCI; the second opened in Hyderabad in 2025. Lilly’s 2026 postings use “LCCI” for Hyderabad too. Why it matters: news about “LCCI” may be about either city.
Eli Lilly Services India Pvt Ltd
Lilly’s Indian subsidiary that employs the Bengaluru and Hyderabad staff; “Pvt Ltd” means private limited company. It is the hiring company on this posting.
Lilly USA
Lilly’s US business: the teams that sell and market its medicines in the United States, its largest market (two thirds of 2025 revenue). Several Bengaluru postings serve it (“Lilly USA Tech”, “Lilly USA Commercial Tech”). Why it matters: this seat most likely serves Lilly USA (our inference).
Customer Insights & Analytics (CI&A)
A Bengaluru team started in 2017, with more than 100 people, that does data and analytics for Lilly’s marketing, sales and medical teams. Its AI Engineering team builds Lilly’s commercial generative-AI and agentic-AI platform. Why it matters: a likely partner, or home, for this seat.
CTI, and CTI-MD
Abbreviations used in Bengaluru postings. One posting expands CTI as “Customer Tech Innovation India” (a commercial group whose Senior Director a Lilly USA Commercial Tech architect reports to). “CTI-MD” is used by clinical, regulatory and product-research technology teams; Lilly does not expand it in the postings we read. Collision: in cybersecurity, CTI usually means cyber threat intelligence, and Lilly Bengaluru is also hiring a “Cyber Intel Analyst”.
Lilly Value & Access (LVA)
The Lilly team that works on market access (see below): payer coverage, real-world evidence, pricing and reimbursement. It has its own Senior Principal AI/ML Engineer seat open in Bengaluru.
PR&D, GRA and GSC
Team names in other Bengaluru postings: Product Research & Development (PR&D), Global Regulatory Affairs (GRA) and Global Scientific Communications (GSC). Not this seat’s area, but they show what else the office builds.
SFO
Part of a Bengaluru posting title (“Associate Director - SFO”) cited in the research. Lilly does not expand it in the text we read, so we leave it unexpanded.
R levels (R3, R4, R5, R6)
Lilly’s job grades for professional roles. A Lilly posting says a Senior Principal Machine Learning Engineer is “level R4 on our engineering ladder”; Technical Lead Architect and Advisor roles are R5. Why it matters: the level sets the scope the seat is expected to have. Collision: Lilly’s requisition numbers also start with R (this seat is R-112121); R-112121 is a posting number, not a level.
Requisition, and Workday
A requisition is an approved job opening inside a company’s hiring system, with its own number. Workday is the hiring system Lilly uses; applications for this seat go there, not to LinkedIn.
Individual contributor (IC)
A senior person who leads through technical work and influence, not by managing people. The job description says this seat is one.
CIDO, CAIO and CISO
Chief Information and Digital Officer (Diogo Rau at Lilly, on the Executive Committee), Chief AI Officer (Thomas Fuchs, since October 2024) and Chief Information Security Officer, who reports to the CIDO.
EISG
Lilly’s Executive Information Security Governance function, a group of senior leaders from security, legal, finance, audit and compliance that manages cybersecurity risk.
Form 10-K, Form 10-Q, and the SEC
The Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is the US regulator of listed companies. A 10-K is the annual report filed with it; a 10-Q is the quarterly report. Why it matters: they are the most reliable public source for Lilly’s size, finances and any job cuts.

The commercial side of a medicine company

Commercial
In a medicine company, everything about selling medicines once they are approved: sales, marketing, market access and customer data. Why it matters: the job description names “commercial workflows” in its first sentence.
Healthcare professional (HCP)
A doctor, nurse, pharmacist or other clinician. HCPs are the main “customers” of a medicine company’s sales force, because they decide what to prescribe. Lilly has a “U.S. HCP and field engagement hub”.
Field force, sales representative, territory manager
The field force is the sales team that visits HCPs in person. A sales representative is one of them; a territory manager covers a geographic patch. Lilly’s 10-K says US promotion uses “employee or contracted sales representatives”.
Field engagement
Everything the field force does with HCPs: visits, calls, emails, samples and follow-ups. Why it matters: the job description prefers experience with “field engagement” platforms, and Lilly’s AI field assistant is built for this group.
Customer engagement, and omnichannel
Customer engagement is every contact between the company and its customers (HCPs, and sometimes patients). Omnichannel means coordinating those contacts across channels (in person, email, websites, webinars) so the messages fit together.
Customer relationship management (CRM)
The system that records every customer, every contact and every planned visit. In pharma the field force lives in it all day. Why it matters: any AI assistant for the field has to read from and write to the CRM, which is integration work.
Veeva, Veeva CRM and Veeva Vault CRM
Veeva is a software company that makes cloud software for life-sciences companies. Veeva CRM was its long-standing CRM, built on Salesforce’s platform; Vault CRM is its newer CRM on its own Vault platform. Lilly committed to Vault CRM globally in August 2026. Why it matters: a CRM migration means data flows and integrations have to be rebuilt.
Call report
A sales representative’s record of a visit or call with an HCP, entered in the CRM. Veeva markets an “Agentic Call Report”, which uses AI to help write it.
Salesforce Marketing Cloud (SFMC), and Salesforce Data Cloud
Salesforce’s tools for running email and digital marketing campaigns, and for bringing customer data together. Lilly is hiring an SFMC technical lead in Bengaluru.
Commercial analytics
Analysis of sales, prescriptions, market share and the effect of marketing, used to decide where the field force spends its time.
HCP master data, and alignment
Master data is the single, cleaned list of every HCP and institution. Alignment is which representative covers which HCPs and territories. Databricks’ write-up of Lilly’s field assistant lists both among its data sources.
Samples
Free starter packs of a medicine that representatives may give to doctors, under strict record-keeping rules. Another data source for the field assistant.
Market access, payer, formulary, and PBM
Market access is getting a medicine covered and paid for. A payer is whoever pays (insurers, employers, governments). A formulary is a payer’s list of covered medicines. A pharmacy benefit manager (PBM) runs prescription-drug benefits for payers. Lilly’s LVA team builds AI for this area.
Real-world data (RWD), and real-world evidence (RWE)
Real-world data is health data from ordinary care, not from clinical trials: insurance claims, health records. Real-world evidence is what analysis of that data shows. Both are sensitive and heavily regulated.
LillyDirect
Lilly’s “direct-to-patient digital health care platform”, which arranges delivery of some Lilly medicines through third-party pharmacies. Another customer-facing system that AI may connect to.
Mounjaro, Zepbound and tirzepatide
Lilly’s two best-selling medicines in 2025, both made from the molecule tirzepatide: Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes, Zepbound for obesity. They explain Lilly’s rapid growth.

Rules and compliance

GxP
Short for “good x practice”, the family of quality rules for medicines: good manufacturing practice, good clinical practice, good laboratory practice and so on. Systems that fall under GxP must be validated and audit-ready. Lilly’s product-research postings say they operate under “strict GxP” rules.
Computer system validation (CSV)
Documented proof that a computer system in a GxP area does what it should, every time. Collision: CSV also means comma-separated values, a simple file format for tables.
Data integrity, and audit readiness
Data integrity means data is complete, accurate and traceable from start to end. Audit readiness means being able to show a regulator that proof at any time.
HIPAA
The US Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, which protects patients’ health information. Lilly’s AI policy paper discusses how AI tools fit under it.
Responsible AI
Designing and running AI so that it is fair, safe, explainable, private and accountable, with clear human ownership. The job description asks the seat to promote it.
Security-by-design, and privacy-by-design
Building security and privacy protections into a system from the first design, not adding them at the end. Both are named in the job description.
Human-in-the-loop
A person reviews or approves what the AI proposes before it takes effect. The job description asks for “human-in-the-loop controls”.

Enterprise AI and integration

Artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML) and generative AI
AI is software that performs tasks that normally need human judgement. ML is AI that learns patterns from data. Generative AI produces new text, images or code.
Large language model (LLM), and small language model (SLM)
An LLM is a very large AI model trained on text that can read and write language; Claude, GPT, Llama and Sonnet are examples or model families. An SLM is a smaller one, cheaper and faster, for narrower tasks.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
An LLM first looks up relevant documents or data, then writes its answer from them, so the answer rests on the company’s own sources. Collision: in project reporting, RAG means Red/Amber/Green status. In this workspace RAG always means retrieval-augmented generation.
Agent, agentic workflow, and supervisor agent
An AI agent is an LLM that can plan steps and call tools or systems to finish a task. An agentic workflow chains such steps. A supervisor agent hands sub-tasks to other agents, as in Lilly’s field assistant. Collision: in pharma sales, “agent” can also mean a contracted sales agent.
Orchestration
Coordinating the steps, models, tools and people in an AI workflow, in the right order, with retries and checks.
Embedding, semantic search, and vector database
An embedding turns text into a list of numbers that captures its meaning. Semantic search finds text by meaning rather than exact words. A vector database stores embeddings for fast semantic search (Pinecone is one).
Knowledge graph, semantic layer, and ontology
A knowledge graph stores things and the relationships between them. A semantic layer gives business-friendly, consistent meanings to raw data. An ontology is the formal vocabulary of things and relationships behind both.
Text2SQL
An AI that turns a question in plain English into a database query in SQL (structured query language). Databricks Genie does this kind of thing.
Databricks, Unity Catalog, and Genie
Databricks is a cloud platform for data engineering, analytics and AI. Unity Catalog is its governance layer: who may see which data. Genie lets people ask questions of data in plain English. All three are part of Lilly’s field assistant.
Azure, Azure AI and Azure OpenAI
Microsoft’s cloud (Azure), its AI services (Azure AI), and its service that runs OpenAI’s models inside a company’s Azure account (Azure OpenAI). The job description prefers them; no public Lilly source mentions them.
AWS, GCP, and the AWS services named
Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Google Cloud Platform (GCP) are the other two big clouds. Lilly’s Bengaluru AI postings name AWS services: EC2 and ECS (virtual servers and containers), S3 (file storage), Lambda (run code without a server), IAM (identity and access management) and CloudWatch (monitoring).
MLOps, LLMOps and DevSecOps
MLOps is the discipline of putting ML models into production and keeping them healthy: versioning, deployment, monitoring and retraining. LLMOps is the same for LLM applications, adding prompts, evaluations and cost control. DevSecOps builds security into software delivery.
CI/CD, Docker, Kubernetes, Prefect and GitHub Actions
Continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD) builds, tests and deploys every change automatically; GitHub Actions is one tool for it. Docker packages software into containers; Kubernetes runs containers at scale; Prefect schedules data and ML workflows. All four appear in Lilly’s Bengaluru AI postings. Collision: CI/CD is not CI&A (Customer Insights & Analytics).
AI evaluation
Testing how good an AI system’s answers are, before and after release, against agreed test sets and measures. The job description asks the seat to set up “AI evaluation practices”.
Observability
Being able to see what a running system is doing from its logs, metrics and traces, so problems can be found quickly.
Data lineage, and data product
Lineage records where each piece of data came from and what happened to it. A data product is a dataset that a team owns and publishes for others, with quality promises.
Application programming interface (API), microservices, and event-driven systems
An API is a defined way for one program to call another. Microservices split an application into small services that talk through APIs. Event-driven systems react to messages (“a call was logged”) instead of calling each other directly.
Integration patterns
Standard, reusable ways to connect systems (for example request and reply, publish and subscribe, batch file transfer). The job description asks the seat to establish them.
Proof of concept (POC), proof of value
A small, fast build that shows an idea works (concept) or pays off (value) before full investment. A sibling Lilly posting asks its architect to build them personally.
NVIDIA DGX SuperPOD, B300, GPU and “AI factory”
A graphics processing unit (GPU) is the chip that trains and runs AI models; the B300 is an NVIDIA model. A DGX SuperPOD is NVIDIA’s large, ready-built cluster of them. An AI factory is Lilly’s term for the whole infrastructure from data to trained model to running service. Drug discovery, not this seat.
Lilly TuneLab, and federated learning
TuneLab is Lilly’s platform that lets biotech companies use its drug-discovery models. Federated learning trains models across several parties’ data without the data leaving each party.
