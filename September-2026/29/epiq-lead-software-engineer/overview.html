The company, its owners and its places

Epiq (Epiq Systems, Epiq Global)
The employer. A company that supplies legal services and legal software to law firms, corporations, courts and governments. It was listed on the Nasdaq stock exchange until 2016 and is now privately owned. Why it matters: the seat builds AI for this company’s legal work, so the users are lawyers, reviewers and litigation-support staff.
Private equity (PE), and take-private
Private equity firms buy companies, usually with a lot of borrowed money, aim to make them more profitable, and later sell them. A take-private buys every share of a listed company, so it leaves the stock exchange. Epiq was taken private in 2016. Why it matters: such owners usually plan to sell after some years; Epiq’s have owned it for ten years and were reported in 2024 to be considering a sale.
OMERS and OMERS Private Equity
OMERS is a Canadian pension plan; OMERS Private Equity is its private-equity arm and Epiq’s main owner. Its Senior Managing Director, Legal sits on an Epiq AI Labs advisory group.
Harvest Partners
A United States private-equity firm that bought Epiq together with OMERS in 2016.
DTI
A legal process outsourcing company that OMERS already owned. It was combined with Epiq when the 2016 purchase completed, and the combined business took the Epiq name.
Divestiture, GBTS and K2 Services
A divestiture is the sale of part of a company. In November 2025 OMERS sold Epiq’s Global Business Transformation Solutions (GBTS) division, which offered operational and business services, to K2 Services, an information-technology services company for the legal industry. Why it matters: it shows the owners reshaping Epiq; it also may explain part of the fall in Epiq’s stated headcount.
Alternative Legal Service Provider (ALSP)
A company that does legal work that is not advice from a law firm: document review, eDiscovery, contract review, settlement administration. Epiq is one of the largest. Why it matters: an ALSP earns money on volume and speed, which is exactly where AI changes the economics.
Legal process outsourcing (LPO)
Sending legal support work, such as document review, to a specialist provider, often in another country. An older name for part of what ALSPs do.
Global capability centre (GCC), and Centre of Excellence (CoE)
A company’s own office in another country, often India, that does work for the whole group; the staff are the company’s own employees, not an outsourcing firm’s. Epiq opened its Hyderabad centre under both names in February 2020.
Sohini Tech Park, Financial District, and Pune
Epiq’s Hyderabad office is on the third floor of Sohini Tech Park in Hyderabad’s Financial District (Nanakramguda). Epiq’s other Indian office is an operations centre in Baner, Pune. Epiq’s record lists this seat in both cities.
Indiris Data Services Private Limited
The company named as the hiring organisation on every Epiq India posting we read (“1128 Indiris Data Services Private Limited”). Our reading is that it is Epiq’s Indian employing company; we did not check a company register.
Epiq AI Labs and Laer AI
Laer AI was a start-up founded by two Cornell-trained researchers; Epiq bought it in 2024. Its founders now lead Epiq AI Labs, Epiq’s AI research and engineering group, which works like “a startup within a global organization” and has engineers in Toronto. Why it matters: AI Labs builds Epiq’s core AI platform; this seat may build on it or next to it.

Legal services: how a lawsuit creates the work

Litigation, and a matter
Litigation is a lawsuit. A matter is one legal case or project; Epiq’s software organises data by matter. Why it matters: data from one matter must never leak into another, which shapes how AI systems are built (see data isolation below).
Discovery, and eDiscovery (electronic discovery)
Discovery is the stage of a lawsuit, mainly in the United States, when each side must hand over relevant documents to the other. eDiscovery is discovery of electronic material: emails, chats, files, databases. A single case can involve millions of documents. Why it matters: this is Epiq’s largest technology business and where its AI products work.
Electronically stored information (ESI)
The legal term for any electronic data that may be evidence.
Legal hold (litigation hold)
An order, inside a company, not to delete anything that may be relevant to a lawsuit. Epiq offers legal-hold and eDiscovery services for clients who use Microsoft 365 and Microsoft Purview. Why it matters: systems that touch client data must respect holds; deleting held data can bring court penalties.
Custodian
In eDiscovery, a person whose emails and files are collected, for example an employee involved in a dispute.
Collection, forensics and processing
Collection copies data from its sources without changing it; forensics is doing that in a way that stands up in court; processing unpacks the data, extracts text and metadata, removes duplicates and makes it searchable. Epiq’s processing engine runs on AWS.
Document review, first-level review, and linear review
Document review is lawyers and reviewers reading documents to decide which are relevant, which are privileged, and which touch each issue in the case. First-level review is the first, high-volume pass. Linear review means reading every document one by one. Why it matters: review is the most expensive part of eDiscovery, and it is what Epiq AI for Review automates.
Responsiveness (relevance)
Whether a document answers what the other side asked for. “Responsive” documents must be handed over.
Review protocol
The written instructions that tell reviewers how to code documents in a matter. Epiq says its AI builds prompts and models automatically from the review protocol.
Technology-assisted review (TAR), and predictive coding
The previous generation of review technology: machine-learning models trained on reviewers’ decisions rank documents by likely relevance. Epiq compares its generative AI against TAR and linear review. Why it matters: TAR has years of court acceptance; generative AI must show it is at least as defensible.
Privilege (attorney–client privilege, work product), and privilege log
Privilege is the legal right to withhold certain documents, mainly communications with a lawyer for legal advice (attorney–client privilege) and material prepared for the case (work product). Handing over a privileged document by mistake can waive the protection. A privilege log lists every withheld document and the reason. Why it matters: Epiq AI for Privilege automates this; an error here harms the client directly, so accuracy and explainability matter more than speed.
Production, and production set
In eDiscovery, production is handing documents to the other side; a production set is one batch handed over. (Not the same as a “production” software environment; see collisions.)
Defensibility, and defensible
Whether a method can be explained and justified to a judge or the other side if challenged. Why it matters: Epiq’s releases stress it; for AI, it means citations, measured accuracy, audit trails and human checks.
Conflict search
A search to see whether a law firm already acts for someone on the other side of a new matter. One of the tasks Epiq AI Accelerate’s agents can run.
Relativity and RelativityOne
The most widely used eDiscovery review software, made by another company; RelativityOne is its cloud version. Epiq hosts Relativity for clients and sells AI add-ons that run inside it.
Managed services
A long-term contract under which Epiq runs a client’s eDiscovery work across many matters, rather than one case at a time.
Antitrust, and a “second request”
Antitrust (competition) law reviews mergers and market power. When regulators look closely at a merger, the companies must produce huge volumes of documents quickly. Epiq AI for Antitrust targets this work.
Cyber incident review
After a data breach, reviewing the stolen or exposed documents to find whose personal data was in them, so that the right people and regulators can be notified. Three of Epiq’s open Hyderabad postings are in this work.
Class action, mass tort, and settlement administration
A class action is one lawsuit on behalf of a large group, such as all buyers of a product. A mass tort is many similar individual injury claims handled together. When they settle, someone must find and notify the class members (legal noticing), take and check claims, and pay out: that is settlement administration, a large Epiq business.
Bankruptcy, Chapter 11, restructuring and trustee services
Chapter 11 is the part of United States law under which a company keeps operating while it reorganises its debts (restructuring). Epiq administers these cases: notices, claims and votes. Trustee services support the court-appointed officials who run bankruptcy estates. Bankruptcy is where Epiq started.
Court reporting
Recording and transcribing what is said in depositions and hearings. Named among Epiq’s services in its older descriptions.
Contract lifecycle management (CLM)
Software and process for drafting, negotiating, signing and tracking contracts. Epiq implements CLM systems for clients.
Law firm, corporate legal department and litigation support
Epiq’s two main kinds of client are law firms and the in-house legal departments of companies. Litigation support is the technical team inside a firm that runs the eDiscovery software. Epiq AI Accelerate is aimed at those teams.

Epiq’s own products

Epiq Service Cloud
Epiq’s umbrella platform: its own software, partners’ software and its services behind one sign-in. More than 2,600 clients and 48,000 registered users, by Epiq’s count. The Hyderabad Data and AI Platforms group builds its data foundations.
Epiq Access
The client portal and single sign-on “front door” to the Epiq Service Cloud.
Epiq Discover and Epiq Discovery
Epiq’s own eDiscovery software. Epiq’s pages use both names; see collisions.
Epiq Narrate
Epiq software for case and trial preparation (organising exhibits and case facts), reachable through Epiq MCP.
Epiq AI, and Epiq AI for Review, Privilege, Antitrust and Compliance
Epiq’s family of AI products. Epiq AI for Review was launched in January 2025 as Epiq AI Discovery Assistant; the others apply the same approach to privilege review, antitrust reviews and compliance investigations. All run on the Epiq AI Laer platform, which Epiq says “orchestrates models, agents, and humans”.
Epiq Assist
Epiq’s conversational AI (a chat assistant) for large legal data sets.
Epiq AI Accelerate (and Epiq AI Accelerators)
AI add-ons for Relativity and Epiq Discover: translation, image analysis, text recognition, and since July 2026 agentic workflows powered by Claude that turn a plain-English request into actions in the software.
Epiq MCP
Model Context Protocol connections that let a client’s own AI assistant search and act on data in Epiq AI, Epiq Discover, Epiq Narrate and Epiq Access without copying the data out.

Compliance and data handling

Confidentiality
Keeping client information secret. Lawyers owe it by law and professional rules; Epiq inherits the duty for the data it holds. Why it matters: an AI system that leaks one client’s documents into another’s answers would be a serious breach.
Data isolation, and multi-tenant
A multi-tenant system serves many clients from shared software; data isolation guarantees one client’s data can never be seen by another. Epiq lists it among the controls on its Claude-powered workflows.
Data residency, and data sovereignty
Data residency means keeping data stored and processed in a particular country or region; data sovereignty means the data is subject to that country’s laws. Epiq’s platform pages and one of its AI postings name both. Why it matters: an AI call to a model hosted in another region can break a residency promise; model and region choices are compliance decisions here.
Audit log, and traceability
A permanent record of who, or which AI agent, did what and when. Epiq lists “audit logs, and AI action traceability” among its agent controls.
Human in the loop
A design in which a person reviews or approves an AI’s output before it takes effect. Epiq’s platform posting asks for it in legal workflows.
Responsible AI
Practices to make AI fair, transparent, secure and accountable. Epiq has a Responsible AI Committee and a public statement of principles; the posting asks the seat to align AI solutions with it.
Data governance, and lineage
Data governance is the rules for who may use which data and how; lineage is a record of where a piece of data came from and how it changed.
Personally identifiable information (PII), and protected health information (PHI)
Data that identifies a person, and health data about a person. Legal matters often contain both; Epiq’s Senior Director posting asks for careful handling of them.
GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA
The European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation; the California Consumer Privacy Act; and the United States Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act, which protects health data. Named in Epiq’s postings and security pages.
ITAR
International Traffic in Arms Regulations: United States rules on defence-related data, which limit who may see it and where it may be stored. Named on Epiq’s infrastructure page.
NIST, FISMA
The National Institute of Standards and Technology, a United States body whose security frameworks are widely used; and the Federal Information Security Modernization Act, the United States law on federal information security. Epiq says it is aligned to both.
Zero Trust, and identity and access management (IAM)
Zero Trust is a security approach that trusts no user or device by default and checks every request. IAM is the system that manages who can sign in and what they may do.
Single sign-on (SSO), OAuth2, OIDC and SAML
SSO lets a user sign in once for many applications. OAuth2 (OAuth 2.0, an authorisation standard), OIDC (OpenID Connect, a sign-in standard built on OAuth2) and SAML (Security Assertion Markup Language, an older enterprise sign-in standard) are the standards behind it. Named in an Epiq AI Labs posting.
Secure software development lifecycle (secure SDLC)
Building security into every step of making software: dependency checks, secrets management, security reviews.
Ransomware
Malicious software that encrypts an organisation’s files and demands payment. Ryuk was the ransomware that hit Epiq in February 2020, reportedly after a TrickBot infection (malware that steals credentials and opens the door for attackers).

Enterprise generative AI

Artificial intelligence (AI), generative AI (GenAI) and machine learning (ML)
Machine learning is software that learns patterns from data. Generative AI produces new text, images or code. The posting uses “AI” mostly to mean generative AI built on large language models.
Large language model (LLM)
A very large model trained on text that can read, write and follow instructions: for example Anthropic’s Claude and OpenAI’s GPT models.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
A pattern in which the system first retrieves relevant passages (for example from a matter’s documents) and then gives them to the LLM to answer from, so that answers are grounded in the client’s own data and can cite it. Why it matters: the posting asks the seat to design “scalable Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architectures”. See collisions for the other RAG.
Embeddings, vector database, and semantic search
An embedding turns a piece of text into a list of numbers so that texts with similar meaning have similar numbers. A vector database stores and searches those numbers quickly (Epiq AI Labs names Qdrant). Semantic search finds documents by meaning rather than exact words. These are the retrieval half of RAG.
Grounding, citations and hallucination
A hallucination is a confident but false answer from an LLM. Grounding ties answers to source documents, and citations show which ones. In legal work these are what make an AI answer usable.
Agent, agentic AI, and agent workflows
An AI agent is an LLM that can take actions through tools (search, move documents, create folders) rather than only answer. Agentic AI is building systems out of such agents; agent workflows chain several steps. Epiq AI Accelerate’s Claude-powered features are agentic.
AI orchestration framework
A software library that wires together models, prompts, tools, retrieval and agents. The posting names LangChain, Semantic Kernel (Microsoft’s), LlamaIndex, AutoGen (from Microsoft Research) and CrewAI; another Epiq posting adds LangGraph and the OpenAI Agents SDK (software development kit).
Prompt engineering
Writing and testing the instructions given to an LLM so that it answers reliably.
Evaluation framework, and model evaluation
A repeatable way to measure an AI system’s quality, for example accuracy against a set of documents that lawyers have already coded. Why it matters: this is how defensibility is shown.
AI observability
Recording what an AI system did on each request (inputs, retrieved passages, outputs, cost, time) so that problems can be found and fixed.
AI accelerators
In this posting, reusable components or templates that let teams build AI features faster. At Epiq the word is also a product name (Epiq AI Accelerators).
Model Context Protocol (MCP)
An open standard, introduced by Anthropic, for connecting AI assistants to tools and data sources in a uniform way. Epiq uses it for Epiq MCP and for its Claude-powered workflows.
Agent-to-agent (A2A)
A protocol for AI agents to talk to each other; the Staff posting names it beside MCP.
Azure OpenAI and Azure AI Foundry
Azure OpenAI is Microsoft’s service for using OpenAI’s models inside Microsoft’s cloud, under Microsoft’s data terms. Azure AI Foundry is Microsoft’s platform for building and running AI applications and agents with many models. Epiq uses Azure OpenAI by its own account.
AWS Bedrock (Amazon Bedrock)
Amazon’s service for using several companies’ models, including Anthropic’s Claude, inside AWS. Named in the posting; no Epiq source confirms Epiq uses it.
Anthropic and Claude; OpenAI
Anthropic is an AI company; Claude is its family of models, which Epiq says powers Epiq AI Accelerate’s agentic workflows. OpenAI is the company behind the GPT models.
Natural language processing (NLP), TensorFlow, PyTorch, scikit-learn
NLP is software that works with human language. TensorFlow, PyTorch and scikit-learn are machine-learning libraries named in the Staff posting.
Optical character recognition (OCR)
Turning pictures of text (scanned pages) into text a computer can search. One of Epiq’s AI accelerators.

Engineering and cloud

Microsoft Azure, and Amazon Web Services (AWS)
The two public clouds Epiq uses. The posting prefers Azure.
Kubernetes, AKS and EKS
Kubernetes runs and scales software packaged in containers. AKS (Azure Kubernetes Service) and EKS (Amazon Elastic Kubernetes Service) are the two clouds’ managed versions; the Staff posting names both.
Containerization, and Docker
Packaging software with everything it needs so that it runs the same everywhere. Docker is the common tool.
Infrastructure as Code (IaC), and Terraform
Describing servers, networks and cloud services in code files, so they can be reviewed and rebuilt. Terraform is the most common tool.
CI/CD, Azure DevOps and GitHub Actions
Continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD) automatically builds, tests and releases code. Azure DevOps (Microsoft) and GitHub Actions are tools for it. DevOps is the practice of development and operations teams working as one.
Microservices, API, and REST
A microservice is a small, separately deployed service. An application programming interface (API) is how software talks to software; REST (representational state transfer) is the common web style of API.
Enterprise integration patterns
Standard designs for connecting business systems, such as message queues, publish–subscribe and adapters.
Relational database, SQL, NoSQL, PostgreSQL and SQL Server
A relational database stores data in tables and is queried with SQL (Structured Query Language); PostgreSQL and Microsoft SQL Server are two. NoSQL databases store data in other shapes, such as documents or key–value pairs.
Snowflake, Databricks, data lake and lakehouse
Snowflake is a cloud data warehouse; Databricks is a cloud data and AI platform built on Apache Spark. A data lake stores raw data cheaply; a lakehouse adds warehouse-style tables on top. Epiq’s India data group uses Snowflake.
ETL and ELT
Extract, transform, load (and extract, load, transform): the steps of moving data from sources into a warehouse.
Power BI, and business intelligence (BI)
Microsoft’s reporting and dashboard tool, part of Epiq’s stack; BI is reporting and analysis for business decisions.
React and Bootstrap
React is a JavaScript library for building web interfaces; Bootstrap is a ready-made style kit for web pages.
C#, .NET and ASP.NET Core
Microsoft’s programming language and platform, and its framework for web services. Epiq’s Operations Engineering team uses them.
Software as a service (SaaS)
Software sold as a hosted online service rather than installed by the customer.
On-call rotation
A schedule under which engineers take turns being reachable outside working hours to fix production problems.

Collisions: words that mean two things

RAG
Here it always means retrieval-augmented generation. In project reports it often means Red/Amber/Green status colours. The posting spells it out, so there is no doubt in this workspace.
Privilege
Legal privilege (the right to withhold lawyer communications) against access privilege in security (what a user account may do, as in “least privilege”). In an eDiscovery AI system both appear, often in the same design document.
Production
Legal production (handing documents to the other side) against the production environment (live software). “Production set” is always the legal one.
Review
Document review (lawyers coding documents) against code review and design review (engineers checking each other’s work). The posting uses both.
Discovery
Legal discovery (exchange of evidence) against ordinary “content discovery” or search. Also, Microsoft sells an unrelated research product called Microsoft Discovery.
Epiq Discover against Epiq Discovery
Epiq’s own pages use both names for its eDiscovery software. We treat them as the same product family; Epiq has not said so.
Epiq against Epic
Epiq (this legal-services company) against Epic Systems (a large United States healthcare-software company). Some web pages confuse the two.
Agent
An AI agent against a human contact-centre agent; Epiq runs contact centres for class-action settlements.
Custodian
In eDiscovery, the person whose data is collected; in data management, the team responsible for a data set.
Lead
Here a job title for a senior individual contributor, not necessarily a people lead who manages a team.
Accelerator(s)
In the posting, reusable AI building blocks; at Epiq, also the product name Epiq AI Accelerators and Epiq AI Accelerate.
MCP
Here Model Context Protocol; elsewhere it can mean Microsoft Certified Professional.
