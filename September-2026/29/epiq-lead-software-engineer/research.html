1. The verdict
A real, open Epiq seat, written as a hands-on technical lead for enterprise generative AI in a legal-services company that already ships AI products; the open questions are its level (Lead, one rung below a Staff seat posted the same day), which team it joins, the work mode, and job security, not whether the job exists

What Epiq is. Epiq calls itself “a worldwide provider of legal and business services, serving law firms, corporations, financial institutions and government agencies”, with work in eDiscovery, class action, bankruptcy and compliance (Epiq, About, Self-claim). In plain words: when a large lawsuit, investigation, settlement or bankruptcy creates millions of documents or millions of claimants, Epiq supplies the software and the people who collect, review and administer them Inference (section 2).

Is the seat real? Yes Checked by us. Epiq’s own Workday job board lists requisition R0035231, posted on 28 September 2026, open to applications on 29 September, with text identical to his paste. LinkedIn’s public page for job 4471321885 gives the same posting date and says it runs until 28 October 2026 (section 3).

What kind of seat. The posting calls it “a hands-on technical leadership role” that mentors “Associate and Senior Software Engineers”. No source we read gives it direct reports. Epiq writes its people-manager seats differently: its Senior Director posting in Hyderabad says “leading a team of 80- 100 people”. So this reads as an individual-contributor (IC) seat Inference. It sits between Epiq’s “Senior” seats (5+ years) and its “Staff Software Engineer/ Solution Architect” seat (12+ years) Checked by us (section 3).

Who owns Epiq. OMERS Private Equity (the private-equity arm of a Canadian pension plan) and funds managed by Harvest Partners took Epiq private in 2016, in a deal valued at about $1.0 billion including debt, and combined it with DTI, a legal process outsourcing company OMERS already owned Self-claim (Epiq, 27 Jul 2016; Epiq, 30 Sep 2016). OMERS Private Equity still lists Epiq in its portfolio Checked by us.

The AI is real product work. Epiq launched Epiq AI Discovery Assistant in January 2025, renamed it Epiq AI for Review, and says 130 clients had adopted Epiq AI by March 2026. In July 2026 it announced that Claude powers new agentic workflows in Epiq AI Accelerate, using the Model Context Protocol (MCP). It also uses Microsoft’s Azure OpenAI service and Amazon Web Services (AWS) Self-claim, specific and repeated (section 4). This is the same list of platforms the posting names, so the posting’s AI section describes Epiq’s actual stack, not a wish list Inference.

The cautions. (1) The level: a “Staff Software Engineer/ Solution Architect - AI Solutions” seat, 12+ years, was posted in Hyderabad the same day. (2) Stability: Bloomberg reported in February 2024 that the owners were considering a sale; a division was sold in November 2025; Epiq’s own headcount statements range from “6100+ people” (careers page) to “4000 people” (July 2026 press release); and the three Hyderabad software-development reviews on AmbitionBox since August 2026 each rate job security 1 out of 5. (3) The work mode: LinkedIn says on-site; Epiq’s own record says hybrid, in Hyderabad or Pune (section 7).

This describes the company and the seat. It is not a recommendation about whether to send it; that decision is his.

2. Epiq: what it does, who owns it, how big it is

What Epiq does

In its own words: “Epiq is a worldwide provider of legal and business services, serving law firms, corporations, financial institutions and government agencies” Self-claim (Epiq, About). Its 2026 press releases add: “Epiq, a technology and services leader, takes on large-scale and complex tasks for corporations, law firms, and the courts by integrating people, process, technology, and data intelligence. Clients rely on Epiq to streamline legal, compliance, and settlement administration workflows” (press release, 5 Mar 2026).

Its business lines, as its own website menu lists them Checked by us (epiqglobal.com):

  • eDiscovery and litigation: discovery services, forensics and collections, document review, managed services, antitrust and competition services, litigation advisory. Its software here includes Epiq Discover, Epiq Narrate and the Epiq AI products.
  • Regulatory, compliance and cyber: global investigations, information governance, compliance advisory, and cyber incident response.
  • Class action and mass tort: class action administration, legal noticing, contact centres, payments, recall and remediation.
  • Bankruptcy: corporate restructuring, trustee services, and bankruptcy data products (Epiq AACER, Epiq Bankruptcy Analytics).
  • Contracts, and legal department and law firm operations: contract review, contract lifecycle management (CLM) implementation, legal department advisory.

A fair description from outside. The law firm that advised OMERS on a 2025 sale calls Epiq “the most advanced Alternative Legal Service Provider (ALSP) in the market” that “plays a critical role in administering complex legal settlements” (Weil, 10 Nov 2025). That is an adviser’s wording for its own client’s company, so read it as Self-claim, owner’s adviser, not as independent praise.

Where it came from. Epiq Systems was a Nasdaq-listed company based in Kansas City (ticker EPIQ) until 2016 (Epiq, 27 Jul 2016). Epiq’s careers page says “35+ years in business” and “NYC headquarters”; its press releases carry a New York dateline (Epiq, Careers).

Who owns it

Private-equity owned since 30 September 2016. On 27 July 2016 Epiq agreed to be bought by “OMERS Private Equity, the private equity arm of the OMERS pension plan, and funds managed by Harvest Partners, LP” for $16.50 a share in cash, “a total value of approximately $1.0 billion including assumed debt obligations”; on completion it would be “combined with DTI, a leading global legal process outsourcing (LPO) company majority-owned by OMERS” Self-claim (Epiq, 27 Jul 2016). The deal completed on 30 September 2016 (Epiq, 30 Sep 2016). OMERS is the pension plan for municipal employees in Ontario, Canada Inference from its name; not read on OMERS’ site.

Still an OMERS company today. OMERS Private Equity’s portfolio page lists Epiq under “Buyout”, sector “Business Services”, “Year of Investment 2014” (the year appears to date from OMERS’ earlier investment in DTI) Checked by us (OMERS Private Equity, Epiq). OMERS Private Equity’s Senior Managing Director, Legal sits on Epiq AI Labs’ AI Strategy Group (Epiq AI Labs).

A sale was explored in 2024. Bloomberg reported on 29 February 2024: “Omers, Harvest Consider Potential $3 Billion Sale of Epiq Systems” Independent (Bloomberg; headline only, the article is paywalled and was not read). We found no report that a sale of the whole company happened.

A division was sold in November 2025. On 10 November 2025 OMERS Private Equity completed the sale of “Epiq’s Global Business Transformation Solutions (Epiq GBTS) division” to K2 Services, “a technology‑enabled services provider specializing in IT managed services and enterprise technology platforms for the legal industry” Independent, from the two law firms on the deal (Weil; McCarthy Tétrault). GBTS “partnered with organizations to deliver strategic expertise in operational and business services”. Inference Selling a non-legal division fits an owner sharpening Epiq around legal work, possibly ahead of a sale; nothing we read says so.

5. What legal data demands of AI, in Epiq’s own words

The posting asks that AI solutions “align with security, compliance, responsible AI, and data governance requirements”. This is what Epiq’s own sources say those requirements are. All Self-claim unless marked.

  • Privilege and defensibility. Epiq AI for Privilege automates “privilege review and log creation”; the review products provide “reasoning, citations, and metrics to validate results” and “transparent metrics to ensure defensibility” (Epiq AI; 5 Mar 2026). Inference In legal review, a result must be explainable to a court or an opposing party; an AI answer without a citation or a measurable error rate is of little use.
  • No training on client data. Epiq AI “runs in secure environments designed for legal and compliance requirements” and “does not train public models with client data” (Epiq AI); the Azure OpenAI terms on its Responsible AI page say the same for that service.
  • Governed agents. The Claude-powered workflows come with “platform-specific user permissions, workflow approvals, data isolation, audit logs, and AI action traceability” (21 Jul 2026). Epiq MCP keeps data in place and uses “existing permissions and controls” (Epiq MCP).
  • Data residency and sovereignty. The Epiq Service Cloud infrastructure page promises operation “adhering to ITAR, HIPAA, and international data laws” and “strict adherence to data sovereignty and privacy laws”, across “physical data centers and cloud infrastructure” (Epiq, Infrastructure). Epiq’s AI platform posting requires the engineer to “Ensure compliance with security, privacy, and data residency requirements” and to build “governance, auditability, and human-in-the-loop controls required for legal and regulated workflows” (R0034838).
  • Personal and health data. The Senior Director posting asks for “responsible-AI data practices such as lineage tracking and PII/PHI handling appropriate for legal data”, awareness of “GDPR, CCPA, etc.” and how they “intersect with AI system design”, and secure development practices “particularly relevant given the sensitivity of legal data” (R0035128).
  • Responsible AI. Epiq has a “Responsible AI Committee” that “oversees our organization’s ethical development and deployment of AI technologies” and says it runs bias assessments (Epiq, Responsible AI).
  • Security. Epiq describes “key tenets of Zero Trust”, an identity and access management (IAM) programme, 24/7 network security with a managed security partner, alignment “to HIPAA, NIST, and FISMA frameworks”, and “Tier 3+ data centers in 10 locations worldwide” (Epiq, Security). The page says Epiq holds “several industry certifications”, but the list did not load for us.
