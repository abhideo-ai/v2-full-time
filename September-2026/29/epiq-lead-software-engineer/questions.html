Epiq: open questions

What is still open about the seat, and who can settle it

The research could not settle these from public sources. Item 1 he can check himself straight away; the rest need the recruiter or the hiring manager. The forcing questions in the next fold turn them into questions he can ask.

  1. Where does LinkedIn’s Apply button lead? LinkedIn says responses are managed off LinkedIn. Epiq’s own careers site carries the same job as requisition R0035231 on its Workday board, so the button almost certainly leads there, but nobody has clicked it (research section 3). Epiq says it writes to candidates only from epiqglobal.com addresses. Who can settle it: he can, by clicking Apply.
  2. Is this the right level for him: Lead, or the Staff Software Engineer/ Solution Architect seat? Epiq posted both on 28 September 2026 in Hyderabad, with very similar AI briefs; Lead asks 8–12 years, Staff asks 12+ (the ladder). Whether the 8–12 band is firm, and whether one application can be considered for both, is unknown. Who can settle it: the recruiter.
  3. Which team does “AI Solutions” belong to? The new Data and AI Platforms group, the Operations Engineering team, or Epiq’s AI product work; all three are inference (research section 3). Who can settle it: the recruiter, then the hiring manager.
  4. Who does the seat report to, and is that person in place? If the seat sits in the Data and AI Platforms group, that group’s Senior Director was only advertised on 25 September 2026. Who can settle it: the recruiter or the hiring manager.
  5. On-site or hybrid, and Hyderabad or Pune? LinkedIn says on-site in Hyderabad; Epiq’s own record says “Hybrid: Work in Office Part-Time”, in Hyderabad or Pune. A Hyderabad engineer’s review reports three office days a week. Who can settle it: the recruiter.
  6. Is it an individual-contributor seat, with no direct reports? The research reads it that way from the posting and from how Epiq writes its other postings; nobody at Epiq has said so. Who can settle it: the hiring manager.
  7. Which language does the team write in? The posting says “Python and/or Java” and React; the Operations Engineering team works mainly in C#/.NET. Who can settle it: the hiring manager.
  8. Which AI platforms run in production? Epiq uses Azure OpenAI and Anthropic’s Claude by its own account; how it reaches Claude, and whether Amazon Web Services (AWS) Bedrock is used at all, is not public (research section 4). Who can settle it: the hiring manager.
  9. Are there on-call duties or fixed evening hours for United States or United Kingdom clients? Nothing in the posting says so; nothing public rules it out. Who can settle it: the hiring manager.
  10. How stable is the group? The owners were reported in 2024 to be weighing a sale, a division was sold in November 2025, and recent Hyderabad engineers rate job security very low (research section 7). Who can settle it: nobody fully; the hiring manager can say whether the group is new, growing, or replacing people.
Forcing questions for the recruiter or the hiring manager

A forcing question asks for a specific answer, so that the answer settles something. These come from the research; they are not interview preparation, which waits until he asks for it. Q1 to Q4 suit the recruiter; Q5 to Q10 suit the hiring manager (Q stands for question). Ask them as curiosity, never as a challenge.

If the call runs short, get Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q5: the level, the work mode and city, the team, and whether it is an individual-contributor seat.

Q1 Lead or Staff?

“I see Epiq posted both this Lead Software Engineer role and a Staff Software Engineer/ Solution Architect role in Hyderabad on the same day. Are they on the same team, and could my application be considered for both?”

Why it matters: the two seats have almost the same AI brief but different levels (8–12 years against 12+). A good answer names the difference in scope and says whether the recruiter can put him forward for either. A weak answer (“just apply to this one”) leaves the levelling question open until late in the process.

Q2 Where, and how many days in the office?

“LinkedIn lists this role as on-site in Hyderabad, and Epiq’s careers site lists it as hybrid in Hyderabad or Pune. Which is right, and how many office days a week does the team keep?”

Why it matters: the two records disagree. A good answer gives one city and a number of days. A weak answer (“it depends on the manager”) means the manager decides, so Q2 moves to the hiring-manager call.

Q3 Which team?

“Which group is the ‘AI Solutions’ team part of, and who would I report to?”

Why it matters: Epiq is hiring a Senior Director for a new Data and AI Platforms group in India, and it also has an Operations Engineering team building AI for its own operations. The work, the language and the manager differ. A good answer names the group and the manager, and says whether the manager is already in post.

Q4 How is the 8–12 years read?

“The posting asks for 8 to 12 years. Is that a firm range, or a guide to the level of the work?”

Why it matters: an upper bound can be used to screen people out. A good answer says the range describes the work; a weak one suggests a candidate above it will be steered elsewhere, which Q1 then covers.

Q5 Individual contributor or manager?

“Is this an individual-contributor role? Would anyone report to me directly, or is the leadership through design reviews, code reviews and mentoring?”

Why it matters: he is looking for individual-contributor seats. The posting reads as one but never says so. A good answer is plain: “no direct reports”. A weak answer (“you’ll lead the team”) needs a follow-up about who writes the team’s performance reviews.

Q6 What does the first year build?

“What would this role build in its first year: products for clients, like Epiq AI, or tools for Epiq’s own teams?”

Why it matters: the posting speaks of “products and AI solutions”, “intelligent automation” and “AI accelerators”, which fit client products and internal operations equally. A good answer names one or two concrete systems.

Q7 Which language and which AI platforms?

“Is the team’s code mainly Python, Java or .NET? And which model platforms run in production today: Azure OpenAI, Claude, or both?”

Why it matters: the posting lists five AI platforms and two languages with “and/or”. Epiq’s own announcements confirm Azure OpenAI and Claude; the rest are unclear. A good answer names what is live, not what is being evaluated.

Q8 How is AI output made defensible?

“For legal work, how does the team show that an AI result can be trusted: evaluation sets, citations, human review, audit logs?”

Why it matters: Epiq’s own releases stress defensibility, privilege, audit logs and data isolation, and the posting asks for “evaluation frameworks” and “responsible AI”. A good answer describes a working practice. A weak answer shows the practice is still to be built, which is also useful to know.

Q9 On-call and hours

“Does the team carry an on-call rotation, and how much overlap with United States or United Kingdom hours is expected?”

Why it matters: the posting is silent, Epiq is headquartered in New York, and its Senior Director posting asks for “stakeholder management across US/UK/EU counterparts”. A good answer gives the rotation and the usual overlap in hours.

Q10 Is the group new or growing?

“Is this a new team, a growing one, or a replacement? How many engineers are in it today?”

Why it matters: several senior data and AI seats were posted in Hyderabad in the same week, and recent reviewers there rate job security low. A good answer describes growth with a reason (a product, a client programme). Ask this one gently; it is about the team, not about Epiq’s owners.
