Glossary: every acronym and unfamiliar term, expanded

This seat sits in a setting that is new for him: a small digital-services agency in India that builds websites, mobile apps, AI agents and automations for small businesses, and a co-founding rather than a job. Each entry says what the thing is and why it matters for this seat. The full list of acronyms, on every page of this workspace, is at the end, followed by the words that mean two different things.

The business: a digital-services agency

Digital-services agency
A business that builds websites, apps and software for other businesses, one client at a time. Why it matters: this is what Zenvyra says it is. The work is many small projects for many clients, not one product of its own.
Client delivery
Building what a client has bought, to the scope and the deadline agreed, and handing it over. Why it matters: in this seat the founder sells and the technical co-founder delivers, so most of the week would be client delivery (our reading).
Scoping
Agreeing with a client exactly what will be built, before any building starts. Why it matters: in a small agency, a loose scope turns into unpaid extra work; who scopes (the seller or the builder) shapes the builder’s week.
Subscription model, and retainer
Selling services as an ongoing monthly plan rather than a one-off project; a retainer is the older name for the same idea. Why it matters: Zenvyra’s one LinkedIn post sells its services this way, which means continuing support for every subscriber, not a handover at the end.
Maintenance and support
Keeping delivered websites, apps and automations running after launch: fixes, updates, hosting, small changes. Why it matters: Zenvyra’s website offers “ongoing support and maintenance packages”; in a one-builder business that work lands on the builder.
Services business versus product business
A services business sells people’s time and skill, project by project. A product business builds one thing once and sells it many times. Why it matters: the posting asks for “Building and launching real products”, but everything public about Zenvyra is services.
Minimum viable product (MVP)
The smallest version of a product that real users can try, built to learn quickly whether it is wanted. Why it matters: agencies like this often sell MVPs to start-up clients, and “launching real products” usually starts as one.
Landing page
A single web page built to get a visitor to do one thing, such as fill in a form. Why it matters: a common small-agency deliverable, listed on Zenvyra’s LinkedIn page.
Lead, and lead qualification
A lead is a possible customer who has shown interest. Lead qualification is sorting leads by how likely they are to buy. Why it matters: Zenvyra offers AI agents for “lead qualification”, one of the most common small-business automations.

The technology the posting names

n8n
A workflow-automation tool (said “n-eight-n”, short for “nodemation”) in which you connect boxes on a screen, each one a step such as “when a form is filled in, add a row to a spreadsheet and send a WhatsApp message”. It can be self-hosted and can call AI models. Why it matters: the posting names it (“n8n, APIs and integrations”); it is the everyday tool of AI-automation agencies in India.
Make and Zapier
Two other workflow-automation tools that do much the same as n8n, run as hosted services. Why it matters: Zenvyra’s website lists “n8n / Make / Zapier” together.
Workflow automation, and business automation
Letting software do the repetitive steps of a business process: follow-ups, data entry, invoices, notifications. Why it matters: “Business automations” is one of the six skills the posting asks for.
AI agent
A program built around a large language model (LLM) that can decide on steps and use tools (look something up, send a message, update a record) to finish a task, rather than only answering a question. Why it matters: the posting asks for “AI agents” and for his “experience with AI agents & automation” in the DM.
Large language model (LLM)
The kind of AI model behind ChatGPT and Claude: trained on a great deal of text, it writes and reasons in ordinary language. Why it matters: every AI agent and AI workflow this agency sells runs on one.
Integration
Connecting two pieces of software so that data flows between them without a person copying it. Why it matters: the posting asks for “APIs and integrations”, and much small-business automation is integration.
Application programming interface (API)
The published way one program asks another for data or action. Why it matters: almost every integration and automation calls APIs; Zenvyra’s website says “If it has an API, we can usually connect it.”
REST (representational state transfer)
The most common style of web API, in which each thing has a web address and is read or changed with standard web requests. Why it matters: Zenvyra’s website lists “REST APIs” in its back-end tools.
Webhook
A web address that one system calls automatically when something happens, so that another system can react at once. Why it matters: webhooks are how most n8n workflows start.
No-code and low-code
No-code tools let people build software by clicking and dragging instead of writing code; low-code tools do the same but allow some code where needed. n8n is usually called low-code. Why it matters: this agency’s automation work is mostly low-code, which is quick to build but is still real systems that must be kept running.
Customer relationship management (CRM)
Software that keeps a business’s customers, leads and conversations in one place (for example HubSpot or Zoho). Why it matters: Zenvyra’s page lists automation “across CRM, WhatsApp, email, forms, databases”.
WhatsApp automation
Sending and answering WhatsApp messages automatically through WhatsApp’s business interface. Why it matters: in India, WhatsApp is often the main way small businesses talk to customers, so it is a common automation job.
Web application, and single-page application (SPA)
A web application is a website that does work (logins, dashboards, forms), not just shows pages. A single-page application loads once and then redraws itself as you use it. Why it matters: “Website & web application development” is the first skill the posting asks for; Zenvyra’s own site is an SPA.
React, Next.js, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS
Common tools for building the part of a website people see: React is a library for building screens, Next.js a framework on top of it, TypeScript a typed form of JavaScript, and Tailwind CSS (cascading style sheets) a way of styling pages. Why it matters: Zenvyra’s website lists all four.
Node.js, Express.js and MongoDB
Node.js runs JavaScript on a server; Express.js is a small framework for web servers on Node.js; MongoDB is a document database. Why it matters: Zenvyra’s website lists them as its back end.
Mobile app: native, cross-platform, React Native and Expo
A native app is built separately for iPhone (iOS) and Android. A cross-platform app is built once for both. React Native is a cross-platform framework from Meta; Expo is a set of tools that makes React Native easier to build and publish. Why it matters: “Mobile app development” is in the posting; the website lists React Native and Expo.
Shopify
A hosted service for running an online shop. Why it matters: the website lists Shopify store setup and themes, a typical small-agency job.
Vercel
A hosting service for websites built with tools like Next.js. Why it matters: Zenvyra’s own website runs on it, and the website lists it.
Continuous integration and continuous delivery (CI/CD)
Automatically building, testing and releasing code every time it changes. Why it matters: the website lists “CI/CD & Vercel”; in an agency it keeps many small client sites releasable by one person.
Search engine optimisation (SEO)
Making a website easier for Google and others to find and rank. Why it matters: clients of small web agencies often expect it; it appears on Zenvyra’s site (“SEO-ready structure”) and in the market section of the research.
Software as a service (SaaS)
Software people rent online rather than install. Why it matters: Zenvyra’s post uses “#SaaS”, though what it sells is a subscription to services, not software (our reading).

Co-founding in India

Co-founder, and technical co-founder
A co-founder is one of the people who start a company together, as opposed to someone employed by it. A technical co-founder is the one who builds the product or service. Why it matters: the posting says twice that this is “not a regular job”. A co-founder seat is not an individual-contributor seat.
Founders’ agreement
A written contract between co-founders that sets out roles, decision-making, time commitment, and who owns the work. Why it matters: the posting states no terms; this is where terms would be written down. Listed here only as a term.
Sole proprietorship
The simplest way to run a business in India: one person trading under a name, with no separate company. It does not appear in the company register. Why it matters: LinkedIn lists Zenvyra as “Self-Employed”, and we found no registered company, so Zenvyra may be run this way (our reading). There is no company to co-found until one is created.
Private limited company (Pvt Ltd)
The usual form for an Indian start-up: a separate legal company with shareholders and at least two directors, registered with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Why it matters: this is the form a co-founding usually takes on paper; nothing called Zenvyra in Telangana is one.
One person company (OPC)
A private company with a single owner. Why it matters: some look-alike names on the register are OPCs; none is this Zenvyra.
Limited liability partnership (LLP)
A partnership whose partners are not personally liable for its debts, also registered with the Ministry of Corporate Affairs. Why it matters: a lighter alternative to a private limited company for a two-person business; none named Zenvyra exists.
Ministry of Corporate Affairs (MCA), and Registrar of Companies (ROC)
The Indian government ministry, and its registrar offices, that register companies and LLPs and keep their public records. Why it matters: the MCA register is where we looked for Zenvyra and did not find it.
Corporate identity number (CIN)
The 21-character number the registrar gives each company. It encodes whether it is listed, its industry code, its state, its year and its type; for example U46524DL2026PTC462039 is an unlisted (U) private company (PTC) registered in Delhi (DL) in 2026. Why it matters: the three “Zenvyra” companies on the register are identified this way.
National Industrial Classification (NIC) code
The Indian government’s code for a business’s industry, carried in the CIN. Why it matters: we read the three registered Zenvyra companies’ codes as trade and real estate, not software.
ZaubaCorp and Tofler
Private websites that copy the MCA register and make it searchable. Why it matters: they are how we checked for a Zenvyra company; they can lag the register by weeks.
Goods and services tax (GST) and Udyam
GST is India’s tax on sales; businesses above a size register for it. Udyam is the government’s registration for small businesses. Why it matters: a sole proprietorship would show up in these, not in the company register; we could not check them.
Domain name, and WHOIS
A domain name is a website’s address, such as zenvyra.tech. WHOIS (“who is”) is the public record of when a domain was registered and through which company. Why it matters: it dates Zenvyra’s website to 19 September 2026.

Words that mean two different things here

  • RAG: in this workspace, retrieval-augmented generation, where an AI model first looks up relevant documents and then answers from them. In project reports, RAG also means Red/Amber/Green status. Different things.
  • Agent: an AI agent is a product Zenvyra sells. “Agent 1, 2 and 3” in this workspace’s notes are the assistants that built its pages. A recruitment agent is a third meaning, and there is none here.
  • Automation: here, business automation (letting software do a firm’s routine steps). In his record, automation often means test automation (software that tests software). Related skills, different jobs.
  • Integration: here, connecting two pieces of software (an API integration). In CI/CD, “continuous integration” means merging and testing code changes often. Different things.
  • Hybrid: a hybrid work mode (some office days, some remote), as in the posting. A hybrid mobile app is a web app wrapped to run as a phone app. Different things.
  • Native: a native app is built separately for each phone platform, while React Native is a cross-platform tool despite its name.
  • Zenvyra: many unrelated businesses share the name, including a Delhi web agency called Zenvyra Tech (look-alike names). Only “Zenvyra-we” and zenvyra.tech are this business.
  • DM: a direct message on LinkedIn; also the name of this workspace’s DM draft tab.
