Zenvyra: open questions

What is still open, and who can settle it

The research could not settle these from public sources. Items 1 and 2 he can settle himself, straight away; the rest need the founder, since there is no recruiter or hiring manager here. The forcing questions in the next fold turn them into questions he can ask.

  1. Does he know Telugu well enough? The posting says “Telugu — must know”. It is a gate, not a technical skill, and nothing in his record answers it. Who can settle it: he can.
  2. Who is the founder? LinkedIn’s public view does not show the poster, and neither the company page nor the website names anyone (research section 4). Signed in to LinkedIn, he can usually see the poster under the posting, and then look at their profile: work history, connections, activity. Who can settle it: he can, signed in; then the founder.
  3. Is there a registered company, and if not, what is the plan? We found no company or limited liability partnership (LLP) called Zenvyra in Telangana; LinkedIn calls it “Self-Employed” (research section 2). A co-founder needs a company to be a co-founder of. Who can settle it: the founder.
  4. Who built the five client projects on the website? The website shows five client stories; the one we could test (PayAtom) is credited to another firm on PayAtom’s own site (research section 3). Whether the founder, freelancers or someone else built them tells him what he would inherit. Who can settle it: the founder.
  5. How many clients and subscribers are there today? The website says “30+ Brands Served”, but its address is ten days old. The answer sets how much delivery and support work is waiting on day one. Who can settle it: the founder.
  6. Client work only, or a product too? The posting asks for “Building and launching real products”, but everything public describes client services (research section 5). Who can settle it: the founder.
  7. Hybrid in Hyderabad, or remote? The posting says Hybrid; the website says “Remote-first”. Who can settle it: the founder.
  8. What are the co-founding terms? Title, time commitment, who decides what, and whether there will be a written founders’ agreement. The posting states none of these. Who can settle it: the founder, in writing.
  9. Full-time from the start, or alongside another job? The posting and LinkedIn say “Full-time”, and also “not a regular job”. What full-time means here is not stated. Who can settle it: the founder.
Forcing questions for the founder

A forcing question asks for a specific answer, so that the answer settles something. There is no recruiter or hiring manager for this seat: the founder is both, so every question goes to them. These come from the research; they are not interview preparation, which waits until he asks for it. Ask them as curiosity, never as a challenge.

If the first conversation runs short, get Q1, Q2, Q3 and Q6: who they are, whether there is a company, who does the building today, and how the partnership would be written down.

Q1 Who are you, and what have you built before?

“Could you tell me a bit about your own background: what you did before Zenvyra, and whether you have started a business before?”

Good answer: a clear work history he can see on their LinkedIn profile, and a straight account of any earlier venture, including one that did not work. Weak answer: vague, or a profile with little history behind it.

Nothing public names the founder or their track record (research section 4). A co-founder is choosing a partner, not an employer.

Q2 Is Zenvyra registered, and in what form?

“Is Zenvyra registered as a company or an LLP today, or is it running as a proprietorship? If not yet a company, when would you set one up, and who would its directors be?”

Good answer: a plain statement of the current form, and a concrete plan with a timeline. Weak answer: “We’ll sort the paperwork later.”

We found no Zenvyra company or LLP in Telangana, and LinkedIn lists it as “Self-Employed” (research section 2). Until a company exists, “co-founder” has no legal meaning.

Q3 Who does the building today?

“Your website shows projects for TFS, Fluent Pro, Conquer, PayAtom and MedFlow. Who built those, and who looks after them now?”

Good answer: named people or freelancers, and which projects are live and supported. Weak answer: no clear answer, or projects nobody can point to.

PayAtom’s own site credits another firm (research section 3). The answer also tells him how much existing work he would be responsible for from day one.

Q4 How many clients are paying today?

“How many clients or subscribers does Zenvyra have right now, and how many projects are in progress?”

Good answer: a specific number and a few named, live engagements. Weak answer: “30+ brands” with nothing specific behind it.

The website says “30+ Brands Served”, but its address was registered on 19 September 2026 (research section 9). The answer sets the size of the workload.

Q5 Client services, or a product of our own?

“Is the plan to stay a services business, or to build a product of Zenvyra’s own at some point? What would the technical co-founder spend a typical week on?”

Good answer: a clear picture of the week, whichever it is. Weak answer: “Everything.”

Everything public describes client delivery on a monthly subscription; the posting also asks for “Building and launching real products” (research section 5).

Q6 How would the partnership be written down?

“How do you see the partnership being set out: titles, who decides what, the time each of us puts in, and a written founders’ agreement? When would that be signed?”

Good answer: a willingness to put it in writing early, with a standard agreement drafted by a professional. Weak answer: “Let’s start working and decide later.”

The posting states no terms at all (research section 9). Y Combinator’s advice is to agree “a plan for what you’ll each work on for the first 6-12 months” before committing. This asks only how the terms would be set down.

Q7 Hyderabad office, or remote?

“The posting says hybrid in Hyderabad, and the website says remote-first. Where would we work, and how often would we meet in person?”

Good answer: a stated place and rhythm. Weak answer: “Wherever.”

The two sources disagree (research section 3). Relocation is open for him, so this is for planning.

Q8 Is any trial work expected?

“Before we decide, would you expect me to build something for a client, or is it conversations first?”

Good answer: conversations first, and any joint work agreed openly. Weak answer: a client project to deliver before anything is agreed.

Nothing so far asks for unpaid work (research section 9). Asking early keeps it that way.
