What the research found

The verdict, in the research page’s own words: “A real, twenty-year-old Hyderabad staffing company in the middle of a rename, with no public track record in AI yet” (AI: artificial intelligence).

The company was registered in Hyderabad on 17 April 2006, is active today, and mainly supplies engineers to overseas clients. Its new website, enhansd.com, names it as the former Delta and shows three arms: Tech, “Your People Partner”, which is active; and Studio, “Your AI Partner”, and Apps, “Your Enterprise Partner”, which are not. The old website being down is a fact; the rename is a plausible reason for it, and the research labels that part an inference. The company’s claim of “1,400+ professionals” is contradicted by independent estimates, which cluster around 500. The United Kingdom’s official company register shows Kim Batcheler sitting on company boards with the family that owns Delta since April 2021, and his public record shows no director disqualification, no insolvency notice and no court case.

No public Delta job posting asks for AI, data or architect skills. From that, and from Kim’s own posts, the research infers, and labels it an inference, that a role created now is most likely connected to the AI arm. Its best-supported readings are the founding architect for Studio’s AI pilots, and the architect who builds AI into the staffing business; in practice those may be one job. Which it is, if either, is the first thing the interview has to settle.

The research describes the company. It makes no recommendation about whether to take the role; that decision is his.

Glossary: every acronym and unfamiliar term on this page, expanded (18)
  • JD: job description. It is the abbreviation behind the jd.md filename, and behind “Full-time JD workspace” in the breadcrumb at the top of this page.
  • Pvt. Ltd.: private limited, the Indian form of a private company. Its shares cannot be offered to the public.
  • Enhansd: the new name the company is taking in 2026. Its new website shows three arms, meaning separate businesses under one brand: Tech (“Your People Partner”, active), Studio (“Your AI Partner”, not active) and Apps (“Your Enterprise Partner”, not active).
  • Staffing company: a company that employs engineers and places them with client companies. The client pays for each engineer’s time, which is what “billed” means on this page. Most of Delta’s business is this kind of work.
  • Referral: a recommendation of a candidate by someone who already works at the company. Here it came from a friend of his who recently joined.
  • URL: uniform resource locator, a web address. The tracker’s source_url for this seat holds the company’s homepage, because no job posting exists.
  • HTTP 503: HTTP is the Hypertext Transfer Protocol, the way browsers fetch web pages. Status 503 means “service unavailable”: the server answers, but it will not serve the page.
  • AI: artificial intelligence.
  • Pilot: a small, quick trial of a system, run to check that it works before a larger rollout. The company describes Studio as using “fast pilots”.
  • Tracker: the database jobs_tracker_v2, which records every seat. The launcher page reads its list of applications from it, and lists a seat marked interviewing under the Heard back tab.
  • Weighted rubric: the scoring table behind the technical score. Each requirement gets a weight, a score out of ten, and the evidence behind that score.
  • Individual contributor: an engineer who designs and builds the work personally, with no people reporting to them.
  • Forcing question: a question that asks for a specific answer, so that the answer settles something rather than leaving it vague.
  • Company register: a country’s official record of its companies, their directors and their filings.
  • Director disqualification: a legal ban on a person acting as a company director. None is recorded against Kim Batcheler.
  • Insolvency notice: an official public announcement that a person or company cannot pay their debts. None was found for Kim Batcheler.
  • Word document (.docx): the file format of Microsoft Word, the form in which a per-seat résumé is produced.
  • Favicon: the small icon shown on the browser tab.
