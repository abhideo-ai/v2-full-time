1. First, the vocabulary

This job description names roughly twenty tools, standards and platforms that do not appear anywhere in your own record. That is not a problem to hide; it is simply a list of words you should be able to say out loud without hesitating. Below, each one gets a plain-English line saying what it actually is, and then a second line saying what the nearest thing in your own confirmed record is. Where your record has nothing close, this page says so in those words rather than stretching.

How to read the "closest in your record" lines. They are written strictly against what CLAUDE.md records as claimable or user-confirmed. Where something is genuinely absent, the line says nothing in the record. That is deliberate. The agent-building frameworks

LangChain
A Python and JavaScript library for wiring a large language model (LLM) to other things: prompts, tools, retrieval, memory, and chains of steps. It is the oldest and most widely used of this family.
Closest in his record: this one is his. LangChain is on the confirmed safe-to-claim list.
LangGraph
Built by the same team as LangChain. Where LangChain thinks in chains (one step after another), LangGraph thinks in graphs, so an agent can loop, branch, retry, and hand control between several agents. This is what people normally mean by "multi-agent orchestration".
Closest in his record: also his. LangGraph is on the confirmed safe-to-claim list.
Semantic Kernel
Microsoft's equivalent of LangChain, written first for C# and .NET and later for Python and Java. Same job — connect a model to tools, memory and planning steps — but it is the Microsoft-ecosystem answer, and it is usually chosen because a shop is already on Azure and .NET rather than on technical merit.
Closest in his record: nothing directly. The honest framing is that it is the same problem shape as LangChain with Microsoft naming, and that he has not used it. The job description says "such as LangChain, LangGraph, Semantic Kernel, AutoGen, or CrewAI", which under the generous-qualifier rule means LangChain and LangGraph satisfy the line outright (requirement text).
AutoGen
A Microsoft Research framework for multi-agent conversation: you define several agents with different roles, and they talk to each other and to tools until a task is done. Its distinguishing idea is that agents converse rather than being called in a fixed sequence.
Closest in his record: nothing by that name. The nearest honest comparison is LangGraph, which solves the same multi-agent coordination problem with an explicit graph instead of a conversation.
CrewAI
A lighter, more opinionated multi-agent framework. You describe a "crew" of agents by role ("researcher", "writer", "reviewer"), give each a goal and tools, and it runs them. Popular for quick builds; less used for large regulated systems.
Closest in his record: nothing by that name. Same category as LangGraph and AutoGen.
Model Context Protocol (MCP)
An open standard, published by Anthropic, for how a language model connects to outside tools and data. Instead of every application inventing its own plug-in format, a system exposes an "MCP server", and any MCP-aware model can call it. It is the universal-adapter idea applied to model tooling.
Closest in his record: this is a direct hit, not an adjacency. Real MCP servers are on his confirmed safe-to-claim list, and Tachyon's own product page advertises an "MCP Connector Framework" reaching ServiceNow, Salesforce, Jira and Splunk (Tachyon Aura page). Of everything in this glossary, this is the term where he is ahead of the job description rather than behind it.

The cloud model services

Azure OpenAI Service / Azure AI Foundry
Microsoft's way of selling OpenAI's models (the GPT family) inside its own cloud, so that an enterprise gets the model behind Azure's contracts, networking and access controls rather than calling OpenAI directly. "AI Foundry" is Microsoft's newer umbrella name for the surrounding build-and-deploy tooling: model catalogue, evaluation, deployment, agent hosting.
Closest in his record: nothing. Azure is on the never-claim list and must stay there. The honest sentence is that he has built the same class of system on Amazon Web Services (AWS) and would ramp on the Azure packaging.
AWS Bedrock
Amazon's equivalent: one application programming interface (API) through which you call many different vendors' models (Anthropic, Meta, Mistral, Amazon's own), with the billing, logging and network controls already inside your AWS account.
Closest in his record: Bedrock by name is not established anywhere in his material — do not claim it. What is his is deep AWS architecture generally, plus LangChain and LangGraph orchestration around models. Bedrock is a ramp item measured in days, and it is fair to say exactly that.
Google Vertex AI / Gemini
Google Cloud's equivalent again. Vertex AI is the platform; Gemini is Google's own model family served through it.
Closest in his record: nothing. Google Cloud Platform is on the never-claim list.

The important thing about all three of these is that the job description joins them with "or", not "and". The requirement reads "Deep, hands-on cloud architecture experience on Azure, AWS, or GCP", and the corresponding responsibility reads "on Azure, AWS, and/or GCP" (job description). Under the house rule on reading "e.g. / or / preferably" generously, his AWS depth satisfies that line as written. Azure here is a ramp item, not a capability cap. Section 5 explains why the room around the seat is still likely to be Microsoft-flavoured even so.

The infrastructure-as-code tools

Terraform
You write your cloud infrastructure — servers, networks, databases, permissions — as text files, and the tool makes the real cloud match the files. It works across clouds. "Infrastructure as code" means exactly that: the environment is defined in version-controlled text instead of assembled by hand in a web console.
Closest in his record: his, and confirmed twice over. Terraform is user-confirmed as hands-on, and it also sits in bullet 10 of the seventeen VoltusWave bullets, where it was adopted as a company-wide standard rather than merely used by him.
Bicep
Microsoft's infrastructure-as-code language for Azure only. It is a friendlier syntax on top of Azure's older JSON template format. Same idea as Terraform, single cloud.
Closest in his record: nothing by that name, and it cannot be otherwise because it is Azure-only. Terraform is the transferable skill and the concepts carry over completely.
CloudFormation
Amazon's own infrastructure-as-code service, and the ancestor of this whole category on AWS. Same idea again, AWS only. Many AWS shops use Terraform instead of CloudFormation by choice.
Closest in his record: CloudFormation by name is not established in his material. Terraform on AWS is, which is the same job done with a different tool, and saying it that way is accurate.

The enterprise platforms the AI has to plug into

This is the part of the job description most worth slowing down on, because it is where Tachyon's actual business lives. Their revenue does not come from building models. It comes from making these systems work for large companies.

ERP — enterprise resource planning
The single large system a big company runs its operations in: finance and accounting, procurement, inventory, manufacturing, payroll. It is the system of record for what the business owns, owes and made.
Closest in his record: no ERP platform work. The honest bridge is that he has integrated many systems of record across organisational boundaries.
CRM — customer relationship management
The equivalent system for everything facing the customer: leads, opportunities, quotes, support cases, marketing campaigns.
Closest in his record: no CRM platform work by name.
SAP
The German company that makes the most widely deployed ERP software in large enterprises, and by extension the software itself. Names that will come up: S/4HANA is the current generation of the core ERP; SuccessFactors is the human-resources product; Business Technology Platform (BTP) is SAP's cloud platform for extending it; IS-U is the utilities-industry version and FI-CA its billing and receivables module.
Closest in his record: nothing. This is genuinely new domain vocabulary, and it is the single largest piece of their published delivery work (see section 3).
Salesforce
The dominant cloud CRM. Sold as separate "clouds" — Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, Marketing Cloud, Financial Services Cloud — which are really configurations of one platform for different departments and industries.
Closest in his record: nothing by name.
ServiceNow
The dominant platform for internal service management: information-technology tickets, incidents, change requests, and increasingly human-resources and facilities workflows too. When a company says "raise a ticket", this is very often the system.
Closest in his record: nothing by name. Worth noting it appears twice in this research — in the job description, and in Tachyon Aura's connector list (Aura page).
Boomi, and "iPaaS"
Not in the job description, but it is the tool named in Tachyon's own published delivery work, so it matters. Boomi is an integration platform as a service (iPaaS): a hosted product for moving and transforming data between enterprise systems, so that Salesforce and SAP and a billing system stay in step. An older name for the same job is enterprise service bus (ESB).
Closest in his record: this is his best transferable card in the enterprise-integration half of the job. Twenty-five hotel suppliers integrated through a MuleSoft enterprise service bus is user-confirmed and safe to claim. MuleSoft appeared zero times anywhere on Tachyon's site, and Boomi appeared once, in their energy-sales case study (case study). So frame it as iPaaS and enterprise-service-bus integration experience, not as a MuleSoft-to-Boomi match.

The data and retrieval vocabulary

Vector database
A database that stores text (or images) as long lists of numbers called embeddings, which capture meaning rather than exact words. Ask it for "the ten chunks most similar in meaning to this question" and it answers fast. It is the storage layer underneath most retrieval-augmented generation.
Closest in his record: careful here. A named vector store is not established anywhere in his material, so do not name one. What is documented is KQ1's stack table putting Elasticsearch as the authoritative serving index, with Amazon Neptune deliberately kept off the serving path. That is a real retrieval-architecture decision and it is the honest thing to talk about.
RAG — retrieval-augmented generation
Instead of hoping the model already knows your company's facts, you first retrieve the relevant documents from your own systems, then paste them into the prompt and ask the model to answer using only those. It is the standard way to make a general model answer questions about private data.
Closest in his record: his, and documented in the killer-query case studies. One correction to keep straight: the earlier bullet claiming retrieval ran over an Amazon Neptune context graph was false and was corrected — KQ1's own stack table names Elasticsearch as the serving index. Never let that one come back.
Enterprise search
Search across everything a company holds — documents, wikis, tickets, email, file shares — with each person's permissions respected, so results differ by who is asking. The permissions part is what makes it hard, not the searching.
Closest in his record: Elasticsearch as a serving index, plus tenant-scoped access work. KQ9's wording is a "tenant-scoped traversal-source wrapper", which is how to say it.
Knowledge graph
Data stored as things and the relationships between them — patient, condition, medication, prescribed-by — rather than as rows in tables. It answers questions about connections and paths cheaply, which relational tables do badly.
Closest in his record: the Context Graph, plus Amazon Neptune, plus the graph neural network that he confirmed is real and which sits in word-position four of his headline. Expect to be asked to walk through it.

The safety and governance vocabulary

Grounding
Tying every statement a model makes back to a specific retrieved source, so an answer can be checked rather than trusted. An ungrounded system says something plausible; a grounded one says something plausible and shows the document it came from.
Closest in his record: the killer-query case studies are built on this idea throughout — a query answers from the patient record, with the evidence attached.
Hallucination risk
The model states something false with complete confidence, because it is predicting plausible text rather than looking anything up. "Risk" here means the engineering question: how likely is it in this system, how would you detect it, and what does it cost when it happens.
Closest in his record: this is prep territory, not résumé territory. Whether the large-language-model narrative guardrails actually shipped is an open confirm-or-reject item that only he settles, and KQ9's narrator ships off by default. Be ready to describe the design; do not assert a shipped guardrail.
Human-in-the-loop
The system is deliberately built so a person approves, edits or vetoes before anything consequential happens. Common in regulated settings: the agent drafts the refund, prepares the order, proposes the clinical note, and a human presses the button.
Closest in his record: the design-review and adversarial-review discipline in the case studies, and the decision to ship a narrator off by default, are both this instinct in practice.
Responsible AI
The umbrella term for fairness, transparency, privacy, safety and accountability in an AI system, usually expressed as a checklist a company must pass before a system goes live. Each large vendor has its own branded version of the same idea.
Closest in his record: the closest genuine analogue is regulated-delivery work — the HIPAA (Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act) certification requirement behind the chat platform's high-availability design, and ISO 27001 at Deque. Keep those two apart out loud: different employers, different certifications, and a listener will merge them if you do not separate them.

2. The verdict

Apply, and apply quickly.

The seat verifies all the way down — it sits on the company's own website, in the company's own applicant tracking system, under its own requisition number TYI-4328, posted 19 September 2026 (live requisition). It is in Hyderabad, where he already lives, against a real office rather than a paper address. The technology asked for maps onto his confirmed stack unusually closely: LangChain, LangGraph, Python, Kubernetes, Terraform, Model Context Protocol, microservices, event-driven architecture and retrieval-augmented generation are all named, and the cloud requirement is written with "or", so his AWS depth satisfies it as written.

The reservation is not about him and it is not about whether to apply. It is that this is a services firm, and the single most consequential fact about the seat — whether it is an internal architecture role on their own platform or a billable architect placed on a client programme — is not stated anywhere and could not be established from outside. That is a question for a call, asked early and asked plainly, not a reason to hold the application back. Applicant count moved from 36 to 41 within a day, so speed matters more than usual here.

One honest asymmetry worth naming to yourself before the call, though never volunteered in an outward-facing message. The job description demands "a production GenAI or Agentic AI system you've personally architected and shipped — not a POC". Meanwhile all seventeen of Tachyon's published case studies are SAP, Salesforce or analytics work, and not one of them is an AI project (case-study index). The candidate is being asked for exactly the evidence the employer has not published. That is not a disqualifier and plenty of firms are in this position in 2026, but it does mean the questions in section 6 about where the AI work actually is are fair, expected, and the sort of thing a senior architect is supposed to ask.

The shape of the business

Tachyon Technologies describes itself as founded in 2011 in Southlake, Texas, "with a clear focus: deliver enterprise technology services with the rigor of a large consultancy and the responsiveness of a boutique" (About Us). Its own timeline runs: foundation 2011–2013, global expansion 2014–2016 including delivery into India, the United Kingdom and the Middle East, platform diversification 2017–2019, scaling 2020–2022, and then an era it labels "The AI-First Era" for 2023–2026.

It sells nine service lines: artificial intelligence and generative AI, enterprise applications, digital transformation, testing services, data analytics and business intelligence, managed services, cloud services, professional information-technology staffing and augmentation, and the Internet of Things. Its technology practices are organised around SAP, Microsoft and Salesforce, with Snowflake, AWS, UiPath, Pyramid Analytics, Celonis and Databricks alongside (About Us). Industries claimed: utilities, banking and financial services and insurance, government, manufacturing, life sciences, oil and gas, retail, high technology, and professional services.

The single most clarifying fact in this whole research pass. Their own LinkedIn specialties list — which is the company's own older self-description, written before the AI rebrand — reads, verbatim: "SAP ERP Solutions, Enterprise Mobility, Business Intelligence and Analytics, Project Management, Staffing Solutions, SAP Fiori, SAP HANA, SAP Lumira, Digital Transformation Consulting, and SAP S/4HANA" (LinkedIn). There is no artificial intelligence in that list at all. This is an SAP-and-staffing business that has recently and genuinely pivoted toward AI, not an AI business with an SAP side.

Their own About page dates the pivot in their own words: in the 2023–2026 entry they write "Launched Tachyon Aura, a proprietary Agentic AI platform built for enterprise operations. Rebranded from 'Digital Transformation Solutions' to 'Digital & AI Solutions.'" So the AI positioning is roughly two to three years old (Our Story). Their mission wording is worth reading slowly too: "combining deep platform expertise in SAP, Salesforce, and Data with AI-first delivery". SAP, Salesforce and data are the expertise; AI is the adjective on delivery.

Tachyon Aura, their AI product

Aura is marketed as "The Agentic Enterprise Platform", deploying "governed AI agents that execute enterprise workflows autonomously". It is sold as staged engagements: a five-day Aura Sprint, a four-week proof of value, a twelve-week enterprise scale-up, with "$0 Setup Fee" and "$0 Until Go-Live" (Aura, how Aura operates).

Aura's headline numbers have no owner, and you should recognise the shape immediately because it is the same shape as KQ2's illustrative served-cell table in your own corpus. This is a forcing question, not a disqualification, and it is one he is unusually well placed to ask politely: which customer produced the 65%, and over what baseline? A firm that can answer that has a reference. A firm that cannot has a positioning page.

4. Green flags and red flags

Green flags

  • The seat verifies end to end. Company website, own applicant tracking system, own requisition number TYI-4328, posted date matching LinkedIn. It is not a job-board ghost and not an agency repost (requisition).
  • The location claim checks out against the company's own site and against two independent outlets. Hyderabad is in the footer of every page they publish, and the 35,000 square foot delivery centre was reported by two Telangana papers (Telangana Today, The Hans India). This is not an American posting with a decorative India tag.
  • Hyderabad is a genuine build-out, not a token office. Ten distinct engineering and architecture roles were published to Hyderabad in the six days from 14 to 19 September 2026, and their full job feed carries 75 open postings, 48 tagged India (jobs archive, their own job feed).
  • They are recruiting in Hyderabad openly and directly, corroborated off their own property. A post on r/hyderabad five days before this research, from a Tachyon person, lists four Hyderabad full-time hybrid engineering openings with a named company email address and links back to their own job pages (r/hyderabad).
  • The cloud requirement is written with "or". "Azure, AWS, or GCP" and "Azure, AWS, and/or GCP". His AWS depth satisfies the line as written, which removes what looked beforehand like the material blocker (job description).
  • The technical ask maps unusually well onto his confirmed stack. LangChain, LangGraph, Python, Kubernetes, containers and serverless, Terraform, microservices and REST, event-driven architecture, relational plus NoSQL plus vector data platforms, retrieval-augmented generation, tool and function calling, multi-agent orchestration, and Model Context Protocol — with Aura's own "MCP Connector Framework" making his MCP server work directly on point rather than adjacent (requirements, Aura).

In-house or agency

In-house. There is no agency middleman anywhere in the chain. The posting is on Tachyon's own domain, in Tachyon's own applicant-tracking tenant, under Tachyon's own requisition number. The hybrid claim, which is weaker than it looks

"Hybrid" comes from the LinkedIn card and from nowhere else. The words hybrid, onsite, on-site, remote and relocation appear zero times in the company's own job description text, zero times in the Oorwin requisition record, and zero times in LinkedIn's guest render of the job. 6. Forcing questions for the call

These are ordered by how much they change the decision, not by how comfortable they are to ask. The first four resolve the single biggest unknown: whether this is an internal architecture seat on Tachyon's own platform, or a billable architect placed on a client programme. Question five resolves the cloud. Everything after that is important but secondary. Each question carries one line on why it matters and one line on what a bad answer sounds like, so the answer can be judged in the moment rather than replayed afterwards.

The four that decide what the job actually is

1. Over the first twelve months, whose system would I be the architect of — Tachyon Aura, or a named client's programme?

Why it matters: this is the whole question. Tachyon runs an internal agentic platform and an openly sold staffing line, and it uses the title "AI Architect" on both sides — the Winchester, Virginia contract placement proves that (that posting). Everything else about the seat follows from the answer.
A bad answer sounds like: "both, depending on demand", "wherever the business needs you", or a long description of flexibility. A good answer names a system or names a client programme, and can say who else is on it.

2. Is this role billable to a client, partly billable, or carried as internal investment? And what happens between engagements?

Why it matters: job security is the lowest-rated axis on every platform that measures this employer, and the stated reason is always the same — Glassdoor's own summary of its reviewers reads "job security is highly dependent on ongoing client projects and their requirements" (Glassdoor). Asking it directly is completely normal in Indian services hiring and nobody will find it rude.
A bad answer sounds like: deflection, or "we always have work". A good answer describes the actual mechanism — a utilisation target, a bench policy, or an internal-investment budget line — without being asked twice.

3. Is this a newly created headcount or a backfill, and is it funded against a signed client programme or a pre-sales bet?

Why it matters: the near-identical predecessor seat, "AI Solution Architect, Agentic Systems", was live in November 2025 and now returns a 404 error, and a separate LinkedIn "AI Architect (12–15 Years Exp)" posting expired (archived posting). The seat family has churned at least once and it is fair to ask why.
A bad answer sounds like: not knowing, or "that was a different requirement" with no detail. A good answer says plainly whether it was filled, cancelled, or folded into this one.

4. Which Tachyon Aura deployment is furthest along today, and who is the customer behind the "65% faster MTTR" figure on the Aura page?

Why it matters: Aura's headline metrics carry no customer, no baseline and no label distinguishing achieved from targeted (Aura). If the platform has a live reference, the seat has a real system to architect against. If it does not, the seat is earlier-stage than the posting implies. He is unusually well placed to ask this politely, because his own corpus contains exactly this distinction between an illustrative response shape and a measured result.
A bad answer sounds like: repeating the marketing numbers back, or "those are from our internal benchmarks" with no baseline offered. A good answer names a deployment, even under a non-disclosure agreement, and describes the baseline.

The cloud, and the technical reality

5. The posting reads "Azure, AWS, or GCP". In practice, which cloud do the AI engagements actually land on, and what proportion?

Why it matters: the requirement is genuinely an "or", so his AWS depth satisfies it as written — but four of ten current Hyderabad roles are Azure-titled or Azure-primary, and the sibling Enterprise Solution Architect wants "strong Azure cloud platform knowledge" (that posting). Interestingly, their own Cloud Services page leads with AWS ahead of Azure (Cloud Services), so the picture is genuinely mixed rather than settled. He should know before day one whether he is ramping on Azure OpenAI or working in his own territory.
A bad answer sounds like: "all three, we're cloud-agnostic". Every consultancy says that and it is almost never true of the actual engagement mix. A good answer gives a rough split and names which practice sits on which.

6. What does the architecture team look like today — how many architects, how many AI engineers, and who would I report to?

Why it matters: the posting says "mentor senior engineers and technical leads", which is mentorship, not line management, and it does not say which. The named practice owner is Sujoe Bose, global head of artificial intelligence and data analytics (leadership page), but nothing says the seat reports to him.
A bad answer sounds like: "we're building that out" with no current number. A good answer gives a headcount today and a reporting line by name.

7. What has the AI practice actually shipped to production for a paying customer, and can you walk me through one of them?

Why it matters: the posting demands production work "not a POC", and none of their seventeen published case studies is an AI project (case studies). Asking an architect to describe a shipped system is the most normal question in the world, and the quality of the answer tells him more than any rating site could.
A bad answer sounds like: pilots, proofs of concept, and the word "roadmap". A good answer describes a live system with its integration points and what broke in production.

The working reality

8. What are the working hours for this seat, and is there an American or European overlap expectation?

Why it matters: this job description is silent on hours, but a sibling AI seat at the same firm states "Remote – 2–11 PM" outright (Lead AI Engineer), and the Why Tachyon section promises "direct exposure to customer stakeholders" at a firm whose customers are in North America, Europe and the Middle East. This is the single most important thing the posting does not say.
A bad answer sounds like: "flexible", with no core hours named. A good answer states core hours and says how often a late call is genuinely required.

9. Is this seat hybrid, and which Hyderabad site would I actually report to?

Why it matters: "hybrid" appears only on LinkedIn's card and nowhere in the company's own text, while the sibling posting from two days earlier says "Hyderabad, India – Hybrid" outright. And three different micro-locations appear across sources — Jubilee Hills (registered office), the Financial District (the 2025 delivery centre) and Madhapur. Those are very different commutes.
A bad answer sounds like: vagueness about days per week, or "we'll work it out". A good answer names the building and the expected days.

10. Is there travel to client sites, in India or abroad, and how often?

Why it matters: employees report "No travel" at 87% on AmbitionBox (AmbitionBox), which is reassuring — but that review base is dominated by a recruiting desk, and a client-facing architect is a different job from a recruiter. The job description says nothing about travel at all.
A bad answer sounds like: "as needed". A good answer gives a cadence, even a rough one.

11. Which legal entity issues the employment contract — Tachyon Technologies in the United States, or Tachyon IT Solutions Private Limited in Hyderabad?

Why it matters: no document anywhere establishes the relationship between the American brand and the Indian company, and neither party's website mentions the other. The applicant-tracking tenant is tachyonind and the requisition prefix is TYI, both pointing at an Indian entity, but that is inference (Tracxn record). It is an ordinary administrative question and easy to ask without loading it.
A bad answer sounds like: confusion, or not knowing. A good answer names the entity immediately, because human resources knows this by heart.

12. The posting asks for 15+ years, but the requisition record says 15–20. Is 20 a filter?

Why it matters: the live requisition TYI-4328 carries "Experience 15-20", narrower than the job description's open-ended "15+" (requisition). A band in an applicant-tracking system is sometimes a screening rule rather than a description, and it is better to know than to guess.
A bad answer sounds like: being unable to say. A good answer is simply "it is a guide" or "it is a filter" — either is fine, and he knows where he stands.

Worth asking if the call has room

13. Which enterprise platforms do the AI integrations most often land on — SAP, Salesforce, ServiceNow — and which integration tooling do you standardise on?

Why it matters: their one published integration case study names Boomi orchestrating into SAP and internal billing (case study), and MuleSoft appears nowhere on their site. This is where his 25-supplier enterprise-service-bus work is genuinely transferable — and where he needs to frame it as iPaaS experience rather than claiming a tool match he does not have.
A bad answer sounds like: "it depends on the client" with nothing further. A good answer names a default and explains when they deviate.

14. How large is the Hyderabad centre now, and how did the 500-person plan announced in January 2025 land?

Why it matters: the plan was publicly announced with a state minister present (Telangana Today), and no independent headcount source shows growth on anything like that scale. It is entirely reasonable to ask how the build-out actually went, and the answer says a lot about how the firm talks about itself.
A bad answer sounds like: repeating the 500 figure as though it happened, or quoting "1,000+ globally" — a figure no independent source supports. A good answer gives a real current number for Hyderabad.

15. Which industries would this seat serve most, and is there a utilities concentration?

Why it matters: the case studies, the DTECH 2026 dinner on "Agentic AI for utility transformation" covering SAP IS-U device management and FI-CA billing (DTECH 2026), and Aura's utilities bundle all point the same way. If the answer is heavily utilities, his non-technical and functional score is low — which per the house rules is informational only, never a gate, and must not touch the technical score or the decision to apply.
A bad answer sounds like: naming nine industries. A good answer names two or three where the AI work actually is.

16. Who would interview me, and could one round be with an engineer who has shipped one of these systems?

Why it matters: no recruiter or hiring-manager name appears anywhere in the posting, the requisition or LinkedIn's public view, so the application has no human anchor. Asking for a technical round with a practitioner is a normal request at this level, and it also answers question seven indirectly.
A bad answer sounds like: a process with no engineer in it. A good answer names people.

Two discipline notes on using this list. First, compensation is not on it and must not be raised — that conversation belongs to a later stage, when a company has responded and the process has actually reached it. Second, if this call happens because a recruiter replied to him, the recruiter-reply rule governs instead: answer exactly what was asked, in the order asked, then put a call on the table, and bring this list to the call rather than to the reply. Every extra question in a written reply is one more thing standing between him and a phone call.
