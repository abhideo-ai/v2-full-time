1. First, the vocabulary

Tachyon's world is enterprise software consulting, which has its own vocabulary. Each entry below explains what the thing actually is and why it matters for this seat, not just what the letters stand for.

TermWhat it means, in plain English
ERP — enterprise resource planningThe big back-office system a large company runs on: finance, purchasing, inventory, payroll, orders, all in one shared database. When a company's accounts, stock and staff records "live in one place", that place is the ERP. It is slow and expensive to change, which is why consultancies exist to change it.
SAPA German software company and, by far, the best-known ERP vendor. "An SAP shop" means a consultancy whose main business is installing, upgrading and running SAP systems for clients. Tachyon's own history says it was founded on SAP work.
SAP S/4HANAThe current generation of SAP's ERP, running on SAP's own in-memory database (HANA). Many large companies are being pushed to migrate from the older version (ECC — ERP Central Component) before support ends, which creates a steady stream of consulting work.
SAP BTP — Business Technology PlatformSAP's cloud platform for building extensions and integrations around the ERP without modifying the ERP itself. "Clean core" is the idea of keeping the ERP unmodified and putting custom logic on BTP instead. It matters here because AI agents that touch SAP would normally connect through this layer.
SAP SuccessFactors · SAP AribaSAP's cloud products for human resources (hiring, onboarding, performance) and for procurement (buying from suppliers). Most of Tachyon's published case studies are SuccessFactors projects.
CRM — customer relationship managementThe system a company uses to track customers, sales opportunities and support tickets. Salesforce is the dominant CRM vendor. Tachyon runs a Salesforce practice alongside its SAP one.
ServiceNow · ITSM — IT service managementServiceNow is the leading software for running a company's internal IT help desk: incident tickets, change requests, service catalogues. ITSM is the discipline it serves. Tachyon's AI platform markets itself heavily at this use case ("agentic IT operations").
Staff augmentationA consultancy supplies individual people who work inside a client's team, usually billed by the hour, instead of delivering a whole project. The consultancy is the legal employer; the client directs the daily work. This matters because Tachyon openly sells it, and the most important question about this seat is whether it is an internal role or a placement.
ATS — applicant tracking systemThe software a company uses to receive and manage job applications. A requisition number (here TYI-4328) is the ATS's internal ID for one open job.
OorwinThe ATS Tachyon uses. Its India job board is hosted at tachyonind.oorwin.ai ("tachyonind" reads as Tachyon India). When LinkedIn says "responses managed off LinkedIn", this is where the application actually lands. It is not a job board and not a recruiting agency.
Agentic AI · multi-agent orchestrationSoftware where a large language model (LLM) does not just answer a question but decides on steps, calls tools, and takes actions — raising a ticket, updating a record. "Multi-agent orchestration" means several such agents, each with a narrow job, coordinated by a controlling layer.
RAG — retrieval-augmented generationBefore the model answers, the system first searches a company's own documents and hands the relevant passages to the model, so the answer is grounded in the company's data rather than the model's memory.
MCP — Model Context ProtocolAn open standard for how an AI model connects to outside tools and data: a system exposes an "MCP server" and any MCP-aware model can call it. Tachyon's platform names an "MCP Connector Framework" as one of its parts.
HITL — human-in-the-loopA design where a person must approve, edit or veto before the system takes a consequential action. The job description (JD) asks for "human-in-the-loop controls".
SLM — small language modelA smaller, cheaper model used for narrow tasks instead of a large general model, to cut cost and latency. Tachyon's platform lists "SLM optimization".
POC — proof of concept · PoV — proof of valueA POC shows something can work technically; a PoV shows it produces a business result on real data. The JD explicitly wants "not a POC"; Tachyon sells a "4-Week Proof of Value".
MTTR — mean time to resolution · FCR — first-contact resolutionTwo help-desk metrics: how long, on average, an incident takes to close; and what share of customer issues are solved in the first interaction. Tachyon's platform page advertises improvements to both.
AML — anti-money-launderingBank monitoring of transactions for signs of money laundering. One of Tachyon's published AI outcome stories is an AML transaction-monitoring project for an unnamed bank.
CMMI Level 3 — Capability Maturity Model IntegrationAn externally appraised rating of how defined and repeatable a firm's delivery process is. Level 3 means processes are standardised company-wide. It is a process rating, not a security or quality certificate.
CIN — corporate identification number · ROC — Registrar of CompaniesThe Indian government's registration number for a company, and the office that holds its filings. Used below to identify Tachyon's Indian legal entity.

Name collisions — do not mix these up. Tachyon Aura is Tachyon's own AI platform; it is not Salesforce's unrelated "Aura" front-end framework. And Tachyon Technologies (tachyontech.com, Texas) is not Tachyum (processors), Tachyon Networks (satellite) or any other "Tachyon". News about those companies is not news about this employer.

4. What the company does

Founding and shape. Tachyon's own About page says it was founded in 2011 in Southlake, Texas, and tells its history in five eras: SAP foundation (2011–2013), expansion into India, the United Kingdom and the Middle East (2014–2016), adding Salesforce and data practices and reaching CMMI Level 3 (2017–2019), crossing 500 employees (2020–2022), and "The AI-First Era" (2023–2026), in which it "launched Tachyon Aura, a proprietary Agentic AI platform" and rebranded from "Digital Transformation Solutions" to "Digital & AI Solutions" (About Us, accessed 24 Sep 2026). Its mission sentence is a fair summary of the whole company: "combining deep platform expertise in SAP, Salesforce, and Data with AI-first delivery."

Service lines. Nine, in the site navigation: AI and generative AI, enterprise applications, digital transformation, testing, data analytics and business intelligence, managed services, cloud services, professional IT staffing and augmentation, and the Internet of Things (IoT). Technology practices are SAP, Microsoft (Azure, Copilot) and Salesforce, with Snowflake, AWS (Amazon Web Services), UiPath, Pyramid Analytics, Celonis and Databricks alongside. Industries claimed: utilities, banking and financial services and insurance, government, manufacturing, life sciences, oil and gas, retail, high tech, and professional services (About Us).

Its older self-description. The company's LinkedIn page — headquarters Southlake, Texas; privately held; founded 2011 — still lists its specialties as "SAP ERP Solutions, Enterprise Mobility, Business Intelligence and Analytics, Project Management, Staffing Solutions, SAP Fiori, SAP HANA…" with no AI in the list (LinkedIn company page, accessed 24 Sep 2026). Read together with the About page, this is an SAP-and-services firm that pivoted toward AI roughly two to three years ago, not an AI company with an SAP side.

Tachyon Aura, the AI product this seat would most likely touch

Aura is marketed as "The Agentic Enterprise Platform" that "deploys governed AI agents that execute enterprise workflows autonomously". Its named parts are multi-agent orchestration, an MCP Connector Framework, context engineering, trust and observability, SLM optimisation, and an "AI Control Tower". It is sold as a 5-day sprint, a 4-week proof of value, and a 12-week enterprise scale-up, with "$0 Setup Fee" and outcome-based fees (Aura, accessed 24 Sep 2026). A second page walks through a five-stage loop — observe, understand, orchestrate, execute, learn — naming ServiceNow, Splunk, Datadog, AppDynamics, Salesforce, SAP S/4HANA and Jira as systems it connects to (How Aura operates).

A Customer Stories page now publishes three Aura outcome stories — an enterprise IT department (45,000+ monthly incidents, "62% MTTR reduction", "78% auto-resolved"), a global bank's AML transaction monitoring ("68% fewer false positives", "82% lower inference cost"), and a health network ("47% less admin burden") (Customer Stories, accessed 24 Sep 2026). None of the three names a customer, and no model, cloud or architecture is named on any Aura page. That is normal for a consultancy's marketing, and it is exactly what the forcing questions below are for.

Delivered work it has published

The case-study library lists 17 entries. Ten are SAP SuccessFactors human-resources implementations; the rest are SAP utility operations, SAP Analytics Cloud and Business Warehouse reporting, SAP Ariba as a service, and three Salesforce projects (energy-sales platforms and a Salesforce Field Service rollout integrated with SAP for an industrial manufacturer, "Presented at Dreamforce 2026") (case-study index, field-service case, accessed 24 Sep 2026). None of the 17 is an AI project; the AI evidence lives only on the separate Aura Customer Stories page above.

7. Forcing questions for the first call

Each one targets something the JD leaves open. Ask them in roughly this order; the first two decide most of the rest.

Q1 Internal platform, or client programme?

"Is this role building Tachyon's own AI capability — Aura and the reusable patterns — or is it an architect placed on a specific client's programme? If it's client work, is it one long engagement or several?"

Good answer: a clear yes to internal, or a named programme with a stated duration and what happens after it ends. Bad answer: "It depends on the project" with no current project named.

Tachyon sells staffing and has an "AI Architect" contract placement live on the same site. This answer decides what kind of job it really is.

Q2 How much is pre-sales versus building?

"Roughly what share of the week is customer-facing — discovery workshops, proposals, proof-of-value pilots — versus designing and building systems that go to production?"

Good answer: a rough split, and an example of a pilot that went on to production. Bad answer: mostly workshops and demos, with delivery handed to others.

The JD lists "technical discovery, architecture workshops… proof-of-concept initiatives" and "Direct exposure to customer stakeholders", while Aura is sold through 5-day sprints and 4-week proofs of value.

Q3 Which cloud is primary?

"The JD says Azure, AWS or GCP. In practice, where do most of your AI workloads run today, and which managed AI service — Azure OpenAI, Bedrock, Vertex — do you use most?"

The seat's own text is cloud-neutral, but sibling postings lean on Azure. Knowing the real answer lets him prepare the right comparisons.

Q4 Which enterprise platforms will the agents actually touch?

"The JD names Salesforce, SAP, ServiceNow and other ERP and CRM systems. Which ones come up in the current programmes, and how do agents connect to them — through Aura's MCP connectors, SAP BTP, or custom integrations?"

Tachyon's roots are SAP and Salesforce, and Aura's pages name ServiceNow, Splunk and Jira. This decides which systems he needs to talk about fluently.

Q5 Is the generative AI work in production now?

"Which Aura deployments are live in production today, and could you tell me more about one of the customer stories — the IT operations one, for example — the baseline behind the 62% figure and what the architecture looks like?"

Good answer: a specific deployment, even if the client stays anonymous, with real details. Bad answer: repeating the marketing numbers.

The JD insists on "not a POC" from the candidate; it is fair to learn how much of Tachyon's own AI work is past that line.

Q6 Team size and reporting line

"How big is the AI and data practice in Hyderabad, how many architects are in it, and who would I report to — is it Sujoe Bose's group?"

The JD says "mentor senior engineers" and "help build strong architecture practice" without saying whether there is one yet.

Q7 Work mode and hours

"The LinkedIn listing says hybrid, but the posting on your site doesn't mention it. How many days in the office, which office — Financial District? — and are there fixed overlap hours with United States clients?"

"Hybrid" appears only on the LinkedIn card; a sibling AI role lists 2–11 PM hours; and Hyderabad addresses differ across sources.
