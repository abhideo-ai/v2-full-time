Glossary: every acronym and unfamiliar term, expanded

Two unfamiliar fields meet in this seat: media measurement, which is Nielsen’s main business, and entertainment metadata, which is Gracenote’s. Each entry says what the thing is and why it matters for this seat. The full list of acronyms, on every page of this workspace, is at the end.

The companies, and who owns them

Nielsen (The Nielsen Company)
A company based in New York that measures what people watch and listen to, best known for television ratings. It is the employer here. Its own description is “a global leader in audience measurement, data and analytics”.
Gracenote
Nielsen’s content-data business unit, and the part of Nielsen this seat works for. It sells the data that describes entertainment: titles, descriptions, cast, pictures, genres, schedules, where to watch, sports results and music details, plus identifiers that link records together. It has its own chief executive and chief technology officer.
Tribune Media Services (TMS)
The TV-listings business, begun in 1965, that grew into Gracenote’s video side. Tribune Media sold Gracenote to Nielsen for $560 million in 2017.
NielsenIQ
Nielsen’s former retail-measurement business, sold to Advent International in March 2021 and now a separate company. News and reviews about NielsenIQ are not about this employer.
Private equity (PE), take-private and consortium
Private equity firms buy companies, usually with a lot of borrowed money, aim to make them more profitable, and later sell them. A take-private buys every share of a listed company, so it leaves the stock exchange. A consortium is a group of investors buying together. Nielsen was taken private in October 2022 for about $16 billion including debt. Why it matters: owners like these usually push hard on costs, which is the background to Nielsen’s layoffs (our reading).
Elliott, Evergreen Coast Capital and Brookfield
Elliott Investment Management is a United States investment firm; Evergreen Coast Capital is its technology-investing affiliate. Brookfield Business Partners is part of the Canadian investor Brookfield. Together they led the consortium that owns Nielsen. Brookfield is listed on stock exchanges, so it reports on Nielsen in its own results.
Earnings call, and refinancing
An earnings call is the conference call in which a listed company discusses its quarterly results with investors; Brookfield’s call on 30 January 2026 described Nielsen’s cost savings. Refinancing means replacing old loans with new ones on better terms.
Divest, and non-core
To divest is to sell off a business unit. Non-core units are those outside a company’s main business. In January 2024 there was press speculation that Nielsen might sell non-core units, possibly including Gracenote; we found no report of a sale since.
Global Capability Centre (GCC), and offshoring
A Global Capability Centre is a company’s own office in another country, often India, that does work for the whole group; the staff are the company’s own employees, not an outsourcing firm’s. Offshoring means moving work to such places. Nielsen’s chief executive outlined GCCs to staff in January 2024, and insiders told the press that work was moving to India, Poland and Mexico. Why it matters: it helps explain why a principal seat is open in India.
Xperi and TiVo
Xperi is a United States technology company that owns the TiVo brand and sells entertainment metadata in competition with Gracenote. Its annual report names Gracenote as a competitor, which is independent evidence of Gracenote’s standing.

Media measurement: Nielsen’s main business

Audience measurement and ratings
Counting who watches or listens to what, and for how long. The resulting ratings are the numbers broadcasters and advertisers use to price advertising. This is Nielsen’s best-known business, though not the part this seat works in.
Panel
A group of households or people who agree to have their viewing or listening recorded; their behaviour stands in for the whole population. “Panel management” was one of the areas hit by Nielsen’s September 2023 layoffs.
Ad-intel
Short for advertising intelligence: tracking which advertisements ran where and when. Another area named in the 2023 layoffs.
Media Rating Council (MRC) and accreditation
The Media Rating Council is the United States industry body that audits audience-measurement services. Its accreditation is a stamp of approval. Nielsen’s national TV service lost it for about a year and a half and regained it in April 2023. That concerns Nielsen’s ratings, not Gracenote.

Entertainment metadata: Gracenote’s business

Metadata
Data about content, as opposed to the content itself: a film’s title, cast, genre, running time, description, picture, age rating and so on. Why it matters: this seat’s platform exists to collect, clean, link and deliver it.
Linear TV, channels and listings
Linear TV is scheduled television, where a channel shows one programme after another at set times, as opposed to on-demand viewing. Listings are the schedules. Gracenote’s Video Data claims details for “75,000+ linear channels”.
Electronic program guide (EPG)
The on-screen TV guide on a set-top box or smart TV. TMS moved from printed listings to EPG data for cable and satellite providers in 1993.
Streaming catalog and availability
A streaming catalog is everything a streaming service offers. Availability data says which title is on which service, in which country, from when to when: the “where to watch” answer. It changes constantly, which makes it an engineering problem of freshness and scale.
FAST (free ad-supported streaming TV)
Free streaming channels paid for by advertising, which look like linear TV but run over the internet. Gracenote lists “nearly 2,000 FAST channels”.
CTV (connected TV)
A television that streams over the internet, directly or through a device plugged into it. Gracenote sells a product called CTV Content Intelligence.
Content IDs (Gracenote IDs)
Stable identifiers (IDs) that give each programme, episode or film one code, so different companies and systems can agree that two records describe the same thing. Why it matters: matching records from many sources to one ID, at scale and without errors, is a core data-engineering problem for this seat.
Knowledge graph
Data stored as things and the relationships between them: this actor appeared in that episode of that series, made by that studio. Gracenote says its graph “maps relationships among programs, cast and crew” and feeds its AI products.
Editorial data, and “machine-driven, human-validated”
Much of Gracenote’s data is written or checked by human editors, many in its content-operations teams, including in Mumbai. Its postings describe the data as “Machine-driven, human-validated”: software proposes, people approve.
Video Descriptors
Gracenote’s tags for a show’s mood, theme, scenario and characters, launched in 2019; its example for one drama gave “Dark” and “Gripping” as moods. Gracenote says they come from data scientists, editors and machine learning together.
Ingestion, processing and delivery
The three stages the job description names. Ingestion brings raw data in from many suppliers; processing cleans, matches and enriches it; delivery sends it to customers as data files, application programming interfaces (APIs) or streams. The seat is asked to make data flow “seamlessly & consistently” across all three.
Platformisation
Building shared platforms that many teams and products reuse, instead of a separate pipeline for each product. Gracenote’s manager postings say it is modernising its metadata pipelines “through platformization”, which is the kind of work a principal engineer leads.

Music, radio, cars and sports

Automatic content recognition (ACR) and fingerprinting
Identifying what is playing from the sound or picture itself. Software turns a few seconds of audio or video into a compact fingerprint and looks it up in a database. Gracenote’s music recognition, sold today as MusicID, dates from 1998, when it began by identifying compact discs.
Smart Radio and Nexus Auto
Gracenote’s products for cars: radio-station names and logos, song details and sports scores on the dashboard. Gracenote says its music recognition “now powers more than 300 million cars globally”.
Sports data
Schedules, live scores, standings and statistics. Gracenote claims 75 sports and about 300,000 games or matches a year, and plans a Sports MCP Server (see the AI words below).

Advertising technology

Programmatic advertising
Buying and selling advertising space automatically, through software and auctions, rather than through salespeople.
DSP (demand-side platform) and SSP (supply-side platform)
A demand-side platform is the software advertisers use to buy advertising space; The Trade Desk is one, and Gracenote announced its “first DSP integration” with it in August 2026. A supply-side platform is the software publishers use to sell space. See the collision note below: DSP also means digital signal processing.
Contextual targeting
Placing an advertisement according to what is on screen, the show and its genre, rather than according to who the viewer is. Gracenote’s metadata makes this possible for TV.

AI words on these pages

AI and ML
Artificial intelligence (AI) is software that performs tasks that normally need human judgement. Machine learning (ML) is the part of AI where software learns patterns from data. The job description asks for “Working knowledge of machine learning and data science concepts”.
LLM (large language model) and SLM (small language model)
An LLM is a very large AI model trained on text, such as the models behind ChatGPT. An SLM is a smaller model trained for a narrower job, cheaper and faster to run. Gracenote plans an “entertainment-focused” SLM.
MCP (Model Context Protocol) and MCP server
An open standard that lets AI models call outside tools and data in a uniform way. An MCP server is the program that offers those tools and data. Gracenote’s Video MCP Server lets a customer’s AI model look up Gracenote’s verified data instead of guessing.
Grounding, hallucination and fabrication
A language model can fabricate (or “hallucinate”) facts that sound right but are wrong. Grounding gives it real data to answer from. Gracenote’s June 2026 study found a model with no grounding made up every detail for 506 of 2,600 titles, and it sells grounding as its answer.
Agents
AI programs that carry out a task in several steps, using tools, rather than only answering one question. Gracenote plans “task-oriented agents”.
RAG (retrieval-augmented generation)
A common grounding method: fetch the relevant documents or records first, then let the model answer from them. It appears in one of Gracenote’s AI job postings. See the collision note below.
Computer vision
AI that understands images and video: finding objects, reading on-screen text, recognising scenes. Gracenote is hiring a principal data scientist for it. It is also shortened to CV; see the collision note below.
YOLO, Mask R-CNN and diffusion models
Named in that posting. YOLO (“You Only Look Once”) is a family of fast object-detection models. Mask R-CNN (a mask region-based convolutional neural network) outlines each object in an image. Diffusion models generate images or video by removing noise step by step.
NVIDIA DeepStream, Triton Inference Server and TensorRT
NVIDIA’s software for running AI models on its graphics chips: DeepStream analyses video streams, Triton serves models to other programs, and TensorRT makes models run faster.
PyTorch, TensorFlow, Amazon Bedrock and SageMaker
PyTorch and TensorFlow are the two main open-source toolkits for building neural networks. Amazon Bedrock gives access to hosted AI models on Amazon Web Services; SageMaker is Amazon’s platform for training and running your own models.

The job description’s engineering words

Microservices
An application built as many small services, each owning one job and its own data, deployed independently.
API, REST and GraphQL
An application programming interface (API) is the agreed way one program asks another for data or actions. REST (representational state transfer) is the common style for web APIs, built on web addresses and the verbs of HTTP (Hypertext Transfer Protocol). GraphQL is a query language for APIs in which the caller asks for exactly the fields it needs; a Gracenote manager posting uses it “to serve metadata”.
Domain-Driven Design (DDD)
Designing software around the business it serves: engineers and domain experts share one vocabulary, and a large system is split into “bounded contexts”, each with its own model.
Event-driven architecture, asynchronous communication and message queues
Services tell each other that something happened by publishing events, and others react in their own time (asynchronously) instead of waiting for a reply. A message queue holds the messages in between. Apache Kafka is the best-known event log of this kind; Amazon MSK runs it as a managed service.
Highly available, fault-tolerant, resilient
A highly available system stays up nearly all the time; fault-tolerant means it keeps working when parts fail; resilient means it recovers quickly when something does go wrong.
Batch and stream processing
Batch processing handles data in large scheduled chunks, for example every night. Stream processing handles each record as it arrives, in real time. The job description wants both.
Lambda architecture and Kappa architecture
Two designs for combining them. The Lambda architecture runs a batch layer that recomputes complete, accurate results and a speed layer that covers the latest events, then merges the two. The Kappa architecture uses a single streaming path and rebuilds results by replaying the stored event log. See the collision note below: this Lambda is not AWS Lambda.
Spark, Flink, Hadoop and Airflow
Open-source tools for large-scale data. Spark processes big datasets across many machines; Flink specialises in streams; Hadoop is an older big-data framework; Airflow schedules and monitors data workflows.
Web crawling, Apache Nutch and Scrapy
A web crawler visits web pages automatically and collects data from them. Nutch (Java) and Scrapy (Python) are crawler frameworks. A Gracenote manager posting lists crawling among the team’s domains.
SQL and NoSQL datastores
SQL (Structured Query Language) is the language of relational databases, which keep data in linked tables: PostgreSQL (Postgres), MySQL, Oracle, Amazon Aurora. NoSQL covers every other kind: key-value and document stores such as DynamoDB and MongoDB, wide-column stores such as Cassandra, search engines such as OpenSearch and Elasticsearch, and in-memory stores such as Redis and Memcached, which are used as fast caches.
AWS (Amazon Web Services) and cloud-native
Amazon’s cloud: computing, storage and managed services rented by the hour. Cloud-native means designed from the start to use such managed services and to scale out, rather than moved over from own servers. The job description says “primarily AWS”.
The AWS services named in Nielsen’s postings
EKS (Elastic Kubernetes Service) runs Kubernetes; MSK (Managed Streaming for Apache Kafka) runs Kafka; EMR (formerly Elastic MapReduce) runs Spark and Hadoop clusters; Glue runs extract-transform-load jobs, which move and reshape data; RDS (Relational Database Service) runs relational databases; S3 (Simple Storage Service) stores files; DynamoDB is Amazon’s NoSQL database. Bedrock and SageMaker are under the AI words above.
FinOps
Short for financial operations: engineers, finance and product owners managing cloud spending together, so the cost of every design choice is known and kept down. The job description asks for “cost-efficient architecture (FinOps)”.
Docker, containers, Kubernetes and orchestration
Docker packages an application and everything it needs into a container that runs the same everywhere. Kubernetes is the orchestration system that runs many containers across many machines, restarting and scaling them automatically.
Infrastructure as code (IaC), Terraform and CloudFormation
Describing servers, networks and cloud services in files that a tool then creates, so infrastructure can be reviewed and repeated like code. Terraform is the common cross-cloud tool; CloudFormation is Amazon’s own.
Observability, distributed tracing and telemetry
Observability is being able to tell what a running system is doing from what it reports. Telemetry is those reports: metrics, logs and traces. Distributed tracing follows one request across all the services it touches. Nielsen names Prometheus (metrics), Grafana (dashboards), Loki (logs) and OpenTelemetry (the open standard for collecting all three).
CI/CD, Jenkins and GitLab CI
Continuous integration merges and tests every change automatically; continuous delivery or deployment releases it automatically. Jenkins and GitLab CI are tools that run these pipelines.
Test-Driven Development (TDD)
Writing a failing automated test first, then the code that makes it pass, then tidying up.
SDLC, Agile and Scrum
The software development life cycle (SDLC) is the full path from idea to design, build, test, release and upkeep. Agile is the family of methods that work in short cycles with frequent feedback; Scrum is its best-known form, with fixed “sprints” and daily stand-ups.
JIRA, Confluence and OpsGenie
Atlassian’s tools, named in a Nielsen posting: JIRA tracks tasks and bugs, Confluence is a shared wiki, and OpsGenie sends alerts and manages on-call rotas.
HTML, CSS and JavaScript
The three languages of web pages: HTML (HyperText Markup Language) gives structure, CSS (Cascading Style Sheets) gives appearance, and JavaScript gives behaviour. The job description only “prefers” familiarity with them.
Java, Scala, Python, Go, Rust and C++
Programming languages. Java, Scala and Python are the job description’s main three; Go (also called Golang) and Rust are “a strong plus” for performance-critical parts; C++ appears in one of Nielsen’s manager postings. Spring and Hibernate are Java frameworks for services and database access; Angular is a framework for web front ends.
Open-source tools in Nielsen’s public code
dbt and Trino transform and query data with SQL; Delta Lake is a table format for data lakes; Superset draws dashboards; Deequ writes “unit tests for data” on Spark; nebula is Nielsen’s own library for data-transformation pipelines. A fork is a copy of someone else’s code repository.
Chef and BIND
Named in one of Gracenote’s old public repositories: Chef is a tool that configures servers automatically, and BIND is long-standing, widely used software for running DNS (Domain Name System) servers, which turn web addresses into machine addresses.

Job titles and hiring words

Individual contributor (IC)
An engineer who designs and builds the work personally and leads through influence, with no one reporting to them. The seats he pursues are IC seats; the research reads this one as IC.
Member Technical Staff (MTS) and the ladder
The family of engineering titles in Nielsen’s India postings: Member Technical Staff (MTS, with levels such as II and 3), Senior Member Technical Staff (Senior MTS), Principal Member Technical Staff (PMTS), and Senior Principal Member Technical Staff. People managers are titled “Senior Manager, MTS” and “Director, MTS”. At many other companies the senior IC levels are called Senior, Staff, Principal and Distinguished; titles never map one-to-one between companies.
Requisition and reference number
A requisition is a company’s internal approval to hire for one seat. This posting’s reference number is 71732775-20260726; the last part reads like a date, 26 July 2026 (our inference).
Applicant tracking system (ATS), SmartRecruiters and Recruitee
Software that companies use to post jobs and keep candidates’ records. SmartRecruiters is the one Nielsen’s careers page links to for every job search. Recruitee is another, cited here only for how such systems handle consent.
Sourced candidate
A person a recruiter adds to the system from elsewhere, such as a job board, instead of someone who applied on the company’s own site. He became one on 28 September 2026: the recruiter told him on her call that she is using his Naukri profile for this job.
Consent-to-process email, privacy notice and GDPR
A message from a company’s hiring system asking a sourced person to agree that the company may keep and use their data for recruiting; the privacy notice explains how. If the person never agrees, the profile is deleted after a set time, 30 days in his email. Privacy laws such as the European Union’s General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) are the usual reason such systems ask.
Naukri, LinkedIn “Easy Apply”, “Promoted by hirer” and “Actively reviewing applicants”
Naukri is a large Indian job board. The other three are labels in his LinkedIn screenshot: Easy Apply applies inside LinkedIn; Promoted by hirer marks a posting the employer is promoting; Actively reviewing applicants is LinkedIn’s way of saying the hirer is looking at applications now. We did not check how LinkedIn sets these labels.
Recruitment fraud
Scammers posing as a real employer to collect personal data or money. Nielsen’s careers page lists warning signs, and its postings say its recruiters write only from @nielsen.com addresses, LinkedIn or official job boards.
Hybrid, and “Smart Work”
In Nielsen’s own words, hybrid workers “work partially from home which is in the same city as the Nielsen office they are employed with and partially from a Nielsen office / site”. Smart Work is Nielsen’s name for its flexible-location policy. LinkedIn calls this seat on-site; Nielsen’s own record calls it hybrid.
On-call, 24/7 and US Eastern time
On-call means being reachable outside working hours to fix production problems. 24/7 means round the clock, every day. Nielsen’s posting for a Gracenote Operations trainer in Mumbai asks for someone “Ready to work in a 24/7 operation” and aligned to United States Eastern time, which is 9 hours 30 minutes behind India while the United States is on summer time.
Forcing question
A question that asks for a specific answer, so that the answer settles something rather than leaving it vague.
AmbitionBox, Blind and Glassdoor
Employee-review websites. AmbitionBox is Indian; Blind checks that members work where they say; Glassdoor refused to be read here. All three are anonymous and self-selected: useful for patterns, never proof.

Words for weighing evidence

Register, SEC, 8-K and 10-K
A register record is an official filing. The SEC (United States Securities and Exchange Commission) holds the filings of listed companies: an 8-K reports a major event such as a completed sale; a 10-K is the annual report. FY2025 means fiscal year 2025.
Self-claim, independent, checked by us, inference
The research page’s labels: what the company says about itself; what someone else recorded; what we read directly on 28 September 2026; and our own reading of the evidence.
Public data feed
A machine-readable version of a website that anyone can read; here, SmartRecruiters’ public list of Nielsen’s open postings, which is how every India posting was counted.
HTTP 403, and a script-rendered page
HTTP 403 is a website’s “forbidden” answer: it refused to be read (Glassdoor, Reddit). A script-rendered page arrives as an empty shell and fills itself in with JavaScript in a browser, so a simple reader sees nothing (Naukri).

Collisions to keep straight.

  • Lambda: the Lambda architecture in the job description is a data-processing design, not AWS Lambda, Amazon’s service that runs small functions on demand. Both appear in this workspace.
  • CV: on Nielsen’s fraud page, CV means curriculum vitae, a résumé. In AI postings, CV can mean computer vision.
  • MCP: here, the Model Context Protocol. On résumés, MCP often means Microsoft Certified Professional.
  • RAG: in AI, retrieval-augmented generation. On project reports, RAG means Red/Amber/Green status colours.
  • DSP: in advertising, a demand-side platform. In audio and music recognition, digital signal processing. Gracenote does both.
  • GCC: here, a Global Capability Centre. Also the Gulf Cooperation Council, and, for programmers, the GNU Compiler Collection.
  • MTS and TMS: MTS is Nielsen’s job-title family (Member Technical Staff); TMS is Tribune Media Services, Gracenote’s ancestor.
  • Nielsen and NielsenIQ: separate companies since March 2021.
  • Principal: at Nielsen, Principal sits below Senior Principal; at other companies the order of Staff and Principal varies.
  • IC: here, an individual contributor, not an integrated circuit.
  • FAST: free ad-supported streaming TV, not the adjective.
  • “Nielsen Sports”: the name on a Naukri listing for a Bengaluru seat. The Gracenote Sports Data product is a different thing, and what that listing is could not be checked.
