First, the vocabulary

His most recent programme was Amura, a chronic-care platform, so clinical data is not new to him. What is new is the United States provider-software industry — its regulator, its certification regime, its billing economics and the specific way an electronic health record vendor gets into legal trouble. Every term below is defined as a concept, because knowing the expansion of the abbreviation is not the part that helps.

Electronic health record (EHR)
The system a clinician documents inside during and after a patient visit: history, examination, diagnosis, orders, prescriptions, notes. It is the operational system of record for care. Distinct from an electronic medical record (EMR) mostly by ambition — an EHR is meant to travel between organisations, an EMR was originally a single practice's chart. In practice the industry uses the words interchangeably and ModMed uses EHR.
Ambulatory and specialty practice
Ambulatory means outpatient: the patient walks in and goes home. It is the opposite of inpatient hospital care. ModMed sells to ambulatory specialty practices — dermatology, ophthalmology, orthopaedics, gastroenterology, ear-nose-and-throat and others — not to hospitals. That is the whole company strategy: rather than one general EHR, eleven deliberately different ones whose workflows and clinical vocabularies are built per specialty. Architecturally this is the interesting part, because it is a multi-product platform problem dressed as a single product, and it is why a centralized architecture team exists at all.
Practice management (PM) and revenue cycle management (RCM)
PM is scheduling, registration and the front desk. RCM is the money: coding a visit into billing codes, submitting a claim to an insurer, chasing denials, collecting. RCM is not a side product — it is roughly half of what a modern EHR vendor sells, and a large share of ModMed's Hyderabad hiring is RCM coding and RCM operations rather than engineering. This matters for reading the site, and it is picked up below.
Protected health information (PHI)
Health information that identifies a person, as defined by United States law. Broader than it sounds: an appointment date attached to a name is PHI. Personally identifiable information (PII) is the wider, non-health category. A breach notice that names both, as ModMed's 2025 one does, means the clinical and the identity data were in the same exfiltrated files.
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act (HIPAA) — and its three rules
United States federal law governing PHI. It is three separate regimes that engineers routinely merge: the Privacy Rule (who may see and disclose PHI), the Security Rule (the technical and administrative safeguards — access control, audit logging, encryption, integrity), and the Breach Notification Rule (what must be reported, to whom, how fast). He has HIPAA experience and it is user-confirmed, attached to the Go chat service and the two-stream Kinesis design, where a certification requirement drove multi-availability-zone deployment, failover and disaster recovery. What ModMed adds is the other two rules at scale, plus everything below.
Covered entity vs business associate, and the business associate agreement (BAA)
A covered entity is the doctor, hospital or insurer. A business associate is a vendor handling PHI on their behalf — which is exactly what ModMed is. The BAA is the contract that pushes HIPAA obligations down the chain, and it flows onward to subcontractors. The consequence for a Hyderabad architect: the United States Department of Health and Human Services has asserted that HIPAA reaches business associates located outside the United States, so an India entity handling United States PHI is inside the regime, not adjacent to it.
Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) — India
India's data-protection statute. The collision worth naming: HIPAA compliance does not confer DPDP compliance and the reverse is equally untrue. They overlap on governance and security controls and diverge on consent, data-principal rights and breach timelines. An India-based engineering site processing United States patient data sits under both, which is a genuine architecture constraint (residency, access paths, key custody, logging) rather than a legal footnote.
Assistant Secretary for Technology Policy / Office of the National Coordinator (ASTP/ONC)
The United States federal office that runs the Health IT Certification Program. Certification is nominally voluntary, but practices need certified software to claim federal incentive payments, so in commercial reality it is mandatory. This is the part with no analogue in his record: a certified EHR carries dozens of prescribed criteria that the software must demonstrably meet, and re-certification is a recurring engineering programme, not a one-time audit.
HTI-1, HTI-2, HTI-5
Successive ASTP/ONC rulemakings amending that programme. HTI-1 added transparency requirements for algorithm-based clinical decision support. HTI-2 amended the information-blocking regulations. HTI-5, proposed January 2026 and open for comment to 27 February 2026, proposes eliminating 34 and revising 7 of the 60 existing certification criteria — a deregulatory swing. The point for an architect is that the compliance surface moves under you on a rulemaking cycle, so the design has to absorb regulatory churn.
Information blocking
A United States legal prohibition on interfering with the access, exchange or use of electronic health information. It is enforced against health-IT developers. Its practical effect is architectural: a design decision that makes data harder to export can be a regulatory violation rather than a product choice, which inverts the usual instinct to lock data in.
Health Level Seven Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources (HL7 FHIR), US Core, TEFCA
FHIR is the modern representational state transfer (REST) and JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) standard for exchanging clinical data. US Core is the American profile of it that certification actually tests against. TEFCA, the Trusted Exchange Framework and Common Agreement, is the national network-of-networks for exchange between organisations. ModMed has a public requisition for a Senior Software Engineer in Application Programming Interfaces and Interoperability, so this is a live workstream, not theory.
Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) and the False Claims Act (FCA)
Two United States laws that seem far from software until you see how they bite an EHR vendor. The AKS forbids paying or receiving anything of value to induce referrals for federally-funded care. The FCA punishes causing false claims to be submitted to the government. An EHR sits in the middle of both, because what the software nudges a clinician to order, and what it auto-populates onto a claim, is evidence. The industry has a run of settlements on exactly this shape, ModMed's included, and it is why an EHR architect's design decisions carry a compliance dimension that a hotel-booking or accessibility platform simply does not have.
Black Book Research
A healthcare technology market-research firm whose rankings come from surveys of verified users of the software, scored across defined key performance indicators. It is a customer-satisfaction ranking, not a technical audit, and the sample size is the thing to look at. Its nearest peer is KLAS Research. This is one of the three award claims in the posting, and it is the one that checks out.
The Globee Awards
A commercial business-awards programme. Entrants submit nominations and pay an entry fee that varies by category and deadline; the organiser states that fees do not influence scoring and that judging is a structured numerical process by volunteer judges. Not a reason to disbelieve a win, but it is a self-nominated award, which is a different class of evidence from a user survey — and it matters below, because this particular claim could not be independently confirmed.
Global capability centre (GCC)
An offshore engineering and operations site owned by the company itself, rather than work outsourced to a services vendor. ModMed's Hyderabad site is a GCC. The distinction that decides this job is inside the category, not at its edge: a captive site can be a genuine co-owner of the product, or an execution arm that builds what another country designs. Both are called GCCs and both are in HITEC City.
Domain-driven design (DDD)
An approach to large systems that makes the business domain the organising principle — bounded contexts with their own models and vocabulary, explicit boundaries between them, and deliberate translation at the seams. Named as required on this posting. Its natural home is exactly a multi-specialty platform, where dermatology and ophthalmology genuinely are different domains that share infrastructure.
Event streaming
A durable, ordered, replayable log of events that many consumers read independently at their own pace, as opposed to a queue where a message is consumed once. The posting names Kafka and NATS as examples of the category. His Amazon Kinesis work is the same category — see the honesty note below, which is load-bearing.
Distributed cache grid
An in-memory data tier spread across many nodes so that cached state survives a node loss and scales past one machine's memory. Named products in this category include Hazelcast, Apache Ignite and Oracle Coherence. The concept is what the posting asks for; the named products are not the only way to satisfy it.
Reactive and functional programming
Reactive means non-blocking, back-pressure-aware processing of asynchronous streams, so a thread is never parked waiting on input or output. Functional means composing behaviour from pure transformations rather than mutating shared state. The posting asks for familiarity, not expertise.
Clearlake's O.P.S. framework
The named operating playbook that Clearlake Capital, ModMed's majority owner since March 2025, applies to its portfolio companies. Private-equity operating frameworks are systematic programmes of operational change rather than a passive shareholding. Relevant here only as context for how a site's mandate can move, in either direction.

Three honesty flags from jd.md are carried into this page unresolved, exactly as recorded.

Event streaming. The posting writes "Event Streaming (e.g. Kafka, NATS)". Per the scoring rule, an "e.g." qualifier is read generously, and his Amazon Kinesis work satisfies the category. Kinesis is confirmed in production on the chat platform. The two-stream Enhanced Fan-Out design is his, and professional-journey.md records in his own words that its limits were never load-tested, for budget reasons — worth knowing before a room asks. Kafka and NATS are never written as his.

Reactive and functional programming. Spring WebFlux is not his and is not claimed. The posting asks for familiarity only, so this is a ramp item, not a capability cap.

Distributed cache grids. Redis is claimable, user-confirmed, and sat in the production request path of systems he owned — Amura's per-tenant-namespaced risk cache and its canonical keyed cache. A named grid product (Hazelcast, Ignite, Coherence) is not his. Worth telling rather than hiding: one Amura query declined a per-request Redis layer in favour of an in-process cache, which is a deliberate engineering call.

One boundary that must not blur. The Amura corpus names no message broker at all — Kafka and Kinesis both return zero hits across all twenty-two case-study files. The Kinesis and high-availability work belongs to the chat platform, a different system. Never cite Amura as streaming evidence.

Verdict

The read, in three sentences

This is the closest technical match to arrive in this workspace — Java and Spring Boot named as deep expertise against his single deepest thread, multi-tenant cloud software-as-a-service against the axe Monitor consolidation, Docker and Kubernetes both user-confirmed, an employer that publicly committed to Amazon Web Services as its strategic cloud in April 2026, and HIPAA already on his résumé — sitting in Hyderabad, where he already lives, at an in-house recruiter two degrees away with fifteen mutual connections.

The company is real, funded, growing and acquisitive: majority-owned by Clearlake Capital since March 2025 at a reported five-point-three-billion-dollar valuation, verified first place across all eleven of its specialty electronic health records in two consecutive Black Book surveys, and buying Bonsai Health in April 2026 to serve nearly fifty thousand providers, which is precisely the platform-consolidation work a centralized architecture team inherits.

The one question that decides the seat is where architectural authority actually sits, because ModMed's own requisition for the same job title reports to a Director of Software Architecture in Boca Raton, Florida, and no equivalent architecture leadership is publicly visible in Hyderabad — which does not settle it either way, but does mean it has to be asked out loud before anything else.

Location is not a question on this one, at all. He lives in Hyderabad. The site is in HITEC City. The posting is listed as in-office, which is a neutral fact rather than a finding, and schedule density is never scored in this workspace. Relocation, commute and week-shape are all off the table, which is unusual and worth saying plainly: everything that normally consumes a first call here is already resolved.

Red flags

Ranked by evidence, not by how bad they sound. Only the first is decision-shaping.

1 — The architecture chain of command appears to run through Florida, and nothing public shows it running through Hyderabad. Sharpest

The posting describes "the centralized System Design Architecture team", which "owns the technological roadmap and architectural ownership of the entire software suite". That is an unusually strong charter, and it is the reason to take this seat seriously. But the only place ModMed publicly describes that team's management is on its own Workday requisition R3948, a Senior Software Architect in Boca Raton, Florida, which states the role sits within the Architecture Organization and reports to the Director of Software Architecture. No India-based architecture director, principal architect or head of architecture surfaces anywhere in public data. What does surface in Hyderabad is a Director of Software Engineering, a Vice President for India Human Resources, a Director for Revenue Cycle Management hired in August 2026, and an open engineering mix of Software Engineer 1, Software Engineer 2, Senior Software Engineer 1 and Principal Cloud Engineer.

Why this is the whole decision and not a quibble. A centralized architecture team headquartered in another country resolves one of two ways. Either Hyderabad holds real seats on that team with genuine decision rights over the roadmap, in which case this is a strong individual-contributor architect job in the city he lives in. Or Hyderabad executes designs authored in Florida, in which case the title says architect and the work is senior engineering with a review function — a materially different job that no amount of stack overlap compensates for. Public evidence leans toward the second but is nowhere near sufficient to conclude it, and the innocent explanation is entirely plausible: this requisition may itself be the first architecture seat being planted in Hyderabad, which would be a genuinely good reason for no India architecture leadership to be visible yet. That reading is testable in one phone call and untestable any other way.

2 — A confirmed 2025 data breach exposing protected health information for 198,795 people, with a class settlement still pending.

A threat actor accessed and copied files from ModMed's internal servers on 9–10 July 2025. Suspicious activity was identified on 21 July and unauthorised access confirmed on 29 July. The exposed material spans full names, dates of birth, addresses, contact details, Social Security numbers, medical record numbers, patient account numbers, provider and practice names, billing and diagnostic codes, prescriptions, diagnosis and treatment information, financial account information, government identification numbers and health insurance information. ModMed's position is that full medical records were not involved and that the exposure varies by individual. The federal Office for Civil Rights portal figure, released 13 January 2026, is 198,795 individuals. A class action, Cavallaro-Kearins v. Modernizing Medicine, Inc., case 9:25-cv-81443-EA in the Southern District of Florida, has a settlement with notice mailed 29 July 2026, an opt-out deadline of 19 October 2026 and a final approval hearing on 17 November 2026, which is after any realistic start date. Reporting also indicates some of the data subsequently appeared for sale.

This cuts both ways and the second edge is sharper than the first. The posting explicitly asks the architect to "shape security architecture practices" and names collaboration with internal information security. A company that was breached fourteen months ago and is still working through the settlement has a real, funded, executive-visible reason to hire for exactly that — which is a far better mandate than a security line item written because job descriptions have one. The risk is the opposite case: that the security charter is boilerplate, the remediation was owned entirely by information security and cloud operations in Florida, and the architect inherits the consequences without the authority. That is question three below.

3 — The posting's company boilerplate does not survive checking, in three places.

The employee count is wrong. The posting claims "4,500+". ModMed's own about-us page says "more than 2,500 employees worldwide", and independent workforce data puts the figure between roughly 2,270 and 2,970 across 2025 and 2026. Nothing supports 4,500.

Two of three award claims cannot be independently confirmed. The Globee Gold for Technology Team of the Year traces only to ModMed's own job postings; the Globee winners list is inaccessible to these tools and ModMed appears not to have press-released it, though it does press-release awards routinely. The Florida Venture Forum "Venture-Backed Company of the Year" has no year attached, no press release and no independent source, and it describes a status ModMed no longer holds, having been private-equity majority-owned since March 2025.

The Black Book claim is dated. The posting cites the 2025 sweep. ModMed repeated it in January 2026. The boilerplate has not been refreshed in more than a year.

Do not inflate this into a character judgement. Stale recruiting boilerplate is ordinary at a company of this size and says nothing about the engineering organisation. It is listed because it establishes one useful thing: the posting's non-technical claims are not load-bearing evidence and should not be quoted back as facts. The technical requirements section, by contrast, is specific, internally consistent and matches a real Workday requisition, so it can be trusted.

ModMed runs at least three architect grades. 7 — Context, not a flag: a resolved 2022 federal settlement, and the industry pattern behind it.

ModMed agreed in November 2022 to pay forty-five million dollars to resolve False Claims Act and Anti-Kickback Statute allegations covering conduct between January 2010 and July 2017: marketing arrangements with the pathology laboratory Miraca Life Sciences, later Inform Diagnostics, and donations of its electronic health record to providers in a way the government alleged drove laboratory referrals and inaccurate reporting for federal incentive payments. Miraca separately settled for sixty-three-and-a-half million dollars in January 2019. A former ModMed vice president of product management brought the whistleblower case. ModMed has since published that it has fully resolved all government claims.

Historical, settled, and it pre-dates both the current ownership and the current chief technology officer. It is listed for one reason only: it is the concrete illustration of the glossary entry above, and it explains why an electronic health record company staffs compliance roles like the Compliance Director for artificial intelligence in revenue cycle management that sits open on ModMed's Workday tenant today. For an architect, the transferable point is that in this industry a design decision about what the software suggests or auto-fills is a compliance artefact.

His words are that the Go chat service and the two data streams carry high-availability design, multi-availability-zone deployment, failover and disaster recovery because certification was required, and the certification is HIPAA. Forcing questions

Ordered so that the three that most quickly reveal whether this seat is what it says it is come first. Each is written so that a vague answer is itself the information.

The three that matter most

  1. "Who does this seat report to, and where do they sit? And how many of the System Design Architecture team are in Hyderabad today versus Boca Raton?"

    This is the decision, compressed into one question a recruiter can answer immediately from the requisition. ModMed's own Boca Raton requisition for the same title reports to a Director of Software Architecture in Florida, and no India architecture leadership is publicly visible. A specific number — "four of us in Hyderabad, eleven in Boca" — is a real answer. "It's a global team" is also an answer, and a different one. If this is the first architecture seat in Hyderabad, that is genuinely interesting and worth hearing said out loud, because a founding seat and a satellite seat are opposite jobs with the same title.

  2. "Tell me about an architectural decision the Hyderabad side of the team made in the last six months that Florida did not overrule."

    The un-fakeable version of question one. Charters are written by whoever writes job descriptions; decisions are made by whoever has authority. A specific example — a datastore choice, a service boundary, a framework standard — settles it in a sentence. An inability to produce one is not an accusation and may simply mean the recruiter does not track that, in which case it becomes the first question for the hiring-manager round. Ask it as curiosity about the work, not as a test.

  3. "After the July 2025 security incident, what changed in the architecture, and is that work part of this seat's remit?"

    The posting asks the architect to shape security architecture practices and to collaborate with internal information security. This question tests whether that is a real, funded, executive-visible charter or a line item — and a company fourteen months past a breach affecting nearly two hundred thousand people, with a class settlement still pending final approval in November, will have a definite answer either way. It also does something no other question does: it signals that he researched the company properly, without any adversarial edge, because the incident is public and the remediation is the interesting part. Asked plainly and without drama, it reads as an architect thinking about the actual problem.

The rest, in order of value

  1. "Which grade is this requisition — Senior Software Architect, or Senior Software Architect 2? Both are posted in Hyderabad." He has nineteen years. Public bands put the first at 10–12 and the second at 14–16. Asking which one, and whether both are open, is a normal levelling question best asked before a process rather than after one.   2. "What does the architecture team actually own — is it review and standards, or does it hold the roadmap and write code?" The posting says roadmap and architectural ownership of the entire suite, which is a strong claim. The distinction between an architecture function that governs and one that builds is the difference between two jobs, and it is not derivable from any posting.

The visible Hyderabad job mix runs Software Engineer 1 through Senior Software Engineer 1 alongside a substantial revenue-cycle operation. An architect's leverage depends entirely on who is downstream of the designs. This is a factual question with a factual answer.
  4. "Which products would I cover? All eleven specialties, or a subset?" Eleven specialty electronic health records under one architecture team is either an enviable platform mandate or an impossible span. The answer shapes what the first year actually looks like.
  5. "How is the Bonsai Health integration being run, and does architecture own it?" Acquired April 2026, to be scaled to nearly fifty thousand providers. Acquisition integration is the canonical centralized-architecture workload, and it is the closest analogue to work he has genuinely done. If architecture owns it, that is a concrete and attractive first project.
  6. "How does the India entity handle protected health information under both HIPAA and the Digital Personal Data Protection Act — is patient data accessible from Hyderabad, or is access mediated?"
