Notes

Why it is worded this way

Hyderabad is stated in both versions, early. The single most common objection to a senior hire is whether he will actually move, and it is usually raised silently before anyone speaks to him. He lives there; the seat is there. Saying it in the first two lines removes the question before it forms rather than answering it later.

Java leads because the posting leads with it. ModMed asks for deep expertise in Java, Spring and Spring Boot in its first required line. That is his single deepest thread, and it is the one claim a talent-acquisition specialist can match against a requisition without needing an engineer in the room.

Healthcare is the second beat, not the first. ModMed is an electronic health record company, so the chronic-care platform and the HIPAA control framework are the part of his history that makes him look like a fit rather than a generalist who found the posting. Held to one sentence.

Three specifics, deliberately. Java and Spring Boot depth, the multi-tenant consolidation at Deque, the HIPAA work in healthcare. A fourth would turn a message into a résumé, and he already has a résumé. Everything else is answered on the call.

No question of his own at the end. Every extra question is one more thing standing between him and a phone call, so the message closes with availability and a number and nothing to reply to except yes or no.

It reads as asking, not pitching. No superlatives, no claim about being a strong fit, no closing line about impact. The facts carry it, and a recruiter reads twenty of these a day.
