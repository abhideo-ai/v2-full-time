Plain-English glossary

The UK National Health Service (NHS) assurance stack

TermWhat it actually is
ISO 27001
International Organization for Standardization standard 27001		 The international standard for an Information Security Management System. It does not certify that your software is secure. It certifies that your organisation runs a documented, audited process for managing information risk: assets and threats identified, controls chosen and justified, owners assigned, the whole thing reviewed on a cycle. An accredited external body certifies you and returns for surveillance audits. For a three-person startup that is months of process-building and evidence-gathering, not a code change. Scope matters to him: his evidence is a release pipeline taken through certification at Deque, which is a contribution to a certification — not running a whole-organisation audit end to end. Be ready to say which.
DSPT
Data Security and Protection Toolkit		 An annual online self-assessment every organisation touching NHS patient data must complete and publish, describing how it protects that data. It is the NHS’s entry ticket: no DSPT, no data-sharing agreement, no deal. It is mid-change, which matters. The old National Data Guardian standards are being replaced by the National Cyber Security Centre’s Cyber Assessment Framework (CAF), which asks you to demonstrate that a security outcome was achieved rather than that a control box was ticked. Phased in from September 2024, extended September 2025; the 2026 submission aligns to CAF version 3.4 with a 30 June 2026 deadline. NHS bodies and relevant suppliers now undergo an independent audit validating the self-assessment, so “self-assessment” is no longer purely self-marked. Anyone describing DSPT experience from before 2024 is describing a materially different exercise.
DTAC
Digital Technology Assessment Criteria		 NHS England’s standard pre-purchase questionnaire for digital health products — clinical safety, data protection, technical security, interoperability, usability and accessibility. It is not a certification. There is no such thing as “DTAC certified”; it is a baseline the buyer asks you to complete, and buyers may add their own questions. Refreshed February 2026: roughly 25% fewer questions, de-duplicated against the DSPT, elements overlapping the medical-device regulations removed, and a new decision tree for working out whether a product is a medical device and which clinical safety standard applies. The previous form was retired with full transition by 6 April 2026. Anyone citing pre-2026 DTAC work is citing a form that no longer exists.
DCB0129
Data Coordination Board standard 0129 — “Clinical Risk Management: its Application in the Manufacture of Health IT Systems”		 A safety standard published under section 250 of the Health and Social Care Act 2012 that applies to the manufacturer — that is, to this company. It requires a clinical risk management process: identify hazards (“what could this software do that harms a patient?”), assess and control each one, and produce a Clinical Safety Case Report plus a hazard log, signed off by a Clinical Safety Officer. Its pair is DCB0160, the same discipline for the deploying NHS organisation. The engineering consequence is the part worth saying out loud: DCB0129 turns hazard analysis into an engineering artefact — every large-language-model failure mode (a hallucinated symptom, a dropped negation, collateral information attributed to the wrong informant) becomes a logged hazard with a named control and an owner. That is precisely what an evaluation harness is for, which is almost certainly why “evaluation harnesses rather than vibes” and “DCB0129” appear in the same job description. (The harness connection is INFERENCE, but it is the reading the JD’s own juxtaposition supports.)

The clinical domain the JD assumes you know

TermWhat it actually is
DIVA-5
Diagnostic Interview for ADHD in Adults, 5th edition		 A semi-structured clinical interview based on Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders, 5th edition (DSM-5) criteria, published by the DIVA Foundation. It walks a clinician through each DSM-5 symptom asking for concrete examples from both childhood and adulthood, plus impairment across settings. It is not a questionnaire the patient fills in — it is an interview a clinician conducts, which is exactly why an ambient tool that listens and completes it is the product.
Collateral information History obtained from someone other than the patient — parent, teacher, partner. Required in ADHD and autism assessment because self-report alone is unreliable for childhood-onset criteria. The engineering implication is the interesting one, and the JD states it plainly: the data model needs multiple authenticated contributors per assessment record, each with their own consent basis, identity and reliability weighting. That is a genuine data-modelling and consent problem, not a user-interface detail — and “6+ years Data Modeling” is the one poster-added bar he clears comfortably.
Ambient AI / ambient scribing Software that listens to a consultation and drafts the documentation. NHS England has published specific guidance on artificial-intelligence-enabled ambient scribing products, so this is a live and actively-governed category rather than a novel one. ⚠ This is his measured zero — no speech-to-text, automatic speech recognition or dictation experience anywhere in his record.
Right to Choose The English NHS rule letting a patient choose their provider for a first outpatient appointment. It is the mechanism most private UK adult ADHD and autism assessment volume flows through, and therefore the likely commercial context. INFERENCE — the posting never names it.
FHIR · HL7
Fast Healthcare Interoperability Resources · Health Level Seven		 FHIR is a health-data exchange standard: it defines the shape of a Patient, an Observation, a Condition and so on, so two systems that have never met can hand records to each other. HL7 is the standards body behind it, and also the name of its older version 2 messaging format still widely deployed in hospitals. Why this is not something he can argue around: the whole point of FHIR is conforming to a resource model and a terminology you do not control. His clinical data modelling is deep and entirely bespoke — he chose his own grain. That is a difference in kind, not a difference in tooling.

⚠ Misleading collisions — getting any of these the wrong way round in a room would be conspicuous

  1. CSO here means CLINICAL Safety Officer, not Chief Security Officer. The JD says he would work “alongside our Clinical Safety Officer”; that person is a registered clinician who owns the safety case and has authority to stop a deployment they judge unsafe.
  2. DCB0129 (manufacturer) versus DCB0160 (deploying organisation). Reversing these is the classic tell that someone has read the acronym but not the standard.
  3. CE marking versus UKCA. CE is the European Union route; UKCA is Great Britain’s, assessed by different bodies. The JD says “CE marking”, but for an NHS England product the relevant mark is UKCA.
  4. ISO 27001 versus SOC 2. SOC 2 is a United States attestation report about a period of time; ISO 27001 is a certification against a published standard. NHS buyers ask for ISO 27001 and the DSPT and never for SOC 2 — and SOC 2 is on his never-claim list.
  5. DTAC is a questionnaire, not a certification.
  6. ABDM is the Ayushman Bharat Digital Mission, India’s national health data architecture, listed presumably because the role is India-based. It is irrelevant to NHS assurance and should not be confused with anything in the UK stack.

4 — The medical-device question is unaddressed in the posting, and the boundary runs straight through this product

Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) guidance published 29 July 2026 on ambient voice technologies draws the line closely. Not devices: transcribing consultations, summarising them, formatting transcripts into structured data for clinician review, drafting discharge summaries for clinician editing. Are devices: generated insights offering suggested diagnoses or treatment options, autonomously finalising records without clinician confirmation. The JD sits on both sides — “draft psychiatric report for clinician review” and “the clinician stays in the loop and signs everything” read as non-device, but a completed DIVA-5 is a DSM-5 criteria tally that maps to a diagnostic threshold, which is far closer to a suggested diagnosis than a discharge summary is. Horizon two pushes further across.

The telling absence: the JD names ISO 27001, DSPT, DTAC and DCB0129 and nothing from the device regime — no UKCA marking, no MHRA registration, no ISO 13485 quality management system, no ISO 14971 risk management. If the product is a device, that omission is the largest single item of unscoped work in the role, and it lands on him. The guidance is sourced; applying it to this product is INFERENCE — classification turns on stated intended use, which the posting never gives. [insideeulifesciences.com, reporting MHRA guidance of 29 July 2026]

5 — Policy risk sits on their wedge, and it reports this summer

An independent review of NHS mental health, ADHD and autism services, chaired by Professor Peter Fonagy, launched 4 December 2025 and publishes in summer 2026. Its scope explicitly includes what is driving rising demand and “the role of diagnosis in accessing help” — that is, whether diagnosis should be the gate to support at all. A company whose horizon one is diagnostic assessment throughput is exposed to that answer. The review is fact; the exposure argument is INFERENCE — it could equally recommend faster assessment, which would be a tailwind. 6 — UK-hosted data with an India-based engineer is a problem about ACCESS, not storage

The JD asks for a data layer that is “UK-hosted, encrypted, auditable, least-privilege” and the engineer is remote within India. India has no UK adequacy regulation, so a restricted transfer needs the Information Commissioner’s Office International Data Transfer Agreement, or the European Union standard contractual clauses plus the UK Addendum, plus what was the Transfer Risk Assessment and is now the “data protection test” under the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Storage is the easy half and they have probably solved it. Remote access by a named engineer in India to identifiable patient data — for debugging, for a production incident, for evaluating a pipeline on real transcripts — is the restricted transfer, and it determines whether the job is doable day to day. ⚠ The specific NHS England “patient data at rest must remain in the UK” guidance came from a secondary compliance blog, not NHS England’s own page, which was not reached — confirm before quoting it at them. [ico.org.uk; dpliance.com; digital.nhs.uk]

Forcing questions

Grounded in the posting’s own words or a cited primary source, in the order they should be asked. Question 1 is not politeness — it unblocks the verification the rules require.

Identity and channel — ask Nexifyr, before anything else

  1. Who would I actually be employed by? What is the company’s name, and is it a UK entity?
  2. Is Nexifyr recruiting for them, or would I be contracting through Nexifyr? Who signs the contract, who pays, who do I report to? Same shape as the agency route on the seat withdrawn from yesterday.
  3. Am I an employee of the UK company, an employee of an Indian subsidiary, or a contractor? If there is no Indian entity, what is the mechanism?
  4. The posting is titled “Lead Engineer — FHIR”, but the description is about being your second engineering hire with the CTO title on a milestone. Those are different jobs. Which are you hiring for, and who wrote the title?
  5. Why is a founding-engineer role being run through a recruitment agency rather than by the founders directly?

The FHIR bar — the question that decides whether the seat is even reachable

  1. Your description lists FHIR under Nice to have, as “FHIR, HL7, or equivalent”. The LinkedIn screening question says 5+ years of FHIR. Which is the actual bar, and did you write both?
  2. Is there a live FHIR endpoint in the product today, or is FHIR what you will need when you connect to NHS systems? The diagnostic one — a pre-integration platform running its own assessment pathway needs no FHIR until it touches NHS infrastructure.
  3. Which NHS integrations are contracted or in flight — GP Connect, Personal Demographics Service, e-Referral Service, NHS Login? GP Connect additionally requires a Health and Social Care Network connection. If none is contracted, FHIR is horizon-two work.
  4. Same question for Python: the body says only “Work experience: 6+ years” total, while the screener reportedly demands 6+ years of Python specifically.

Regulatory — where the technical literacy shows, and the best counterweight he has

  1. Do you hold a written agreement with the DIVA Foundation covering this architecture? Their FAQ says DIVA may not be converted into an AI interview or any system that interprets or generates DIVA content, and that they review the algorithm before approving a digital implementation. Has yours been through that?
  2. Have you had a regulatory determination on whether this is a medical device? The MHRA’s July 2026 ambient-voice guidance says summarising and drafting for clinician editing are not devices, but generated insights offering suggested diagnoses are. A completed DIVA-5 is a criteria instrument. Which side did you land on, and who decided?
  3. Where does the intended-purpose statement live today, and who wrote it? Under the MHRA’s framing, intended purpose is what decides classification — a startup that has not written one has not made the decision.
  4. DTAC was refreshed in April and now includes a decision tree for device status and which clinical safety standard applies. Have you run it, and what did it return? A founder who has will answer in one sentence. This question also demonstrates current, specific regulatory literacy — the best available counterweight to a résumé showing zero FHIR.
  5. Who is your Clinical Safety Officer today, and have they completed NHS England’s clinical safety practitioner training? Splitting it precisely: who authors the hazard log and the Clinical Safety Case Report, and who signs them? I would expect to write the evidence and a registered clinician to own the clinical judgement.
  6. Has any DCB0129 work started, or would I build the clinical risk management system from zero? The standard requires risk analysis from the earliest development stage, and they already have a product — retrofitting is expensive.

The data layer, and whether the job is doable from India

  1. The data layer is UK-hosted and the engineer is in India. What am I actually allowed to access from here? Not the storage — the access.
  2. Do you have an International Data Transfer Agreement or standard contractual clauses plus the UK Addendum in place, and has anyone completed the data protection test? If not, is that mine to drive?
  3. When a clinician reports that the DIVA-5 extraction produced something wrong on a real patient, how do I debug it? De-identified transcripts, a UK-based bastion with session recording, synthetic data, or does someone in the UK reproduce it for me? The answer determines whether the role works day to day.

Evaluation — his strongest ground, and where he should spend time

  1. What does your evaluation look like today? Is there a golden set of transcripts with clinician-adjudicated ground truth, and how big is it? For a clinical extraction product this is the question. Without adjudicated ground truth there is no evaluation, only inspection.
  2. Who adjudicates disagreement between the model and the clinician — do the founders do it themselves, or have you built a labelling process?
  3. What are your failure modes today, and how do you know? For structured extraction into a criteria instrument I would care most about hallucinated symptom endorsement and dropped negatives — do you measure those separately?
  4. Are you evaluating per DIVA-5 item, or on the report as a whole? Item-level is where the clinical risk actually lives.
  5. Is the model pinned, and can you reproduce exactly what a clinician saw six months ago when a report is challenged? ⚠ This invites him to describe replay determinism and claim grammar — and whether those guardrails SHIPPED is an open confirm-or-reject item only he settles. He should be ready to describe the design precisely and be honest about deployment state if pushed.
  6. What is your position on hosting — are you calling a frontier API with UK patient transcripts, or running something you control?

The team, the runway and the milestone

  1. Who is the first engineering hire, how long have they been there, and what is already built? Separates “greenfield with a peer” from “inheriting someone’s prototype and their departure” — one of the highest-information answers available.
  2. As the second engineer, what does on-call look like — is there a rota, or is it the two of us? And is the expectation UK hours, overlap hours, or Indian hours with UK escalation?
  3. You say the CTO title is on the table on a defined milestone. What is the milestone, in one sentence, and who judges whether it has been met? Is it a funding event, a certification, a headcount number, or a revenue number? If it slips for reasons outside engineering, does the title move with it?
  4. When did the seed close, what is the runway to the next raise, and what has to be true for it? If horizon two is not funded, what does this role become? Company financials, not compensation.
  5. Who are your customers today — NHS trusts, Right to Choose, or direct to private patients? Determines whether the DCB0129 and DTAC work he would own has a customer waiting for it.
