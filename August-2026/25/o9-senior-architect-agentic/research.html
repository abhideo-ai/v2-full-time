First, the vocabulary

He has never worked in supply-chain planning. The terms below are the ones that decide what this platform actually does — and two of them are collisions where a familiar word means something different here than it does in his own record.

Supply-chain planning (SCP)
Deciding, ahead of time, how much of a thing to make, buy, move and hold, and where. Not logistics execution — no trucks, no warehouses. It is a forecasting-and-allocation problem run over a company's own history, and the output is a plan that people and systems then act on. This matters to his fit: it is a longitudinal-data problem before it is an artificial-intelligence (AI) problem.
Integrated business planning (IBP) · sales and operations planning (S&OP)
The management ritual SCP software sells into: a monthly cycle where sales, supply, finance and operations reconcile one number. The commercial point of o9's product is that these functions historically planned in separate spreadsheets and disagreed. "Breaking silos" is the whole pitch.
Value leakage
o9's own term, in its own marketing copy, for money lost to bad or slow planning decisions — stockouts, excess inventory, missed promotions. It is how o9 frames its return on investment. Treat it as a sales frame, not a measured quantity.
Enterprise Knowledge Graph (EKG)
o9's core data structure: a graph model of a business — products, plants, customers, suppliers, demand signals — continuously fed from enterprise systems and external market data, so that a question can be answered by traversing relationships instead of joining tables. This is the thing the seat would own the architecture of, and it is also the thing at the centre of o9's litigation against SAP. It is close in spirit to his own Context Graph work, though the domain is different.
Digital Brain
o9's product name for the whole platform sitting on top of the EKG. A collision worth naming: "digital brain" and "digital twin" get used interchangeably in this market's copy, but a digital twin normally means a simulation of a physical asset. o9's is a model of a planning process, not of a machine.
Neurosymbolic AI
Deliberately combining a neural model — a large language model (LLM), a deep network — with symbolic computation: explicit rules, constraint solvers, optimisation, formal logic. The argument is that a language model can read and reason over context but cannot be trusted to produce a feasible production schedule, so the hard constraints are enforced by a solver rather than by the model. This is o9's stated architectural direction and it is the most important term on this page for him — see green flag 2.
APEX operating model
o9's branded end-state for customers: fully integrated, largely autonomous, AI-driven planning. It appears in o9's own job postings as the "north star". A marketing frame, but a live one — it is what the chief technology officer (CTO) is publicly measured against.
VUCA
Volatility, uncertainty, complexity, ambiguity. Borrowed from military planning, now standard supply-chain vocabulary for "the world stopped being predictable". o9 uses it constantly. Knowing it is table stakes in this domain.
Go-live
The moment a customer's o9 implementation starts running their real planning cycle. o9 reports go-live counts as a commercial metric. Relevant because it reveals the shape of the company: a large part of o9's India headcount exists to deliver go-lives, not to build the platform.
Model registry
A versioned catalogue of trained machine-learning (ML) models — which version is in production, what data trained it, what it scored, how to roll back. Named explicitly in the requirements. It is the one named MLOps artefact his record never evidences, which the rubric already records; retraining pipelines and drift detection it does evidence.
Drift — model drift and data drift
Model drift: the world changes and a model's accuracy decays without anything breaking. Data drift: the inputs change shape or distribution. Both are silent failures, which is why continuous monitoring is a named duty here rather than an afterthought.
MLOps · CI/CD for ML
Machine-learning operations. Applying continuous-integration and continuous-delivery (CI/CD) discipline — automated build, test, release — to models rather than only to code, so that retraining and redeployment are a pipeline, not a person.
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG)
Fetching relevant context from a data store at query time and putting it in front of the language model, instead of relying on what the model memorised in training. Named in the requirements alongside vector databases.
Transformers · state-space models · diffusion models
Three families of deep-network architecture. Transformers underpin most current language models. State-space models are a rival sequence architecture that scales better on very long sequences — directly relevant to supply-chain time series. Diffusion models generate by iteratively denoising, best known for images but increasingly used for time series. These three appear in the listing's skill tags but not in the job-description body, which turns out to matter — see red flag 1.
Distributed computing frameworks · Spark
Engines that run a computation across a cluster rather than one machine. Apache Spark, and its Python interface PySpark, is the default in this market. A collision worth naming: DuckDB and Parquet — which he does have — solve an adjacent problem (fast analytical queries over columnar files) but are not a distributed cluster engine. Multiple o9 postings name PySpark and Databricks directly, so this is a real house tool, not job-description boilerplate.
"Solution Architect" at o9
The most misleading collision on this page. At o9, and especially at o9 Bengaluru, "Solution Architect" and "Technical Architect" overwhelmingly mean customer implementation — configuring the platform for a client, running blueprint workshops, building data interfaces, hypercare after go-live. It is a consulting track. It is not what this seat is. Every employee-review signal attached to those titles therefore describes a different job, and that distinction carries a lot of weight below.
Individual contributor (IC)
A seat with technical authority and no direct reports or appraisals. o9's own postings use the phrase explicitly for some architect roles. Load-bearing here because this workspace builds IC seats only.

Verdict

The read, in three sentences

The seat is real and the pasted description is o9's own requisition text verbatim — it is JR102297 on o9's Workday careers site, a Bengaluru platform-architecture role at a Gartner-recognised, well-capitalised product company whose stated architectural direction, neurosymbolic AI, happens to be the exact argument his own portfolio is built around.

But three things about the posting do not line up, and all three point the same way: the requisition has been open since 26 March 2026, a second and entirely different requisition sits under the same title in the same city, and o9's own newest architect posting says the technology organisation is being restructured right now — with the ground-up core AI function being built in the United States, not Bengaluru.

So this is worth a call and the call has one job: establish which requisition he is actually being sourced against and where that seat sits in the new structure — because on the evidence available, the difference between the two candidate requisitions is the difference between a platform-architecture seat he fits well and an applied-mathematics research seat gated on a doctorate.

Location and schedule are not scored, and nothing below treats them as negatives. Bengaluru is neutral — relocation is open. Schedule density is neutral. Two things are surfaced as separate axes and both belong on the call: travel cadence to customer sites, and call-window and on-call expectations against United States and European time zones, which is the specific thing o9's India reviews complain about. Separately worth asking, and purely as an option rather than a requirement: o9 has an R&D office in Hyderabad, where he already lives.

Compensation appears nowhere on this page, by instruction. It is deferred until a conversation actually reaches that stage. The listing's stated band is recorded as a neutral fact in jd.md and is not carried, compared or reasoned about here.

2 — The requisition has been open for five months.

JR102297's posting date on o9's own system is 26 March 2026. Today is 26 August 2026. o9's public rendering flattens this to "Posted 30+ Days Ago", which hides it — the precise date only surfaces in the underlying record.

Three explanations fit and none can be separated from outside: the bar is genuinely high and o9 has not found the person; the requisition is stale and kept open as a pipeline collector; or the spec has changed and the posting has not. All three are worth knowing and all three are answerable in one question on a first call. A recruiter working from a live conversation with the hiring manager will know why; one who is not will deflect, and the deflection is itself the answer.

As part of this transformation, we are building a world-class core AI function from the ground up to serve as the foundational intelligence layer across all product engineering domains. 6 — Live litigation, and it touches precisely what this seat would own.

On 25 November 2025 o9 filed a trade-secret misappropriation complaint against SAP SE and SAP America in the United States District Court for the Northern District of Texas (case 3:25-cv-03245). It alleges three former o9 executives — Stephan de Barse, then chief revenue officer; Stijn-Pieter van Houten, then senior vice president and knowledge innovation lead; and Sean Zonneveld, then global senior vice president — downloaded more than 22,000 confidential files before joining SAP in 2025. The complaint invokes the federal Defend Trade Secrets Act (DTSA) and the Texas Uniform Trade Secrets Act (TUTSA), plus breach of contract and tortious interference.

The material alleged to have been taken includes "technical architecture and design for o9's Enterprise Knowledge Graph" — the core of the platform this seat is advertised to define, own and evolve.

Status, as of two weeks ago: on 11 August 2026 United States Magistrate Judge David Horan recommended denying SAP's motions to dismiss and for a more definite statement, finding o9's pleading adequate across eight groupings of alleged trade secrets. The case proceeds. SAP's public position is that it "respects the intellectual property rights of others" and will "respond, as appropriate, within the legal process".

This is not a reason to avoid the seat, and it should not be scored. A company defending its intellectual property is behaving normally, and o9 is the plaintiff, not the defendant. What it is is a set of practical day-to-day questions nobody volunteers: what confidentiality and information-barrier obligations attach to someone architecting the EKG while it is in dispute; whether document-preservation duties reach engineering artefacts; whether architecture decisions in this area route through counsel; and whether he could be drawn into discovery. Ask, in a neutral register, on the call.

Green flags

1 — The seat is real and the description is o9's own words. That check is the one that matters, and it passed cleanly.

An agency listing that names no client is exactly the shape that usually cannot be verified. This one can. Requisition JR102297 on o9's own careers system reproduces the pasted job description sentence for sentence — the role summary, the six responsibilities, the six requirement blocks, and o9's closing boilerplate. There is no agency embellishment and no invented seniority in the body; the only divergence is the title rendering and the skill tags.

Practically, this gives him something to open a call with: a requisition number the recruiter may not expect him to have. It converts "tell me about the role" into "is this JR102297", which is a different and much more efficient conversation.

2 — o9's stated architectural direction is neurosymbolic AI, and that is his own argument. This is his strongest positioning asset on this seat, and it is not on his résumé.

o9's chief technology officer has published the thesis directly. o9's own newest architect requisition points candidates at it by name:

Dr Ashwin Rao, CTO, o9 Solutions — "The Neurosymbolic Imperative for Enterprise AI"

"Large Language Models alone cannot deliver reliable agentic AI for enterprise planning and execution." Only by deliberately fusing neural AI with symbolic AI — a neurosymbolic architecture — can you build agents enterprises trust to run operations.

o9 — The Neurosymbolic Imperative for Enterprise AI · Davos Insights: Turning VUCA into Value with Neuro-Symbolic AI · Neuro-Symbolic Agentic AI for Agile and Adaptive Enterprises

His portfolio's stated through-line is "knowing when not to use a large language model." The rubric currently books that as a limitation, because it means no fine-tuning and no pre-training. Against this company it is the opposite of a limitation — it is the house thesis. And he has artefacts, not opinions: a query that explicitly declined a caching layer because an in-process cache was the right answer; a retrieval design that deliberately kept a graph store off the serving path and made the search index authoritative; deterministic contracts around generated narrative rather than free generation. That is neurosymbolic discipline described in engineering terms. It reframes the deep-learning-years gap from "he has fourteen months" to "he has been making the exact trade-off this CTO says the industry gets wrong" — which is an architecture argument, and architecture is what the seat is for. Positioning note only. No résumé wording changes here, and none of the open claims are touched.

His Context Graph work is directly adjacent in shape — graph-structured enterprise data, traversal-scoped by tenant, feeding retrieval. The graph neural network in his headline is on the confirm-or-reject list and is his call alone; it is surfaced here as a talking point and deliberately not rewritten, characterised or scoped. The honest framing for a call is the transferable one: he has built and served graph-structured domain data in production, in a different domain.

Forcing questions

Sharp, answerable on a first call, and ordered so the three that most quickly establish whether this is worth his time come first. Each is written so that a vague answer is itself informative.

The three that matter most

  1. "Is this requisition JR102297 or JR102298? You have two Senior Software Architect roles open in Bangalore and they're different jobs — one is platform architecture, the other is applied mathematics and statistical modelling with a Masters-or-PhD requirement."

    This is the highest-value sentence he can say on this call. It resolves the conflated skill tags, establishes which bar applies, and tests in one move whether the recruiter is working from a live requisition or from a forwarded description. If the answer is JR102297, the conversation proceeds on his strengths. If it is JR102298, the seat is a research role and the honest read changes. If the recruiter does not know there are two, that tells him how close this agency is to the hiring manager.

  2. "JR102297 has been open since 26 March. What's made it hard to fill, and has the specification changed since it was written?"

    Five months is the fact the posting hides — o9's public page renders it as "30+ days". A recruiter in real contact with the hiring manager will name the bar, the failed loops, or the revised spec. Deflection means the requisition is a pipeline collector, and that is worth knowing in minute four rather than after three rounds. The follow-up if the answer is thin: "how many candidates have reached the hiring manager?"

  3. "Your Senior AI Architect posting says o9 is restructuring the technology organisation around purpose-built engineering groups and building the core AI function from the ground up. Where does this Bengaluru seat sit in that structure, and who does it report to?"

    This is o9's own published language, so it is a fair and well-informed question rather than a challenging one. It tests three things at once: whether the seat survived a reorganisation that post-dates its posting, whether the reporting line still holds, and whether the artificial-intelligence centre of gravity has moved to Dallas — the ground-up core AI function is advertised United States-only. It also signals he has done the homework, which changes the tone of everything after it.

The rest, in order of value

  1. "Is this a product-engineering seat or a customer-implementation seat?" Not rhetorical. Most o9 Bengaluru roles carrying "Architect" are implementation — the Technical Architect posted six days ago asks for hypercare and customer service-level agreements. The requisition text reads product, but the surrounding population does not, so make him say it.
  2. "Does the seat carry direct reports and appraisals, or is it pure individual contributor?" The requisition names mentoring and design reviews and never names people management. o9 uses the phrase "individual contributor" explicitly elsewhere. Confirm rather than infer — this workspace builds IC seats only.
  3. "What's the distributed-computing stack, and how much of the platform actually runs on it — Spark, Databricks, Ray, something in-house?" The one requirement o9's other postings prove is load-bearing rather than boilerplate. The answer tells him exactly how large the ramp is, and asking it precisely is itself evidence he knows the difference between a cluster engine and a fast local query engine.
  4. "The five years in deep learning — is that a hard filter, or directional? It's written as a compound with data engineering." Asked neutrally, this is a scoping question, not a negotiation. The requirement genuinely reads as a compound and the halves should not be averaged. It also surfaces the constraint early rather than after four rounds, which is the cheaper place to discover it.
  5. "Which deep-learning and agentic work is in production today, versus on the roadmap?" Separates the published neurosymbolic thesis from shipped code. A company can have an excellent architectural argument and very little of it running. The answer also tells him what he would actually be architecting in month one.
  6. "How does the SAP litigation touch this role day to day — any confidentiality or document-preservation obligations, and do architecture decisions in the knowledge-graph area go through counsel?" Neutral register, practical framing. o9 is the plaintiff and the complaint specifically names Enterprise Knowledge Graph architecture, which is what this seat owns. Nobody volunteers this and it is entirely reasonable to ask.
  7. "What are the working-hours expectations for this seat — is there a standing United States or European overlap, and does it carry 24/7 on-call?" Asks about this seat rather than quoting anyone's reviews. Schedule density is never a negative here; the useful information is whether the platform track shares the delivery track's call windows or does not. Ask travel cadence to customer sites as a separate question — it is a different axis and the answers are often different.
  8. "Could this seat sit in your Hyderabad office?" One sentence, no downside. o9's Hyderabad office does research and development, he lives there, and the requisition is Bangalore-only with no remote type set. If the answer is no, Bengaluru is neutral and nothing is lost.
  9. "What does the interview process look like, and does the CTO organisation run the technical round?" Given a nine-week-old CTO with a research background, who runs the technical round says a great deal about what the seat is really being hired for — platform architecture or modelling.

Where these go, and where they do not. Per this workspace's recruiter-reply rule, a written reply answers exactly what was asked, in the order asked, then puts a call on the table — with no added questions, however useful they look. Every extra written question is one more thing standing between him and a phone call. All twelve above belong on the call cheat-sheet and get asked out loud. The one arguable exception is question 1: naming a requisition number in writing is short, specific and saves an entire call if the answer is the wrong requisition. That is his call, not this page's.
