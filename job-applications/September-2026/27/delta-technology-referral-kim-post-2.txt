The worst offshoring experiences I've seen share one thing in common.

Not the time zones. Not the cultural differences. Not even the cost-cutting.

It's opacity.

Promises made at the sales stage that quietly dissolve once the contract is signed. Senior talent showcased in the pitch — junior talent deployed in the delivery. CVs that look impressive on paper, work that tells a different story. Dashboards full of green RAG statuses, while the actual project quietly unravels.

I've sat on both sides of this. And I can tell you: the damage isn't just to the project. It's to trust. And once that's gone, it doesn't come back.

Here's what I've also learned, though.

The best offshore talent I've worked with — in India, across Southeast Asia, in Eastern Europe — is genuinely world-class. These are engineers, analysts, and architects who are hungry, rigorous, and deeply skilled. They're not a compromise. In many cases, they're the best people on the team.

But you only ever discover that when the relationship is built on transparency.

Transparency about who is actually doing the work. Transparency about capacity, pressure points, and where things are slipping. Transparency about what AI is augmenting — and what it isn't. Transparency that flows both ways: clients who share context, constraints, and real feedback, not just SLA scorecards.

The offshore model doesn't fail because of geography. It fails because too many organisations treat it as a transaction rather than a partnership — and too many providers let them.

What I look for now, and what I advise clients to demand:

→ Meet the actual team before you sign anything
→ Insist on direct access — not just a single account manager layer
→ Ask how attrition is handled and who owns continuity
→ Make quality a two-way conversation, not a quarterly review
→ Treat your offshore team as an extension of your culture, not a service desk

The depth of talent available globally is extraordinary. But depth without transparency is just potential — unrealised and unverifiable.

The organisations winning with offshore and AI-augmented teams aren't just sourcing smarter. They're communicating better. And that starts with demanding honesty from day one.
