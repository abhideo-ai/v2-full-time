The outsourcing debate has the wrong question at its centre.

For years, UK businesses have asked: "Should we offshore this?" Then AI arrived and the question shifted to: "Can we automate this?"

Both miss the point.

The real question — the one that will define competitive advantage in the next decade — is: "How do we combine the best of human talent, wherever it sits in the world, with the power of AI to deliver outcomes that neither could achieve alone?"

We are entering the era of AI-augmented resourcing. And the businesses that understand this early will leave the rest behind.

Here's what I'm seeing on the ground:

Traditional offshoring was a labour arbitrage play. Move the work, cut the cost. It created enormous industries — but it also created quality gaps, cultural friction, and a race to the bottom on price.

Then came the AI-first wave. Vendors promising 40–70% productivity gains. C-suites cutting headcount on the promise of automation. The reality? Many of those gains require significant operating model redesign that organisations simply aren't ready for.

But something more interesting is emerging in the space between those two failed models.

The highest-performing teams I'm working with aren't asking "AI or people?" — they're building environments where AI handles the volume, the repetition, and the pattern recognition, while skilled humans — in London, in Bangalore, in Dubai — bring the judgment, the context, and the creativity that no model can replicate.

KPMG put it well recently: what differentiates outsourcing providers today isn't the size of their offshore teams — it's the platforms they've built and the AI they've embedded into delivery.

I'd go further. The winners won't just be providers. They'll be the enterprise leaders who rethink their entire sourcing strategy around this hybrid model — treating AI as infrastructure and talent as the strategic layer on top.

For UK businesses navigating rising costs, skills shortages, and pressure to innovate at speed, this isn't a future conversation. It's happening now.

The question is whether you're building for it — or waiting to react.
