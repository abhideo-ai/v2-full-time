The loop three of these queries sit inside

The ten queries are not ten unrelated dashboards. At the moment a decision is made, the person on the call — a guidance counsellor with a prospective patient, a doctor with an existing one, a health coach on a minor ailment — has two things side by side. An AI recommender, a separate suggestion service, proposes an option and carries a confidence with it. The precedent panel (KQ1) supplies ranked comparable past decisions and what observably happened to them. The panel emits no option and no confidence of its own: it is evidence, not advice, and its job is to make accepting the suggestion — or departing from it — an informed judgment rather than a gut call. It does not decide, and it does not record the decision either.

The human decides, and the decision is captured on the append-only spine as first-class events — what the AI suggested, whether a human accepted or rejected it, and, where rejected, what they chose instead. Those events are what make the AI-versus-clinician comparison (KQ6) answerable at all. Confidence calibration (KQ7) sits on the other side of the same loop — did a stated confidence track the outcome that followed — and what the two of them find is what improves what gets proposed next time.

Two properties of that loop are worth stating plainly. The comparison could not be answered honestly before those events existed, so the design refused to guess: it specified the missing inputs as a contract with the upstream team and wired the query to refuse to run until they arrived. That is the block-on-prereq verdict KQ6 carries — the contract was signed and the query is live, and its final design-review approval is still pending. And the evidence surface is allowed to refuse. A prospective patient has no decision history and no graph neighbourhood, so for a guidance counsellor the cold-start gate commonly returns an honest "insufficient comparable precedents" state — a plain cohort count, explicitly labelled as a filter count and explicitly not as vector similarity — rather than a thin ranked list dressed up as precedent. The human then decides without precedent evidence. Which means KQ6's human arm is an evidence-assisted one, and sometimes an unassisted one: a real constraint on how that comparison may be read, and naming it is what makes the comparison defensible rather than less so.

Scale, ownership, and what is deliberately not shown

Scale. The platform served roughly 10,000 patients across 80+ chronic conditions, over about nine years of history hydrated into the append-only event spine. That depth is what makes the ten queries answerable at all: matured outcome labels, enough overridden decisions per policy version to match on, and a decision chain a regulator can reconstruct years later.

Ownership. Sole owner, design through production — architecture, the statistical and governance contracts, the implementation, and the deployment. Where a query depended on another team (an upstream audit-event contract, for instance), the case study says so and describes the contract that was negotiated rather than claiming the work.

Method. Built on LangChain and LangGraph, with a self-hosted Llama 3.1 in-VPC so patient data never leaves the network. The adversarial review loops these pages describe were run with frontier LLM agents — a Principal-engineer lens on each design, a hiring-CTO lens on the runnable code — in cumulative rounds whose findings are carried forward with an explicit disposition until the reviewer signs off. The full audit trail exists; the pages report its verdicts without softening them.

What is not here. No measured production numbers. Every latency, recall, precision and interval on these pages is a pre-registered acceptance target — the bar the system was designed, reviewed and deployed against — not an observed result. The observed figures belong to the client and are not mine to publish. Rather than dress a target as an outcome, the portfolio says plainly which is which, on every page. Treat that as the sample of how these systems get built: the number you cannot defend does not ship.

The shared-platform ledger

Seven of the ten queries began by inlining their own copy of an isolation, statistics or correction contract, and each design review promoted the reusable ones into shared platform components so a sibling query inherits the fix rather than re-implementing it. Because every page names the subset it depends on, the names drifted; this is the single canonical inventory and its current status. All but one have landed at the platform layer — the queries now bind to the shared version. The exception is stated as an exception.
