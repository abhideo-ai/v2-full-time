Deep-dive Q&A · Override analytics

When the platform overrides its own protocol — who did it, and did it work?

A hundred questions a Senior Principal engineer or CTO would actually ask about the override-analytics query for a chronic-care platform covering 80+ chronic conditions — and honest answers. The query compares overridden decisions against propensity-matched controls behind deterministic statistical gates, reconstructs every fact as of the moment it happened from an append-only event spine, and keeps a language model off every number. Answers teach the mechanism and its consequence; the uncomfortable ones are answered honestly, and the design's real limits are named at the end.

01 The question and why it is hard

What "who overrode, and did it work?" really asks, and why a single GROUP BY answers it wrong.

screening What does this query answer, in one sentence?

It finds every decision where a clinician deviated from an in-force protocol — the alignment flag reads overridden — and reports who overrode, the rationale they recorded, and how those patients fared at ninety days against a propensity-matched control of similar non-overridden decisions. The output is a set of policy-level and actor-level cells, each carrying an outcome delta with a confidence interval, a balance status, and an explicit observational tag. It is an analytics surface, not a recommendation engine.

screening Why is this harder than a GROUP BY on an "overridden" flag?

The GROUP BY gives you a rate; it tells you nothing about whether overriding helped, and it invites the reader to compare clinicians as if the rate were performance. The hard part is the counterfactual: an override is only meaningful against what would have happened to a comparable patient who was not overridden, and clinicians override on their least-typical patients, so the comparison is confounded before you start. Everything expensive in this design — as-of reconstruction, propensity matching, balance and stability gates, governance — exists to turn a misleading one-liner into a defensible comparison, or to suppress it honestly when it cannot be made defensible.

deep Why is a naive override-rate-per-clinician wrong three ways at once?

First, it is confounded by design: clinicians override on their sickest and most unusual patients, so a raw override-versus-followed comparison contrasts non-comparable populations. Second, small cohorts manufacture confident-looking rates from a handful of cases, so a clinician with twelve decisions can look like an outlier by noise alone. Third, the instant the table exists it reads as a scorecard — even a statistically clean rate can end a career if it is presented as a ranking rather than an observation. The design treats each as a first-class problem with a named control rather than hoping presentation will smooth them over.

deep What makes this the most politically charged query in the product, and how does that shape the design?

Every other query describes patients or the system; this one describes people's judgment calls, and any number attached to a named clinician is read as an evaluation of them. That is why governance is a first-class part of the pipeline rather than a UI afterthought: pseudonymous actor ids by default, leadership-and-self-view access, an audit entry written before any actor row is served, and an alphabetic, never-rankable ordering. The political charge is also why the statistical bar is set high — a fragile or under-powered actor cell is suppressed rather than shown, because the cost of a wrong, person-attributed number here is far larger than the cost of showing nothing.

adversarial Isn't comparing overridden against non-overridden decisions inherently confounded? How can any answer be trusted?

It is confounded, and the design never pretends otherwise. Propensity matching narrows the comparison to patients who look alike on the covariates that were measured, and the balance gate refuses to show a headline unless the two arms are demonstrably similar on those covariates. But matching can only balance what was measured — unmeasured confounding is never ruled out — so every surface stays observational, not causal, and the numbers are framed as "these matched patients differed by X," never "the override caused X." Trust here is bounded and stated, not claimed absolutely.

deep What does "override" mean mechanically — how is a decision marked overridden?

Each decision event on the spine carries a rationale with a policy_alignment field; a value of overridden means the recorded decision deviated from the protocol that constrained it at that moment. The cohort builder does not trust the current graph for this — it reconstructs policy_alignment, the free-text rationale, the acting person or agent, and the exact policy version from the spine creation events as of the decision timestamp. That reconstruction is what lets the query say this was an override against version 4 of this policy rather than against whatever the policy says today.

screening Who consumes this, and what decisions does it inform?

Clinical leadership and clinical-ops use it to see which protocols get overridden most and whether those overrides track with better or worse ninety-day outcomes — a signal that a protocol may be miscalibrated for a subgroup, or that a pattern of deviation deserves a closer look. Individual clinicians can see their own row (self-view). It informs protocol revision and review conversations; it is deliberately not wired into any automated action, incentive, or ranking.

adversarial If the honest answer is "we can't establish causality," why build the query at all?

Because a well-gated observational comparison is still far more useful than the raw rate it replaces, and the alternative — leadership eyeballing override counts with no comparison arm — is worse on every axis. A balanced, powered, caliper-stable outcome delta is a legitimate signal to investigate a protocol; it just is not a verdict on a clinician. The value is in surfacing where overrides cluster and whether they co-occur with different outcomes, with enough statistical honesty that nobody mistakes the signal for proof.

deep Why does the design default to suppression over emission?

Because in this domain a false emit — a headline number that should have been withheld — is far more costly than a false suppress. A wrong, person-attributed override delta shown with a tight interval can drive a bad protocol change or an unfair judgment; a suppressed cell that says "insufficient data" with its denominators merely withholds a signal until it is trustworthy. So every gate fails toward suppression with a stated reason and full denominators, and the evaluation is built around gate-check precision rather than coverage.

02 Architecture and data flow

Snapshot-only analytics with a matched control arm — where every number is pre-computed offline.

screening What is the high-level pipeline, from spine to served cell?

A nightly snapshot is exported from the decision graph into columnar files; a cohort builder scans them, reconstructs each override as of its moment from the append-only spine, anchors one decision per treatment episode, and computes pre-treatment covariates. A propensity matcher pairs each override with the closest non-overridden decision and emits a versioned match manifest. An aggregation step applies the statistical gates and writes by-policy, by-actor, and outcome-delta metric tables, which an embedded analytical engine serves behind role resolution and an audit log. Nothing on the request path recomputes statistics or touches the live graph.

deep Why snapshot-only, never touching the live graph at query time?

Three reasons compound. Correctness: a point-in-time snapshot with a manifest hash is reproducible, so the same request returns the same cells and every number can be replayed; a live graph mutating under the query cannot promise that. Isolation of load: cohort scans and matching are heavy and must never contend with the online decision path clinicians depend on. And governance: a frozen snapshot plus pre-computed tables means the numbers a reviewer audits are exactly the numbers that were served, with no read-time drift. The cost is bounded staleness, which is made explicit through freshness tiers rather than hidden.

deep What does the cohort builder actually do, step by step?

It runs a tenant-scoped analytical scan over the snapshot for candidate decision events of the relevant types, filtered to those mature enough to have a ninety-day outcome and above the minimum supported payload schema. For each candidate it reconstructs the alignment, rationale, actor, and policy version as of the decision timestamp from the spine, keeps only the overrides, and anchors one decision per treatment episode to avoid counting restarts multiple times. It then computes pre-treatment covariates as of the decision, attaches the outcome label through the shared outcome library, and writes a cohort table partitioned by tenant and snapshot hash, with every row carrying its as-of stamps and source sequence numbers.

deep Why DuckDB and Parquet rather than querying the graph directly for the cohort?

Cohort construction is a vectorized set operation over columns — scan, filter, join, group — which a columnar engine does in one pass, whereas per-row graph traversals would issue thousands of point lookups per refresh and put analytics load on the online graph. Parquet also gives content-addressable, partitioned artifacts that carry a manifest hash, so a cohort is a fixed, reproducible object rather than the result of a live traversal that could differ between runs. The rule the design enforces is blunt: no per-row Gremlin on the analytics path — everything reads the snapshot or the spine.

screening Where do the served numbers come from at request time?

From pre-computed metric tables. By the time a dashboard asks for override analytics, the balance gates have run, the confidence intervals are computed, and the cells are written to columnar files keyed by tenant and snapshot. The request path resolves the viewer's role, writes an audit entry, builds a canonical cache key, and reads the matching cells — it never recomputes a bootstrap or a match at request time, which is what keeps latency predictable and the served numbers identical to the audited ones.

adversarial A nightly snapshot means numbers can be up to a day stale. Why is that acceptable for clinical analytics?

Because this is a retrospective analytics surface, not a bedside alert: it answers "over the mature ninety-day cohort, how did overrides on this protocol compare?" — a question whose answer does not meaningfully move within a day. The design still refuses to hide the staleness: under 24 hours serves normally, 24–48 hours serves with a visible banner, and past 48 hours the service blocks and escalates rather than serve quietly outdated statistics. And any upstream correction triggers a targeted rebuild of just the affected slices within a five-minute P95, so material changes propagate far faster than the nightly cycle.

deep How does the matcher fit into the build path, and what does it emit?

It sits between the labelled cohort and the aggregation step: given the overridden decisions and a pool of eligible non-overridden candidates, it pairs each override with its nearest pre-treatment neighbour inside a caliper, seeded from the snapshot manifest and without replacement. Beyond the matched control rows, it emits a match_manifest_id — a hash over the covariate schema, snapshot manifest, cohort filters, caliper, replacement policy, seed, and tie-break rule. That manifest is the contract that lets a sibling cohort query reuse the exact same control arm, or rebuild it identically, instead of silently producing a slightly different one.

deep Why is the decision graph in the architecture at all if it is never queried at analytics time?

The graph is the projection target that the nightly snapshot is exported from, and it serves the online decision experience; the analytics pipeline consumes a frozen export of it, not the live database. Keeping it on the diagram but off the request path is deliberate — it marks the graph as a deliberate absence for this query, so no one mistakes a convenient live traversal for a supported access pattern. The source of truth for reconstruction is the append-only spine, and the snapshot is a derived, reproducible view of the graph at a pinned moment.

adversarial What breaks if someone adds a per-row graph call to "enrich" the cohort?

Reproducibility and isolation both break. A live traversal reads current graph state, which can differ from the pinned snapshot and can reflect edits made after the decision — reintroducing exactly the as-of leakage the design forbids — and it makes the cohort non-deterministic, so the golden replay test can no longer prove two builds produce identical parquet. It also puts analytics load on the online graph. The prohibition on per-row graph calls is a design invariant precisely because a single well-meaning enrichment call quietly voids the correctness and determinism guarantees the rest of the pipeline depends on.

03 As-of correctness and future leakage

Reconstructing what was true at the moment of the decision — not what is true now.

screening What is "as-of reconstruction," and why does this query need it?

As-of reconstruction rebuilds a decision's facts from the append-only spine using only events at or before the decision timestamp, rather than reading the current graph. This query needs it because every input — the alignment flag, the rationale, the acting actor, the constraining policy version, and the matching covariates — can be edited after the fact, and using a later value would leak the future into a historical comparison. Without it, an override could be scored against a covariate the patient only acquired weeks later, or attributed to a policy version that did not exist when they deviated.

deep Policy text changes mid-window. How do you avoid attributing an override to the wrong policy version?

Overrides are referenced to an immutable, versioned policy, not to a mutable policy id. The builder reconstructs the constraining-policy edge and its policy_version_id plus a policy_text_hash as of the decision, so an override is bound to the exact protocol text that was in force when the clinician deviated. Grouping and comparison then happen by policy version, which is also why the downstream policy-friction query can trust that two pipelines are talking about the same protocol. If policies were keyed by mutable id, a mid-window edit would silently pool overrides against two different texts into one cell.

screening Which Decision fields are reconstructed from the spine rather than read from current state?

The alignment flag, the free-text rationale, the "made by" actor edge, the "constrained by" policy edge, and the policy version and text hash — plus the pre-treatment covariates used for matching. Each is rebuilt from spine creation events at the decision's source sequence number, and the row persists the sequence and an as-of timestamp for every field. Reading any of these from the current graph is explicitly forbidden, because all of them are mutable after the decision.

adversarial If you read a covariate "as of the decision," how do you prove the value wasn't back-edited afterward?

Because the reconstruction reads the spine's append-only creation events, not the current property, and it selects the latest valid observation whose event time is at or before the decision — a later correction is a new event with a later sequence number and is simply not in scope for that as-of read. Every emitted row records the source_event_seq and feature_as_of_ts it used, so on replay you can point at the exact event that produced each value. A back-edit does not overwrite history on an append-only spine; it appends, and the as-of read deterministically ignores anything after the cutoff.

deep What is the leakage risk in the matching covariates, and how is it prevented?

Two distinct risks. Temporal leakage: matching on a value observed after the decision — a later lab, a later program state — imports the future; the fix is that every covariate is reconstructed strictly as of the decision timestamp. Endogeneity leakage: matching on something caused by the patient being override-eligible in the first place — a recent-escalation count, a prior protocol-exception flag — bakes the treatment into the "control," collapsing the comparison; the fix is that the covariate set is pre-registered as pre-treatment only, and endogenous fields are deliberately excluded. Both are audited as part of the review, not left to the featurizer's discretion.

deep Why persist a feature-as-of timestamp and a source sequence on every row?

So feature freshness is auditable on replay rather than assumed. When a regulator or a reviewer asks "how was this override's baseline HbA1c determined?", the row names the exact event and the exact time basis it used. It also makes correction handling precise: a correction event carries a sequence range, and only rows whose recorded source sequences overlap it are rebuilt. Without those stamps, freshness and correction scope would both be guesswork.

adversarial A late-arriving observation with an event time before the decision could legitimately change the as-of value. How is that handled?

That is exactly why the two time axes are kept separate: an observation carries both when it happened (event time) and when the system learned of it, and the as-of read is by event time, so a legitimately earlier-but-late-arriving observation does update the reconstructed value. The snapshot manifest carries an allowed-lateness watermark so the build knows how long to wait for stragglers, and when a late arrival lands after a cohort is built it is treated as a correction: the dependent slices are rebuilt within the five-minute P95. The design does not assume events arrive in order; it assumes only that event time and sequence are recorded.

deep Why is reading current graph properties on a historical decision explicitly forbidden?

Because a current property read on a historical decision can silently return a value edited after the decision — a rationale that was amended, a policy edge that was re-pointed, a confidence that was revised — and there is no signal at read time that it happened. The prohibition is categorical rather than case-by-case because "this particular edge is probably immutable" is the kind of assumption that quietly rots; requiring spine reconstruction for every Decision-tied field or edge makes the correctness property hold by construction. It is the difference between hoping nothing was edited and proving the read used only pre-decision events.

screening What is the single worst as-of mistake this design is built to prevent?

Scoring an override with information that only existed after the clinician deviated — most dangerously, an outcome-adjacent covariate — which would make overrides on patients who later deteriorated look systematically different for reasons that had nothing to do with the override. That single mistake would bias the entire comparison and would be invisible in the output. As-of reconstruction, the pre-treatment covariate rule, and the persisted as-of stamps exist first and foremost to make that class of error impossible to commit silently.

04 Tenancy and isolation

A three-field tenant scope enforced at partition, at serve, and in the cache key.

screening What is the tenant scope for this query?

Three fields — account_id, workspace_id, and controlunit_id — resolved from the caller's authenticated token, never from a client-supplied parameter. Every cohort row, metric cell, cache key, and served response is scoped to that triple. Isolation here is a three-field predicate, not a single-field account check, because the platform's tenancy model nests workspaces and control units within an account.

deep How is tenant isolation enforced end to end for this query today?

Three layers in series. The candidate scan filters on the full tenant triple before any rows are loaded; the derived cohort, matched-control, and metric parquet are physically partitioned by that triple plus the snapshot hash; and a runtime assertion fails closed if any served cell's tenant fields do not equal the caller's. The cache key also carries the triple, so a cached cell for one tenant can never be returned to another. Defense in depth is intentional — no single predicate is trusted to be the only thing standing between two tenants.

adversarial A single forgotten predicate leaks cross-tenant rows into a snapshot forever. What actually stops that?

Two things beyond the query author's discipline. First, physical partitioning by the tenant triple means a scan that names the caller's partition cannot see another tenant's rows even if a WHERE clause is fumbled. Second, the fail-closed runtime assertion re-checks every served row against the caller's tenant, so a leak that slipped through the scan surfaces as a paging incident rather than a silent disclosure. The honest limit is that per-query discipline is fragile, which is why the design named a shared tenant-injecting access wrapper as a platform dependency rather than assuming it — it has since landed, and the query now binds to it, removing the discipline from every author's hands.

deep Why partition the cohort by tenant AND assert tenant at serve time — isn't one enough?

They fail differently, so both are kept. Partitioning is a build-time property: it prevents a scan from reading across tenants and keeps the artifacts physically separated on disk. The runtime assertion is a serve-time property: it catches a mistake anywhere downstream — a mis-keyed cache entry, a join that pulled an unpartitioned column, a bug in the API layer — before a byte reaches the caller. Belt-and-braces here is cheap and the failure it guards against is catastrophic and irreversible, so the redundancy is warranted.

deep What is the runtime tenant assertion, and when does it fire?

It is a fail-closed check that every row in a served response — and every row written by the builder — carries the caller's exact account, workspace, and control unit. It fires at write time in the cohort builder and again at serve time after the cache returns, so even a cached answer is re-checked for the current caller rather than trusted because it was served once before. A mismatch is not degraded around; it pages, because a cross-tenant row is an incident, not a metric.

adversarial The design once said it "awaits a shared access wrapper." Now that it has landed, what was the exposure until then?

The per-query controls are in place and enforced inline: tenant-scoped scans, partitioned artifacts, and the fail-closed assertion all ship with the query. The shared wrapper that injects the tenant predicate automatically on every access — so no future author can forget it — has since landed at the platform layer, and the query now binds to it. Before it landed, isolation for this query rested on the inline predicate plus the runtime assertion — strong, but dependent on each new code path repeating the pattern, which is precisely the fragility the shared wrapper was meant to remove. It was named as a platform dependency rather than quietly assumed solved.

deep Why must the cache key carry the full three-field tenant scope?

Because a cache is a place where a cell computed for one caller can be handed to another, and dropping any tenant field from the key is how a cross-tenant hit happens. The canonical key is built from sorted JSON over the full triple plus the query shape and freshness pins, so two different tenants can never collide on the same key. A cache keyed on the metric-table version alone — the exact bug this design was reviewed to avoid — would silently serve one tenant's cells to another on a hit.

screening What happens on a cross-tenant assertion failure?

It pages. A returned row lacking the caller's tenant triple, or a parquet partition that does not match the requested scope, is treated as an isolation breach and escalated immediately rather than logged and served. This is deliberately not a soft-fail: there is no "return what we can" path, because a single cross-tenant cell in a healthcare context is a disclosure, not a degraded result.

05 The core mechanism · propensity matching

Pairing each override with its closest comparable non-override — and being honest about the ones with no match.

screening Why propensity matching rather than a raw override-versus-rest comparison?

Because the two groups are not comparable to begin with — overrides happen on atypical patients — so a raw comparison measures the difference between populations, not the effect of overriding. Matching pairs each override with a non-overridden decision that looks alike on pre-treatment covariates, so the outcome delta contrasts similar patients rather than dissimilar ones. It does not manufacture causality, but it removes the crudest confounding on the covariates that were measured, which is the minimum bar for the comparison to be worth showing.

deep What are the matcher's pinned parameters, and why are they pinned?

The version, the seed (derived from the snapshot manifest), the base caliper, a without-replacement policy, a deterministic tie-break, and the covariate schema hash are all fixed and recorded. They are pinned because matching is the step most prone to silent drift: change the seed and you get different pairs, change the caliper and you get a different cohort, and either change moves the confidence interval. Pinning them and hashing them into the match manifest is what makes a matched cohort a reproducible object that two pipelines can agree on rather than a fresh guess each night.

deep How is the control pool constrained — what makes a decision eligible to be a control?

A candidate control must be a non-overridden decision of the same archetype, in the same policy family, at the same program phase, within the same tenant, and it must be comparable only on pre-treatment covariates. Same policy family matters most: comparing an override of one protocol against a followed decision on an unrelated protocol would confound the policy with the override. The eligibility rules are part of the cohort-filter hash in the match manifest, so the definition of "eligible control" is itself pinned and reproducible.

adversarial Matching on baseline HbA1c is fine; matching on "recent escalation count" is not. How do you keep endogenous covariates out?

By pre-registering the covariate list as pre-treatment only and auditing it explicitly during review — endogeneity is the first critical question the design is examined against. A covariate that was itself caused by the patient being override-eligible — a recent escalation count, a prior protocol-exception flag — encodes the treatment into the control and biases the comparison toward null, so those fields are excluded by policy, not by the featurizer's judgment. The measured set is age, BMI, country, program, phase, baseline HbA1c, prior adherence, and tenure — all fixed before the decision — and the covariate schema hash pins that choice so it cannot drift unaudited.

deep Why same-policy-family controls only, rather than any non-overridden decision?

Because the question is "did overriding this protocol help?", and a control drawn from an unrelated protocol would confound the effect of the override with the effect of being on a different protocol entirely. Restricting controls to the same policy family holds the protocol context roughly fixed, so the contrast is override-versus-followed within a comparable clinical situation. The cost is a smaller control pool per cell, which is one more reason the query needs deep history to clear the power floor.

deep What is the match manifest, and what problem does it solve across sibling queries?

It is a hash over everything that determines the matched cohort — covariate schema, snapshot manifest, cohort filters, caliper, replacement policy, seed, and tie-break rule — committed to disk alongside the matched controls. The downstream policy-friction query is supposed to reuse this cohort's matches; the manifest is what makes "reuse" verifiable: it may reuse the controls verbatim only when every manifest field matches, and otherwise must rebuild with the same binary. Without it, two queries copy-pasting their own matchers would drift into divergent cohorts and divergent intervals, and the reuse claim would be unfalsifiable.

adversarial A downstream query "reuses this query's matches." How do you guarantee it gets the identical cohort and not a silently divergent one?

The guarantee is the manifest-equality check plus a shared binary. The downstream query compares every field of the match manifest — covariate schema hash, snapshot hash, cohort-filter hash, caliper, replacement, seed, tie-break — and reuses the controls only on an exact match; any mismatch forces a full rebuild with the same matcher binary rather than a partial or fresh-fallback patch. A cross-query parity fixture in the golden suite asserts both pipelines produce the identical matched cohort, and it exists because a real sign-flip between two pipeline versions was caught during review. The honest caveat is that this equality holds only once one shared matcher binary is the platform component both queries call.

deep Why k=1 without replacement, rather than k-nearest or with-replacement matching?

One-to-one without replacement gives each override a single, distinct comparator and keeps the control arm a set of real, non-reused patients, which makes the enrollment-clustered bootstrap and the balance accounting straightforward to reason about and to audit. With-replacement matching reuses good controls and complicates the variance estimate; larger k trades bias for the risk of pulling in less-similar neighbours. The design favors the interpretable, auditable choice and instead spends its robustness budget on the caliper sensitivity sweep, where the trade is made explicit rather than buried in the estimator.

adversarial Propensity matching only balances measured covariates. Doesn't that make every emitted delta potentially confounded?

Yes, and the design states it rather than hiding it: matching balances only the covariates that were measured, so unmeasured confounding is never ruled out, and every surface is tagged observational for exactly this reason. What matching buys is the removal of confounding on the measured, pre-registered set — a real improvement over the raw comparison — and the balance gate refuses to headline a cell where even that measured balance is not achieved. The delta is honest about being an association within matched strata, not a causal effect; the selling language never uses causal verbs.

deep How are unmatched treated rows handled — dropped, or accounted for?

An override with no control inside the caliper is excluded from the estimate but counted: every cell reports an n_unmatched. Silently dropping unmatchable overrides would bias the comparison toward the easy-to-match cases and make overrides look more routine than they are, so the denominator is kept honest by surfacing exactly who the estimate could and could not speak for. If unmatched rates climb, that itself is a signal that the cell's comparison is on thin support.

deep What is the tie-break policy in matching, and why does it matter for reproducibility?

When two candidate controls are equidistant from an override, the matcher breaks the tie by a canonical decision id rather than by whatever order the rows happened to arrive. Without a deterministic tie-break, two runs over the same data could pick different controls purely from row ordering, producing different cohorts and different intervals — and the golden replay test would fail for no real reason. The tie-break rule is part of the match manifest, so it is pinned and its effect is reproducible.

06 Statistics, gates and suppression

A gate ladder in code: balance, stability, power, uncertainty — and a schema that refuses a gate-failing number.

screening What is the SMD gate, and what does failing it do?

The standardised mean difference measures how far apart the two arms are on each pre-registered covariate; the gate requires every covariate's SMD to sit at or under 0.1 before a cell may show a headline number. If any covariate exceeds the threshold, or common support is poor, the cell is tagged unmatched, its headline is suppressed, and it is routed to descriptive counts only. It is the balance check that decides whether the two arms are comparable enough to compare at all.

deep Why is the balance gate applied per cell, before any headline number is shown?

Because balance is a property of a specific policy-by-cohort slice, not of the pipeline as a whole — a matcher can produce well-balanced arms for one protocol and poorly balanced arms for another with a thin control pool. Checking per cell, before emission, means a poorly matched slice never gets a headline delta at all; it degrades to descriptive-only with its reason recorded. Applying it globally would let a well-behaved average hide a handful of badly confounded cells, which are exactly the ones a reader would over-interpret.

deep What is the sensitivity sweep across calipers, and how is it different from the SMD gate?

The SMD gate asks "are the matched arms balanced?"; the sensitivity sweep asks "is the finding an artifact of the matching radius?" Each comparison is recomputed at a strict, a base, and a loose caliper, and if the sign of the delta flips or the interval moves materially across them, the cell is marked unstable and its headline and any recommendation are withheld. It is a robustness check on the match itself, distinct from and additional to the balance gate — a cell can be perfectly balanced at the base caliper yet fall apart when the radius changes, and that fragility is worth surfacing.

adversarial If a finding is stable across calipers, does that make it causal?

No. Caliper stability shows the finding is not an artifact of one arbitrary matching radius; it says nothing about confounders you never measured. A delta can be balanced on every measured covariate and stable across every caliper and still be driven entirely by an unmeasured factor that co-varies with overriding. Stability raises confidence that the observational estimate is not a matching artifact — it does not promote an association to a cause, and the surface keeps saying observational regardless of how many robustness checks it passes.

deep Why a minimum of thirty patients in each arm, and what is emitted below the floor?

Below roughly thirty per arm, bootstrap intervals are wide and unstable and a handful of cases can swing a rate, so a small cell manufactures false precision — the second of the three ways a naive dashboard misleads. When either arm falls below the floor, the cell emits an explicit "insufficient data" with its full denominator breakdown rather than a headline number. The user sees that the question was asked and why it could not be answered, instead of a confident figure built on a dozen patients or a silent gap.

deep How are the confidence intervals computed, and why cluster by enrollment?

Through a shared bootstrap library, fixed at 2000 resamples, seeded from the snapshot manifest, percentile intervals by default, with the resampling unit being the enrollment rather than the row. Clustering by enrollment matters because a single patient's enrollment can contribute multiple correlated decisions; resampling rows independently would treat those as independent observations and understate the interval. Clustered resampling produces an honest width, and seeding from the snapshot hash makes that width reproducible byte-for-byte.

adversarial Bootstrap CIs on observational deltas can look precise and still be biased. What stops a decision-maker from over-reading them?

Nothing about a confidence interval can fix bias — it quantifies sampling uncertainty, not confounding — and the design does not pretend otherwise. The counter-measures are contextual: every cell ships with its balance status, its sensitivity status, its censoring counts, and its unmatched count, and every surface carries the observational tag, so the interval is never presented as the whole story. The evaluation is also built around gate-check precision, meaning the intervals only appear on cells that already cleared balance, stability, and power — the interval is the last rung, not the first.

deep What is the gate ladder, and why is it modeled as code rather than a checklist?

The ladder is four rungs — balance, then caliper stability, then power, then a seeded bootstrap interval — and every rung has an explicit exit that produces a suppressed cell with a stated reason and full denominators. It is code, not a checklist, because a checklist is advisory and drifts; seeded, replayable code applies the same reasoning every run and can be tested against known ground truth. The point is that the "chain of thought" behind a shown number is deterministic and auditable, not a human remembering to check four things.

deep How does the type system make a gate-failing number unrepresentable?

The output schema's validator raises an exception if an outcome delta is attached to a cell whose gates did not pass — a cell carrying a headline with failed balance, instability, or under-power simply cannot be constructed. That converts a statistical policy into a compile-time-style guarantee: it is not that showing a bad number is discouraged, it is that the type refuses to hold one. The value is durability — the guarantee survives future refactors and new call sites, because the invalid state has no representation for anyone to accidentally reach.

adversarial Suppression hides findings. How do you keep it from being used to bury inconvenient results?

Suppression here is never a silent drop: a suppressed cell still renders, with its reason (unmatched, unstable, or insufficient data) and its full denominators, so an absent headline is visible and accountable rather than a gap. The suppression rules are deterministic and pre-registered — they key off balance, caliper stability, and the sample floor, not off whether the result is convenient — and the golden regression pins which cells suppress and why. Someone wanting to bury a result cannot do it by tuning a threshold at request time, because the thresholds are fixed in the seeded build and the decision is recorded on the cell.

screening What does a suppressed cell actually show the user?

It shows the reason for suppression and the raw counts behind it — references, overrides, and the censoring and unmatched breakdown — but not the headline outcome delta or any actor or policy recommendation. For an unstable cell it may show raw rates with no delta; for an under-powered cell it says "insufficient data" with denominators. The user always learns that the question was asked and why it could not be answered confidently, which is a very different experience from a blank or a fabricated number.

07 Caching, determinism and versioning

Canonical keys, pinned seeds, and a replay test that makes two builds byte-for-byte identical.

screening What goes into the canonical metric-cache key?

Canonical sorted JSON over the tenant triple, the query namespace, the split and filter shape, the metric schema hash, the snapshot manifest hash, the match manifest id, the matcher binary version, the statistical-plan version, the maximum source event sequence, and a correction epoch. Every result-affecting input is in the key, so a change to any of them yields a different key rather than a stale hit. Building the key from sorted JSON avoids the subtle bug where two logically identical requests hash differently because their fields were ordered differently.

The last three deserve their own line. The match manifest id pins which control arm the cells were computed against; the matcher binary version pins the code that built it, because the manifest hashes the matcher's inputs and not the matcher itself; and the statistical-plan version pins the bootstrap and gate configuration applied afterwards. Omit any one of them and a cached delta can outlive the cohort it describes.

deep Why does the key include a correction epoch and a snapshot manifest hash?

The snapshot hash pins which point-in-time state the cells were built from, so a new snapshot cannot serve under an old key. The correction epoch is bumped when an upstream correction rebuilds affected slices, so a cell invalidated by a correction is no longer reachable through the old key even within the same snapshot. Together they make freshness a property of the key, not of a TTL — the cache cannot serve a cell that a newer snapshot or a landed correction has superseded.

adversarial TTL caches are simpler. Why not just expire metric results after an hour?

Because a TTL answers "how old is this entry?" and this query needs "is this entry still correct?" — different questions. Within an hour a correction can land that flips a cell, and a TTL would keep serving the stale value until it happened to expire; conversely, a stable cell would be needlessly recomputed on expiry. Freshness by content — snapshot hash plus correction epoch plus source sequence — invalidates exactly the cells that changed and keeps the rest, which is both safer and cheaper than time-based expiry.

deep How is the whole pipeline made byte-for-byte reproducible on replay?

Every stage is pinned to content, not to wall-clock or arrival order: the snapshot export is deterministic under shuffled spine order, the matcher is seeded from the snapshot hash with a canonical tie-break, and the bootstrap is seeded the same way. Each derived artifact carries its manifest and schema hashes, and the golden regression asserts that re-running the build over the same inputs produces identical parquet. The net effect is that any served number can be regenerated exactly and any two builds of the same as-of state agree.

screening What is the deterministic exporter, and why is it a platform-level dependency?

It is the component that turns a moment in the spine into a snapshot whose parquet hash is identical no matter what order events were replayed, including late arrivals, corrections, and supersede chains. It is platform-level because every snapshot-backed query depends on it — if the same as-of moment could yield two different snapshots, every downstream cohort and interval would be silently non-reproducible. This query names it as a dependency rather than re-implementing export determinism per pipeline.

adversarial Two replays of the same as-of moment could produce different parquet hashes if ordering isn't pinned. Where exactly is order pinned?

At three places. The exporter resolves point-in-time state deterministically regardless of the spine replay order, so shuffled ingestion produces one canonical snapshot. Any as-of reconstruction sorts by event time and source sequence, never by write order, so the bulk load can write years in whatever shard order is fastest without affecting the result. And the matcher's canonical tie-break removes the last source of order-dependence when two controls are equidistant. The golden replay fixture — same as-of moment, shuffled spine, late arrivals, corrections, supersede chains — asserts identical hashes precisely to catch any ordering leak.

deep How is the matcher made deterministic across runs?

Its seed is derived from the snapshot manifest hash, so the same snapshot always seeds the same pseudo-random draws; its replacement policy and caliper are fixed; and equidistant candidates are resolved by a canonical decision id rather than by arrival order. Because all of these are hashed into the match manifest, "deterministic" is not a hope but a checkable property — a rebuild with the same manifest fields must produce the same pairs. This is also what lets a sibling query trust a reused cohort.

deep Why seed the bootstrap from the snapshot manifest hash rather than a fixed constant?

A fixed constant would make every snapshot's resamples identical in their random structure, which sounds reproducible but couples unrelated snapshots and hides seed-related fragility. Seeding from the snapshot hash ties the resampling to the exact data it ran on, so the same snapshot always reproduces the same interval while different snapshots get independent draws. It keeps reproducibility per snapshot without pretending two different datasets should share a random seed.

adversarial If everything is cached and pinned, how does a legitimate upstream correction ever reach the dashboard?

Through the correction listener, which is the deliberate escape hatch from the pinning. A spine correction carries a source-sequence range; the listener maps that range to the derived slices whose recorded sequence ranges overlap it, rebuilds just those, and bumps the correction epoch so the old cache keys no longer resolve. The freshness is targeted — only the touched cells rebuild, at a five-minute P95 — so pinning does not trap stale data; it holds everything stable until a correction explicitly moves the affected slices.

08 Historical hydration · what it buys this query

Roughly nine years of overrides replayed into the spine — the difference between "insufficient data" and an estimate.

screening What does roughly nine years of hydrated history buy this query specifically?

Statistical power at the cell level. Propensity matching needs each policy version to have accumulated enough overridden decisions — and enough comparable non-overridden controls — to draw balanced pairs from, and a per-cell arm has to clear the thirty-patient floor. Nine years of replayed overrides, rationales, and outcomes is what turns most policy-by-cohort cells from "insufficient data" into estimable comparisons; a cold system would suppress almost everywhere for want of matchable support.

deep Why does propensity matching in particular depend on deep history?

Matching within a caliper only succeeds when a similar-enough control exists in the pool, and it restricts controls to the same policy family and program phase, which shrinks the pool further. With little history, many overrides have no control inside the caliper — they become unmatched, the arm falls under the power floor, and the cell suppresses. Depth of history is what populates the control pool densely enough that a nearest neighbour usually falls inside the caliper at an acceptable SMD, which is the precondition for the comparison to exist at all.

adversarial If you replay nine years of events on load day, don't they all look like they happened at once?

They would if event time and ingestion time were collapsed, which is exactly the failure the hydration guards against. Each legacy record keeps the moment the decision actually happened, with separate fields for when the system learned of it, and every as-of reconstruction reads by event time. The hydration watermark also runs in a mode where a nine-year spread of late-arriving data is normal rather than the small live-mode tolerance that would drop almost all of it. Collapse the two and every as-of reconstruction of what a clinician knew when they deviated would be void.

deep How are old payloads with different schema versions handled in the cohort?

Nine years means many payload-schema versions, so each gets an explicit upconverter and every candidate is filtered to be at or above a minimum supported schema before it enters the cohort. A row that cannot be mapped to a schema — or to a tenant — is quarantined and counted, never silently defaulted, because a defaulted field is how a mis-typed override or a cross-tenant row slips into the comparison. The reconstruction reads the payload version stamped on each event, so it always interprets a field the way that event's schema meant it.

deep Why filter on payload schema version in the candidate query?

Because a field like the alignment flag or the rationale structure may not have existed, or may have meant something different, in the earliest schema versions, and reading it under today's assumptions would misclassify old decisions. Filtering to a minimum supported schema ensures the fields the query depends on are present and comparably defined across the cohort. It is the difference between a defensible cohort and one silently contaminated by decisions whose payloads predate the concept of a recorded override.

adversarial Older overrides used policies that no longer exist. How can they sit in the same cohort as current ones?

They are keyed to the immutable policy version in force when they happened, not to a current policy id, so a retired protocol is a distinct, still-referenceable version rather than a dangling reference. Comparison and grouping happen by policy version, and controls are drawn from the same policy family, so an old override is compared against contemporaneous followed decisions on that same version, not against today's protocol. The history is not flattened onto the current policy set; each decision keeps its own as-of policy context.

deep What would this query look like without the hydrated history — where does it degrade?

It would still run, but most cells would suppress: thin arms would fail the thirty-patient floor, unmatched rates would climb because control pools would be sparse, and the surviving cells would carry wide intervals. The governance and gate machinery would all function correctly — they would just have very little to say. The degradation is graceful and honest rather than silent: the dashboard would show "insufficient data" widely instead of confident numbers on tiny cohorts, which is the correct behavior, merely a less useful one.

screening Does more history make the numbers more trustworthy, or just larger?

Both, but for different reasons. More history widens the control pool and lifts arms over the power floor, which narrows intervals and lets more cells clear the gates — that is real added trustworthiness for the measured comparison. It does not touch unmeasured confounding, so it cannot make an observational delta causal; a bigger cohort estimates the same possibly-confounded quantity more precisely. History buys power, not identification.

09 Governance, PHI and the LLM boundary

Audit-first access, pseudonymous actors, never-rankable ordering — and a language model kept off every number.

screening What is the access model for the actor-level breakdown?

Actor-level override data is need-to-know: by default only clinical leadership sees the full breakdown, and an individual clinician sees only their own row. The viewer's role is resolved from the authenticated token, never from the client, and any other caller is denied the actor breakdown and served the policy view only. Every actor-level request writes an audit entry before it is served. These are enforced mitigations that make misuse hard and visible, not a promise that the data can never be read as a ranking.

deep Why is the audit entry written before the data is served, not after?

Because a log written after serving can be skipped by any path that errors mid-response, which would disclose actor-level data with no record it was ever seen. Writing the audit entry first — viewer, target, purpose, and time, with the access-log id returned in the response — makes the access trail complete by construction rather than best-effort. The ordering is the whole point: the record exists before the disclosure can, so there is no window in which someone is shown a clinician's data untracked.

deep Why are actor IDs pseudonymous by default, and when is re-identification allowed?

Pseudonymous ids by default mean the routine leadership view shows comparative structure without naming individuals, which keeps casual browsing from becoming casual judgment. Re-identification requires an explicit purpose-of-access drill-in by a leadership viewer, and that drill-in itself writes a separate audit entry. The design makes seeing a real name a deliberate, logged act rather than the default state, so the barrier and the trail both scale with the sensitivity of the request.

deep Why is the actor list alphabetically ordered and never rank-sortable?

Because a table sorted by override rate is a leaderboard, and a leaderboard invites the exact scorecard reading the design works to prevent. Fixing the order to alphabetic — and refusing to expose a rate-sortable payload — removes the affordance that turns an observational breakdown into a ranking. It does not stop a determined viewer from sorting a copied spreadsheet, which is why the ordering is one mitigation among several (pseudonymity, RBAC, audit, observational tagging) rather than a standalone guarantee.

adversarial Can you guarantee this dashboard never becomes a scorecard?

No, and the design does not claim to. What is actually enforced is a set of mitigations that make misuse hard and visible: pseudonymous actor ids by default, leadership-and-self-view access, an audit entry written before any actor row is served, alphabetic never-rankable ordering, and an observational tag on every surface. A leader with legitimate access could still read a balanced table as a ranking — the controls raise the cost and the traceability of that misuse, and they log it, but they cannot make a human interpretation impossible. Honesty about that boundary is part of the governance, not a gap in it.

screening Where is the LLM in this system, and what is it forbidden to do?

There is exactly one language-model call, and it is an optional, post-hoc explainer: after a cell has passed every gate, the model is handed the finished numbers and asked to restate them in neutral, observational wording. It is forbidden to compute, re-rank, conclude, or reach for any fact outside the cell it was handed. Statistics, gating, suppression, ordering, and access control are all deterministic code — this is the portfolio's clearest case of knowing when not to use a language model.

adversarial If the explainer narrates the numbers, how do you stop it from inventing or misstating one?

Two ways. First, the claims never live in the generated text: the neutrality rules and the substantive statements ship as deterministic UI copy backed by the typed cell, and the explainer is additive colour, so a hallucinated figure has no authority even if it appears. Second, evaluation includes a faithfulness check — quoted numbers must match the cell and the prose must carry no verdict language. Because the model is handed one already-computed cell and asked only to restate it, there is no corpus to pull a wrong number from; the numbers were authoritative before the call and remain the sole authority after.

deep How is PHI kept safe when the explainer runs?

The production path is single and explicit: the explainer runs on a self-hosted model inside the VPC, so patient data never leaves the network. It also happens that the explainer is handed only the finished numbers — delta, interval, arm sizes, balance status — and never the reconstructed rationale free-text or any clinical identifiers, so its input is essentially de-identified even before the in-VPC boundary. If an external provider were ever used it would be permitted only with a de-identified payload; a contractual no-retention agreement is not a substitute for network residency, and the two are not treated as equivalent.

adversarial Doesn't temperature-zero make the narration deterministic and therefore auditable?

No — temperature zero does not make a language model's output deterministic. Pinning the model, the prompt, and the temperature, and hashing the input and output, gives you drift detection and reproducible auditing, not generation determinism; the hashes detect drift, they do not prevent it. Determinism in this system lives in the structured cell and the deterministic gate code, not in the narrator, and the faithfulness check re-validates the prose against the cell on every response. That is why the narrative is only ever optional colour and the cell — not the sentence — is the authoritative evidence.

deep Why keep governance rules as deterministic UI copy rather than in the model's prompt?

Because rules in a prompt can drift with a model update and can, in principle, be jailbroken, whereas rules shipped as code plus role-based access cannot. The neutrality framing, the observational tag, and the access branching are all deterministic, so they hold regardless of what the model does or whether it is running at all. There is nothing to jailbreak because the claims never pass through the model — the model only ever narrates a cell it is handed, and losing it loses a sentence of prose, not a control.

10 Failure modes, degradation and operations

What pages, what degrades gracefully, and what the system does when a gate, a snapshot, or a shared component is missing.

screening What happens when the snapshot is stale?

Freshness runs in tiers. Under 24 hours old, the service serves normally; between 24 and 48 hours it serves with a visible staleness banner so consumers know the numbers are aging; past 48 hours it blocks serving and escalates rather than quietly return outdated statistics. Projector lag beyond 30 minutes also trips the yellow tier. The behavior is explicit and visible at every stage instead of a silent slow decay.

deep How does the correction-event listener keep derived tables fresh, and what's the SLA?

Every derived artifact records the source-sequence range and snapshot hash it depends on. When a correction lands on the spine, the listener maps its sequence range to the slices whose recorded ranges overlap it and rebuilds just those — cohort, matched control, and metric cells — then bumps the correction epoch so stale cache keys stop resolving. The target is a P95 under five minutes from correction to fresh tables. The rebuild is dependency-targeted rather than a full nightly recompute, so a single correction does not force the whole cohort to rebuild.

adversarial A correction flips an override's outcome. How fast does the served number change, and what if the rebuild fails?

The listener rebuilds the affected slices within the five-minute P95 and bumps the correction epoch, so the old cell is no longer reachable through its prior cache key and the next read gets the corrected value. If the targeted rebuild fails, the correct posture is to fall closed for those slices rather than keep serving a value known to be superseded — a stale-but-known-wrong cell is worse than a temporarily unavailable one in this domain. The dependency index is what makes that fall-closed precise, touching only the slices the correction actually affects.

deep What does the system do when a whole cell fails the balance gate?

The cell is tagged unmatched, its headline outcome delta is suppressed, and it is routed to descriptive-only: raw counts and denominators, no comparison. It is not dropped from the response — the reader sees the policy or cohort, its reference and override counts, and the reason the comparison could not be made. That way a badly confounded slice degrades to honest descriptive statistics rather than to either a misleading delta or an unexplained absence.

deep How does projector lag degrade the service differently from a stale snapshot?

They are different clocks measuring different risks. Snapshot age measures how old the last successful export is; projector lag measures how far the graph projection has fallen behind the spine, which can make even a fresh snapshot miss recent events. The design surfaces both: projector lag beyond its threshold trips the yellow tier independently of snapshot age, because a recent snapshot built off a lagging projection is quietly incomplete. Treating them as one signal would let a fresh-but-incomplete snapshot look healthy.

adversarial The shared components weren't all shipped at first. How did the query run in the meantime, and what was fragile about that?

Where a shared component was not yet live, the query wired its contract against a stub or an inline implementation that returned representative results — the tenant predicate, the outcome labeller, the matcher, the bootstrap, and the actor-access policy all shipped inline in the query while their shared platform versions were pending. The fragility was real and named: inline implementations could drift from the shared contract, and cross-query guarantees like "reuse the same matched cohort" only truly held once one shared binary was what both queries called. Those components have since landed at the platform layer and the query now binds to the shared versions — but landing a component changes its own state, not the review verdict: the design still stands at "ship with changes," not approved.

deep What pages an operator, versus what degrades silently within tolerance?

Correctness and isolation breaches page: a cross-tenant row or a partition mismatch, a snapshot past the 48-hour block threshold, a correction rebuild that cannot complete. Bounded, expected conditions degrade visibly but do not page: a 24–48 hour snapshot serves with a banner, an under-powered or unbalanced cell suppresses with a reason, the explainer being down drops a sentence. The dividing line is whether the condition could mislead or disclose — those page — versus merely reduce coverage, which is surfaced and tolerated.

deep How do you detect that matched-cohort quality is drifting over time?

Through the observability the review called for: the per-refresh distribution of covariate SMDs, the unmatched rate per policy, and the share of cells that suppress for balance or stability. A rising unmatched rate or a widening SMD distribution signals that control pools are thinning or that the population is shifting away from matchable support, well before any single cell looks wrong. These are build-time diagnostics rather than request-time metrics, because the quality of a match is decided in the nightly build.

screening If the LLM explainer is down, what does the user lose?

A sentence of narration and nothing else. Every number, gate, interval, and access control is deterministic code that runs independently of the explainer, and the substantive claims live in UI copy, not in generated text. The dashboards are fully functional with the explainer absent — it is additive colour, so its outage is a cosmetic degradation, not a functional one.

11 Evaluation and acceptance criteria

Gate-check precision as the binding metric, on seeded synthetic cohorts where every gate decision is known.

screening What is the single binding metric this system is evaluated on?

Gate-check precision — whether every emitted cell genuinely passed balance, stability, and power. It is evaluated on seeded synthetic cohorts where the ground truth of each gate decision is known by construction, so a false emit (a headline that should have been suppressed) is detectable. That asymmetry — a false emit costs far more than a false suppress — is the axis the whole evaluation is built around.

deep Why is gate-check precision the binding metric rather than recall of real effects?

Because the cost of the two error types is wildly asymmetric here: a suppressed real effect withholds a signal until it is trustworthy, while an emitted spurious effect on a named clinician or a protocol can drive a bad decision that is hard to walk back. Optimizing recall would push the system to emit marginal cells; optimizing gate-check precision pushes it to emit only defensible ones. The design accepts lower coverage as the price of not headlining a number that should not exist.

deep How is the golden regression test constructed, and what does it guarantee?

It pins specific cells produced from seeded fixtures — including late-arrival, correction, and supersede replay chains — and asserts that a rebuild reproduces identical cells byte-for-byte. It guarantees that the pipeline is deterministic under exactly the conditions most likely to introduce order-dependence, so a refactor that quietly changes a matched pair or a suppression decision fails the test rather than shipping. It is the executable proof behind the reproducibility claims.

deep What is the cross-query parity fixture, and what real bug did it come from?

It asserts that the downstream policy-friction query, reusing this query's match manifest, reproduces the exact same matched cohort — identical pairs, identical cells. It exists because a real sign-flip was caught between two versions of the pipeline during review: the same override appeared to produce opposite outcome directions across the two consumers, which is exactly the divergence a shared matcher and a strict manifest check are meant to prevent. The fixture began life failing on that sign-flip and is wired into the golden suite as a permanent guard.

adversarial Your eval runs on synthetic cohorts. How do you know the gates behave on real data you can't label?

Synthetic evaluation is deliberate: it is the only place the ground truth of each gate decision is known, so it is where gate-check precision can actually be measured. On real data, where the true effect is unknowable, the design leans on the observability signals instead — SMD distributions, unmatched and suppression rates, censoring counts — to detect when the gates are behaving pathologically, plus the golden and parity fixtures to detect regressions. The honest limit is that no offline test proves the observational estimate is unconfounded on real data; that is why every surface stays observational rather than validated as causal.

deep How is tenant safety evaluated, and why isn't it treated as a metric?

It is checked as a pass/fail invariant, not a rate: a cross-tenant row or a partition mismatch is an incident that pages, with a target of zero, because there is no acceptable non-zero rate of cross-tenant disclosure in healthcare. Framing it as a metric would imply a tolerance, and there is none. The evaluation asserts the assertion fires on injected cross-tenant fixtures and that partitioning holds, treating any breach as a stop-the-line event.

screening What are the pre-registered acceptance targets a reviewer signs off against?

The SMD balance gate at or under 0.1 on the pre-registered covariates, a minimum of thirty patients per arm, a caliper sensitivity sweep with sign and interval stability, a seeded 2000-resample enrollment-clustered bootstrap, the snapshot freshness tiers, the sub-five-minute correction-freshness P95, and the actor-access governance rules. These are design-time acceptance targets, not measured production results — they are the bar the system was built, reviewed, and deployed against, and no measured outcomes are published.

12 Tradeoffs, alternatives, what I would change

Why matching over regression, why suppress over caveat, the weakest assumption, and what is still gated.

deep Why matching rather than regression adjustment or inverse-probability weighting?

Matching produces an explicit, inspectable control arm and a per-cell balance diagnostic, which makes the comparison legible to a clinical reviewer and easy to gate on — you can see the SMDs and refuse to headline an unbalanced cell. Regression adjustment and IPW can be more efficient but bury the comparability assumption inside a model and make "was this cell balanced?" a harder question to answer at the point of display. For a politically charged surface where the balance check itself must be visible and auditable, an inspectable matched arm is the more defensible primitive.

adversarial A difference-in-differences or instrumental-variable design could get closer to causal. Why not use one?

Both would require assumptions this data does not credibly support: difference-in-differences needs a parallel-trends assumption across overridden and non-overridden groups that clinicians' selective overriding almost certainly violates, and a valid instrument for "chose to override" is not available. Adopting either would trade an honestly-labelled observational estimate for a causal claim resting on an unverifiable assumption — a worse position for a surface people will read as judging clinicians. The design chooses the method whose limits it can state plainly over one whose limits it would have to hope hold.

deep Why suppress fragile cells rather than emit them with a large caveat?

Because caveats are reliably under-read, especially on a table someone wants to use to make a case, and a fragile delta shown with a footnote is still a delta someone will quote. Suppression removes the headline entirely and replaces it with the reason and the denominators, which is harder to misquote than a number plus a warning. The asymmetry of harm — a wrong person-attributed number versus a withheld one — makes withholding the safer default; the caveat lives in the observational tag on the cells that do survive.

deep What is the honest ceiling on what this query can claim, and why?

At most it can say: among matched patients balanced on the covariates we measured, overrides on this policy version were associated with a different ninety-day outcome by this interval, with these robustness checks passed. It cannot say overriding caused the difference, because matching balances only measured covariates and unmeasured confounding is never ruled out. Everything above that ceiling — protocol changes, clinician judgments — is a human decision the query informs, not a conclusion it draws.

adversarial If you had to name the single weakest assumption in the whole design, what is it?

That the pre-registered covariate set captures enough of why a clinician chose to override that matching on it yields comparable arms. Clinicians override on tacit signals — a patient's demeanor, a hunch, context not in structured fields — and none of that is measured, so residual confounding by indication is the most likely way an emitted delta is wrong despite passing every gate. The design mitigates it with the pre-treatment-only rule and the observational framing, but it cannot eliminate it, which is exactly why the query stays a signal to investigate rather than a verdict.

deep The shared platform components have since landed — why does the query still carry a "ship with changes" verdict rather than "approved"?

Five findings were promoted from the review into shared platform components: the tenant-injecting access wrapper, the shared outcome library, the shared matcher binary with its manifest, the deterministic exporter and its replay test, and the platform actor-access-and-audit policy. Those components have since landed at the platform layer, and the query now binds to the shared versions rather than its earlier inline stand-ins, so its strongest guarantees — automatic tenant enforcement, verifiable cross-query cohort reuse, and byte-for-byte export determinism — now fully hold. But landing the components changed their own state, not the review outcome: final design-review sign-off was never revisited, which is why the verdict stays "ship with changes" hardened into v2, not approved.

deep What would you do differently if you rebuilt this today?

I would sequence the shared platform components ahead of the per-query work rather than in parallel, so the query is built against the real tenant wrapper, matcher binary, and exporter from day one instead of inline stand-ins that must later be reconciled. I would also invest earlier in the observability of match quality — the SMD and unmatched-rate dashboards — because those are what tell you a cell is degrading before it produces a wrong number, and they are easy to add late and expensive to miss early.

adversarial Isn't shipping "ship with changes" and calling it live a contradiction?

No — the two describe different milestones. "Ship with changes" was the design-review verdict: the design is sound but conditioned on a named set of changes, most absorbed into the hardened v2 and a few promoted to shared platform components, which have since landed. "Live" describes the deployed service running against that hardened design with its inline controls in place. The query runs and serves governed dashboards; the remaining gate is the final design-review sign-off itself — a review outcome, not a component state. Landing the components did not convert the verdict, and the design names that distinction explicitly rather than papering over it.

screening Where else does this pattern transfer outside healthcare?

Anywhere you must answer "who deviated from the baseline, and did it work?" without turning the answer into a blame exercise: underwriting exceptions, SRE runbook deviations, fraud-ops overrides, content-moderation appeals. The transferable core is a matched observational comparison behind deterministic gates, governed access with an audit trail, and a language model kept away from the arithmetic. The domain changes; the discipline — honest numbers about human judgment calls, gated and governed — does not.

Honest gaps · what this query cannot answer today

Where the design stops, stated plainly

It is observational, not causal — and cannot be made otherwise on this data. Matching balances only the covariates that were measured; residual confounding by indication (the tacit reasons a clinician overrides) is never ruled out, so every surface stays observational and no delta is a proof that overriding helped or hurt. A powered, balanced, caliper-stable cell is a signal to investigate a protocol, not a verdict on a clinician.

Governance mitigates misuse; it does not make it impossible. Pseudonymous actor ids, leadership-and-self-view access, an audit entry written before serving, and alphabetic never-rankable ordering raise the cost and the traceability of reading the breakdown as a scorecard — but a viewer with legitimate access could still interpret a balanced table as a ranking. The controls make that hard and visible, not unthinkable.

The design-review verdict was "ship with changes," and it stays that way. A structured adversarial design review returned ship-with-changes with findings across correctness, statistics, governance, and scalability; most were absorbed into the hardened v2, and five were promoted into shared platform components — the tenant-injecting access wrapper, the shared outcome library, the shared matcher binary and manifest, the deterministic exporter and its replay test, and the platform actor-access-and-audit policy. Those components have since landed at the platform layer, and the query now binds to the shared versions rather than the inline controls it originally shipped with — its strongest guarantees — automatic tenant enforcement, verifiable cross-query cohort reuse, byte-for-byte export determinism, and a single actor-access policy shared across sibling dashboards — now fully hold. This query was never marked "approved," and landing the shared components does not change that: it was hardened and shipped with the platform dependencies named, not assumed solved, and approval remains a separate, still-pending review outcome.

What the language model does not do. The only model call is an optional post-hoc explainer handed finished numbers; it is forbidden to compute, re-rank, or conclude, and its output is additive colour re-validated against the cell. Pinning the model, prompt, and temperature gives drift detection and reproducible auditing, not generation determinism — the determinism lives in the deterministic gate code and the structured cell, never in the narrator.

Client and product identifiers are anonymised; this describes the design and hardening of an override-analytics system for a chronic-care platform covering 80+ chronic conditions, not a named product. The platform served roughly 10,000 patients across those conditions over about nine years of history hydrated into the append-only event spine. Role: sole owner — design through production.
