Theme 01 The question and why it is hard

Why joining three domains is the architecture's strongest proof and its most dangerous surface

screening What business question does this query actually answer?

It finds the patients whose financial, adherence, and support signals all went wrong inside one window, then reports what care decisions followed and how those patients fared. Concretely: a granted refund exception, a collapse in early adherence, and an escalated complaint, all for the same patient, followed by the follow-up care decisions and a 90-day outcome. The point is not any single domain but the intersection — the patients no single-domain dashboard would ever surface.

screening Why is this called the architecture's hardest proof?

The platform's defining bet is that decision traces compound across domains — that finance, adherence, and operations are not separate silos but one timeline per patient. This query is the single strongest argument for building a context graph at all, because it is only answerable when three subsystems' events share one identity, one clock, and one snapshot. It is also the most prone to producing misleading numbers, which is why almost all of the design is guardrails rather than joins.

screening What are the three domains and their qualifying signals?

Finance: a refund exception granted within a trailing 365-day lookback. Adherence: an early adherence score below 0.50 within the patient's first 30 days of enrollment. Operations: an escalated complaint within 365 days. Each threshold is a pre-registered design parameter, not a tuned result, and each event is read from a frozen snapshot rather than a live system.

deep Done naively, what are the four distinct ways this query fails at once?

Coincidence dressed as a pattern: three events that merely happened within a year are not a cross-domain episode, so without a window rule you manufacture cohorts from unrelated timelines. Confounding by design: patients whose finances, adherence, and support all break down are sicker and more complex, so a raw comparison is confounded before it starts. Cross-domain identity collisions: joining three subsystems on a loose key silently mixes the wrong people, and across tenants that is a reportable incident. Blame framing: "what happened to the patients who complained and missed doses" reads as a service-quality scorecard the moment the table exists. Each of the four is treated as a first-class requirement with a named control.

deep Why can't one SQL join across three tables answer this?

A raw join gives you co-occurrence within a lookback, not a cross-domain episode: it has no window rule, so it admits unrelated timelines; no as-of reconstruction, so it leaks values observed after the fact; no tenant identity discipline, so it collides across scopes; and no matched control, so any outcome it reports is confounded. The query is 10% intersection and 90% the machinery that makes the intersection honest — overlap predicate, snapshot pinning, identity key, propensity matching, and the suppression gates. The join is the easy part and the least of the risk.

deep Why is this cohort a "help-seeking population by construction," and why does that matter?

Every patient in the cohort is there precisely because they generated friction — a refund exception, a low adherence score, a complaint. That is a selection process, not a random sample, so the cohort differs systematically from the average patient on severity, complexity, and engagement. It matters because any naive outcome comparison against non-cohort patients is confounded by the very thing that defined the cohort, which is why the design forces a propensity-matched control and labels every surface observational.

adversarial Isn't this just a dashboard? Why call it multi-agent orchestration?

The orchestration is a declared, deterministic state graph, not a chat loop: three narrow finder personas fan out across the three domains, a barrier fans them in and intersects their candidate sets, a per-patient pipeline drills in, and a gated reduce runs the statistics. Calling it multi-agent is accurate about the coordination structure, but the honest framing is the opposite of hype — the agents are cheap, blind, and allowed only to propose candidates. Every join, the intersection, and every statistic are deterministic code, so the "dashboard" is the output of orchestration you could audit edge by edge.

adversarial If the interesting patients are rare, is the query even worth building?

Rarity is expected and is exactly why the statistical-reliability floor exists — most as-of values produce a small cohort that returns descriptive facts only. The value is twofold: the cross-domain window itself, which no single-domain view can construct, and the proof that decision traces compound across domains, which is the whole architecture's thesis. A query that admits "this cohort is too small to make a confident claim" is more valuable than one that manufactures a confident claim from a handful of complex patients.

Theme 02 Architecture and data flow

Two stages: a nightly snapshot cohort screen, then an on-demand per-patient chain walk

screening What are the two stages and how do they differ?

Stage A is a cohort screen: a nightly, snapshot-only pass that finds the patients whose finance, adherence, and ops signals all broke inside one 90-day window, using tenant-scoped analytical joins over frozen columnar files. Stage B is a per-patient drill-in: when a reviewer opens a single patient, the service delegates to a deterministic single-patient chain service with an explicit 90-day window. Stage A is pre-computed nightly; Stage B is on-demand only and never pre-walked.

screening Which part does a user actually wait on?

The request path is the only thing a clinician waits on, and it is deliberately cheap: the service resolves the caller's role, writes the audit entry, and reads a pre-computed cell from the cache or the columnar serving store. The heavy work — snapshot export, cohort build, matching, bootstrap — runs in the nightly lane with nobody waiting. A cohort number is never computed at request time; the request only reads a cell that was computed the night before.

screening Walk the data flow from the event spine to a served cohort number.

The append-only event spine feeds a nightly projection that also emits an ungated observation store where finance, adherence, and ops events are all visible. That projection freezes into a snapshot at an as-of instant, carrying a manifest hash. Three filters pull the qualifying finance, adherence, and ops rows; the overlap-window predicate keeps only patients whose three events fall in one 90-day window; the outcome labeller attaches a 90-day outcome; the propensity matcher builds a control; the aggregate builds the histogram and a seeded bootstrap CI. The result is written as pre-computed cells the serving endpoint reads.

deep Why is Stage A snapshot-only, with no live graph reads at all?

Reading the live decision graph per patient would be non-deterministic, expensive at cohort scale, and would leak current state into a historical cohort. A frozen snapshot makes the whole nightly build reproducible — the same corpus yields the same cohort — and reading a columnar file with predicate pushdown is far cheaper than a per-patient graph traversal. The live graph is touched only by the on-demand per-patient drill-in, never by the cohort screen.

deep Why compute the cohort nightly rather than on demand?

The cohort screen intersects three filtered domains, builds a propensity-matched control, and runs a 2000-resample clustered bootstrap — none of which fits inside a request budget. Pre-computing it nightly turns the request into a cache read of a finished cell, and it makes the number reproducible and auditable rather than dependent on whatever the graph happened to look like at query time. The tradeoff is freshness: a patient who qualifies today is not in the cohort until tonight's build, which the response surfaces explicitly.

deep How does Stage B relate to the single-patient causal-chain query?

Stage B does not re-implement graph traversal — it delegates to the platform's deterministic single-patient chain service, passing an explicit window from the pivot to the pivot plus 90 days plus the tenant context. That service returns a structured chain artifact with an export hash and validation metadata. Reusing it means the drill-in inherits the chain service's determinism and its bounded-walk discipline instead of growing a second, divergent traversal that would drift over time.

deep Why does the aggregate not recompute statistics at request time?

Every metric cell is built in the nightly lane and stored keyed by a canonical cache key over the tenant, the filters, and every version and freshness pin. The request path scans those pre-computed cohort and metric tables and asserts the tenant on each row; it never re-runs matching or the bootstrap. That keeps request latency bounded and, more importantly, keeps the served number identical to the one that was reviewed — recomputing at request time would let two identical requests disagree.

adversarial Stage A reads a snapshot and Stage B reads a live-derived chain — isn't that an inconsistency a user sees?

It is a deliberate mixed model, and the design's answer is to make the split visible rather than hide it. Every response carries the snapshot metadata — as-of, manifest hash, and source-event-sequence high-water mark — and the per-patient drill-in additionally carries the live walk's as-of, its last-event-sequence, the projector lag, and a degradation status. A patient who newly qualifies today is missed by tonight's cohort; that is stated through the as-of metadata, not silently reconciled, because a nightly metric with an honest timestamp is more trustworthy than a live number with a hidden one.

adversarial If the nightly build fails, is the dashboard simply down?

No — the serving store still holds the last good snapshot, and the response carries its as-of and a degradation tier so the staleness is legible rather than invisible. A user sees yesterday's cohort clearly labelled as of yesterday, not a spinner or a silent gap. The design's stance throughout is that a stale number with an honest timestamp is acceptable; a stale number presented as current is the failure.

Theme 03 As-of correctness and future leakage

Every value the cohort sees existed before the pivot, and the outcome window proves itself mature

screening What is "future leakage" here, and why is it the cardinal sin?

Future leakage is letting any value observed after the pivot influence the cohort, the features, or the outcome — for example reading a patient's current program when the decision was made under a different one, or scoring an outcome before the follow-up window has actually elapsed. It is the cardinal sin because it makes the system look prescient: it silently borrows hindsight and reports it as foresight. Every as-of control in the design exists to make that structurally impossible rather than merely discouraged.

deep How does the cohort screen avoid current-state leakage in its filters?

Stage A reads the frozen snapshot and the ungated observation projection as of a pinned instant, never the current graph's property maps. Each cohort row persists its feature-as-of timestamp, its source event sequence, and the snapshot manifest hash, so on replay you can prove exactly which spine events fed the row and that none of them post-date the pivot. There is no path in Stage A that reads a live "latest" value.

deep How is the 90-day outcome window kept honest against immature follow-up?

The shared outcome labeller emits a status, not a bare 0/1: an outcome counts as mature only when the pivot plus 90 days is at or before the snapshot's as-of; otherwise it is marked not-mature or missing and excluded from the rate. Patients who drop out, switch programs, or transition phase before day 90 are censored via a Kaplan-Meier treatment rather than counted as failures. Without this, a cohort assembled near the as-of edge would silently score half its patients as failures simply because their window had not elapsed.

deep Why is the pivot the end of the overlap window rather than any single event?

The pivot is set to the window end — the latest of the three qualifying event times — so that "what followed" is measured strictly after all three cross-domain signals are present. Anchoring on an earlier event would let one of the three qualifying signals fall inside the outcome window, which would leak a defining feature into the thing you are trying to measure. Pinning the pivot at the window end, and persisting it with the rule version, makes the before/after boundary unambiguous and reproducible.

deep How do you stop a later care decision from stealing credit for an outcome?

The outcome labeller records an attribution decision id, the attribution rule version, and the distance in days between the attributed decision and the outcome, using a nearest-decision-before-outcome rule. When a later decision would otherwise claim the same outcome, the status records that it was censored by a later decision instead of being double-counted. This makes attribution a first-class, auditable field rather than an implicit "whatever happened closest."

adversarial The adherence filter is "first 30 days," but you evaluate at an arbitrary as-of — doesn't that mix time frames?

No, because the two clocks are separate and both pinned. "First 30 days" is enrollment-relative and computed from the patient's immutable enrollment events as of the snapshot, so it always refers to the same real early window regardless of when you evaluate. The 90-day overlap window and its pivot are a different, event-time axis. Each row persists both the enrollment-day basis and the window bounds, so there is no ambiguity about which clock any predicate was measured on.

adversarial If the snapshot is already deterministic, why persist a source event sequence on every row?

Determinism tells you the build is reproducible; the per-row source event sequence tells you which spine events any given row depends on. That dependency record is what lets a correction invalidate exactly the rows it touches instead of forcing a full rebuild, and it is what lets an auditor confirm on replay that a row's freshness matches its claim. Determinism and provenance answer different questions, so the design keeps both.

deep What does "the same corpus yields the same cohort" actually guarantee?

It guarantees that the snapshot exporter is order-independent: replaying the same spine in shuffled order, with late arrivals, corrections, and supersede chains, produces a byte-identical snapshot at the same as-of. Because the cohort is built deterministically on top of that snapshot with a pinned pivot rule, two runs produce the same patients and the same pivots. The consequence is that a downstream query cannot silently produce a slightly different cohort, and a regression fixture can assert exact reproduction.

Theme 04 Tenancy and isolation

A three-field tenant scope, a five-field identity key, and a filter that runs before the data loads

screening What is the tenant scope for this query?

The tenant scope is three fields — account, workspace, and control-unit — resolved from the caller's authenticated context, not passed as a parameter. Every parquet partition, every scan predicate, and every runtime assertion is keyed on all three together. There is no single-field tenant check anywhere in the path; a request is scoped by the full triple or it is denied.

screening How is the tenant scope different from the cross-domain identity key?

The tenant scope isolates whose data you may touch; the identity key decides which rows are the same person. The cross-domain identity key is the three tenant fields plus enrollment and patient — five fields — and the three domains are joined only on that full key. Using the tenant triple as part of the join key is what prevents a finance row for one patient from joining an adherence row for a different patient who happens to share a looser identifier.

deep Where is the tenant filter enforced in Stage A?

The tenant predicate is pushed into the columnar scan, so rows outside the caller's scope are never loaded into memory in the first place. The cohort and metric tables are physically partitioned by tenant, snapshot hash, and pivot rule, so a query reads only the caller's partition. After the join, a runtime assertion checks every emitted row against the caller's scope and fails closed on any mismatch. Filter-at-scan plus partition plus fail-closed assertion is belt-and-braces rather than a single predicate anyone could forget.

deep Why is cross-domain identity collision more dangerous here than in a single-domain query?

A single-domain query joins within one system's identifiers; this query joins finance, adherence, and operations records, three subsystems that historically used their own keys. A loose join across them can mix the wrong people even within a tenant, and across tenants it becomes a cross-tenant data exposure — a reportable incident, not a bug. Because there are three domains rather than one, the surface area for a wrong join is multiplied, which is why the identity key is the full five-field tuple and is applied at the join, not after it.

deep What is enforced inline, and what did the shared platform layer take over, for isolation?

Inline, the design already carries the filter-at-scan, the tenant partitioning, and the per-row fail-closed assertion, so isolation does not depend on a per-author predicate. The shared traversal-source wrapper that injects the tenant scope as a single enforced choke point across every query in the programme has since landed, and this query now binds to it — so its isolation is centrally enforced rather than replicated inline, closing what had been a maintenance risk rather than a correctness hole.

deep How is the per-patient drill-in kept tenant-scoped once it leaves this service?

The drill-in delegates to the chain service with the caller's tenant context, and that service enforces its own tenant-scoped traversal and a response-time assertion on every vertex and edge it returns. Any cache the drill-in uses is account-prefixed. So the tenant scope travels with the request across the service boundary rather than being dropped at it, and a chain artifact that came back with a foreign tenant would fail the assertion before it reached the reviewer.

adversarial A single forgotten predicate leaks a tenant — how is discipline made structural rather than per-query?

The design deliberately does not rely on every author remembering the predicate. Three independent controls have to all fail for a leak: the scan-time filter, the physical partitioning, and the fail-closed row assertion, with a page-out on any cross-tenant row. That is real defense in depth, and it is honest to say the gap the design review named was that these were enforced inline per query rather than by one shared wrapper. That wrapper has since landed at the platform layer, and this query now binds to the shared version, so isolation is one enforced choke point rather than a predicate each author must remember.

adversarial Could the ungated observation projection expose cross-tenant signals?

No — "ungated" refers only to the Decision-attachment gate, not to tenancy. The observation projection is ungated in the sense that a finance, adherence, or ops event is visible even when no care decision references it, which the cohort needs for completeness. The tenant predicate still runs at the scan over that projection, exactly as it does over the vertex and edge files. The two gates are orthogonal, and only the Decision gate is relaxed.

Theme 05 The core mechanism: cross-domain orchestration

An overlap-window predicate that rejects rows, and a state graph where a hallucinated patient cannot get in

screening What is the overlap-window predicate?

It is the rule that every qualifying patient's three events — the refund exception, the early-adherence failure, and the escalated complaint — must fall inside a single 90-day window. The window start and end are the earliest and latest of the three event times; the pivot is the window end; and the rule version is pinned. A row whose three events span more than 90 days fails the predicate and is rejected outright — the window is never stretched to force a match.

screening What does the orchestrator look like structurally?

It is a declared, deterministic state graph: the nodes and edges are fixed code, the state passed between nodes is typed, and the only place a language model may appear is inside a single node body. Nothing about the control flow is decided by a model. The graph is a compiled artifact the in-VPC runtime serves directly, which is why it can be run and audited edge by edge rather than inferred from a prompt.

deep What are the orchestration primitives, expressed as graph structure?

Five. Parallel blind finders — three finder personas for finance, adherence, and ops fan out and run concurrently, each blind to the others. A barrier — a fan-in node that runs once, after all three return, and intersects their candidate id sets through the overlap-window predicate. A pipeline drill-in — each matched patient's chain is built independently. A gated reduce — the matched-control comparison and clustered bootstrap run behind power and balance gates. And a synthesis loop — an optional narration node inside a real citation-validation cycle. Each primitive is graph structure, not a prompt instruction.

deep Why are the finder agents blind to each other?

Each finder is a narrow persona that only proposes candidate patient ids for its own domain, and keeping it blind stops one domain's results from priming another's — the intersection has to be earned at the barrier, not negotiated between agents. Blindness also makes the finders independently cacheable and independently testable. The coordination that matters happens deterministically at the barrier, not inside any agent.

deep A finder could hallucinate a patient — how is that contained?

The barrier does more than wait: it re-verifies every proposed patient id against the frozen snapshot and the overlap-window predicate before that id is allowed to count. A finder can only ever propose; it can never admit. So a finder that invents a patient, or proposes one whose three events do not actually satisfy the window, is silently dropped at the barrier and never enters the cohort. This is the structural reason a language model at the edge cannot corrupt the numbers.

deep Why intersect at a single barrier rather than letting finders coordinate?

A single fan-in node makes the intersection one deterministic operation over three id sets, with one place to re-verify and one place to audit. If the finders coordinated among themselves, the cohort would depend on the order and content of inter-agent messages, which is neither reproducible nor reviewable. The barrier also enforces a happens-before: nothing downstream — no drill-in, no statistics — starts until the intersection exists, so there is no partial cohort leaking into the reduce.

deep How is the pivot chosen and pinned, and why does that choice matter downstream?

The pivot is the window end — the latest of the three qualifying event times — and it is persisted per row alongside the window start, the window end, and the pivot rule version. Everything downstream keys off it: the outcome window is pivot to pivot plus 90 days, the maturity gate is pivot plus 90 days at or before the as-of, and the drill-in passes that exact window to the chain service. Pinning the pivot and its rule version is what makes two runs of the same cohort measure outcomes over the identical interval.

deep Why match with a shared matcher binary rather than this query's own?

Several sibling cohort queries run propensity matching, and if each shipped its own the seed, caliper, replacement policy, and covariate hash would drift, producing divergent matched cohorts and divergent confidence intervals for the same patients over time. The shared matcher pins those choices and emits a match manifest id over the covariate schema, snapshot, caliper, replacement policy, seed, and cohort filter. A cell keyed on that manifest id is reproducible and comparable across queries; a private matcher would make cross-query reuse claims unverifiable.

adversarial Isn't "multi-agent" overkill for what is fundamentally a three-way set intersection?

Fairly asked, and the honest answer is that the intersection itself does not need agents — it is deterministic code. The finders are cheap-tier personas that propose candidates per domain; you could replace them with plain scans and the numbers would be identical, because the barrier re-verifies everything against the snapshot anyway. What the graph buys is the disciplined structure — fan-out, barrier, gated reduce, and a bounded synthesis loop — expressed as auditable code with typed channels, and a runnable work sample. The value is the orchestration discipline, not the agents doing arithmetic, which they never do.

adversarial Why express this as a framework state graph instead of a plain batch DAG?

The state graph reuses the platform's existing orchestration runtime, gives every channel a typed, validated contract, and expresses the one genuine cycle — the synthesis reject-and-re-prompt loop — as a first-class conditional edge rather than ad-hoc retry code. It is also the concrete runnable artifact a reviewer can run in CI with no keys. A plain batch DAG would work for the joins and stats, and in fact those parts are effectively a DAG; the graph framework earns its place at the barrier, the gated reduce, and the feedback cycle.

Theme 06 Statistics, gates and suppression

Two gates decide whether a number is allowed to exist, and suppression beats a caveated delta

screening What two gates decide whether a cross-domain delta is shown?

First a power gate: the matched-pair count, the effective sample size, and the confidence-interval width must all clear their thresholds. Then a balance gate: every covariate's standardised mean difference must sit under the gate and the sensitivity bounds must hold. Both run deterministically, in order, before any delta reaches a screen. If either fails, the delta does not exist for that cell.

screening What happens when a gate fails?

The cell returns descriptive facts only: the cohort count, the matched count, the full denominator breakdown, and — for a balance failure — the failed-balance diagnostics. No causal-looking delta is emitted. The headline number is withheld and labelled with the reason it was withheld, so a reader sees "not enough evidence" rather than a confident figure with fine print.

deep Why is balance-fail "suppress" rather than "tag the delta and return the raw number"?

A raw delta from an unmatched cohort looks exactly like a causal effect, and a tag underneath it is read past — the number anchors the reader before the caveat registers. The design review explicitly rejected returning a raw delta on failed balance for precisely this reason. Suppression is the only responsible default: if the arms are not comparable, the honest output is that no comparison can be made, not a comparison with a warning label. The cost is a blank headline; the benefit is that the system never publishes a misleading effect.

deep What is the estimand, and why pre-register it?

The estimand is an observational, matched-cohort comparison of the 90-day outcome for the cross-domain cohort versus a propensity-matched control, with the covariate list and matched-control eligibility fixed in advance. Pre-registering it prevents the most common way these analyses mislead: choosing covariates, calipers, or an as-of after seeing which combination produces a striking delta. Because the estimand and its parameters are pinned and versioned, the number is a property of the design, not of the analyst who ran it.

deep Which covariates are matched, and why those?

Baseline clinical severity, program, tenure, demographics, prior utilization, prior adherence, refund and complaint history, and engagement. These are the axes on which a help-seeking, high-friction cohort predictably differs from an average patient, so they are the confounders most likely to drive an outcome gap that has nothing to do with the care decisions. Matching on them narrows the comparison to patients who looked similar before the cross-domain episode — but only on these measured axes, which is the crucial limit.

deep What do the sensitivity bounds add beyond the SMD balance check?

Standardised mean difference only tells you the measured covariates are balanced; it says nothing about a confounder you did not measure. The sensitivity bounds — a Rosenbaum-style gamma and an E-value — quantify how strong an unmeasured confounder would have to be to explain away the observed delta. If a modest unmeasured confounder could erase it, the bound is tight and the result is not robust. This is how the design keeps an observational number honest about the confounding it cannot rule out.

deep Why is small-N treated as statistical reliability rather than only a privacy floor?

A privacy N-floor asks "are there enough people that I might identify one"; a reliability floor asks "are there enough people that this number means anything." Those are different questions, and for this cohort the second bites first — a confident delta from a handful of unusually complex patients is exactly the failure this query would otherwise produce. So the headline carries matched-pair N, effective N, and CI width, and the cell goes descriptive-only when those fail their thresholds, independent of the separate privacy floor.

adversarial Matching makes this sound causal — how do you stop a reader inferring cause?

Every emitted surface carries observational, non-causal labels, and the follow-up histogram is labelled "observed follow-up decisions," never "recommended action," so no emitted surface presents itself as "do this to get that outcome." Labelling constrains the causal misreading; it does not make it impossible, and the design does not claim otherwise. The design is explicit that matching can only balance what was measured, so unmeasured confounding is never ruled out. Nothing here is presented as a causal claim or a service-quality score, and the wording is governed by Policy and Clinical Ops rather than left to a chart title.

adversarial Bootstrap CIs on tiny cohorts — aren't they close to meaningless?

On a tiny cohort the CI is wide, and that is the point: the CI width is itself one of the power-gate thresholds, so a cohort too small to say anything produces a CI too wide to pass, and the cell goes descriptive-only. The bootstrap clusters by enrollment rather than by row, so a single patient's repeated events cannot masquerade as independent evidence and artificially narrow the interval. A wide interval is not a bug to hide; it is the signal that routes the cell away from a headline delta.

adversarial Could a motivated analyst pick an as-of that makes the delta emit?

The metric is a nightly, reproducible cell built under a pinned pivot rule and pre-registered estimand, not an ad-hoc query an analyst re-runs with different knobs, so cherry-picking would be visible in the version pins and the deterministic build. The gates are deterministic functions of the data, so an as-of that produced a balanced, well-powered cohort would be exactly the case where a delta is defensible. What the design forecloses is choosing covariates or thresholds after the fact — those are fixed and versioned, and any change to them bumps a hash that the cache key records.

Theme 07 Caching, determinism and versioning

The cache key that makes a hit safe, and where determinism does and does not come from

screening What is in the canonical cache key?

A canonical sorted key over the tenant triple, a query namespace, the endpoint, the filters, and every input that can change the number: the metric schema hash, the match manifest id, the outcome-library version, the bootstrap-library version, the snapshot manifest hash, the source-event-sequence high-water mark, the correction epoch, and the pivot rule version. If any of those differs, it is a different key and a different cell.

screening Why isn't a TTL enough for freshness?

A TTL only bounds how old a value can be by wall-clock time; it says nothing about whether the underlying data changed. A correction can land two minutes after a cell is cached, making a one-hour TTL serve a wrong number for 58 minutes. Freshness here comes from the source-event-sequence watermark and the correction epoch in the key, plus event-driven invalidation — the cell is evicted when its inputs change, not when a timer expires.

deep Why does the cache key include library version pins?

Because the outcome labeller and the bootstrap both shape the number, upgrading either changes the answer for the same data. The matcher is the honest exception: it enters only through the match manifest id, and that manifest hashes the matcher's inputs, not its code — so a matcher upgrade does not by itself mint a new key here. It is traced through the matcher version stamped on the result, not keyed on. KQ3's key, which pins the matcher binary version as its own field, is the stronger form; this one does not claim to be. If the key dropped those versions, a cache hit computed by last month's bootstrap would be served as if it were current, and two deploys would silently disagree. Pinning the library versions means an upgrade produces a fresh key and a recompute rather than a stale hit, and it makes any served number traceable to the exact code that produced it.

deep How does a correction invalidate exactly the right cache entries?

Every derived artifact — cohort rows, metric cells, and the per-patient drill-in cache — records the range of source event sequences it depends on in a dependency index. When a correction lands for a finance, adherence, ops, decision, or outcome event, the listener maps that event to the slices whose dependency ranges include it and rebuilds only those, targeting a P95 under five minutes. A full nightly recompute would be both too slow and unnecessary; the dependency index is what makes the rebuild surgical.

deep Where does determinism in this system actually come from?

From three deterministic sources: the snapshot exporter, which produces byte-identical output under shuffled replay; the structured single-patient chain, reconstructed deterministically from the spine; and the seeded bootstrap. None of it comes from the language model. The through-line of the whole design is that the deterministic core owns every number, and the model is confined to the edges where a non-deterministic output costs a sentence, never a figure.

deep The bootstrap is random — how is it reproducible?

Its seed is derived from the snapshot manifest hash, so the same snapshot always drives the same resamples; the resample count is fixed at 2000, the estimator is percentile by default, ties are handled deterministically, and resampling clusters by enrollment. Given the same snapshot and the same library version, the interval is identical on every run. That is what lets the golden regression assert byte-for-byte reproduction of the statistics, not just approximate agreement.

deep Two feedback loops share the word "loop" — how do they differ?

Loop A is deterministic data-plane re-derivation: a correction lands, a dependency index evicts the derived keys that depended on it, and just those are rebuilt — no model is involved. Loop B is model-in-the-loop self-correction: a synthesis narrative cites a node that is not in the chain, a typed validator flags it, and the node re-prompts once with the offending citation flagged; if it still fails, the narrative is suppressed. Same word, two different machines — one re-derives data with code, the other re-prompts a model behind a code gate — and keeping them distinct is deliberate.

adversarial You pin temperature to zero on the narrator — doesn't that make its output deterministic and cacheable like the numbers?

No, and this is a distinction the design is careful about. Pinning the model version, the system prompt, and temperature zero, and hashing the input chain and the output narrative, gives you drift detection and reproducible auditing — you can tell when a narrative changed and re-derive what a given response said. It does not give you generation determinism: a language model can still vary its output, so the hashes detect drift, they do not prevent it. That is exactly why the numbers never flow through the model and why the structured chain, not the narrator, is the authoritative, deterministic artifact.

Theme 08 Historical hydration and what it buys this query

Nine years of finance, adherence, and ops replayed through one spine, on the axis the window is measured on

screening What does roughly nine years of hydrated history buy this query specifically?

It buys the cross-domain window itself. You can only judge whether a refund exception, an adherence collapse, and a complaint fall inside one 90-day pivot if finance, adherence, and operations events all coexist over the same historical span, at the same event-time resolution, under the same tenant scope. Without the hydrated history there is no cross-domain episode to find and the overlap predicate would have almost nothing to admit or reject.

screening Was the history a throwaway backfill?

No — it was hydrated through the same append-only spine that now carries live events, as a first-class, quarantine-first, proof-carrying pipeline. The distinction matters because a throwaway backfill would have its own quirks, and a cross-domain cohort built on a load that behaves differently from the live stream would be measuring the load, not the patients. History and live share one spine and one projection code precisely so the cohort means the same thing across the whole span.

deep Why is "event time is not ingestion time" existential for this query?

The overlap predicate is measured on true event time. If any one domain's event time collapsed to the moment the system ingested it, all of that domain's events would appear to land on load day, the 90-day window would admit everyone or no one, and the cross-domain episode would dissolve into an artefact of the load. So each hydrated record keeps its real-world time, and an invariant asserts that event time falls strictly before received time — the tripwire that catches an event time accidentally defaulted to ingestion time.

deep What proves the hydration is trustworthy rather than merely large?

Five properties. Event time is preserved, not collapsed to load time. The load is idempotent — content-addressed event ids and conditional writes mean hydrating twice yields an identical spine and snapshot hash, an executable proof rather than an assurance. Nothing is silently coerced — unmappable rows are quarantined and counted, never defaulted. History replays through the same projection code that serves live. And the load is proof-carrying — zero-event-loss reconciliation, per-tenant and per-class counts, as-of spot checks, and a golden replay gate the load before anything downstream is built, with a fenced cutover proving no gap or duplicate at the boundary.

deep How does the four-field event-time split apply across three subsystems with different clocks?

Each event class chooses among event time, observed-at, received-at, and written-at explicitly, and the choice is persisted per row. The real-world moment and the source's observed-at come from the record; received and written are pinned to a single load fence rather than the load clock, so they record provenance only. Because finance, adherence, and ops ran on their own clocks, forcing one field for all three would misalign the window; letting each class name its axis and recording that choice is what keeps the overlap comparison meaningful.

deep What invariant catches an accidental ingestion-time default?

An assertion that every historical event's real-world event time falls strictly before the moment the system received it. A row whose event time equals or exceeds its received time almost certainly had its event time defaulted to the ingestion clock, so it is quarantined and counted rather than admitted. This single invariant protects the axis the entire overlap predicate is measured on, which is why it is enforced at load time rather than discovered downstream.

adversarial If nine years were written back in arbitrary order, how is as-of order stable?

As-of order sorts by event time and then source sequence, never by the order rows happened to be written back, so the arbitrary write order is irrelevant. The exporter is proven order-independent by a golden replay that shuffles the spine and asserts an identical snapshot hash. The consequence is that the cohort you build from nine years of back-loaded data is the same cohort you would build if those events had streamed in live in their true order.

adversarial A defaulted tenant on a legacy row — what stops it becoming a cross-tenant leak?

Any row that cannot be mapped to a known schema or to a tenant is quarantined and counted, never defaulted to a placeholder tenant. A defaulted tenant is precisely the cross-tenant leak this query's identity discipline exists to prevent, so the load refuses to guess: an unresolved row is held out and reported rather than admitted under a wrong scope. The quarantine count is itself a monitored signal, so a spike in unmappable rows surfaces rather than silently shrinking a cohort.

Theme 09 Governance, PHI and the LLM boundary

The model writes words, never numbers, and where it runs is a network fact, not a contract

screening Where is a language model allowed, and where is it forbidden?

Forbidden in the intersection, in every per-patient chain, in the balance gate, and in every statistic — those are deterministic code. Allowed only at two edges: the cheap-tier finder personas that propose candidate patients per domain, and an optional synthesis narrator that puts words to a single patient's chain the code already assembled. This is the portfolio's sharpest case of knowing when not to use a model — it lives at the edges, gated, and never touches a number.

screening What is the RBAC split, and when is the audit entry written?

Cohort aggregates are served to an exec or ops role; the per-patient drill-in is narrowed to clinical or ops need-to-know with a stated purpose. The audit entry — viewer, target patient, purpose, tenant, as-of, and endpoint — is written before the response is served, not after, and the access log id is returned in the payload. Writing the audit first means an access is recorded even if the serving step then fails.

deep Where does the narrator run, and how does PHI stay contained?

The production path is single and explicit: the narrator runs on a self-hosted Llama 3.1 model inside the VPC, served through the native local runtime, so patient data never leaves the cluster and there is no vendor SDK or external egress. An external provider is permitted only for a de-identified payload — never for raw clinical text. Keeping the one narrative call in-VPC is what lets the rest of the governance story be about content rather than about trusting a third party with PHI.

deep Isn't a business-associate agreement with a no-retention clause equivalent to running in-VPC?

No, and conflating the two would be a real error. A contractual no-retention agreement is a legal promise about what a provider does after it receives the data; it is not a network-residency control and does not stop clinical text from crossing the network to that provider in the first place. The design treats network residency as the primary control — the payload stays in-VPC — and only permits an external provider when the payload has been de-identified. The two are not interchangeable options that both keep clinical text on the network; only self-hosting does that.

deep How does the self-hosted cascade route between the small and large models?

A pre-hoc classifier sends easy calls to the smaller model, boosted with structured output, a few-shot prompt, and self-consistency; a post-hoc confidence gate escalates the uncertain tail to the larger model. Most traffic clears on the small model and only the flagged tail escalates, and every routing decision is auditable code rather than a hidden prompt. The routing is a cost and latency optimization that never changes the guardrails — both tiers run in-VPC and both are held to the same citation validation.

deep How is the narrator prevented from stating anything the code didn't compute?

A typed claim grammar decomposes every factual sentence into allowed templates backed by cited chain fields and cited edges; any sentence that cannot decompose, or that cites a node not present in the chain, is rejected. A rejected narrative re-prompts exactly once with the offending citation flagged, and if it fails again the narrative is suppressed with failure metadata while the chain still ships. Because the numbers are computed by code and the model only narrates a chain it is handed, there is no path for the model to introduce a figure.

adversarial Doesn't a table of "patients who complained and missed doses" become a service-quality scorecard the moment it exists?

The risk is real, and the honest framing is that the design makes that misuse hard and visible rather than claiming it is impossible. The controls are concrete: the cohort is labelled neutrally as patients with finance, adherence, and ops friction signals; the histogram key is "observed follow-up decisions," never "recommended action"; the delta is suppressed unless it survives the balance and power gates; the drill-in is audit-first and need-to-know; and the UI wording is governed by Policy and Clinical Ops. These are mitigations that make a scorecard reading harder to reach and easy to catch — not an absolute guarantee that no one could ever misread the table.

adversarial The narrative is off by default — is any of the LLM story actually live, then?

The finders and the orchestrator are live; the synthesis narrator is gated on the shared narrative-guardrail contract and ships off by default until that contract is signed. That is deliberate honesty, not a hedge: the words that describe a patient's care are the one place a model could mislead, so they wait for the field-level claim grammar and PHI deployment to be settled at the platform level. When the narrator is on it runs behind the full guardrail, and either way every number is already computed and served by code.

adversarial If the numbers are code, what is the residual risk of the narrator hallucinating?

A confidently ungrounded sentence can actively mislead care even when the numbers beside it are correct, because a reader trusts the prose. That is why citation faithfulness is a hard gate — every claim must resolve to a real chain node, and the evaluation carries a deliberate fabricated-citation negative control the harness must catch. The residual risk is bounded to a suppressed sentence, never a wrong number or a bypassed gate, because a failed validation suppresses the narrative while the structured chain ships regardless.

Theme 10 Failure modes, degradation and operations

What pages someone at 3am, what degrades quietly, and how a correction propagates in minutes

screening What pages someone at 3am versus what degrades quietly?

A cross-tenant row or a partition mismatch pages immediately — that is an incident, not a metric, and the target is zero. Staleness degrades quietly with a surfaced tier: a slow projector or a missed nightly build shows yesterday's cohort with an honest as-of and a degradation status rather than an outage. The dividing line is isolation failures escalate hard; freshness failures degrade visibly.

screening What operational signals are monitored before the exec dashboard ships?

Refresh lag, suppression rate, failed-balance rate, cohort churn week over week, correction rebuild counts, live-walk latency when the narrator is on, and drill-in audit volume. These are required before an exec-facing dashboard is allowed to ship, because a cross-domain metric that quietly suppresses most cells, or churns its cohort weekly, is misleading in a way no single response reveals. The observability is how you tell a healthy quiet from a broken one.

screening What does a response tell a user about freshness?

Every response carries the snapshot's as-of, its manifest hash, and its source-event-sequence high-water mark; a per-patient drill-in additionally carries the live walk's as-of, its last event sequence, the projector lag in milliseconds, and a degradation status. So a reader can always see how fresh the cohort is and, for a drill-in, how far behind the live projection is running. Freshness is a field in the payload, not an assumption.

deep What is the degradation behavior when the snapshot is fresh but the live chain is slow?

Because the two stages read different sources, they degrade independently. Cohort numbers keep serving from the fresh snapshot at full confidence, while the per-patient drill-in surfaces the projector lag and a degraded status so a reviewer knows the live chain is running behind. The design documents the snapshot SLA and the live SLA separately precisely so a slow live path never silently taints a cohort number that did not depend on it.

deep A patient qualifies today but tonight's cohort hasn't run — is that a bug?

No — snapshot-only Stage A is a nightly metric by construction, so a newly qualifying patient appears in the next build, and the as-of metadata makes the boundary explicit. Treating this as a bug would push toward live cohort reads, which would sacrifice determinism and leak current state. The accepted contract is a nightly cadence with an honest timestamp, which is the right tradeoff for a governed analytical surface rather than a real-time alert.

deep How fast does a correction propagate, and why targeted rather than a full rebuild?

A correction listener maps each spine correction to the metric and cohort slices whose dependency ranges include it and rebuilds only those, targeting a P95 under five minutes. A full overnight recompute would be too slow — a late refund reversal would keep serving a wrong cell until the next night — and it would rebuild vast amounts of unaffected data. The dependency index is what makes the rebuild surgical, so a single correction refreshes in minutes rather than waiting for, or forcing, a full recompute.

deep What is the last valid dependency when a correction arrives mid-serve?

Reads stay consistent because the correction epoch is part of the cache key: a cell served before the rebuild carries the old epoch, and once the targeted rebuild lands, the new epoch produces a distinct key. So a request either reads the pre-correction cell or the rebuilt one, never a half-updated blend, and the epoch on the response tells a reader which world their number came from. The invalidation is atomic at the granularity of a keyed cell rather than a global flush.

adversarial What happens if the narrator model is down at request time?

It fails quiet: a model outage costs a sentence of prose, never a number, a gate, or a control. The cohort and the chain are served exactly as before, and the observational labels and neutrality rules are deterministic UI copy that never passed through the model, so they are unaffected. Because the narrator is optional and off the request's critical path, its availability has no bearing on whether the answer ships.

adversarial The suppression rate spikes — is the query broken, or is the world?

You cannot tell from a single response, which is exactly why suppression rate and failed-balance rate are first-class monitored signals rather than silent outcomes. A spike could be genuine — cohorts that really are too small or too imbalanced to compare — or it could be a covariate or schema regression that is breaking the balance check. The design's stance is to surface the spike and investigate rather than to relax a gate to make cells emit, because an emitted-but-wrong delta is the worse failure.

Theme 11 Evaluation and acceptance criteria

Per stage, which metric binds, proven on synthetic cohorts where the ground truth is known by construction

screening How is a regulated surface evaluated beyond "the model seems good"?

Evaluation names, per stage, the single binding metric and proves the golden set clears its bar — on seeded synthetic cohorts where the ground truth of every gate decision and every citation is known by construction. "The model seems good" is not admissible on a care surface, so each stage has a specific, checkable acceptance target rather than a vibe. These are pre-registered acceptance targets the system was designed and reviewed against, not measured production results.

screening What is the citation-faithfulness bar?

It is a hard gate at 1.0: every claim in a generated narrative must cite a real chain node, with no exceptions. The golden set carries a deliberate fabricated-citation negative control that the validation harness must catch, so the bar tests both that true citations pass and that a planted false one fails. A confidently ungrounded citation on a care surface actively misleads, which is why this is the one metric that admits no tolerance.

deep Why grade Stage A on recall but the gates on precision?

For Stage A, the failure that matters most is silently missing a qualifying patient, so the binding metric is recall against the seeded cross-domain cohort — the screen must recover the patients it was built to find. For the balance and power gates, a false emit is far worse than a false suppress, because emitting a delta that should have been withheld puts a misleading number on screen, so the gates are graded precision-dominant. The asymmetry reflects which mistake does real harm at each stage.

deep What is in the golden regression fixture set?

Seeded fixtures for late arrivals, corrections, supersede chains, and events near the 90-day window boundary, which must reproduce identical results across rebuilds. These are the four situations where a determinism or boundary bug hides, so the regression is not a smoke test but a targeted assault on the exact edges the design claims to handle. Byte-for-byte reproduction, not approximate agreement, is the pass condition.

deep Why test events specifically near the 90-day boundary?

The overlap predicate is a hard cutoff, so a patient whose three events span exactly 90 days versus 91 days is the difference between inclusion and exclusion. Boundary events are where an off-by-one, a rounding choice, or a pivot-rule change silently flips a patient's membership, changing the cohort without any visible error. Fixing fixtures right at the boundary is what proves the predicate and its rule version behave identically on every rebuild.

deep Is tenant safety a graded metric or a gate?

A gate with a target of zero and a page-out on any violation — a cross-tenant row or a partition mismatch is an incident, not a number you tolerate above some threshold. It is deliberately not expressed as a rate you try to minimize, because "we leak one tenant in a thousand" is not an acceptable posture for PHI. The evaluation treats it categorically: zero, or it fails.

adversarial These are pre-registered targets, not measured results — why should a client trust them?

Because they are the bars the system was designed, reviewed, and deployed against, and because publishing them as targets rather than as measured wins is itself the trust signal. The determinism, recall, precision, and faithfulness properties are proven on synthetic ground truth where the correct answer is known by construction, which is a genuine engineering claim. The observed production numbers belong to the client and are not mine to publish, so this page states targets and review outcomes and is explicit that no measured metric appears on it.

adversarial Synthetic golden cohorts — do they prove anything about real patients?

They prove the machinery behaves correctly — that the screen recovers a seeded cohort, that the gates emit only when they should, that citations are faithful, and that rebuilds are deterministic — because those are properties you can only test where the ground truth is known. What they deliberately do not claim is a real-world effect size; the design never publishes one, and the observational labels and suppression gates exist precisely because a real effect size on this cohort would be confounded. The synthetic evaluation validates the honesty of the pipeline, not a clinical conclusion.

Theme 12 Tradeoffs, alternatives, and what I would change

The verdict was ship-with-changes, and the honest boundaries of what this query can claim

screening What was the design-review verdict, honestly?

Ship-with-changes. Under a structured adversarial self-review this query — the most exposed in the whole system — drew the most blood: twenty findings across correctness, statistics, governance, and operations, with an unambiguous "do not implement as written." Those twenty query-local findings were absorbed directly into the v2 design body. It was never marked approved, and it is not approved now.

deep Why snapshot-only Stage A instead of a live cross-domain graph walk?

A live cross-domain walk would be non-deterministic, expensive at cohort scale, and prone to leaking current state into a historical cohort. Snapshot-only buys reproducibility, cost, and leakage-safety; the price is nightly latency and the fact that a patient who qualifies today is not in the cohort until the next build. For a governed analytical metric that tradeoff is correct — the honesty of a reproducible number outweighs the freshness of a live one — and the freshness gap is surfaced rather than hidden.

deep Why suppress a delta rather than show it with a caveat?

Because a caveat under a number is read past — the figure anchors the reader before the warning registers, and on a care surface that is dangerous. When the arms are not comparable, the only responsible output is that no comparison can be made, so the delta is withheld and the reason is stated in its place. The cost is a blank headline that some readers will find unsatisfying; the benefit is that the system never ships a confident number it cannot stand behind.

deep Why reuse shared statistical libraries instead of query-local ones?

One outcome labeller, one propensity matcher with versioned manifests, and one seeded clustered bootstrap, reused across every cohort query, mean the same cohort produces the same number in every query and that number is reproducible and auditable. Query-local copies would drift — different seeds, calipers, and CI methods — and make any cross-query reuse claim unverifiable. The tradeoff is coordination cost and a dependency on those libraries landing at the platform level — they since have, and the query binds to the shared versions. The one dependency still open is the narrative-guardrail contract.

deep Why keep the narrator off by default rather than shipping it behind a feature flag?

Because its guardrail contract — the field-level claim grammar, the PHI deployment, the citation validation — is still being settled at the platform level, and shipping words that describe a patient's care before that contract is signed would be the one place a model could mislead on a care surface. Off-by-default is stronger than a flag an operator could flip prematurely: the narrator is simply not eligible until the contract is live. The numbers ship regardless, so nothing of value waits on the narrator.

adversarial What can this query simply not answer today?

It cannot make a causal claim, it cannot serve as a service-quality score, and it cannot say anything about a patient who newly qualifies inside the nightly gap. On small or imbalanced cohorts it often returns descriptive facts only, with no delta at all. And even when a delta survives both gates, unmeasured confounding is never ruled out — matching balances only the covariates that were measured, so the result is an observational summary, not a demonstrated effect.

adversarial The shared components landed — so what still blocks full approval?

The cross-cutting dependencies this query relies on have since landed at the platform layer: the tenant wrapper, the outcome and bootstrap libraries, the correction backbone, the deterministic exporter proof, the four-field event-time schema, the ungated observation projection, the metric-cache-key contract, and the deterministic single-patient chain artifact this query's drill-in delegates to — the query now binds to the shared versions. Two things still hold it at ship-with-changes: the shared narrative-guardrail contract is unsigned, so the synthesis narrator ships off by default; and the finance, adherence, and ops upstream teams must emit schema-versioned events on the agreed time axis. The runnable orchestrator also faces the same hiring-CTO review loop the precedent-search work sample went through — and landing the components does not by itself move the verdict, which remains a review outcome.

adversarial If you rebuilt it, what would you prove first or change?

I would want the deterministic exporter's golden replay and the ungated observation projection proven at the platform level before trusting cohort completeness, because a biased projection or a non-deterministic export would quietly poison every cohort downstream. I would want the narrative-guardrail contract signed before any words ship. And I would calibrate the effective-N and CI-width thresholds against the real distribution of cohort sizes rather than assumed values, since those thresholds decide how often the query can say anything at all — and getting them wrong either floods the surface with under-powered deltas or suppresses everything.

Honest gaps

The verdict of record for this query is ship-with-changes, absorbed into a v2 design — not approved. Across the ten-query programme only the precedent-search query ever reached approved-with-nits; this one, the most exposed in the system, drew a twenty-finding adversarial review with an explicit "do not implement as written," and the twenty query-local findings were folded into v2. Its cross-cutting dependencies were promoted into shared platform components so every sibling query inherits the same fixes — those components have since landed, and the query now binds to the shared versions rather than its own inlined copies.

The shared backbone, now landed — and the one contract still open:

  • the shared tenant-isolation wrapper — isolation is now enforced through one central choke point, not replicated inline;
  • the shared outcome labeller, propensity matcher, and clustered bootstrap libraries;
  • the correction-event backbone and the deterministic exporter's golden-replay proof;
  • the four-field event-time schema and the ungated observation projection that cohort completeness relies on;
  • the deterministic single-patient chain artifact this query's per-patient drill-in delegates to;
  • still open: the shared narrative-guardrail contract — until it is signed, the synthesis narrator ships off by default.

What the query cannot do even when everything is green: it makes no causal claim and is never a service-quality score; it is silent on patients who qualify inside the nightly gap; it frequently returns descriptive facts only on small or imbalanced cohorts; and it never rules out unmeasured confounding, because matching balances only the covariates that were measured. The determinism it offers comes from the structured chain and the seeded pipeline, not from the language model — pinned model, prompt, and temperature give drift detection and reproducible auditing, not generation determinism, so the hashes detect drift, they do not prevent it.
