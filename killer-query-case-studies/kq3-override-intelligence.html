Case study · ML system design · Healthcare

When a clinician overrides the protocol — what happens next?

For a chronic-care platform covering 80+ conditions, I designed the override-analytics system — the most politically charged question in the product — and took it to production. Every number a regulator could audit is computed by deterministic, seeded, replayable code; a language model appears only after the statistical gates pass, to narrate numbers it is handed. The same discipline applies anywhere "who deviated, and did it work?" must be answered without becoming a blame exercise: underwriting exceptions, incident postmortems, fraud-ops overrides.

01 The problem

One GROUP BY away from a scorecard

An override rate per clinician is one GROUP BY away — and it would be wrong three ways at once. Clinicians override on their sickest, most unusual patients, so a naive override-vs-followed comparison is confounded by design. Small cohorts produce confident-looking rates from a handful of cases. And the moment the dashboard exists, an override table reads as a scorecard — even a statistically sound one can end careers if it is presented as a ranking rather than an observation.

So the design treats this as three first-class problems, each with a named control:

  • Confounding — every override is compared only against a propensity-matched control built from similar patients on the same policy family, and the comparison is suppressed if the match is not demonstrably balanced. Matching can only balance what is measured — unmeasured confounding is never ruled out, which is exactly why every surface stays observational rather than causal.
  • False confidence — under-powered cells and caliper-fragile findings are suppressed with reasons and full denominators, never silently dropped and never headlined.
  • Scorecard misuse — neutral codes in the data layer, pseudonymous actors, alphabetic never-rankable ordering, role-based views, and an audit entry for every actor-level look. Every surface says observational, not causal.
02 The architecture

Snapshot-only analytics with a matched control arm

The pipeline never touches the live graph at query time. A nightly snapshot feeds a cohort builder that reconstructs every override as of the moment it happened — alignment, rationale, actor, and the exact policy version — by replaying the append-only event spine. A propensity matcher pairs each override with the most similar non-overridden decision, and everything downstream is pre-computed into columnar metric tables served by an embedded analytical engine.

The match manifest

Every matched cohort ships with a versioned match manifest — a hash over the covariate schema, snapshot manifest, cohort filters, caliper, replacement policy, seed, and tie-break rule. A downstream query that needs the same control arm either reuses the manifest verbatim or rebuilds it with the same matcher binary — it can never silently produce a slightly different control group. Two dashboards disagreeing about the same cohort is a bug class this removes outright.

The manifest hash covers the matcher's inputs, not the matcher itself, and not the statistical plan applied afterwards. So matcher_binary_version and stat_plan_version are pinned as their own fields in the canonical cache key rather than assumed to ride along inside the manifest. A matcher rebuilt from identical inputs by a different binary, or the same cohort re-summarised under a revised bootstrap plan, is a different result and must miss the cache — otherwise a cached delta could outlive the cohort it was computed from.

The pipeline above hides an ordering that turns out to matter. Each override is reconstructed, labelled, and matched through a fixed chain of hand-offs — and the versioned match manifest is minted at exactly one step, by exactly one binary. Drawing the hand-offs as a sequence makes that single point of truth visible: the covariates are read as of the decision before the outcome is attached, and the manifest is born the moment the controls are written.

That sequence ends by writing matched_control.parquet. It is worth seeing what a matched cohort actually is. The matcher pairs each overridden decision with the single most similar non-overridden one — nearest neighbour on pre-treatment covariates, and only when that neighbour falls inside a caliper. A treated override with no control close enough is not quietly discarded: it is excluded from the estimate and counted, so the denominator stays honest.

Dropping that fourth override silently would bias the whole comparison toward the easy-to-match cases and quietly make overrides look more routine than they are. Counting it — and reporting n_unmatched on every cell — is what keeps the estimate honest about who it could and could not speak for.

03 Hydration

Nine years of overrides, replayed into the spine

This system was not built against an empty world. Roughly nine years of prior decisions — the overrides, the rationales attached to them, and the outcomes that followed — already existed in a private production system, and that history is hydrated through the same append-only spine before any override is ever compared. The platform ships a first-class, proof-carrying hydration pipeline; five properties keep the replayed history trustworthy enough to run statistics on.

  • Event time is not ingestion time. Each legacy record keeps the moment the decision actually happened; separate fields record when the system learned of it. Collapse the two and nine years of overrides would look as though they all occurred on load day — and every as-of reconstruction of what a clinician knew at the moment they deviated would be void. The watermark runs in a hydration mode where a nine-year spread of late-arriving data is normal, not the small live-mode value that would drop almost all of it.
  • Hydrating twice is a no-op. Content-addressed event IDs plus conditional writes make the load idempotent and resumable: re-running the pipeline over the same corpus yields an identical spine and an identical snapshot-manifest hash. That equality is an executable proof, not an assurance.
  • Nothing is silently coerced. Nine years means many legacy payload-schema versions; each gets an explicit upconverter, and any row that cannot be mapped to a schema — or to a tenant — is quarantined and counted rather than defaulted. A defaulted tenant would be a cross-tenant leak straight into the override cohort.
  • The same projection code runs for history and for live. History is replayed through the projection logic that serves the live stream. Otherwise the hydrated graph would be a different product from the streaming one, and every determinism guarantee behind the gate ladder that follows would be theatre. The bulk load writes those years in whatever shard order is fastest — safe only because every as-of reconstruction sorts by event time and source sequence, never by the order rows were written.
  • The load is proof-carrying. Zero-event-loss reconciliation against the source, per-tenant and per-class counts, as-of spot checks against known-good historical reports, and a golden replay over a historical slice all gate the load before a single override cell is computed. A fenced cutover then attaches the live stream with a proof of neither gap nor duplicate at the boundary.

What those nine years buy this query in particular: propensity matching only works when each policy version has accumulated enough overridden decisions — and enough comparable non-overridden controls — to draw balanced pairs from. Hydrated history is what lets a per-cell arm clear the ≥ 30-patient floor and lets the matcher find a control within caliper at an SMD ≤ 0.1, rather than suppressing the comparison for want of matchable support. A cold system would return "insufficient data" almost everywhere; nine years is what turns most override cells from suppressed into estimable.

Those stages exist to protect a single field-level rule, and it is worth drawing on its own because every override comparison rests on it. A decision record carries the moment it actually happened; the load only records when the spine learned of it. Reconstructing what a clinician knew at the instant they deviated — the patient's covariates, the policy version in force — is sound only when history is ordered by event time, never by the order the bulk load happened to write rows.

Collapse event time into load time and all nine years would appear to have happened on load day, voiding every as-of covariate the matcher depends on. The strict event_time < received_at assertion is the tripwire that would catch such a slip before a single override cell was ever computed.

04 The gate ladder

Chain-of-thought as deterministic code

Before any override cell may show a headline number, it climbs four rungs — and the reasoning is not a prompt, it is seeded, replayable code. Failing any rung produces a suppressed cell with a stated reason and full denominators: the dashboard says "insufficient data" or "arms not comparable", never a confident number built on sand, and never a silent gap.

The last box is the part I care most about: the output schema's validator raises an exception if an outcome delta is attached to a cell whose gates failed. A number that failed its gates is not merely discouraged — in this system it is unrepresentable. That single design choice converts a statistical policy into a compile-time-style guarantee that survives every future refactor.

05 The query path

Who is asking matters as much as what they ask

Actor-level override data is need-to-know. The API resolves the viewer's role from auth — never from the client — writes an audit entry before serving, and branches the response shape by role. Actors are pseudonymous by default and listed alphabetically; the payload is deliberately never rank-sortable. Re-identification requires an explicit purpose-of-access drill-in by a leadership viewer, which itself writes an audit entry.

The flowchart shows the branches; a sequence shows the ordering that makes them safe. The audit entry is written before a single row is read, and the tenant assertion and pseudonymisation run after the cache returns — so even a cached answer is re-governed for every caller rather than trusted because it was served once before.

Were the audit written after serving instead of before, a request that errored mid-response could disclose actor-level data with no record it was ever seen. Ordering the log first is what makes the access trail complete by construction, not best-effort.

06 Guardrails

Every way this dashboard could mislead has a named control

What could go wrongThe control
Confounded comparison Propensity-matched controls on pre-treatment covariates only; any covariate imbalance above the standardised-mean-difference (SMD) gate marks the cell unmatched and suppresses its headline.
False confidence from small cells Minimum 30 patients per arm; below the floor the cell reports "insufficient data" with its full denominator breakdown.
Fragile findings Every comparison is recomputed at strict, base, and loose matching calipers — a robustness check on the match itself, distinct from the observational caveat above. If the sign flips or the interval moves materially, the finding is marked unstable and withheld.
Scorecard misuse Neutral codes in the data layer — no "policy too strict" or "who was right" labels; pseudonymous actors; alphabetic, never-rankable ordering; role-based views; an audit entry for every actor-level request; observational caveat on every surface.

Upstream corrections ignored A correction listener maps each spine correction to the metric slices that depend on it and rebuilds just those — P95 under five minutes from correction to fresh tables.
Irreproducible statistics Bootstrap seeded from the snapshot manifest and clustered by enrollment; matcher seeded the same way; every response carries the manifests and hashes to replay its numbers byte-for-byte.
07 The LLM boundary

The language model never touches a number

This is the portfolio's clearest case of knowing when not to use an LLM. Statistics, gating, suppression, ordering, and access control are all deterministic code. The only language-model call in the entire system is an optional, post-hoc explainer: after a cell has passed every gate, the model is handed the finished numbers and asked to narrate them in neutral, observational wording. It is forbidden to compute, re-rank, or conclude — and if it is down, the dashboards are unaffected because the claims live in deterministic UI copy, not in generated text.

  • Governance as code, not prompt: the neutrality rules shipped as deterministic UI copy plus role-based access — they cannot drift with a model update, and there is no prompt to jailbreak, because the claims never pass through the model.
  • Self-hosted inference for PHI: when enabled, the explainer runs on Llama 3.1 inside the VPC; patient data never leaves the network.
  • Fail-quiet by design: the explainer is additive colour. Losing it loses a sentence of prose, not a number, a gate, or a control.
Why this is not RAG

The explainer resembles retrieval-augmented generation, but it is not: nothing is retrieved to ground the sentence. The model is handed exactly one already-computed cell — its delta, its confidence interval, its arm sizes, its balance status — and asked only to restate that cell in words. There is no corpus to search and no fact outside the cell the narration could reach for; the numbers were true before the model was called and remain the sole authority after it returns.

08 The stack

Boring infrastructure, deliberate framework choices

The analytical core is deliberately unexciting — columnar files and an embedded query engine, each doing one job. The AI layer reuses the platform's LangChain seam and LangGraph runtime, with observability designed for PHI from the start.

The two tables below say what each component is; the picture first says when it is touched. Three lanes matter: the request path a clinician actually waits on, the nightly jobs that pre-compute every number offline, and the one-time historical hydration that seeds the spine. Everything runs inside the client VPC — the only thing outside it is the clinician's browser, reaching in over TLS — so no PHI, and no self-hosted model, ever crosses the boundary. The two boxes with a dashed outline are the deliberate absences: the graph database and the language-model seam are both kept off the path a clinician waits on for a number, reached — if at all — only by the dotted edges.

10 The review gauntlet

The review that changed the whole programme

Before anything shipped, the design went through structured adversarial self-review under a Principal-AI-Engineer lens — and this one drew blood: nineteen findings across correctness, statistics, governance, and scalability, with an unambiguous "do not implement as written."

  1. Design review · round 1 Ship-with-changes, 19 findings — confounding exposure, statistical rigour gaps, and the governance risks of an actor-level dashboard.
  2. Design revision · v2 Fourteen findings absorbed directly into the design; five deliberately promoted into shared platform components (tenant wrapper, outcome library, matcher binary, replay test, actor-access policy) so sibling queries inherit the same fixes instead of reimplementing them. All five have since landed at the platform layer, and the query now binds to the shared versions.
  3. Programme impact The review minted a new governance requirement — actor-dashboard access control and de-identification — adopted as the programme's standard for every actor-facing dashboard. The hardened v2 is the design that shipped; the runnable work sample goes through the same hiring-CTO review loop documented in the precedent-search case study.

11 Delivered

What was delivered — and where it transfers

  • A production deployment — the override-analytics service live on the platform's self-hosted, in-VPC stack, serving governed dashboards. Shipped after executive sign-off on the design.
  • A complete design packet — cohort construction spec, the matcher contract with versioned manifests, the pre-registered statistical plan (balance gate, sensitivity sweep, power floor, seeded bootstrap), API envelopes with full provenance pins, the RBAC/audit governance spec, and freshness SLAs.
  • A runnable work sample — an offline demo of the gate ladder where the typed contract raises on any attempt to attach a delta to a failed-gate cell; runs deterministically in CI with zero API keys.
  • A review audit trail — a nineteen-finding adversarial design review, its absorption into v2, and a prepared work-sample review as the next gate.

The pattern generalises well beyond healthcare: any system that compares people's judgment calls against a baseline — underwriting exceptions, SRE runbook deviations, fraud-ops overrides, content-moderation appeals.
