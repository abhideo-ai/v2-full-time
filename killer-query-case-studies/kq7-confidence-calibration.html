Case study · ML system design · Healthcare

When a clinician says “high confidence” — does the outcome rate actually rise?

For a chronic-care platform covering 80+ conditions, I designed the confidence-calibration system — the query that checks self-reported certainty against what actually happened — and took it to production. Its first structured review came back with a single word, redesign, and eight required changes; the version described here is that redesign. Every number a regulator could audit is computed by deterministic, seeded, replayable code, and no language model is anywhere near the calibration path. The same discipline transfers wherever stated confidence must be checked against outcomes: credit adjudication, triage, forecasting desks, model-confidence audits.

01 The problem

A calibration question with a statistics trap inside it

Every decision on the platform carries a self-reported confidence — but as an ordinal bucket: low, medium, or high. Not a numeric probability. So the honest deliverable is a bucket-versus-outcome comparison and a monotonicity check — does the outcome rate rise from low to high — not a smooth calibration curve. The system ships exactly that, and never dresses three buckets up as a probability model.

Underneath the plain question sit failure modes that a single GROUP BY would walk straight into. Each is treated as a first-class requirement with a named control:

  • Selection on outcome availability — if a decision's 90-day outcome has not matured, or is missing, silently dropping it makes the confident buckets look better than they were. This was flagged in review as a P0 statistics risk. Missing and censored outcomes must enter the denominator with an explicit status.
  • False confidence from small cells — a handful of mature outcomes produces a confident-looking rate. Below a floor, the cell must say so.
  • Monotonicity that isn't real — noise makes low edge past medium in some cell every night; that must never page a human unless the difference is statistically real.
  • Denominator gaming — pushing decisions into one bucket can manufacture a clean result; the distribution itself has to be watched.
  • Scorecard misuse — the moment the numbers split by actor, the table reads as a ranking that can end a career, even when the statistics are sound.
02 The architecture

An append-only spine, a graph kept off the hot path, and a snapshot-only cohort

In one line: every calibration number is computed once a night from a frozen snapshot, never from the live graph, so any figure can be reproduced exactly. The platform's source of truth is an append-only event spine (DynamoDB). A projection keeps a decision graph (Neptune) current, and a nightly snapshot writes columnar files. The pivotal design decision — and the one the first review forced — is that the cohort is built snapshot-only over DuckDB, and the graph is never scanned at cohort-build time. Instead the spine is replayed as of each decision to reconstruct the confidence bucket, the acting clinician, and the covariates, each carrying the event sequence it was read at. Outcomes come from one shared 90-day labeller, so missing follow-up is recorded, never dropped.

The central honesty knob

Some early decisions predate the confidence field. The tempting shortcut is to default them to medium so the cohort looks complete. The design refuses: pre-field rows are excluded, never invented. A confidence bucket that was never recorded is not a bucket — it is a gap, and the cohort says so rather than fabricating a middle value that would quietly bias every rate.

03 Hydration

Nine years of calibration history, replayed into the spine

The calibration service did not start against an empty world. Roughly nine years of prior decisions and their matured outcomes were hydrated through the same append-only spine by a first-class, quarantine-first pre-seed pipeline — not a throwaway backfill — so the platform carried its full history the day it went live. Five properties are what make that load trustworthy:

  • Event time is not ingestion time. Each legacy record's real-world moment — when the decision was made, when the outcome resolved — is preserved as its own timestamp, distinct from the received and written times that record only when the load ingested it. Collapse the two and nine years of decisions would look as though they all happened on load day, voiding every as-of reconstruction the calibration cohort depends on. The fourth timestamp, observed, is itself a source-provided field — when the source resulted the value — so it too is carried from the record, never stamped at load. In hydration mode the watermark and allowed-lateness span the whole corpus, so a nine-year spread of "late" data is kept rather than dropped; live mode keeps the small operational value.
  • Hydrating twice is a no-op. Content-addressed event IDs plus conditional writes make the load idempotent and resumable; re-running it over the same corpus yields a byte-identical spine and an identical snapshot-manifest hash. That is an executable proof of determinism, not an assurance in a document.
  • Nothing is silently coerced. Nine years means many legacy payload-schema versions; each gets an explicit upconverter, and any row that cannot be mapped to a schema — or to a tenant — is quarantined with a reason and counted, never defaulted. A defaulted tenant would be a cross-tenant leak, and a defaulted confidence bucket would poison the very calibration the query exists to measure.
  • The same projection code runs for history and for live. History is replayed through the identical projection logic that serves the live stream; otherwise the hydrated graph would be a different product from the streaming one and every determinism guarantee would be theatre.
  • The load is proof-carrying. Zero-event-loss reconciliation against the source, per-tenant and per-class counts, as-of spot checks against known-good historical reports, and a golden replay over a historical slice all gate the load before anything downstream is built. A fenced cutover then attaches the live stream with a proof of neither gap nor duplicate at the boundary.

One detail inside that load carries the entire calibration query, and it earns a figure of its own: how time is stamped. Each hydrated decision receives four timestamps from two different sources. The real-world moment — when the decision was made, when its outcome resolved — is read from the record; the two ingestion moments are pinned to a single load-fence constant, never to now(). An invariant then asserts that the real-world time falls strictly before the ingest time on every historical row, which is precisely the guard that stops a nine-year spread of event times from quietly collapsing onto load day.

For this query the depth of that history is the difference between a verdict and a shrug. A calibration cell is only testable once its confidence bucket holds enough matured 90-day outcomes to clear the thirty-per-cell floor — and it must clear that floor inside every archetype, and every governed actor slice, before monotonicity can be checked at all. A cold spine would suppress most cells for insufficient data and leave the low-to-high comparison untestable; nine years of hydrated, matured outcomes populate the buckets deeply enough that the comparison can actually be run, gated, and — only when the confidence intervals separate — trusted.

04 The redesign

Four condemned shortcuts, and the controls that replaced them

Most reviews in the programme returned changes. This one returned a verdict: do not implement as written, with eight required changes before a line of production code. The first design read outcomes off the live graph, silently dropped decisions whose follow-up had not matured, defaulted the missing confidence field to medium, and paged on a bare low-above-medium rate comparison. Each of those is a way to look calibrated without being calibrated. The version that shipped inverts all four.

05 The core mechanism

A suppression ladder, not a single rate

A calibration query does not return a number and hope. It runs a ladder, and a cell has to clear every rung before it earns a headline rate. Access is resolved and audited first; then the cell passes through maturity, sample size, gaming, and monotonicity checks; then, on the way out, every row is asserted against the caller's tenant scope — the account, workspace, and control-unit resolved from the caller's auth, never a single-field check. Anything that fails a rung is not deleted — it is returned as a typed, suppressed cell that still carries its denominators, so a suppressed result is honest, not silent.

Two thresholds carry the calibration judgement itself. A monotonicity inversion pages a human only when the two buckets' bootstrap intervals separate — a bare low-above-medium is informational, never an alert. And archetypes differ: modify and initiate decisions carry a spread floor and an inversion alarm, while override and escalate decisions, whose natural spread is small, carry a lower floor and no inversion alarm, because a flat curve there is expected, not a defect. No pooled verdict ships across archetypes without clinical-ops sign-off.

That first threshold — page only when the intervals separate — is the single control the original review cared most about, so it is worth drawing on its own. The rejected design compared three bare rates and paged whenever low edged past medium. The shipped design compares each adjacent pair by whether their confidence intervals actually separate, and only then asks whether the direction inverts and whether this archetype even carries an inversion alarm:

Delete the overlap gate and the naive path on the left takes over: with three buckets resampled every night, one pair crosses by chance most nights, the alarm fires constantly, and the on-call rota learns to ignore the one signal that matters. The overlap test is what keeps a real inversion legible.

The payoff of all this discipline is that the gated cell becomes a governed artifact. 06 Guardrails

Every way this dashboard could mislead has a named control

What could go wrongThe control
Selection on outcome availability A shared 90-day outcome labeller; missing and censored outcomes enter the denominator with an explicit status. If any bucket's excluded share passes 20%, the headline rate and spread are suppressed and only the maturity breakdown is shown.
False confidence from small cells A minimum of 30 mature outcomes per cell; below the floor the cell reports "insufficient data" with its full per-bucket breakdown rather than a rate.
Monotonicity that isn't real An inversion pages someone only when the two buckets' bootstrap intervals separate; a bare low-above-medium is informational. Override and escalate archetypes, whose natural spread is small, carry a lower spread floor and no inversion alarm.
Denominator gaming A chi-square test against the population bucket distribution flags decisions pushed into one bucket; the old "90% in one bucket" rule is kept only as a sanity floor.
Scorecard misuse Per-actor rows are pseudonymous, ordered alphabetically with no rankable surface, gated behind leadership or self-view access, audited on every request, and carry an observational caveat; no pooled verdict ships without clinical-ops sign-off.
Stale snapshot behind the numbers Freshness tiers: under 24h serves normally; 24–48h serves with a staleness banner; past 48h the service blocks and escalates. A projector lag beyond 30 minutes flips the tier to yellow.
Upstream corrections ignored A correction listener maps each spine correction — to a confidence bucket, an actor, or an outcome — to the calibration rows that depend on it and rebuilds just those; P95 under five minutes from correction to fresh tables.
Irreproducible statistics The bootstrap is seeded from the snapshot manifest and clustered by enrollment; the same as-of with a shuffled spine yields an identical calibration-artifact hash.

One row in that table is worth tracing end to end, because it is the mechanism that keeps every other guarantee current: what happens when the spine is corrected after the fact. A clinician amends a confidence bucket, an attribution is fixed, a late outcome finally resolves — and the calibration rows that depended on that fact must move, but only those. The correction listener never rebuilds the whole table on an edit; it walks a dependency index to the exact cells that changed and rebuilds just them.

Without the dependency index the only safe response to any correction would be a full nightly-scale recompute, and the five-minute freshness SLA would be impossible; targeting the rebuild is what makes correctness and speed compatible.

07 The LLM boundary

There is no language model anywhere in the calibration path

This is the portfolio's purest case of knowing when not to use an LLM. A confidence-versus-outcome comparison is buckets, outcome rates, confidence intervals, and gate decisions — arithmetic and governance from end to end. There is nothing here for a model to summarise that a deterministic template does not already own, and everything here for a model to get subtly, unaccountably wrong. So the calibration path contains zero model calls. The platform ships a self-hosted Llama 3.1 seam inside the VPC for the queries that genuinely narrate — the graph-traversal and orchestration surfaces — and calibration deliberately opens none of it. There is not even a retrieval-augmented generation surface to narrate around, and that absence is a judgment rather than a gap: a calibration verdict is a monotonicity test across three ordinal buckets with a confidence interval on every rate, so retrieving evidence for a model to retell would only bolt a new failure mode onto a result whose entire worth is that it was computed, gated, and — where support runs thin — suppressed without one.

  • No prompt to jailbreak: the neutrality rules, suppression logic, and access controls are code and configuration, not instructions to a model. They cannot drift with a model update, because there is no model in the loop.
  • Nothing to hallucinate: every number on the surface is computed and version-pinned; no generated sentence ever carries a claim.
  • The seam still exists: the platform's model door is one import away for a sibling query. The discipline is choosing, deliberately, not to open it here.
08 The stack

Boring infrastructure, one job each

The analytical core is deliberately unexciting — columnar files, an embedded query engine, and shared statistics libraries, each doing exactly one thing. Above it sits a thin contracts-and-observability layer designed for PHI from the start — and a language model that is present in the platform but pointedly absent from this query.

Before the tables list what each component is, this diagram shows when each one is touched and where the trust boundary sits. Three lanes run at three different cadences: a request path a clinician actually waits on, a nightly and event-triggered lane that does the heavy statistics offline, and a one-time historical hydration lane that pre-seeded the spine. Everything runs inside the client VPC; the only thing that crosses the boundary is a governed, tenant-asserted response to the clinician's browser over TLS. Two components the tables below list are deliberately drawn off the request path — the graph database, and the model seam this query never calls.

09 Evaluation

The binding metric: never emit a number that should have been suppressed

For a calibration surface, a false emit — a headline rate that is really immature or underpowered — costs far more than a false suppress. Evaluation is built around that asymmetry, on seeded synthetic cohorts where the ground truth of every gate decision is known by construction, and on golden fixtures that pin the pipeline byte-for-byte.

10 The review gauntlet

The review that returned a single word

Before anything shipped, the design went through structured adversarial self-review under a Principal-AI-Engineer lens. This one did not come back with a punch-list of tweaks. Its recommendation was one word — redesign — with eight required changes before a line of production code. The version described in this case study is that redesign, absorbed and shipped.

  1. Design review · round 1 Redesign. Headline finding: selection on outcome availability is a P0 statistics risk. Eight required changes spanned the cohort path, as-of reconstruction, the outcome library, tenant partitioning, the cache-key envelope, correction invalidation, the rebuilt statistics, and actor governance.
  2. Design revision · v2 Five changes absorbed directly into the design — snapshot-only cohort, as-of reconstruction, the shared outcome labeller, the canonical envelope, the rebuilt statistics. Three deliberately promoted to shared platform components — the tenant wrapper, correction-invalidation with a replay test, and the actor-access policy — so sibling queries inherit the same fixes instead of reimplementing them. All three have since landed at the platform layer, and the query binds to the shared versions rather than an inlined stand-in.
  3. Programme impact The actor-dashboard access-and-de-identification requirement became the programme standard for every actor-facing surface. This query is also a producer: its gated calibration artifact is what the downstream AI-versus-human comparison reads before trusting a "high confidence" cohort. The separate runnable-work-sample gate — a hiring-CTO review loop — is documented in the precedent-search case study.
