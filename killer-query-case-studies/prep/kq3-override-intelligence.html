Override intelligence

When a clinician overrides the protocol — who overrode, what rationale did they give, and did those patients fare differently? Built for a chronic-care platform covering 80+ chronic conditions and roughly 10,000 patients, with about nine years of history hydrated into the event spine. Sole owner, design through production. Live.

01 · What is it

The most politically charged question in the product

Open with these two sentences

Every other query on that platform describes patients or the system; this one describes people's judgment calls, and the answer everyone wants is one structured query language (SQL) GROUP BY away — and would be wrong three different ways at once. So I built it as a propensity-matched observational comparison behind four deterministic statistical gates, where every number a regulator could audit is computed by seeded, replayable code, and a language model is only allowed to narrate a cell after the gates have already passed it.

The situation, in plain language

A clinician looks at a patient, decides the standing protocol is wrong for this person, and deviates from it. That deviation is recorded as an override, with a free-text rationale attached.

Clinical leadership wants to know which protocols get overridden most, and whether those patients did better or worse ninety days later — because a protocol that is constantly overridden for one subgroup is probably miscalibrated for that subgroup. A regulator wants something narrower and harder: for any number on that screen, show me exactly how it was computed, from which data, as of which moment, and prove you would get the same number again tomorrow.

And an individual clinician wants to know something neither of them is asking: is this table going to be used to judge me? That third reader is why the design looks the way it does. The output is an analytics surface, not a recommendation engine, and it is deliberately not wired into any automated action, incentive, or ranking.

The exact query the system answers

"Show all cases where a protocol was overridden — who overrode, what rationale was given, and what were outcomes versus non-overridden cases?"

Why it is genuinely hard — the three ways a naive build fails

This is the part to say out loud early. A single grouped count is not a slightly worse version of this system; it is wrong three ways simultaneously, and each way has its own named control.

1 · Confounded by design

Clinicians override on their sickest and least typical patients. So a raw override-versus-followed comparison contrasts two populations that were never alike to begin with — it measures the difference between the patients, not the effect of the override. The confounding is not an accident of sampling; it is the mechanism by which overrides happen.

Control: every override is compared only against a propensity-matched control drawn from similar patients on the same policy family, and the comparison is suppressed outright if the match is not demonstrably balanced.

2 · False confidence from small cells

A clinician with twelve decisions can look like a dramatic outlier by noise alone. Small cohorts manufacture confident-looking rates out of a handful of cases, and a tight-looking number is exactly what a reader will quote.

Control: a minimum of thirty patients in each arm, below which the cell reports "insufficient data" with its full denominator breakdown — visible, not a silent gap.

3 · Scorecard misuse

The instant the table exists, an override breakdown reads as a scorecard. Even a statistically immaculate rate can end a career if it is presented as a ranking rather than an observation.

Control: neutral codes in the data layer, pseudonymous actor identifiers, alphabetic and deliberately never rank-sortable ordering, role-based views, and an audit entry written for every actor-level look.

And a fourth, quieter one — as-of leakage. The single worst mistake this design exists to prevent is scoring an override using information that only existed after the clinician deviated — most dangerously an outcome-adjacent covariate. That would make overrides on patients who later deteriorated look systematically different for reasons that had nothing to do with the override, it would bias the entire comparison, and it would be completely invisible in the output. Nothing on the dashboard would look wrong.

Terms, in plain English

Override / policy alignment
Each decision event carries a policy_alignment field. A value of overridden means the recorded decision deviated from the protocol that constrained it at that moment.
Propensity matching
Pairing each overridden decision with a non-overridden one that looks alike on the characteristics measured before the decision, so the comparison contrasts similar patients instead of dissimilar ones. It removes confounding on what was measured. It does not create causality.
Caliper
The maximum distance allowed between a treated row and its nearest neighbour for the pair to count as comparable. Beyond the caliper, there is no match.
SMD — standardised mean difference
How far apart the two arms sit on a given covariate, in standard-deviation units. Here every covariate must sit at or under 0.1 before a cell may show a headline number.
Covariate · pre-treatment covariate
A measured patient characteristic used for matching. Pre-treatment means it was fixed before the decision. The measured set here is age, body mass index (BMI), country, program, program phase, baseline HbA1c (glycated haemoglobin, a roughly three-month average blood-sugar marker), prior adherence, and tenure.
Endogeneity
Matching on something that was itself caused by the patient being override-eligible — a recent-escalation count, a prior protocol-exception flag. It bakes the treatment into the "control" and collapses the comparison toward no effect. Those fields are excluded by policy.
CI — careful, this collides
Confidence interval throughout this page: the range the estimate is compatible with. It quantifies sampling uncertainty, never confounding. The other CI — continuous integration, the automated build — appears only where the work sample is described as running "in CI". Do not let the two blur in a room.
Bootstrap
Estimating an interval by repeatedly resampling the data. Here: 2,000 resamples, seeded from the snapshot hash, resampling enrollments rather than rows.
Censoring
A patient whose ninety-day outcome cannot be fully observed — they left, or the window has not matured. Counted and reported, never quietly dropped.
As-of reconstruction
Rebuilding a decision's facts from the append-only event spine using only events at or before the decision timestamp, rather than reading whatever the current graph says today.
Event spine · append-only
The source of truth. Nothing is ever updated in place — a correction is a new event with a later sequence number — so any past instant can be reconstructed exactly.
virtual private cloud (VPC) — virtual private cloud
The client's private network boundary. Protected health information (PHI) never crosses it, and neither does the self-hosted model.
RBAC — role-based access control
What a caller may see is decided by the role resolved from their authenticated token, never from anything the client sends.
LLM — large language model
Here: exactly one optional, post-hoc call that narrates a finished cell. It computes nothing.
95th-percentile (P95) — 95th percentile
The value 95% of observations fall below. Used here for the correction-freshness target.
02 · How did we solve it

Snapshot-only analytics with a matched control arm

One sentence for the whole thing: nothing on the request path computes a statistic. Every number a dashboard can show was built offline the night before, gated, and published with the manifests needed to replay it.

0Numbers written by a language model
2,000Bootstrap resamples per interval, seeded from the snapshot hash
≤ 0.1Covariate SMD above which a cell cannot show a headline
≥ 30Patients per arm, or the cell is suppressed
90 daysOutcome maturity — nothing scored before the window closes
P95 < 5 minCorrection on the spine to rebuilt metric slices

Say this before anyone asks. Every threshold, service-level agreement and statistical bar above is a pre-registered acceptance target — the bar the system was designed, reviewed and deployed against. They are not measured production results. Observed outcomes are not his to publish, so there is no achieved latency, adoption, error rate, cost saving or accuracy figure anywhere in this material, and inventing one in a room would be the only real failure here.

The nightly build — what the data does as it moves

  1. Trigger. The build runs after the snapshot export lands, not on a clock alone. A snapshot is a frozen, content-hashed point-in-time export of the decision graph.
  2. Scan. A columnar analytical scan over the snapshot, filtered three ways at once: to the caller's tenant scope, to a minimum supported payload-schema version, and to decisions old enough to have a mature ninety-day outcome.
  3. Reconstruct as-of. For each candidate, rebuild from the append-only spine the alignment flag, the free-text rationale, the acting person or agent, and the exact constraining policy version plus its text hash — using only events at or before the decision. Reading any of these from the current graph is categorically forbidden, because every one of them is mutable after the fact.
  4. Anchor and featurise. Keep one decision per treatment episode, so a restarted program is not counted several times, then compute pre-treatment covariates only.
  5. Label. Attach the ninety-day outcome through the shared outcome library — status, attribution, and censoring — one implementation used by every cohort query in the programme, so there is no per-team drift in what "outcome" means.
  6. Match. Pair each override with its single nearest non-overridden neighbour on pre-treatment covariates, inside a caliper, restricted to the same policy family, same program phase, same tenant. One-to-one, without replacement, seeded from the snapshot manifest, with a canonical tie-break on decision identifier.
  7. Mint the manifest. Commit the matched controls and a versioned match_manifest_id — a hash over the covariate schema, snapshot manifest, cohort filters, caliper, replacement policy, seed, and tie-break rule.
  8. Gate and aggregate. Run the four-rung gate ladder per cell and write the by-policy, by-actor and outcome-delta metric tables, with seeded, enrollment-clustered bootstrap intervals at 2,000 resamples.
  9. Publish. Write with manifests and schema hashes attached, so canonical cache keys roll automatically — there is no manual invalidation step to forget.
  10. Corrections. A listener maps each spine correction's source-sequence range to the derived slices whose recorded ranges overlap it, rebuilds only those, and bumps a correction epoch so old cache keys stop resolving.

The match manifest — the contract that stops two dashboards disagreeing

A downstream query that needs the same control arm either reuses the manifest verbatim, field for field, or rebuilds it with the same matcher binary. It can never silently produce a slightly different control group. That removes an entire bug class: two dashboards quoting different numbers for the same cohort.

The subtlety worth volunteering, because it is the tell that this was actually built: the manifest hashes the matcher's inputs, not the matcher itself, and not the statistical plan applied afterwards. So matcher_binary_version and stat_plan_version are pinned as their own fields in the canonical cache key rather than assumed to ride along inside the manifest. A cohort rebuilt from identical inputs by a different binary, or the same cohort re-summarised under a revised bootstrap plan, is a different result and must miss the cache.

"otherwise a cached delta could outlive the cohort it was computed from"

The gate ladder — chain-of-thought as deterministic code

Before any cell may show a headline number it climbs four rungs, in this order. Failing any rung produces a suppressed cell with a stated reason and full denominators — never a confident number built on sand, and never a silent gap.

  1. Balance. Maximum covariate SMD at or under 0.1. Fail → tagged unmatched, headline suppressed, descriptive counts only.
  2. Sensitivity. Recompute at strict, base and loose calipers (0.1 / 0.2 / 0.3); the sign and the interval must stay stable. Fail → marked unstable, headline and any recommendation withheld. This asks a different question from rung 1: rung 1 asks "are the arms balanced?", rung 2 asks "is this finding an artifact of the matching radius?"
  3. Power. At least thirty patients in each arm. Fail → "insufficient data" with the full denominator breakdown.
  4. Uncertainty. Only now is the interval computed — seeded bootstrap, 2,000 resamples, clustered by enrollment.

Then the part that makes it durable: the output schema's validator raises if an outcome delta is attached to a cell whose gates failed. A gate-failing number is not discouraged; it is unrepresentable.

The request path, in order — and the order is the safety property

  1. Auth resolves the tenant triple (account_id, workspace_id, controlunit_id) and the viewer's role — from the token, never from the client.
  2. An audit entry is written before a single row is read: viewer, target, purpose, time. The access_log_id comes back in the response.
  3. The response shape branches by role. Leadership sees the full by-policy and by-actor view, pseudonymous and alphabetically ordered. A clinician sees only their own row. Anyone else is denied the actor breakdown and served the policy view.
  4. A canonical cache key is built from sorted JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) over: the tenant triple, query namespace, split and filter shape, metric schema hash, snapshot manifest hash, match manifest id, matcher binary version, statistical-plan version, maximum source event sequence, and correction epoch. Sorted JSON, so two logically identical requests cannot hash differently on field order.
  5. Hit or miss, the answer is pre-computed cells — either from cache or a columnar read over the published metric tables. No graph calls. No statistic recomputed at query time.
  6. After the cache returns: assert every row carries the caller's exact tenant triple — fail closed, and page, because a cross-tenant row is an incident, not a metric — then pseudonymise the actor unless the viewer is leadership. Even a cached answer is re-governed for every caller rather than trusted because it was served once before.
  7. Return the cells with their intervals, balance status, sensitivity status, n_unmatched, censoring counts, denominators for the suppressed cells, an observational tag on every surface, and the access_log_id.

Tenancy — three layers, because they fail differently

The candidate scan filters on the full tenant triple before any rows load. The derived cohort, matched-control and metric Parquet files are physically partitioned by that triple plus the snapshot hash. And a fail-closed runtime assertion re-checks every served row against the caller. Partitioning is a build-time property that stops a fumbled WHERE clause from seeing another tenant at all; the assertion is a serve-time property that catches a mis-keyed cache entry, a bad join, or an application programming interface (API)-layer bug before a byte reaches the caller. Neither substitutes for the other.

Hydration — what nine years actually buys

This was not built against an empty world. Roughly nine years of prior overrides, rationales and outcomes already existed in a private production system and were hydrated through the same append-only spine before any override was ever compared. Five properties keep replayed history trustworthy enough to run statistics on:

  • Event time is not ingestion time. Each legacy record keeps the moment the decision actually happened; separate fields record when the system learned of it. The watermark runs in a hydration mode where a nine-year spread of late-arriving data is normal rather than the small live-mode tolerance that would drop nearly all of it.
  • Hydrating twice is a no-op. Content-addressed event identifiers plus conditional writes make the load idempotent and resumable — re-running over the same corpus yields an identical spine and an identical snapshot-manifest hash. That equality is an executable proof, not an assurance.
  • Nothing is silently coerced. Nine years means many payload-schema versions; each gets an explicit upconverter, and any row that cannot be mapped to a schema or to a tenant is quarantined and counted, never defaulted. A defaulted tenant would be a cross-tenant leak straight into the override cohort.
  • The same projection code runs for history and for live. The bulk load may write those years in whatever shard order is fastest — safe only because every as-of reconstruction sorts by event time and source sequence, never by write order.
  • The load is proof-carrying. Zero-event-loss reconciliation, per-tenant and per-class counts, as-of spot checks against known-good historical reports, and a golden replay over a historical slice all gate the load before a single override cell is computed. A fenced cutover then attaches the live stream with a proof of neither gap nor duplicate at the boundary.

What that buys this query specifically is statistical power at the cell level. Matching inside a caliper only succeeds when a similar-enough control exists, and restricting controls to the same policy family and program phase shrinks the pool further. Deep history is what populates that pool densely enough for a nearest neighbour to usually fall inside the caliper at an acceptable SMD, and for a per-cell arm to clear the thirty-patient floor. A cold system would return "insufficient data" almost everywhere.

"History buys power, not identification."

The language-model boundary

Statistics, gating, suppression, ordering and access control are all deterministic code. There is exactly one model call in the entire system: an optional, post-hoc explainer. After a cell has passed every gate, the model is handed the finished numbers — delta, interval, arm sizes, balance status — and asked to restate them in neutral, observational wording. It is forbidden to compute, re-rank, conclude, or reach for any fact outside the cell it was handed. When enabled it runs on a self-hosted Llama 3.1 inside the VPC, so patient data never leaves the network, and it is handed no free-text rationale and no clinical identifiers, so its input is essentially de-identified even before the network boundary.

If it is down, the dashboards are unaffected: the substantive claims live in deterministic user interface (UI) copy backed by the typed cell, so losing the explainer loses a sentence of prose — not a number, not a gate, not a control.

The stack

  • DynamoDB — the append-only event spine. Source of truth for as-of reconstruction.
  • Amazon Neptune — the decision graph, projected off the spine. Feeds the nightly snapshot; never queried per-row at analytics time.
  • Parquet + DuckDB — snapshot-only analytical store. Cohort, matched-control and metric tables partitioned by tenant and snapshot hash; queries read pre-computed cells.
  • Propensity matcher — seeded one-to-one caliper matching with the versioned manifest.
  • Shared statistics libraries — one outcome labeller, one seeded cluster-aware bootstrap, used by every cohort query in the programme.
  • FastAPI — the analytics service: role-resolved views, audit-first serving, canonical cache keys, tenant assertion on every row. The metric cache for this query is in-process.
  • LangChain — one provider seam returning a chat model for the optional explainer persona (mocked in continuous integration); typed, validated cell contracts in Pydantic v2, where the "misleading number is unrepresentable" guarantee lives; and a PHI-safe callback tracer that logs metadata and payload hashes, never raw clinical text.
  • LangGraph — the platform's in-VPC runtime hosting the optional explainer persona alongside the other queries' graphs. That runtime is an API server plus PostgreSQL, Redis, and Ollama.
  • LangSmith, self-hosted in-VPC — production tracing and evaluation datasets run without PHI leaving the network.
03 · Why did we do what we did

Every significant decision, and the alternative it beat

This is the section to have fluent. Quoted lines are his own reasoning from the case study and its question-and-answer companion — worth recalling close to verbatim, because they are already compressed.

Statistical method

Propensity matching, not a raw override-versus-rest comparison

Rejected A grouped override rate per clinician, or an override-versus-everyone-else outcome comparison.

The two groups are not comparable to begin with — overrides happen on atypical patients — so a raw comparison measures the difference between populations, not the effect of overriding. Matching does not manufacture causality; it removes the crudest confounding on the covariates that were measured, which is the minimum bar for the comparison to be worth showing at all.

"Everything expensive in this design — as-of reconstruction, propensity matching, balance and stability gates, governance — exists to turn a misleading one-liner into a defensible comparison, or to suppress it honestly when it cannot be made defensible."

Matching, not regression adjustment or inverse-probability weighting

Rejected Regression adjustment; inverse-probability weighting (IPW), which reweights observations by how likely they were to receive the treatment.

Matching produces an explicit, inspectable control arm and a per-cell balance diagnostic. You can see the SMDs and refuse to headline an unbalanced cell.

"Regression adjustment and IPW can be more efficient but bury the comparability assumption inside a model and make 'was this cell balanced?' a harder question to answer at the point of display. For a politically charged surface where the balance check itself must be visible and auditable, an inspectable matched arm is the more defensible primitive."

Not difference-in-differences, and not an instrumental variable

Rejected Difference-in-differences (comparing the change over time in two groups); an instrumental variable (a third factor that shifts who overrides but otherwise does not affect the outcome).

Difference-in-differences needs a parallel-trends assumption across overridden and non-overridden groups that clinicians' selective overriding almost certainly violates, and a valid instrument for "chose to override" is not available. Either would trade an honestly-labelled observational estimate for a causal claim resting on an unverifiable assumption.

"The design chooses the method whose limits it can state plainly over one whose limits it would have to hope hold."

One-to-one within a caliper, without replacement

Rejected k-nearest-neighbour matching with k > 1; matching with replacement.

One-to-one without replacement gives each override a single, distinct comparator and keeps the control arm a set of real, non-reused patients, which makes the enrollment-clustered bootstrap and the balance accounting straightforward to reason about and to audit. With-replacement matching reuses good controls and complicates the variance estimate; a larger k trades bias for the risk of pulling in less-similar neighbours. The robustness budget is spent instead on the caliper sensitivity sweep, "where the trade is made explicit rather than buried in the estimator."

Controls drawn only from the same policy family

Rejected Any non-overridden decision as a candidate control.

The question is "did overriding this protocol help?" A control drawn from an unrelated protocol would confound the effect of the override with the effect of being on a different protocol entirely. The cost is a smaller control pool per cell — which is one more reason the query needs deep history to clear the power floor.

Pre-treatment covariates only, pre-registered — endogenous fields excluded by policy

Rejected Letting the featurizer decide which fields are useful predictors.

Two distinct leakage risks. Temporal: matching on a value observed after the decision imports the future. Endogeneity: matching on something caused by the patient being override-eligible in the first place — a recent-escalation count, a prior protocol-exception flag — bakes the treatment into the control and biases the comparison toward null. So the covariate list is pre-registered as pre-treatment only, endogenous fields are excluded by policy rather than by judgment, and the covariate schema hash pins the choice so it cannot drift unaudited.

Unmatched overrides counted, never dropped

Rejected Quietly discarding an override with no control inside the caliper.

"Dropping that fourth override silently would bias the whole comparison toward the easy-to-match cases and quietly make overrides look more routine than they are. Counting it — and reporting n_unmatched on every cell — is what keeps the estimate honest about who it could and could not speak for."

And a rising unmatched rate is itself a signal: the cell's comparison is standing on thin support.

Bootstrap clustered by enrollment, seeded from the snapshot hash

Rejected Resampling rows independently; seeding from a fixed constant.

A single patient's enrollment can contribute multiple correlated decisions; resampling rows independently would treat those as independent observations and understate the interval. Clustered resampling produces an honest width. And a fixed seed constant "would make every snapshot's resamples identical in their random structure, which sounds reproducible but couples unrelated snapshots and hides seed-related fragility" — seeding from the snapshot hash ties the resampling to the exact data it ran on.

Gating and suppression

The gate ladder as seeded code, not a review checklist

Rejected A documented set of checks a human is expected to apply before publishing a figure.

"It is code, not a checklist, because a checklist is advisory and drifts; seeded, replayable code applies the same reasoning every run and can be tested against known ground truth. The point is that the 'chain of thought' behind a shown number is deterministic and auditable, not a human remembering to check four things."

The type system makes a gate-failing number unrepresentable

Rejected A lint rule, a code-review convention, or a runtime warning.

"The last box is the part I care most about: the output schema's validator raises an exception if an outcome delta is attached to a cell whose gates failed. A number that failed its gates is not merely discouraged — in this system it is unrepresentable."

The value is durability. "It is not that showing a bad number is discouraged, it is that the type refuses to hold one" — so the guarantee survives every future refactor and every new call site, because the invalid state has no representation for anyone to accidentally reach.

Suppress the fragile cell rather than show it with a caveat

Rejected Emitting the delta with a footnote or a warning badge.

"Because caveats are reliably under-read, especially on a table someone wants to use to make a case, and a fragile delta shown with a footnote is still a delta someone will quote. Suppression removes the headline entirely and replaces it with the reason and the denominators, which is harder to misquote than a number plus a warning."

Underneath it is an asymmetry of harm: a false emit — a headline that should have been withheld — costs far more than a false suppress. Which is also why the binding evaluation metric is gate-check precision rather than recall of real effects: optimising recall would push the system to emit marginal cells.

Balance gate applied per cell, not globally

Rejected A pipeline-level balance check.

Balance is a property of a specific policy-by-cohort slice. A matcher can produce well-balanced arms for one protocol and badly balanced arms for another with a thin control pool. "Applying it globally would let a well-behaved average hide a handful of badly confounded cells, which are exactly the ones a reader would over-interpret."

Architecture and determinism

Snapshot-only — the live graph is never queried at request time

Rejected Reading the decision graph directly, or "enriching" the cohort with per-row graph traversals.

Three reasons compound. Correctness: a point-in-time snapshot with a manifest hash is reproducible, so the same request returns the same cells and every number can be replayed. Isolation of load: cohort scans and matching are heavy and must never contend with the online decision path clinicians depend on. Governance: a frozen snapshot plus pre-computed tables means the numbers a reviewer audits are exactly the numbers that were served. The cost is bounded staleness — made explicit through freshness tiers rather than hidden.

The prohibition on per-row graph calls is a design invariant "precisely because a single well-meaning enrichment call quietly voids the correctness and determinism guarantees the rest of the pipeline depends on."

Freshness by content, not by time-to-live

Rejected Expiring cached metric results after a fixed time-to-live (TTL).

"Because a TTL answers 'how old is this entry?' and this query needs 'is this entry still correct?' — different questions. Within an hour a correction can land that flips a cell, and a TTL would keep serving the stale value until it happened to expire; conversely, a stable cell would be needlessly recomputed on expiry."

Snapshot hash plus correction epoch plus source sequence invalidates exactly the cells that changed and keeps the rest — safer and cheaper than time-based expiry.

Matcher binary version and statistical-plan version pinned separately in the cache key

Rejected Assuming the match manifest already covers them.

This is the detail that shows the system was actually reasoned about. The manifest hashes the matcher's inputs, not the matcher itself, and not the statistical plan applied afterwards. A cohort rebuilt from identical inputs by a different binary, or the same cohort re-summarised under a revised bootstrap plan, is a different result and must miss the cache — "otherwise a cached delta could outlive the cohort it was computed from."

Event time kept separate from ingestion time through hydration

Rejected Collapsing the two, and treating load time as the event's timestamp.

Collapse them and all nine years would appear to have happened on load day, voiding every as-of covariate the matcher depends on. A strict event_time < received_at assertion on every row is the tripwire that would catch such a slip before a single override cell was ever computed. It also means a legitimately earlier-but-late-arriving observation still updates the reconstructed value, because the as-of read is by event time.

History replayed through the same projection code that serves the live stream

Rejected A separate, faster bulk-load path for historical data.

"Otherwise the hydrated graph would be a different product from the streaming one, and every determinism guarantee behind the gate ladder that follows would be theatre."

Fall closed when a correction rebuild fails

Rejected Continuing to serve the last known value while the rebuild is retried.

"a stale-but-known-wrong cell is worse than a temporarily unavailable one in this domain"

The dependency index is what makes falling closed precise: only the slices the correction actually touched go dark, not the whole dashboard.

Governance

The audit entry is written before any row is read

Rejected Logging the access after the response is assembled.

"Were the audit written after serving instead of before, a request that errored mid-response could disclose actor-level data with no record it was ever seen. Ordering the log first is what makes the access trail complete by construction, not best-effort."

Pseudonymous actors, alphabetic, deliberately never rank-sortable

Rejected A table sortable by override rate.

"a table sorted by override rate is a leaderboard, and a leaderboard invites the exact scorecard reading the design works to prevent"

Re-identification requires an explicit purpose-of-access drill-in by a leadership viewer, and that drill-in writes its own audit entry — "the design makes seeing a real name a deliberate, logged act rather than the default state."

Governance shipped as deterministic UI copy and role branching, not as prompt instructions

Rejected Encoding the neutrality rules in the explainer's system prompt.

Rules in a prompt can drift with a model update and can, in principle, be jailbroken; rules shipped as code plus role-based access cannot. "There is nothing to jailbreak because the claims never pass through the model."

The model narrates; it never computes, re-ranks or concludes

Rejected Letting a model summarise, rank, or draw the conclusion — and equally, calling this retrieval-augmented generation.

It resembles retrieval-augmented generation but is not: nothing is retrieved. The model is handed exactly one already-computed cell and asked only to restate it in words. There is no corpus to search and no fact outside the cell it could reach for — "the numbers were true before the model was called and remain the sole authority after it returns."

A point worth making unprompted, because interviewers get it wrong: "temperature zero does not make a language model's output deterministic." Pinning model, prompt and temperature, and hashing input and output, gives drift detection and reproducible auditing — not generation determinism. The determinism lives in the structured cell and the gate code, never in the narrator.

The design review this absorbed

Before anything shipped, the design went through a structured adversarial self-review under a Principal-AI-Engineer lens. It returned nineteen findings across correctness, statistics, governance and scalability, with an unambiguous "do not implement as written." The verdict was ship-with-changes; all nineteen were dispositioned.

  • Fourteen were absorbed directly into the hardened v2 design — the design that shipped.
  • Five were deliberately promoted into shared platform components rather than fixed locally, so sibling queries inherit the same fixes instead of reimplementing them: the tenant-injecting access wrapper, the shared outcome library, the shared matcher binary and its manifest, the deterministic exporter and its replay test, and the platform actor-access-and-audit policy. All five have since landed at the platform layer, and the query now binds to the shared versions rather than the inline stand-ins it originally shipped with.

Three concrete things changed because of the review:

  • The cache key was rebuilt. A key on the metric-table version alone was identified as a live cross-tenant hazard — on a hit it would serve one tenant's cells to another. The canonical key now carries the full tenant triple plus every result-affecting pin.
  • The cross-query parity fixture exists because of a real bug. A sign-flip was caught between two versions of the pipeline during review — the same override appeared to produce opposite outcome directions across two consumers. The fixture began life failing on that sign-flip and is now a permanent guard in the golden suite.
  • The review minted a new programme-wide governance requirement — actor-dashboard access control and de-identification — adopted as the standard for every actor-facing dashboard in the programme, not just this one.

Handle this exactly, because it sounds like a contradiction and is not. The service is live in production, running on the platform's self-hosted in-VPC stack and serving governed dashboards, deployed after executive sign-off on the design. The design-review verdict nonetheless still reads "ship with changes," not approved — because landing the five shared components changed their state, not the review outcome, and the final design-review sign-off was simply never revisited. "Ship with changes" is a review milestone; "live" is a deployment milestone. The honest line: it shipped, it runs, and the one thing still outstanding is a review signature, not a deployment.

What he would do differently

"I would sequence the shared platform components ahead of the per-query work rather than in parallel, so the query is built against the real tenant wrapper, matcher binary, and exporter from day one instead of inline stand-ins that must later be reconciled. I would also invest earlier in the observability of match quality — the SMD and unmatched-rate dashboards — because those are what tell you a cell is degrading before it produces a wrong number, and they are easy to add late and expensive to miss early."

What flows where

One box sits outside the boundary: the clinician's browser, reaching in over Transport Layer Security (TLS). Everything else — data, models, tracing — is inside the client's virtual private cloud, so protected health information never crosses it and neither does the self-hosted model.

Inside, three lanes, and which lane a component sits in is the argument:

  • One-time historical. Hydration and pre-seed write roughly nine years of legacy overrides, rationales and outcomes into the DynamoDB append-only spine. Runs once, proof-gated.
  • Nightly offline. Snapshot → cohort builder → matcher (which mints the manifest) → metrics with bootstrap intervals. Every number a dashboard can ever show is computed in this lane.
  • Request path. FastAPI analytics API → role-based access control and audit → metric cache → DuckDB over the published Parquet. No statistic is ever computed in this lane.

Four cross-lane edges carry the meaning. The spine projects into Neptune; Neptune is what the nightly snapshot is exported from; the spine also feeds the cohort builder directly, because the spine — not the graph — is the source of truth for what was true at the decision; and the published metric tables are what DuckDB reads on the request path.

The two gates — and they are absences, not components

The two dashed boxes are deliberate absences. The API touches Neptune only along a dotted edge labelled not queried at request time, and the explainer only along a dotted edge labelled optional, post-hoc, off the numbers path. Corrections reach the metric tables by a third dotted edge: rebuild only the touched slices.

What breaks without them

Put the graph on the request path and three things break at once: a live traversal reads current state, reintroducing exactly the as-of leakage the design forbids; the cohort becomes non-deterministic, so the golden replay test can no longer prove two builds produce identical Parquet; and analytics load lands on the online decision path clinicians depend on. Put the explainer on the numbers path and a model outage becomes a dashboard outage, while a hallucinated figure acquires authority it should never have had.

Fig 1 · Reconstruct, match, pre-compute — corrections rebuild only what they touch

What flows where

Eight boxes in one straight line, and the ordering is the content. A snapshot lands, and that landing — not a clock — triggers the build. The scan applies three filters simultaneously: tenant scope, a minimum payload-schema version, and outcome maturity. Each surviving candidate is then reconstructed as of the moment it happened, anchored to one decision per treatment episode, featurised on pre-treatment covariates only, matched, committed with a manifest, aggregated behind the gates, and published with the hashes that let cache keys roll on their own.

The gates, box by box

  • Maturity filter. Only decisions with a ninety-day-mature follow-up enter. Without it, the system would score outcomes that have not happened yet.
  • Schema-version floor. Fields like the alignment flag may not have existed, or may have meant something different, in the earliest of nine years of payload schemas. Without the floor, old decisions get misclassified under today's assumptions.
  • As-of reconstruction. This is what lets the query say this was an override against version 4 of this policy rather than against whatever the policy says today. Overrides are keyed to an immutable, versioned policy with a text hash — key them to a mutable policy identifier and a mid-window edit silently pools overrides against two different texts into one cell.
  • Episode anchoring. One decision per treatment episode, so a restarted program is not counted several times.
  • Manifest minting. The manifest is born at exactly one step, written by exactly one binary, at the moment the controls are written. Mint it anywhere else and "reuse the same cohort" stops being verifiable.
  • Correction listener. The deliberate escape hatch from all that pinning. Without it, pinning traps stale data; with it, a correction's source-sequence range maps to exactly the dependent slices and rebuilds only those.

The ordering the picture hides

Covariates are read as-of the decision before the ninety-day outcome label is attached. Reverse those two and outcome-adjacent information gets into the matching set — the worst as-of mistake in the system, and the one that leaves no trace in the output.

What flows where

Left column: four overridden decisions, the treated arm. Right column: the pool of candidate controls — non-overridden decisions of the same archetype, in the same policy family, at the same program phase, within the same tenant, compared only on pre-treatment covariates. Three treated rows find a nearest neighbour whose distance falls inside the caliper and pair with it, one-to-one, each control consumed once. The fourth has no neighbour close enough. It leaves along a dotted edge into a box that is not a bin: it is excluded from the estimate and counted as n_unmatched.

The three gates in this picture

  • The caliper. Without it, every override gets a "match" including bad ones, and the downstream balance gate becomes the only thing standing between the reader and a comparison of unlike patients.
  • Without replacement. Each control is used once, so the control arm stays a set of real, distinct patients. Reuse them and the variance estimate stops being straightforward to audit.
  • The canonical tie-break. Not drawn, but it lives on these edges: when two controls are equidistant, the matcher picks by canonical decision identifier, not by whichever row arrived first. Without it, two runs over the same data produce different cohorts and different intervals, and the golden replay test fails for no real reason.

What breaks without the counting

Silently dropping the fourth override biases the whole comparison toward the easy-to-match cases — which are the routine overrides — and makes overriding look more ordinary than it is. Reporting n_unmatched on every cell is what keeps the denominator honest about who the estimate could and could not speak for.

    EMIT --> TYPE[Enforced by the type system —<br/>a cell carrying a delta with failed gates<br/>is unrepresentable · the validator raises]

Fig 6 · Every exit is explicit, and the schema itself refuses a gate-failing number

What flows where

One input: a single policy-by-cohort cell with its matched treated and control arms. Three decision diamonds down the spine, each with a suppression exit to the side, then a computation box, then emission, then the type-system enforcement box underneath it.

What each gate does, and why it is in this position

  • Rung 1, balance. Maximum covariate standardised mean difference at or under 0.1. Asks: are these two arms comparable at all? Fail and the cell is tagged unmatched and routed to descriptive counts only, with the reason shown. It is first because nothing downstream means anything if the arms are not alike.
  • Rung 2, sensitivity. Recompute at strict, base and loose calipers; the sign and the interval must stay stable. Asks a different question: is this finding an artifact of the matching radius I happened to pick? A cell can be perfectly balanced at the base caliper and fall apart when the radius moves. Fail and the headline and any recommendation are withheld.
  • Rung 3, power. At least thirty patients in each arm. Below that, bootstrap intervals are wide and unstable and a handful of cases can swing a rate. Fail and the cell says "insufficient data" — with its full denominators, so the reader learns the question was asked and why it could not be answered.
  • Rung 4, uncertainty. Not a gate. It is the thing you are finally permitted to compute once the first three pass: a seeded bootstrap interval, 2,000 resamples, clustered by enrollment.

Two properties worth stating out loud

First, every exit is explicit. A suppressed cell still renders, carrying its reason and its denominators, so an absent headline is visible and accountable rather than a gap — and because the thresholds are fixed in the seeded build and the gate decision is recorded on the cell, nobody can bury an inconvenient result by tuning a threshold at request time.

Second, the order matters. Computing the interval first and then checking balance would leave a tight, attractive interval sitting in memory that should never have existed — and someone would eventually render it.

The last box is the whole point

The validator raises if an outcome delta is attached to a cell whose gates failed. That converts a statistical policy into a compile-time-style guarantee: the invalid state has no representation, so no future refactor and no new call site can reach it.

Whiteboard recipe

Four boxes top to bottom, three arrows peeling off to the right into suppression exits, then one emit box, then a box underneath labelled the type refuses to hold a gate-failing number. Say the names in order: balance, stability, power, uncertainty.

Likely questions

Headline first, detail only if asked

One sentence has to land alone. Stop there and let them pull. The paragraph underneath is what you say if they ask for it — not what you say next.

1 · Why is this harder than a GROUP BY on an "overridden" flag?

Because a grouped rate tells you who overrode but nothing about whether overriding helped — and clinicians override on their least typical patients, so the comparison is confounded before you start.

The hard part is the counterfactual. An override is only meaningful against what would have happened to a comparable patient who was not overridden. Everything expensive in the design — as-of reconstruction, propensity matching, the balance and stability gates, the governance layer — exists to turn a misleading one-liner into a defensible comparison, or to suppress it honestly when it cannot be made defensible. And a rate invites the reader to compare clinicians as if it were performance, which is the third failure mode on top of the statistical two.

2 · Propensity matching only balances the covariates you measured. Doesn't that make every emitted delta potentially confounded?

Yes — and the design states it rather than hiding it: every surface is tagged observational, never causal, for exactly that reason.

What matching buys is the removal of confounding on the measured, pre-registered set, which is a real improvement over the raw comparison, and the balance gate refuses to headline a cell where even that measured balance is not achieved. The honest ceiling is precise: among matched patients balanced on the covariates we measured, overrides on this policy version were associated with a different ninety-day outcome by this interval, with these robustness checks passed. It cannot say the override caused the difference. "Trust here is bounded and stated, not claimed absolutely." If they push on caliper stability — no, stability shows the finding is not an artifact of one arbitrary matching radius; it says nothing about confounders never measured.

3 · Why matching rather than regression adjustment, inverse-probability weighting, difference-in-differences, or an instrument?

Because matching produces an explicit, inspectable control arm and a per-cell balance diagnostic you can gate on — and the alternatives either bury the comparability assumption inside a model or need an assumption this data does not support.

Regression adjustment and inverse-probability weighting can be more efficient, but they make "was this cell balanced?" a harder question to answer at the point of display — and on a politically charged surface the balance check itself has to be visible and auditable. Difference-in-differences needs a parallel-trends assumption that clinicians' selective overriding almost certainly violates, and a valid instrument for "chose to override" simply is not available. The framing to land: the design chooses the method whose limits it can state plainly over one whose limits it would have to hope hold.

4 · What happens to an override with no comparable control — dropped, or accounted for?

Excluded from the estimate and counted — every cell reports an n_unmatched, because silently dropping unmatchable overrides would bias the whole comparison toward the easy-to-match cases.

Those easy-to-match cases are the routine overrides, so dropping the rest quietly makes overriding look more ordinary than it is. Surfacing the count keeps the denominator honest about who the estimate could and could not speak for — and a rising unmatched rate is itself a signal that a cell's comparison is standing on thin support, which is one of the build-time diagnostics tracked alongside the SMD distribution and the suppression rate.

5 · Why suppress a fragile cell instead of showing it with a caveat — and what stops suppression being used to bury an inconvenient result?

Caveats are reliably under-read, and a fragile delta shown with a footnote is still a delta someone will quote — and suppression here is never silent, so it cannot be used to bury anything.

A suppressed cell still renders, with its reason — unmatched, unstable, or insufficient data — and its full denominators, so an absent headline is visible and accountable rather than a gap. The suppression rules are deterministic and pre-registered: they key off balance, caliper stability and the sample floor, never off whether the result is convenient. The thresholds are fixed in the seeded build and the gate decision is recorded on the cell, so nobody can tune one at request time, and the golden regression pins which cells suppress and why. Underneath it all is an asymmetry: a false emit costs far more than a false suppress, which is also why the binding evaluation metric is gate-check precision rather than recall.

6 · Why is the audit entry written before any row is read — and can you guarantee this dashboard never becomes a scorecard?

Audit first, because a log written after serving can be skipped by any path that errors mid-response — and no, I cannot guarantee it never becomes a scorecard; I can make that misuse hard, traceable and logged, which is what is actually enforced.

Writing the entry first — viewer, target, purpose, time, with the access-log identifier returned in the response — makes the access trail complete by construction rather than best-effort. There is no window in which someone is shown a clinician's data untracked. On the scorecard question, the enforced mitigations are: pseudonymous actor identifiers by default, leadership-and-self-view access, alphabetic and deliberately non-rank-sortable ordering, an audit entry per actor-level look, and an observational tag on every surface. A leader with legitimate access could still read a balanced table as a ranking. The controls raise the cost and the traceability of that. Honesty about that boundary is part of the governance, not a gap in it.

7 · Where is the language model in this system, and what is it forbidden to do?

There is exactly one call — an optional, post-hoc explainer that narrates a cell after every gate has already passed it — and it is forbidden to compute, re-rank, conclude, or reach for any fact outside the cell it was handed.

Statistics, gating, suppression, ordering and access control are all deterministic code. This is the clearest case in the portfolio of knowing when not to use a model. The claims live in governed UI copy backed by the typed cell, so a hallucinated figure would have no authority even if it appeared, and evaluation includes a faithfulness check — quoted numbers must match the cell, and the prose must carry no verdict language. If it is down, the user loses a sentence of narration and nothing else. Two things worth volunteering: this is not retrieval-augmented generation, because nothing is retrieved — the model gets one already-computed cell and no corpus to reach into. And temperature zero does not make a model's output deterministic; pinning model, prompt and temperature and hashing input and output buys drift detection and reproducible auditing, not generation determinism. The determinism lives in the structured cell and the gate code, never in the narrator.

8 · Name the single weakest assumption in the whole design — and what would you do differently?

That the pre-registered covariate set captures enough of why a clinician chose to override that matching on it yields comparable arms — and I would sequence the shared platform components ahead of the per-query work instead of in parallel.

Clinicians override on tacit signals: a patient's demeanor, a hunch, context that is not in any structured field. None of that is measured, so residual confounding by indication is the most likely way an emitted delta is wrong despite passing every gate. The pre-treatment-only rule and the observational framing mitigate it; nothing eliminates it, which is exactly why the query stays a signal to investigate a protocol rather than a verdict on a clinician. On what I would change: building against real tenant wrapper, matcher binary and exporter from day one would have avoided inline stand-ins that later had to be reconciled — and I would have invested earlier in match-quality observability, the SMD and unmatched-rate dashboards, because those tell you a cell is degrading before it produces a wrong number, and they are easy to add late and expensive to miss early.

If they ask for a result. "I own the design and the production rollout; I left before the outcome window closed, so the measured results are not mine to publish — every figure I can give you is a pre-registered acceptance target the system was built, reviewed and deployed against." Say it once, plainly, and move to the mechanism. Do not estimate, do not round, do not reconstruct a number from memory.

Where it transfers, if they ask

Anywhere you must answer "who deviated from the baseline, and did it work?" without turning the answer into a blame exercise — underwriting exceptions, site-reliability runbook deviations, fraud-operations overrides, content-moderation appeals. The transferable core is a matched observational comparison behind deterministic gates, governed access with an audit trail, and a language model kept away from the arithmetic. "The domain changes; the discipline — honest numbers about human judgment calls, gated and governed — does not."
