01 What is it?

One GROUP BY away from a false conclusion

Open with these two sentences

A clinical operations lead wants one number: of the patients we started on metformin, what share got their blood sugar down, and did adding a nutrition protocol move that share? It looks like a single GROUP BY, and a single GROUP BY would be wrong three ways at once — none of which shows up as a query error.

The situation, in plain language

Metformin is the first-line drug for type 2 diabetes. HbA1c — haemoglobin A1c, glycated haemoglobin — is the blood marker that reflects roughly three months of average blood sugar, so a drop of more than 1.0 is a real clinical improvement rather than a good day. A nutrition protocol is a structured dietary programme a care team can run alongside the drug.

So the person asking is a clinical operations lead deciding whether to fund the nutrition protocol across a programme, or a reviewer asking the platform to justify a claim it has already made. Both need the same thing: a comparative rate for each arm, carried with its uncertainty — not a verdict on whether the protocol works. Everything downstream exists to make that comparison honest rather than merely computable.

Why it is genuinely hard

The naive answer is one line of structured query language (SQL) (structured query language): filter to metformin starts, split by whether a nutrition protocol ran alongside, divide responders by the total. Three failure modes ride along with it.

Failure mode of the naive queryWhat actually goes wrong
Confounded comparison Nobody randomised this. Treatment here is a clinical choice, and the patients who also get a nutrition protocol are systematically different — often more engaged, earlier-stage, better-controlled at baseline. A raw with-versus-without gap measures who got the protocol as much as what it did.
False confidence
from small cells		 Split finely enough and some cells hold a handful of patients, where two lucky outcomes read as a confident 100%. The cell looks like every other cell on the dashboard.
Future leakage Country, programme phase and prior adherence all change over time — often because of the care pathway. Compute them from today's graph and you describe a past decision with facts that arrived after it, quietly inflating the apparent effect.

There is a fourth, quieter one. "Within 90 days" is doing two jobs at once. It is a maturity horizon — a patient whose 90 days have not elapsed by the snapshot has no outcome yet and must not be scored as a failure — and it is an outcome window, which requires deciding which HbA1c reading counts as the 90-day value. Handle either carelessly and the rate is biased before any statistics run.

The asymmetry the whole design turns on

A false emit costs far more than a false suppress. A confident headline that should have been withheld can change a clinician's protocol; a withheld number just sends someone to the drill-down. Every gate in the system is therefore tuned to withhold on doubt rather than publish on hope. If an interviewer asks for the single most dangerous failure mode, this is the answer, and it explains every other choice on the page.

Why this one query mattered beyond itself

Its shape — filter a cohort, reconstruct covariates as-of the decision, label a matured outcome, gate for balance, report a rate with a seeded confidence interval — is the shape of five sibling cohort queries that differ only in their filters. The controls proven here are the controls everywhere: a fix in one is a fix in all. That is the line that turns "I built a query" into "I set the standard the programme runs on".

02 How did we solve it?

Snapshot-only analytics, built overnight from a frozen past

Two rules shape everything else. First, nothing is computed from live graph state: a nightly snapshot freezes the world at a pinned as-of timestamp, and every number is built from that frozen state plus an as-of replay of the event spine. Second, the graph database is never on the serving path: the cohort is assembled with tenant-scoped columnar joins, not per-row graph traversal.

Three lanes, three cadences

The clearest way to hold the whole system in your head is as three lanes running at three different speeds, all inside the client's network.

LaneWhen it runsWhat it does
One-time hydration once, up front About nine years of legacy records — every drug start, nutrition protocol and HbA1c result the platform had ever seen — normalised, upconverted, quarantined on failure, content-addressed and replayed into the append-only spine, then proved before anything downstream is built.
Nightly build every night Freeze the snapshot at a pinned as-of, build the cohort, label the matured outcome, gate for balance, route to adjustment where needed, compute seeded intervals, and write every metric cell to columnar files.
Request path what a clinician waits on The metric application programming interface (API): build a canonical cache key, look it up in an in-process cache, fall back to a columnar scan of the pre-computed metric table on a miss, assert tenancy on every cell, return.

The upstream shape, and the second data path

The event spine sits on DynamoDB — an append-only log of drug starts, nutrition plans and HbA1c labs. One projection library reads it and writes two things: the decision graph, on Neptune, and a separate observation snapshot in Parquet columnar files.

That second path is the subtle one, and it is worth volunteering unprompted. The default projector emits an observation only when a decision references it. A matured 90-day HbA1c that no follow-up decision ever cited would simply vanish — biasing the denominator toward "someone responded" cases. So a second projection writes lab observations to their own snapshot, ungated by decision attachment, and the cohort reads both snapshots.

What the cohort builder actually reads

Three sources against one frozen manifest:

  • the decision snapshot, for metformin starts and structured features;
  • the ungated observation snapshot, for the HbA1c outcomes;
  • the event spine directly, for as-of covariate reconstruction by source-event sequence.

It joins those into a cohort table partitioned by tenant and snapshot hash, using DuckDB — an embedded columnar query engine — with no live reads and no per-row graph calls. It anchors treatment episodes and de-duplicates restarts, so a patient who stops and restarts metformin is not counted as two independent decisions. Each covariate is read as the latest valid value at or before the decision's event time, and every field on every row carries three things: its value, its as-of timestamp, and the event sequence it was read at. That is what makes the leak-free property auditable rather than asserted.

The outcome label is a contract, not a boolean

A shared outcome library — not per-query code — labels each cohort member and emits five things: the status, the attribution decision, the rule version, the distance in days, and the censoring reason. Statuses are explicit: mature, not-mature, missing, dropout, programme-switch, censored-by-a-later-decision. A missing lab is never counted as a zero. A versioned attribution rule — nearest same-domain decision before the outcome — stops two overlapping decisions both claiming the same HbA1c move.

The honesty ladder: the order is the design

A raw rate must survive a fixed sequence before it is allowed to be a headline. It reads like a statistician's chain of thought, but it is deterministic code and every exit is explicit.

  1. Sample-size floor. Every cell needs at least 30 mature patients. Below the floor the cell reports insufficient_data with its full denominator breakdown, never a rate.
  2. Balance gate. SMD across eight-plus pre-treatment covariates. All within 0.1 and the descriptive rate may stand.
  3. The branch. Any covariate above the gate and the cell routes to propensity matching plus IPW, and the adjusted rate becomes the displayed headline while the raw rate drops to a drill-down carrying an observational tag.
  4. Window sensitivity. The same matched cohort is scored through three pre-registered windows. A sign flip or a materially different magnitude withholds the headline.
  5. Emit. Rate plus seeded bootstrap CI, with an observational tag attached that records which path it came down: balance-gated if raw, adjustment-applied if matched.

The ordering is not cosmetic. The floor runs first because a balance statistic on six patients is meaningless. The balance gate runs before any headline rather than sitting in a methods footnote, because that is the difference between a control and a disclaimer.

The statistics, pinned

  • Bootstrap: 2,000 resamples, clustered by enrollment rather than by row, seeded from the snapshot manifest hash, percentile interval by default. Bias-corrected and accelerated (BCa) intervals are an opt-in, not the default.
  • Windows, pre-registered: A is −7 to +30 days, B is −14 to +45 days, C is 0 to +30 days. Fixed in the statistical plan before any number is computed.
  • Covariates, pre-registered: baseline HbA1c, age band, BMI, country, programme, phase, prior adherence, prior nutrition exposure — all reconstructed as-of the decision, so the balance check is itself leak-free.

The request path is a gauntlet of refusals

By query time there is almost no statistics left to compute; the cells were built overnight. What remains is a sequence of ways to say no: a complete cache key, a split allowlist, the sample-size floor, the balance gate, and a tenant-scope assertion on every cell, even on a cache hit.

The canonical cache key is sorted JavaScript Object Notation (JSON) (JavaScript Object Notation) that names every input which can change the answer:

  • account_id · workspace_id · controlunit_id — the three-field tenant scope, resolved from the caller's authenticated token, first in the key by construction
  • query namespace · split · filters — what was actually asked
  • metric-schema hash — responder rule, attribution rule version, censoring policy, windows, bootstrap settings
  • snapshot manifest hash — which frozen world it was computed from
  • source event-seq max — how much of the log that world contained
  • correction epoch — which generation of corrections it reflects

Library and statistical-plan versions also move a number; those are pinned per deploy and recorded with the result rather than enumerated as key fields. Tenancy is enforced at three layers at once: wrapped at the call site so the scope is injected rather than remembered, partitioned on disk so a job physically reads only its own partition, and asserted at write time and on every served cell. A cross-tenant cell or a partition mismatch fails closed and pages an operator; it is an incident, not a metric.

Freshness, and what happens when something moves

  • Snapshot age tiers. Under 24 hours serves normally; 24–48 hours serves with a visible staleness banner; past 48 hours the service blocks and escalates rather than serve quietly outdated statistics.
  • Projector lag. Beyond 30 minutes it trips the yellow tier, surfacing staleness well before the 24-hour boundary is even reached.
  • Corrections. A listener maps each spine correction through a dependency index to exactly the cohort and metric slices that depend on the affected event-sequence range, rebuilds only those, and bumps the correction epoch. Target: P95 under five minutes from correction to fresh cells.
  • Degradation is per cell. A thin stratum returns insufficient_data with its denominator breakdown while well-populated cells in the same response emit normally.

Hydration: five rules that keep nine years honest

This system was not switched on against an empty world, and that is the part most interviewers have never had to build. Hydration is a first-class, quarantine-first, proof-carrying pipeline.

  1. Event time is not ingestion time. Each legacy record carries two real-world moments — event_time_ms, when it actually happened, and observed_at_ms, when its result was observed — while separate received and written stamps record only when the load learned of it. The watermark runs in a distinct hydration mode, because a nine-year spread of "late" data is expected there; the small live-mode value would classify almost the entire corpus as too-late and drop it.
  2. Hydrating twice is a no-op. Content-addressed event identities plus conditional writes make the load idempotent and resumable. Re-running the whole pipeline over the same corpus yields a byte-identical spine and an identical snapshot-manifest hash — an executable proof of determinism, not an assurance.
  3. Nothing is silently coerced. Nine years means many legacy payload-schema versions; each gets an explicit upconverter, and any row that cannot be mapped to a known schema — or to a tenant — is quarantined with a reason, never defaulted.
  4. The same projection code runs for history and for live. Hydration and the live stream are two drivers over one projection library, not two implementations.
  5. The load is proof-carrying. Zero-event-loss reconciliation against the source, per-tenant and per-class counts, as-of spot checks against known-good historical reports, and a golden replay over a historical slice all gate the load before anything downstream is built. A fenced cutover then attaches the live stream with a proof of neither gap nor duplicate at the boundary.

What nine years buys this query specifically is maturity at scale. A 90-day responder rate needs decisions old enough for the horizon to have closed. With a deep back-catalogue the outcomes arrive already settled — hydrated, not awaited — so per-cell counts clear the thirty-patient floor across programmes and segments instead of collapsing into suppression. A confounder-gated comparison needs both arms populated in every cell it reports.

03 Why did we do what we did?

Every decision, and the alternative it beat

This is the section that separates a Staff answer from a Senior one. The architecture is recall; the tradeoffs are judgement. Quotes below are his own reasoning, verbatim from the design record.

Data architecture

Snapshot-only analytics, frozen at a pinned as-of

Instead of
Querying live graph state at read time.
Because
Freezing the world makes every number reproducible and lets every covariate be read as-of the decision. The cost — numbers are up to a day behind — becomes an explicit, surfaced SLA instead of a silent inconsistency.
Live state moves under you: two identical requests can disagree, and a covariate can change mid-query.

Remove the snapshot and three guarantees collapse at once: per-row traversal cost returns to the request path, reproducibility goes because live state drifts between calls, and the as-of guarantee goes because covariates silently reflect edits made after the decision.

The graph database feeds the nightly snapshot and nothing else

Instead of
Per-row graph traversal on the build path or the read path.
Because
The graph's job is projection, not query. Assembling the cohort with tenant-scoped columnar joins removes per-row graph cost entirely from both the nightly build and the request. Per-row graph traversal was one of the sixteen review findings.

A second, ungated observation projection

Instead of
Relying on the default projector, which emits an observation only when a decision references it.
Because
Without it, a matured outcome that nobody followed up on disappears from the denominator — and the ones nobody follows up on are not a random sample.
A matured 90-day HbA1c that no follow-up decision ever cited would then vanish, biasing the denominator toward "someone responded" cases.

Pre-compute every cell nightly

Instead of
Computing the gates, the match and the bootstrap per request.
Because
The honest work is expensive and must be deterministic. Doing it offline means the request path only looks up a cell whose gate decisions were already made and pinned, and every served number is a replayable function of the snapshot rather than a fresh, unrepeatable calculation.
Nothing statistical is computed while a clinician waits.

Embedded columnar engine over Parquet files

Instead of
A data warehouse.
Because
The workload is a bounded nightly batch of columnar joins over a frozen snapshot, not a high-concurrency serving store. An embedded engine over tenant-partitioned columnar files is enough, keeps the whole computation inside the client network, and carries no per-row graph cost. The bias is deliberately toward boring infrastructure that is cheap to reason about and fully reproducible.

Event time from the record; receive and write time pinned to a load fence

Instead of
Stamping ingestion time, or trusting the loader's wall clock.
Because
Both the as-of covariate reconstruction and the outcome window key off event time. An assertion enforces the separation on every historical row — event time must fall strictly before receive time — so a bug that quietly defaulted event time to load time fails the load rather than silently redating nine years of decisions.
Collapse a real-world moment into a load stamp and nine years of decisions would look as though they all happened on load day.

Quarantine anything unmappable, with a reason

Instead of
Defaulting an unknown schema or an unknown tenant to something plausible.
Because
Quarantined rows are counted and reported, so a data defect surfaces as a number rather than as a plausible-looking clinical finding months later.
A defaulted tenant would be a cross-tenant leak, the one failure this platform treats as unforgivable.

One projection library with two drivers

Instead of
A separate bulk-hydration implementation alongside the streaming one.
Because
History moves in bulk and the live stream moves through its streaming transport, but both call the exact same projection logic, so there is no second implementation to diverge from.
Were that a separate library rather than a separate driver, the hydrated graph would be a different product from the streaming one and every determinism guarantee would be theatre.

Statistics

Gate on SMD, not on a balance p-value

Instead of
A significance test for covariate balance.
Because
SMD is independent of sample size, so it flags the imbalance that actually biases the estimate regardless of how many patients are in the cell.
A p-value for balance shrinks as N grows, so it would wave through large-but-imbalanced cohorts — exactly the ones where confounding bites.

The adjusted rate becomes the headline

Instead of
Showing the raw rate as the headline with the adjustment in a footnote.
Because
Promoting the propensity-adjusted rate and demoting the raw one to an observational-tagged drill-down makes the honest number the default one seen — which is the whole point of doing the adjustment at all.
A footnote invites the reader to anchor on the flattering raw number and treat the adjustment as a caveat.

Matching plus IPW

Instead of
A regression adjustment, or a full structural causal model.
Because
Matching and weighting are transparent, deterministic, and easy to audit against a pinned covariate set, which suits a governed clinical surface better than a fitted model whose coefficients drift with the data. A causal model would promise more than observational data here can support and would be harder to defend line by line to a reviewer.
The design prefers a defensible correlation over an impressive-looking causal claim.

The matcher lives in a shared library

Instead of
Each of the sibling queries carrying its own matcher.
Because
Copied matchers drift on seed, caliper, replacement policy and covariate hashing, producing divergent matched cohorts and divergent intervals over time. A single matcher emits a match manifest over its inputs, so a sibling reuses a matched set only when every manifest field agrees, and rebuilds otherwise.

Cluster the bootstrap by enrollment

Instead of
Resampling rows.
Because
A patient contributes correlated rows — episodes, repeated labs — and row-level resampling treats them as independent, understating the interval. Resampling whole enrollment clusters preserves the within-patient correlation, so the interval reflects the real effective sample size.
Getting the resampling unit wrong is a quiet way to look more certain than the data warrants.

Seed the bootstrap from the snapshot manifest hash

Instead of
Wall-clock randomness, or a hard-coded seed someone could change.
Because
The same snapshot always yields the same CI and a different snapshot yields a different, correctly attributable one. Because the seed derives from the data, it cannot be shopped for a friendlier draw.
Reproducibility becomes a property you can rely on in a golden test, not a coincidence you hope for on re-run.

Percentile intervals by default

Instead of
BCa everywhere.
Because
Percentile is simpler, fully determined by the seed, and adequate for the headline rates here; BCa's bias-and-acceleration correction is an opt-in for the cases that need it. Pinning the default in a shared library is what stops each sibling query drifting to a different interval method and reporting a different CI for the same cohort.

Pre-register the windows and the threshold

Instead of
Letting an analyst choose an outcome window after seeing the data.
Because
The windows and the 0.1 threshold are pinned in the metric schema, so changing either moves a version hash and shows up in the golden regression. Any later change is visible and versioned rather than silent.
Pre-registration exists exactly so that no one can retune the gate after seeing which setting produces the nicer number.

Suppress fragile findings rather than show them with a wide interval

Instead of
Emitting the number with a caveat and a wide CI.
Because
Suppression with a visible denominator refuses the anchor while keeping the underlying data in a drill-down. All three windows stay available, so a genuinely time-dependent pattern remains fully visible to an analyst who reads them.
A wide interval is still a headline number a reader will anchor on.

The one-line rebuttal to "isn't that discarding signal?" — it withholds the headline, not the data.

Multiple-testing control by design, not by correction

Instead of
A false-discovery-rate correction over many comparisons.
Because
This query reports a small, pre-registered set of cells rather than mining hypotheses, and high-cardinality splits are rejected outright, which bounds the comparison count by construction. The sibling policy-friction query, which genuinely scans many policies, is where explicit false-discovery control lives.

Serving, tenancy and operations

An in-process cache, deliberately not Redis

Instead of
A distributed cache in front of the metric API. Redis is on the stack and is a tool he uses; it was simply not the right tool for this read path.
Because
What sits behind the cache is already a pre-computed columnar metric table, so the cache is an in-process convenience over data that is itself cheap and immutable for the life of a snapshot. Adding a network hop and a second store would buy nothing and add a place to go stale. The discipline transfers regardless of the store.
An in-process or columnar cache can serve a stale or cross-tenant cell just as easily as a distributed one if its key is under-specified.

This is a good answer to give confidently: the interviewer is often probing whether you reach for infrastructure reflexively.

A cache key that names every result-affecting input

Instead of
Keying on query plus filters and trusting a bare table version.
Because
Drop the tenant fields and a hit can serve another tenant's cell; drop the snapshot hash or the sequence maximum and you serve yesterday's numbers; drop the metric-schema hash and a definition change silently reuses stale cells. A cache key that omits a result-affecting input is not a performance detail — it is a way to serve a confidently wrong or foreign number.

This became a programme-wide requirement, and it started as a finding in this query's design review.

Correction epoch in the key, not a time-to-live

Instead of
Expiring cached cells on a TTL and hoping the window is short enough.
Because
Every cell built under the old epoch is logically retired the instant the rebuild completes, without scanning or walking the cache.
Freshness becomes a key comparison rather than a time-to-live guess.

Dependency-targeted rebuilds

Instead of
A full rebuild on every correction, or waiting for the next nightly run.
Because
Re-deriving nine years of cohorts on every correction would blow the freshness budget many times over; ignoring corrections until the next night would serve known-wrong numbers. The dependency index makes each rebuild proportional to the correction rather than to the corpus, which is what lets the system be fresh and correct at the same time.

If the dependency mapping were ever incomplete, the safe fallback is a broader rebuild. The recorded dependency set keeps the common case narrow; it is not the only thing standing between a correction and a stale cell.

Three-field tenant scope, enforced at three layers

Instead of
A single account check, or trusting one query filter.
Because
Account, workspace and control-unit travel together in every predicate, partition and assertion, so a slip at one layer is caught by another. Tenancy is also first in the cache key by construction, so two tenants can never collide on a cached cell.
One forgotten predicate leaks cross-tenant rows into a snapshot that then propagates forward into every downstream artifact — the failure is silent and permanent.

Why re-assert on a cache hit: the assertion, not the key's correctness, is the last line of defence. And the stance on failure is blunt — serving nothing is strictly better than serving a single row from the wrong tenant.

Hard block past 48 hours

Instead of
Serving a warned, stale number indefinitely.
Because
For population statistics that inform protocol decisions, a silently stale rate is more dangerous than an absent one, and the 24–48 hour banner already gives a graded warning well before the block. If a client's risk tolerance differed, the tier boundaries are policy.
Past two days the design chooses a visible outage over an invisible error.

Evaluate on seeded synthetic cohorts

Instead of
Validating against real clinical outcomes.
Because
The thing under test is the gate logic, and on synthetic data the correct decision of every gate — suppress, adjust, emit — is known by construction. Real data with unknown ground truth cannot prove that the balance gate routes when it should.
Green means the machinery is correct. It makes no claim that a given correlation is clinically meaningful.

Ten pinned golden cells must reproduce within one percentage point across rebuilds, and the fixtures deliberately include late arrivals, corrections and supersede chains — the paths most likely to break silently. The rate band is loose on purpose because floating-point equality across platforms is brittle; the exporter hash test, by contrast, is exact.

The boundary: no model, on purpose

Zero LLM calls anywhere in the pipeline

Instead of
Narrating the result, or letting a model summarise the cells.
Because
Rates, gates, matching, censoring, suppression and confidence intervals are arithmetic and a statistical plan. The platform's self-hosted model seam runs in-network for the three sibling queries that actually generate prose; this one calls into none of it.
A single paraphrase is all it takes to turn a hedged number into a confident-sounding one.
The engineering judgment is to leave a capable model on the shelf because the task does not call for it.

No retrieval layer either

Instead of
A RAG surface over the clinical corpus to give the number context.
Because
An outcome-correlation cell earns its meaning from the balance gate, the suppression floor and the observational tag the code already stamped on it. There is nothing for a retrieval step to fetch or a model to narrate — it would be surface area without a job, and every new surface is a new place for an ungrounded claim to enter.

The number can never become a clinician scorecard

Instead of
Allowing arbitrary split dimensions.
Because
This query compares treatment arms, not actors. Clinician and site enter only as as-of covariates to balance, never as the headline split; the split allowlist rejects high-cardinality actor splits; the 30-patient floor and the observational tag keep even an authorised drill-down from surfacing a rankable per-actor rate; and no league-table ordering is emitted. These are mitigations that make misuse hard and visible — not a claim that it is impossible.

The review gauntlet

Before anything shipped, the design went through a structured adversarial self-review under a Principal-engineer lens. It drew blood.

  1. Design review · round 1 Ship-with-changes, sixteen findings, and an unambiguous "do not implement as written." Tenant scoping not fail-closed · current-state country leakage · an ad-hoc outcome label that dropped missing labs · a weak and late confounding gate · repeated decisions treated as independent · unhandled censoring · an arbitrary outcome window · an under-specified bootstrap · per-row graph traversal · split-cardinality explosion · an under-keyed cache.
  2. Design revision · v2 Eleven findings absorbed directly into the design: the snapshot-only builder, tenant assertion at write and at serve, the schema-upconverted drug code, treatment-episode anchoring, as-of covariate reconstruction, pre-registered windows, the SMD gate, the propensity-routed headline, the clustered bootstrap, the canonical cache key, and correction-event invalidation. Five more were deliberately promoted into shared platform components — the tenant wrapper, the outcome library, the ungated observation projection, the deterministic exporter replay test, and the four-field event-time schema — so every sibling cohort query inherits the same fixes.
  3. Programme impact The review minted two programme-wide requirements: canonical cache-key completeness, now standard across the analytics programme, and a shared, seeded bootstrap-CI library, which has since landed at the platform layer. All five promoted components have since landed, and this query binds to the shared version of each.
  4. Shipped The hardened v2 is the design that shipped to production, after executive sign-off. The final adversarial design-review sign-off re-runs now that the five shared platform components have landed, so approval is earned against the running platform rather than declared on paper.
Hardened & shipped 16/16 findings dispositioned verdict: ship-with-changes
Do not let this get garbled

"Ship-with-changes" and "approval pending" describe the internal design-review sign-off, not the deployment. The system is live in production and serving governed clinical-ops dashboards. If an interviewer reads it as "so it never shipped", correct it plainly: it shipped on executive sign-off with all sixteen findings dispositioned, and the remaining gate is a re-run of the design review against the now-landed shared components. Across the whole programme, only the precedent-search query ever reached approved-with-nits — that is how strict the bar was.

What to concede first

Volunteering the limits is what makes the rest credible. Four, in the order worth offering them:

  • Balance is gated before adjustment, not re-gated after it. The SMD check decides whether a raw rate may be a headline; a cell that fails routes to matching plus IPW and the adjusted rate is emitted. The pipeline does not re-measure post-adjustment SMD and withhold a cell that matching could not balance. So the balanced tag on an adjusted cell records that adjustment was applied, not that residual imbalance was verified under the threshold. A cell with poor covariate overlap can be adjusted and still emitted.
  • Exporter-versus-projector agreement is structural, not asserted. One projection library with two drivers means there is no second implementation to diverge from, and the golden replay proves the export path is deterministic — but determinism is not agreement. A projector that diverged deterministically would replay green every time. There is no standing fixture that re-exports as-of and diffs it against live-projector state.
  • Aggregate reconstruction is mitigated, not solved. The split allowlist and the sample floor make differencing a suppressed cell hard, but this query does not claim formal differential-privacy accounting across correlated reads.
  • It cannot tell you the protocol worked. Matching balances only the covariates we measured; unmeasured confounding is never ruled out, and no statistical step in the pipeline upgrades a correlation to a cause.

And when asked what would age first: the pre-registered covariate set and the windows. They encode today's understanding of what confounds a nutrition-protocol effect. The risk is bounded by versioning — any change moves a schema hash and re-runs the golden regression — but the choice of what to balance is a modelling judgement, and modelling judgements age faster than infrastructure does.

04 Architecture

Four diagrams, and how to redraw each on a whiteboard

These are the diagrams from the case study, unaltered. Each walkthrough says what flows where, what each gate does, and what breaks without it.

Fig 1 · The whole system: three lanes and one boundary

What flows where

Draw one big box first: the client's private network. Everything except the clinician's browser lives inside it. The only thing crossing the boundary is a suppressed aggregate cell travelling over encrypted transport. No patient row ever leaves.

Inside, draw three sub-boxes stacked by cadence. One-time: the legacy corpus flows through upconvert-and-quarantine into the append-only event spine. Nightly: the spine projects into the decision graph, the graph feeds the snapshot exporter at a pinned as-of, the exporter feeds the cohort builder — which also reads the spine directly for as-of covariates, and that second arrow is the one people forget — the builder feeds the aggregator, and the aggregator writes the metric table. Request: the browser hits the metric API, which reads its in-process cache and, on a miss, scans the metric table.

What each gate does

Two dotted lines carry the argument. The decision graph has a dotted line to the API labelled not on request path, and the model seam has one labelled not called here. Both are present on the platform; neither is touched by anything a clinician waits on. The correction listener dots into the aggregator, which is how freshness is reclaimed between nightly runs.

What breaks without it

Remove the boundary box and the whole regulatory story goes: PHI would need to leave the network for any external service. Solid-line the graph into the API and you have reintroduced per-row traversal cost on the hot path plus live-state drift. Solid-line the model seam and every number becomes something a paraphrase can soften.

Fig 2 · The nightly build, stage by stage

What flows where

A straight chain of five stages with a sixth hanging off the side. The snapshot is frozen at the as-of timestamp and carries three identifiers forward — the manifest, the snapshot hash, and the source event-sequence maximum. Those three travel with every number to the end of the line.

The cohort builder joins tenant-scoped columnar data with no per-row graph queries, reconstructs every covariate as-of the decision, anchors treatment episodes and de-duplicates restarts. The outcome library then attaches a five-part label to each member: status, attribution decision, rule version, distance in days, censoring reason. The aggregator gates on SMD over eight-plus covariates, routes imbalanced cells to propensity adjustment, and computes the seeded bootstrap CI clustered by enrollment. Serving reads the result. Evaluation replays golden fixtures that deliberately include late arrivals, corrections and supersede chains.

What each gate does

The dotted correction listener touches three stages, not one — the builder, the aggregator, and the serving layer — because a correction has to re-derive the cohort, re-run the statistics on it, and retire the cached cells. Drawing only one dotted arrow is the common mistake.

What breaks without it

Without episode anchoring and restart de-duplication, one patient's stop-and-restart becomes two independent decisions — a review finding in its own right, and it inflates apparent sample size. Without the shared outcome library the labelling drifts per query and a missing lab quietly becomes a zero. Without the correction listener the system is either always a night stale or always rebuilding nine years of cohorts.

Fig 3 · The honesty ladder

What flows where

This is the diagram to draw if you can only draw one. A raw rate enters at the top and meets three diamonds in a fixed order: the sample-size floor, the balance gate, the window-stability check. Two of the three exit sideways into suppression. The balance gate does not suppress — it branches, and that asymmetry is the point.

What each gate does

Floor: fewer than 30 mature patients in any cell and the cell reports insufficient data with its full denominator breakdown. Not a rounded rate, not a rate with an asterisk — no rate. Balance gate: if all eight-plus covariates sit within 0.1 SMD the descriptive rate stands; if any exceeds it, the cell routes to propensity matching plus IPW and the adjusted rate becomes the displayed headline, with the raw rate demoted to a tagged drill-down. Window check: the finding must agree across three pre-registered windows; a sign flip or a materially different magnitude withholds the headline. Whatever survives is emitted with a bootstrap CI and an observational tag recording which path it came down.

What breaks without it

Drop the floor and a two-patient cohort reports a confident 100%. Drop the balance gate and you are publishing selection effects as treatment effects. Drop the window check and an analyst can quietly publish whichever window told the better story — the agreement gate makes that impossible by construction rather than by policy. Ordering matters too: run balance before the floor and you are computing a balance statistic on six patients.

If asked which gate would go under delivery pressure, the answer is the window sweep, because its data survives in the drill-down and its absence degrades to a caveated headline rather than a wrong one. The floor and the balance gate are not negotiable: dropping either produces a confidently wrong number.

Fig 4 · Event-time provenance

What flows where

Two boxes side by side. On the left, the two timestamps that come from the record: when the drug start or lab actually happened, and when the lab result was observed. On the right, the two the loader stamps — received and written — pinned to a single load-fence constant, not to the wall clock.

Only the left-hand pair does work. Event time drives the as-of sort, with source event-sequence breaking ties, and that ordering drives as-of inclusion: a covariate is rebuilt from the latest value at or before the decision instant. Observed-at drives the 90-day outcome window and is explicitly not a sort key. The right-hand pair feeds only a guard.

What each gate does

The guard asserts on every historical row that event time falls strictly before receive time. A row that fails is quarantined. That single assertion is what makes "we preserved event time" a checked property rather than a claim: a bug that silently defaulted event time to load time fails the load instead of redating nine years of decisions to import day.

What breaks without it

Everything this query rests on. Every as-of covariate — baseline HbA1c, programme, prior adherence at the decision — would collapse onto load day, so the confounding controls would be measuring nothing while still returning confident-looking numbers. And the outcome window would start from the wrong origin, so a decade-old decision would be scored against the wrong labs. This is the rule the query cannot survive without, which is why it earns its own picture.

05 Likely questions

Headline first, detail only if asked

The bold line is the whole answer. Stop there and let them pull. Everything under it is what you offer if the follow-up comes.

Q1 · screening

Why isn't this just a GROUP BY?

Because nobody randomised who got the nutrition protocol, so a raw with-versus-without gap measures who received it as much as what it did.

Two more ways the same query is wrong. Split finely enough and some cells hold a handful of patients where two lucky outcomes read as a confident 100%. And covariates read from today's graph describe a past decision with facts that arrived after it — country, programme phase and prior adherence all change, often because of the care pathway. Three ways to be wrong at once, and none of them shows up as a query error.

Q2 · the core mechanism

What stops an analyst picking the outcome window that flatters the result?

The three windows are pre-registered before any number is computed, and a finding has to agree across all three or its headline is withheld.

The same matched cohort is scored through window A at −7 to +30 days, B at −14 to +45, and C at 0 to +30. If the sign flips or the magnitude shifts materially, the cell is marked window-sensitive and no headline is emitted; all three results stay in the drill-down. It withholds the headline, not the data. The threshold and the windows are pinned in the metric schema, so changing either moves a version hash and shows up in the golden regression — a retune after the fact is visible and versioned, never silent.

Q3 · adversarial

Matching only balances the covariates you measured — so isn't your "balanced" headline still a dressed-up correlation?

Yes, and the system never claims otherwise: every emitted number carries an observational tag, and no statistical step in the pipeline upgrades a correlation to a cause.

What the machinery buys is a fairer comparison on known factors, plus reproducibility, plus a refusal to publish where the data cannot support a headline. Unmeasured confounding is never ruled out. I'd also concede the sharper version before you ask it: balance is gated before adjustment, not re-gated after. A cell that fails the gate routes to matching plus IPW and is emitted, but the pipeline does not re-measure post-adjustment SMD, so the balanced tag records that adjustment was applied, not that residual imbalance was verified. A cell with poor covariate overlap can be adjusted and still emitted. A randomised trial answers the causal question this deliberately does not; a target-trial emulation would be the natural next step, and it would have to sit on exactly this substrate.

Q4 · systems

What's in your cache key, and why does dropping one field become a correctness bug?

Every input that can change the answer is in the key, because a cache key that omits one is not a performance detail — it is a way to serve a confidently wrong or foreign number.

The canonical key is sorted JSON: the three tenant fields first — account, workspace, control-unit — then the query namespace, the split, the filters, the metric-schema hash, the snapshot manifest hash, the source event-sequence maximum, and the correction epoch. Drop the tenant fields and a hit can serve another tenant's cell. Drop the snapshot hash or the sequence maximum and you serve yesterday's numbers. Drop the metric-schema hash and a definition change silently reuses stale cells. The correction epoch is what replaces a time-to-live: every cell built under the old epoch is logically retired the instant a targeted rebuild completes, so freshness is a key comparison rather than a guess. Library and statistical-plan versions are pinned per deploy and recorded with the result rather than keyed. And the tenant assertion still runs per cell on a hit, because the assertion — not the key's correctness — is the last line of defence. This became a programme-wide requirement; it started as a finding in this query's review.

Q5 · statistics

What happens to a patient who drops out at day 60?

They are censored under a Kaplan–Meier policy with an explicit status, never scored as a non-responder.

Outcomes carry six statuses — mature, not-mature, missing, dropout, programme-switch, censored-by-a-later-decision — and each cell reports its mature, missing and censored denominators separately. Treating a censored exit as a zero would bias the rate downward; treating a missing lab as a zero would do the same. The choice of denominator is where an observational rate is most quietly manipulated, so all the buckets are surfaced rather than collapsed into one ratio. There's also a versioned attribution rule — nearest same-domain decision before the outcome — so two overlapping decisions cannot both claim the same HbA1c move.

Q6 · the boundary

You're an artificial intelligence (AI) engineer — why is there no model anywhere in this pipeline?

Because a correlation carried with its confidence interval is already a finished, auditable statement, and a single paraphrase is all it takes to turn a hedged number into a confident-sounding one.

Rates, gates, matching, censoring, suppression and intervals are arithmetic and a statistical plan; none of that needs generation. The platform has a self-hosted model seam running inside the network for the three sibling queries that actually produce prose — this one calls into none of it. There's no retrieval layer either, and that's a judgement rather than an omission: an outcome-correlation cell earns its meaning from the balance gate, the suppression floor and the observational tag the code already stamped on it, so there is nothing for a retrieval step to fetch or a model to narrate. If a future dashboard did want narration, it would run on the self-hosted model inside the network boundary; an external provider would be permitted only on a de-identified payload, and a contractual no-retention promise is not a network-residency control.

Q7 · operations

An upstream correction lands at ten in the morning. Walk me through what happens.

A listener maps the correction through a dependency index to exactly the slices that depend on it, rebuilds only those, and bumps the correction epoch — target P95 under five minutes from correction to fresh cells.

Every derived row records the source-event-sequence range and dependency set it was built from, registered in a dependency index. The listener resolves the correction's sequence against that index, the targeted rebuild recomputes cohort and metric for the dependency set only, and the epoch bump retires every cached key carrying the old epoch without walking the cache. The alternative designs are both bad: re-deriving nine years of cohorts on every correction blows the freshness budget many times over, and waiting for the next nightly run serves known-wrong numbers. If the dependency mapping were ever incomplete, the safe fallback is a broader rebuild — the index keeps the common case narrow, it isn't the only thing standing between a correction and a stale cell. Separately, staleness is graded and visible: normal under 24 hours, a banner at 24–48, and past 48 the service blocks and escalates rather than serve quietly outdated statistics.

Q8 · judgement

You're behind schedule. Which gate do you relax?

The window sweep, because its data survives in the drill-down and its absence degrades to a caveated headline rather than a wrong one.

The sample-size floor and the balance gate are not negotiable: dropping either produces a confidently wrong number, which is the one failure mode the whole design is built against. Knowing which gate is load-bearing and which merely improves an already-honest answer is the point of the ordering. I'd also want to be explicit about what "behind schedule" cost elsewhere: when the shared platform components weren't ready, the query's contract was wired against stubs returning representative payloads — the tenant wrapper, the outcome and bootstrap libraries, the ungated observation projection, the deterministic exporter — so this query's own logic could be built and tested before those dependencies landed, which they since have. The gating dependencies were named per build step so nothing was quietly assumed present.
