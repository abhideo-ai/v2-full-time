Deep-dive Q&A · Outcome correlation

Did the nutrition protocol help — or did healthier patients just get it?

A senior-engineer question bank for the outcome-correlation query: the cohort comparison that asks whether pairing metformin with a nutrition protocol moved HbA1c further than metformin alone. These are the questions a Principal engineer or CTO actually asks in a design review — confounding, censoring, as-of correctness, tenancy, caching, and the discipline that keeps a correlation from being sold as a cause. Answers are grounded in the design of record and invent no measured result.

Theme 01 The question and why it is hard

Why a one-line clinical question is a statistics problem wearing a SQL disguise.

screeningWhat is this query actually asking, in plain terms?

Among patients whose metformin start is recorded, what fraction achieved an HbA1c reduction greater than 1.0 within about 90 days — split by whether a nutrition protocol ran alongside the metformin. The deliverable is a comparative rate for each arm with a confidence interval, not a verdict on whether the protocol worked. Everything downstream exists to make that comparison honest rather than merely computable.

screeningWhy isn't this just a GROUP BY?

A naive group-by measures who got the protocol as much as what the protocol did, because the arms aren't randomised. Split finely enough and some cells hold a handful of patients where two lucky outcomes read as a confident 100%. And covariates read from today's graph describe a past decision with facts that arrived after it. Three ways to be wrong at once, and none of them shows up as a query error.

deepWhat is the single most dangerous failure mode here?

A false emit: a confident headline that should have been suppressed, because a clinician may change a protocol on the strength of it. The entire design is built around that asymmetry — a false emit costs far more than a false suppress — so every gate is tuned to withhold on doubt rather than to publish on hope.

deepWhy is confounding treated as the first-class problem rather than an afterthought?

Treatment here is a clinical choice, not a coin flip: patients who also receive a nutrition protocol tend to be more engaged, earlier-stage, and better-controlled at baseline. Without balancing those pre-treatment differences, a with-versus-without gap partly measures selection into the protocol. That is why a balance gate runs before any raw rate is allowed to be a headline, rather than sitting in a methods footnote.

deepWhy call this the "canonical" query of the whole analytics programme?

Its shape — filter a cohort, reconstruct covariates as-of the decision, label a matured outcome, gate for balance, report a rate with a seeded confidence interval — is the shape of five sibling cohort queries that differ only in their filters. So the controls proven here (as-of reconstruction, the sample floor, the balance gate, the seeded bootstrap) are the controls everywhere; a fix in one is a fix in all.

deepWhy is "within 90 days" harder than it first appears?

Ninety days is doing two jobs at once. It is a maturity horizon — a patient whose 90 days haven't elapsed by the snapshot has no outcome yet and must not be scored as a failure — and it is an outcome window — you must define which HbA1c reading counts as the 90-day value. Handle either carelessly and the rate is biased before any statistics run.

adversarialIsn't the honest answer just "you can't know this without a randomised trial"?

Largely, yes — and the system says so out loud: every number is tagged observational, and matching balances only the covariates we measured. The value on offer is a disciplined, reproducible, suppression-gated correlation carried with its uncertainty, which is a legitimate decision-support artifact. It is explicitly not a substitute for a trial, and no surface pretends it is.

adversarialIf an effect is genuinely real, why would the system ever refuse to show it?

Because at the moment of computation a real association on a cell below the sample floor, or one that flips sign across the pre-registered windows, is indistinguishable from noise or from window-shopping. Suppression protects the reader from a finding the data cannot yet support; the underlying rows stay in a drill-down, so a genuine association resurfaces once the cohort matures or the sensitivity settles.

Theme 02 Architecture and data flow

Snapshot-only analytics built from a frozen past, with the graph database kept off the read path.

screeningWhat are the moving parts, end to end?

An append-only event spine feeds a projection that writes both a decision graph and a second, ungated observation snapshot. A nightly export freezes the world at a pinned as-of timestamp into columnar files. A tenant-scoped builder assembles the cohort, a shared library labels the matured outcome, an aggregator gates for balance and computes seeded bootstrap intervals, and a metric service serves the pre-computed cells. Six stages, one frozen manifest tying them together.

screeningWhere does the graph database sit on the request path?

Nowhere. The decision graph feeds only the nightly snapshot; the cohort is assembled with columnar joins over the frozen files, and the serving endpoint reads pre-computed cells. There is no per-row graph traversal at analytics time and none while a clinician waits — the graph's job is projection, not query.

screeningWhy snapshot-only rather than querying live state?

Live state moves under you: two identical requests can disagree, and a covariate can change mid-query. Freezing the world at a pinned as-of timestamp makes every number reproducible and lets every covariate be read as-of the decision. The cost — the numbers are a snapshot behind — becomes an explicit, surfaced SLA instead of a silent inconsistency.

deepWhy is there a second, ungated observation projection?

The default projector emits an observation only when a decision references it. A matured 90-day HbA1c that no follow-up decision ever cited would then vanish, biasing the denominator toward "someone responded" cases. A separate observation snapshot, not gated on decision attachment, keeps those unpaired outcomes visible, so the responder rate is computed over who was actually eligible. KQ-OVR-008

deepWhat does the cohort builder actually read?

Three sources against one frozen manifest: the decision snapshot for metformin starts and structured features, the ungated observation snapshot for the HbA1c outcomes, and the event spine directly for as-of covariate reconstruction via source-event sequence. It joins those into a cohort table partitioned by tenant and snapshot hash — no live reads, no per-row graph calls.

deepWhy pre-compute every cell nightly instead of computing on request?

The honest work — the balance gate, the propensity route, the seeded bootstrap, the three-window sweep — is expensive and must be deterministic. Doing it offline means the request path only looks up a cell whose gate decisions were already made and pinned. Nothing statistical is computed while a clinician waits, and every served number is a replayable function of the snapshot rather than a fresh, unrepeatable calculation.

deepWhy an embedded columnar engine over Parquet rather than a data warehouse?

The workload is a bounded nightly batch of columnar joins over a frozen snapshot, not a high-concurrency serving store. An embedded engine over tenant-partitioned columnar files is enough, keeps the whole computation inside the client network, and carries no per-row graph cost. The design's bias is boring infrastructure that is cheap to reason about and fully reproducible.

adversarialA snapshot means every number is up to a day stale — is that acceptable for clinical use?

For cohort statistics, yes, and it is preferable: these are population rates informing protocol decisions, not a patient's live chart, and the staleness is bounded and shown (green under 24h, a banner at 24–48h, a block past 48h). A correction that genuinely matters does not wait for the next nightly build — a dependency-targeted rebuild lands within a five-minute P95 target. Freshness where it counts is reclaimed without giving up reproducibility.

adversarialWhat actually breaks if you removed the snapshot and queried the graph directly at read time?

Three guarantees collapse. You reintroduce per-row traversal cost on the request path; you lose reproducibility because live state drifts between calls; and you lose the as-of guarantee, so covariates silently reflect edits made after the decision. The projection-and-snapshot split is precisely what buys determinism, cheap reads, and future-leak safety — removing it forfeits all three.

Theme 03 As-of correctness and future leakage

Describing a past decision only with facts that were already true when it was made.

screeningWhat is "future leakage" in one sentence?

Characterising a past decision with facts that only became true after it — using today's country, program phase, or adherence to describe a metformin start from two years ago — which quietly inflates the apparent effect because the "cause" is being described with its own downstream consequences.

screeningHow is each covariate reconstructed?

As-of the decision's event time, from the spine's source-event sequence: the latest valid value at or before that moment, never current graph state. Every field on every row carries three things — its value, its as-of timestamp, and the event sequence it was read at — so the reconstruction is auditable rather than asserted. KQ-OVR-001

deepWhich timestamp anchors the as-of read, and why that one specifically?

The decision's event time — when the clinical event happened — not when it was captured or written to the store. Anchoring on capture or write time would let ingestion lag reorder history, changing which observations count as "before" the decision and therefore which value each covariate takes. Event time is the only clock under which the as-of read is stable across replays.

deepThe platform splits four event-time fields — how does this query use each?

Event time anchors the covariate as-of reads and the origin of the outcome window; observed-at scopes which HbA1c reading is the 90-day value; received-at together with the manifest's allowed-lateness watermark bounds how long a late arrival can still change an already-built cell. Each derived row persists which basis it used, so no builder silently picks its own clock. KQ-OVR-011

deepWhy persist the as-of timestamp and source sequence on every single row?

So freshness is provable on replay. Given any response, you can show exactly which observation each covariate came from and that none of them post-dates the decision. Without those pins, "we reconstructed as-of" is an unverifiable claim; with them, a reviewer can reconstruct the cohort and confirm the leak-free property field by field.

deepHow does the outcome stay a "90-day" outcome without leaking the future?

The responder lab is drawn from an observed-at window bracketing the 90-day mark after the decision's event time, and the maturity gate additionally requires that horizon to have closed before the snapshot's as-of. So the outcome sits firmly in the decision's future but entirely in the snapshot's past — future relative to the treatment, historical relative to the frozen world the query is allowed to see.

deepIf a correction rewrites a historical covariate, does a past answer change — and is that itself a leak?

The answer changes, and that is correct rather than a leak: the correction says the as-of value was recorded wrong. The dependency index rebuilds exactly the affected cells and bumps a correction epoch so stale cached cells retire, and the before-and-after manifest hashes make the change auditable. What would be a leak is using a later value to describe an earlier decision — which as-of reconstruction structurally prevents.

adversarialBaseline HbA1c is measured right around the decision — how do you stop the treatment contaminating the baseline?

Baseline is reconstructed as-of the decision's event time, so a reading taken after the metformin start cannot be used as baseline no matter how close it is. If no valid at-or-before HbA1c exists, the row simply lacks a baseline covariate — it does not borrow the nearest later value, which would fold part of the treatment response into the "starting point."

adversarialIsn't as-of reconstruction just re-deriving today's value most of the time — so why pay for it on every field?

For genuinely static attributes, maybe. But the covariates that matter here — program, phase, prior adherence, prior nutrition exposure — change precisely because of the care pathway, which makes their current values the most leaked of all. Paying for as-of on every field is cheaper and safer than maintaining a list of which fields are "provably stable" and being wrong about one of them.

Theme 04 Tenancy and isolation

A three-field tenant scope enforced at the call site, on disk, and on every served cell.

screeningWhat is the tenant identity?

A three-field scope — account_id, workspace_id, and controlunit_id — resolved from the caller's authenticated token. It is never a single account check; every query predicate, disk partition, and runtime assertion carries all three fields together. KQ-OVR-002

screeningWhere is tenancy enforced?

At three layers at once: wrapped at the call site so the scope is injected rather than remembered, partitioned on disk so a job physically reads only its own partition, and asserted at write time and on every served cell — including cache hits. A cross-tenant cell or partition mismatch fails closed and pages an operator.

deepWhy belt-and-braces rather than trusting a single query filter?

One forgotten predicate leaks cross-tenant rows into a snapshot that then propagates forward into every downstream artifact — the failure is silent and permanent. Defense in depth means a slip at one layer is caught by another: the disk partition constrains what can even be scanned, and the runtime assertion refuses to return any row lacking the caller's full scope. Per-query discipline alone is too fragile for data this sensitive.

deepWhat actually happens on a cross-tenant assertion failure?

It is handled as an incident, not a metric: the response is refused and an operator is paged; a partition mismatch pages too. The system's stance is that serving nothing is strictly better than serving a single row from the wrong tenant, so the failure mode is a loud page rather than a degraded result.

deepWhy re-assert tenancy even on a cache hit?

A hit still returns stored cells, and if the key or store were ever wrong a hit could hand back another tenant's numbers. Re-checking the three-field scope per cell on the hot path makes the cache incapable of leaking even under a key-construction bug — the assertion, not the key's correctness, is the last line of defense.

deepHow does the cache key itself encode tenancy?

The canonical key is sorted JSON that begins with the three tenant fields, then the query namespace, split, filters, and the version and freshness markers. Because tenancy is part of the key by construction, two tenants can never collide on the same cached cell — isolation is structural, and the per-cell assertion is a redundant second guarantee on top of it.

adversarialCould a high-cardinality split be abused to isolate down to a single patient within a tenant?

Splits must come from an allowlist; a high-cardinality split is rejected before any cell is computed, and every emitted cell must clear the 30-patient floor or suppress. Together those make a single-patient cell unreachable through the split dimension — you can neither request the fine split nor receive a below-floor cell.

adversarialIs a snapshot shared in one file store across tenants a leak risk in itself?

The snapshot is partitioned by the three-field scope plus the snapshot hash, and the builder filters to the caller's partition before it loads anything, so a tenant's job never scans another's rows. The shared thing is the storage layout and format, not the data; isolation comes from partitioning plus assertion, not from trusting that queries are well-behaved.

Theme 05 The core mechanism — confounding and matching

The honesty ladder: what a raw rate must survive before it is allowed to be a headline.

screeningWhat is the "honesty ladder"?

A fixed decision procedure a raw rate must pass to become a headline: sample-size floor, then balance gate, then a descriptive-or-adjusted branch, then a window-sensitivity check, then emit with an observational tag. It reads like a statistician's chain of thought, but it is deterministic code and every exit — suppress, adjust, emit — is explicit.

screeningWhat triggers a switch from a descriptive rate to an adjusted one?

Covariate imbalance above the gate. If any of the eight-plus pre-treatment covariates shows a standardised mean difference above 0.1 between arms, the cell routes to a propensity-matched estimate with inverse-probability weighting, and the adjusted rate becomes the displayed headline while the raw rate drops to a tagged drill-down.

deepWhat is SMD, and why gate on it rather than on a balance p-value?

Standardised mean difference measures how far apart the arms sit on a covariate in pooled-standard-deviation units, independent of sample size. A p-value for balance shrinks as N grows, so it would wave through large-but-imbalanced cohorts — exactly the ones where confounding bites. SMD flags the imbalance that actually biases the estimate regardless of how many patients are in the cell.

deepWhich covariates are balanced, and why those?

Baseline HbA1c, age band, BMI, country, program, phase, prior adherence, and prior nutrition exposure — the pre-treatment factors that plausibly drive both who receives a nutrition protocol and who improves. All are reconstructed as-of the decision, so the balance check is itself leak-free; balancing on a covariate that had leaked the future would launder the bias rather than remove it.

deepWhy does the adjusted rate become the headline instead of living in a footnote?

A footnote invites the reader to anchor on the flattering raw number and treat the adjustment as a caveat. Promoting the propensity-adjusted rate to the headline — and demoting the raw rate to an observational-tagged drill-down — makes the honest number the default one seen, which is the whole point of doing the adjustment.

deepWhat is inverse-probability weighting adding on top of matching?

Weighting each subject by the inverse of their probability of treatment makes the weighted arms resemble a common covariate distribution; paired with matching it absorbs residual imbalance the match itself didn't fully remove. Both steps are deterministic given the pinned covariate set and seed, so the adjusted headline is reproducible rather than a one-off model fit.

deepWhy does the matcher belong to a shared library rather than living in this query?

Three sibling queries also run propensity matching; if each copied its own matcher, the seed, caliper, replacement policy, and covariate hashing would drift, producing divergent matched cohorts and divergent intervals over time. A single matcher that emits a match manifest over its inputs lets a sibling reuse a matched set only when every manifest field agrees, and rebuild otherwise. KQ-OVR-009

adversarialMatching only balances measured covariates — so isn't the "balanced" headline still just a dressed-up correlation?

Yes, and the system never claims otherwise. Matching balances the covariates we measured; unmeasured confounding is never ruled out, and no amount of statistical machinery upgrades a correlation to a cause. Every surface tags the result observational, so the adjustment buys a fairer comparison on known factors — not a causal license.

adversarialWho chose the 0.1 SMD threshold and the three windows — couldn't those be tuned to flatter a result?

They are pre-registered in the statistical plan and pinned in the metric schema, so changing either moves a version hash and shows up in the golden regression. Pre-registration exists exactly so that no one can retune the gate after seeing which setting produces the nicer number; the threshold is fixed before the data is looked at, and any later change is visible and versioned rather than silent.

Theme 06 Statistics, gates and suppression

Small-cell floors, seeded intervals, honest denominators, and a bias toward withholding.

screeningWhat is the sample-size floor, and what happens below it?

Every cell needs at least 30 mature patients. Below that, the cell reports insufficient_data with its full denominator breakdown rather than a rate. This is the guard that stops a two-patient cell reporting a confident 100% — the floor refuses the number, it does not quietly round it.

screeningHow are the confidence intervals computed?

By a shared, seeded bootstrap: 2,000 resamples, clustered by enrollment rather than by row, seeded from the snapshot manifest hash, percentile interval by default. Every response carries the hashes needed to replay the interval, so the CI is reproducible rather than a one-time draw. KQ-OVR-013

deepWhy cluster the bootstrap by enrollment instead of resampling rows?

A patient can contribute correlated rows — episodes, repeated labs — and row-level resampling treats them as independent, understating the interval. Resampling whole enrollment clusters preserves the within-patient correlation, so the interval reflects the real effective sample size rather than an inflated one. Getting the resampling unit wrong is a quiet way to look more certain than the data warrants.

deepWhy seed the bootstrap from the snapshot manifest hash?

So the interval is a deterministic function of the frozen data, not of wall-clock randomness. The same snapshot always yields the same CI, and a different snapshot yields a different, correctly attributable one. Reproducibility becomes a property you can rely on in a golden test, not a coincidence you hope for on re-run.

deepWhy percentile intervals by default rather than BCa?

Percentile is simpler, fully determined by the seed, and adequate for the headline rates here; BCa's bias-and-acceleration correction is an opt-in for the cases that need it. Pinning the default in a shared library is what stops each sibling query from drifting to a different interval method and reporting a different CI for the same cohort.

deepThe denominator has several buckets — mature, missing, censored, not-yet-mature — why surface all of them?

The choice of denominator is where an observational rate is most quietly manipulated. Reporting mature responders over a transparent breakdown of missing labs and censored exits lets the reader see what was excluded and why, instead of trusting a single ratio. A missing lab is never silently counted as a zero, and a dropout is never treated as a failure — both are visible categories, not hidden assumptions.

deepHow is censoring handled when a patient drops out or switches programs before day 90?

Outcomes carry explicit statuses — mature, not-mature, missing, dropout, program-switch, censored-by-a-later-decision — under a Kaplan-Meier censoring policy, so a patient who exited before the horizon is censored rather than scored as a non-responder. Treating a censored exit as a zero would bias the rate downward; making the exit an explicit status keeps the denominator honest. KQ-OVR-004

adversarialEight covariates times several splits is a lot of comparisons — where is the multiple-testing control?

This query reports a small, pre-registered set of cells rather than mining many hypotheses, and high-cardinality splits are rejected outright, which bounds the comparison count by design. The sibling policy-friction query, which genuinely scans many policies, is where explicit false-discovery control lives; here the defenses are pre-registration and the split allowlist rather than an after-the-fact correction.

adversarialSuppressing window-sensitive findings could hide a genuinely time-dependent effect — isn't that discarding signal?

It withholds the headline, not the data. All three windows stay in the drill-down, so a real time-dependent pattern is fully visible to an analyst who reads them. What is refused is the single confident number that a time-dependent effect cannot honestly support — the fragility is surfaced, not deleted.

Theme 07 Caching, determinism and versioning

A cache key that names every result-affecting input, and a pipeline reproducible byte-for-byte.

screeningWhat goes into the metric-cache key?

A canonical sorted key over the three tenant fields, the query namespace, the split, the filters, the metric schema hash, the snapshot manifest hash, the source-event-seq max, and the correction epoch. Every data input that can change the result is in the key; nothing result-affecting is left to a bare table version. The library and statistical-plan versions that also move a number are pinned per deploy and recorded with the result rather than enumerated as key fields. KQ-OVR-012

screeningIs this a Redis cache?

No — it is an in-process cache in front of pre-computed columnar metric tables, with no per-request Redis layer for this query. The completeness discipline still applies: an in-process or columnar cache can serve a stale or cross-tenant cell just as easily as a distributed one if its key is under-specified.

deepWhy does dropping any one of those key fields become a correctness bug?

Drop the tenant fields and a hit can serve another tenant's cell; drop the snapshot hash or seq-max and you serve yesterday's numbers; drop the metric schema hash and a definition change silently reuses stale cells. A cache key that omits a result-affecting input is not a performance detail — it is a way to serve a confidently wrong or foreign number, which is why the review made key completeness a programme-wide requirement.

deepWhat makes the whole pipeline reproducible byte-for-byte?

A deterministic exporter — the same as-of, replayed from a shuffled spine with late arrivals, corrections, and supersede chains, must yield the same columnar hash — plus deterministic ordering in the builder, a seeded bootstrap, and pinned library versions. Given a manifest hash, every downstream number is a pure function of it, which is what lets a golden test assert exact reproduction. KQ-OVR-010

deepWhat is the correction epoch, and why is it in the cache key?

It is a counter bumped whenever a targeted rebuild lands after a spine correction. Putting it in the key means every cell built under the old epoch is logically retired the instant the rebuild completes, without scanning or walking the cache. Freshness becomes a key comparison rather than a time-to-live guess, so corrections invalidate exactly and immediately.

deepHow is the metric definition itself versioned?

The responder rule, the attribution rule version, the censoring policy, the windows, and the bootstrap settings are all folded into a metric schema hash carried on every response. Changing any of them changes the hash, invalidates dependent cache entries, and surfaces as a golden-regression diff — so a definition change can never masquerade as the same number computed differently.

adversarialA seeded bootstrap is reproducible, but is it correct — couldn't a bad seed give a misleading interval?

The seed fixes which resamples are drawn, not the statistic being estimated. Across 2,000 resamples the interval is a stable estimate whatever the particular seed, and because the seed is derived from the data it cannot be shopped for a friendlier draw. Reproducibility and correctness are separate properties; the design has both, and confuses neither for the other.

adversarialIf two snapshots differ only by an irrelevant late arrival, you get a new hash and a full cache miss — isn't that wasteful churn?

The nightly snapshot is the unit of change, so a fresh manifest once a day is expected, not churn. Within a day, only corrections invalidate, and those are dependency-scoped rather than global. The design trades a bounded amount of recompute for the guarantee that no stale or mis-keyed cell is ever served — a trade it makes deliberately, because a wrong cached number is far more expensive than a recomputed one.

Theme 08 Historical hydration — nine years

What a deep, event-time-faithful history actually buys a 90-day responder rate.

screeningWhat does about nine years of hydrated history buy this query specifically?

Matured outcomes at scale. A 90-day responder rate needs decisions old enough for the horizon to have closed, so a deep history means the cohort is not limited to the last quarter's metformin starts. Cells clear the sample floor, rarer strata become analysable, and the balance gate has enough patients in each arm to route rather than suppress.

screeningHow is legacy data brought in without corrupting the timeline?

It is replayed into the append-only spine with original event time preserved, upconverted to the current schema, quarantined if it fails validation, and content-addressed on write. Nothing is coerced to "now," so a decade-old decision keeps the clock it was actually made under.

deepWhy does preserving original event time matter so much for a rate this old?

Because both the as-of covariate reconstruction and the outcome window key off event time. If hydration stamped ingestion time instead, an old decision would be reordered against its own labs, its covariates would be read at the wrong point, and its 90-day window would start from the wrong origin. Event-time fidelity is precisely what makes a historical row usable rather than misleading.

deepWhy must the hydration load "prove itself" before any cohort number is built?

A silent hydration defect — a dropped lab class, a mis-upconverted drug code — biases every downstream rate without any query being wrong. Validating the load (counts, schema conformance, quarantine of bad rows) before it feeds cohorts stops a data defect from surfacing later as a plausible-looking clinical finding, which is the hardest kind of error to catch after the fact.

deepHow does hydration interact with the drug-code schema-versioning problem?

Metformin may have been recorded under an older code in earlier years. Hydration upconverts to a canonical drug code at decision, and the cohort filter additionally screens by payload schema version so pre-supported rows are excluded rather than silently mismatched. Without both, the metformin cohort would be undercounted for exactly the oldest, most-matured years that the query most needs.

adversarialNine years spans guideline and protocol changes — doesn't pooling that era produce a meaningless average?

It can, which is why phase, program, and prior exposure are as-of covariates in the balance gate, and why the split allowlist and pre-registration constrain what is compared. Era effects surface as covariate imbalance, which routes the cell to propensity adjustment rather than letting a raw pooled rate stand as the headline. The system reports a balanced, caveated correlation for a defined cohort — not a timeless truth about metformin.

adversarialOlder records surely have more missing labs — doesn't that bias the responder rate toward recent years?

Missingness is made visible rather than assumed away. The ungated observation snapshot keeps unpaired labs countable, and each cell reports missing and censored denominators separately from mature responders. A reader can therefore see whether a cell's rate rests on thin, old, or well-observed data, and the sample floor blocks any cell too sparse to interpret.

adversarialIf hydration is one-time, how do you re-run it deterministically when a bug surfaces years later?

Because the load is content-addressed and event-time-preserving, re-hydrating the same corpus yields the same spine rows, and the deterministic exporter yields the same snapshot hash. A hydration fix is a correction event flowing through the same dependency-targeted rebuild path as any other correction — not a mysterious full re-import whose output no one can predict.

Theme 09 Governance, PHI and the LLM boundary

The clearest case of knowing when not to use a model — and what actually keeps the number honest.

screeningHow many language models are in this pipeline?

Zero. Rates, gates, matching, censoring, suppression, and confidence intervals are all deterministic, seeded code. This is the portfolio's clearest case of knowing when not to reach for a model — the numbers need arithmetic and a statistical plan, not generation.

screeningWhere does patient data live, and what leaves the network?

Everything runs inside the client's network boundary; the only thing that crosses to the clinician's browser is a suppressed aggregate cell over TLS. No patient row ever leaves the boundary, which is why the analytical core was chosen to be self-contained rather than reliant on any external service.

deepWhy is not using an LLM the right call here rather than a missed opportunity?

A correlation carried with its confidence interval is already a finished, auditable statement. Handing it to a model to reword adds nothing the interval does not say more honestly, and a single paraphrase is all it takes to turn a hedged number into a confident-sounding one. The engineering judgment is to leave a capable model on the shelf because the task does not call for it.

deepWhy is there no retrieval-augmented layer either?

An outcome-correlation cell earns its meaning from the balance gate, the suppression floor, and the observational tag the code already stamped on it. There is nothing for a retrieval step to fetch or a model to narrate — adding retrieval would be surface area without a job, and every new surface is a new place for an ungrounded claim to enter.

deepIf a future dashboard did want a narrated summary, what would the platform require?

Any narration would run on the platform's self-hosted model inside the network boundary, so clinical text never leaves the network; that is the single, explicit production path. An external provider would be permitted only on a de-identified payload — and a contractual no-retention promise is not a network-residency control, so it would not substitute for keeping the text in-network. Determinism, where it is needed, would come from the structured statistical chain, not from the narrator.

deepWhat stops this number from becoming a clinician or site scorecard?

This query compares treatment arms, not actors: clinician and site enter only as as-of covariates to balance, never as the headline split. The split dimension is an allowlist that rejects high-cardinality actor splits, the 30-patient floor plus the observational tag keep even an authorised drill-down from surfacing a rankable per-actor rate, and the query emits no league-table ordering. These are mitigations that make actor-ranking misuse hard and visible — not a claim that it is impossible.

deepWhat makes each served number auditable after the fact?

The response envelope carries the snapshot manifest hash, the source-event-seq max, the correction epoch, the metric schema hash, and the definition block — responder rule, attribution rule version, censoring policy, windows, and bootstrap settings. Given a response you can re-derive the exact cohort and interval; nothing is served as a number without a provenance trail that reconstructs it.

adversarial"Observational, not causal" reads like a disclaimer to dodge accountability — what actually enforces it?

It is enforced in the pipeline, not the footer. The balance gate must pass or the cell routes to adjustment; the raw rate is demoted behind an observational tag; the window sweep withholds fragile headlines; the sample floor suppresses thin cells. The caveat is the visible output of those controls, not a caption added afterward to excuse a number the machinery didn't actually discipline.

adversarialAggregates can still leak — how do you keep a suppressed cell from being reconstructed by differencing two queries?

The primary defenses are the split allowlist and the sample floor, which bound how finely a cohort can be diced and refuse any cell under 30 mature patients, so the fine-grained cells a differencing attack needs are simply not emitted. This is a genuine hard problem — formal differential-privacy accounting across correlated queries is not something this query claims to solve — and the honest position is that the allowlist and floor make casual reconstruction hard rather than proving it impossible.

Theme 10 Failure modes, degradation and operations

Graded staleness, dependency-scoped rebuilds, and per-cell degradation rather than all-or-nothing.

screeningWhat happens when the snapshot goes stale?

Freshness tiers: under 24 hours the service serves normally, 24–48 hours it serves with a visible staleness banner, and past 48 hours it blocks and escalates rather than serve quietly outdated statistics. Staleness is a graded, surfaced signal, not a silent condition a reader has to guess at.

screeningWhat happens when a spine correction lands?

A correction listener maps it through a dependency index to exactly the cohort and metric slices that depend on the affected event-seq range, rebuilds only those, and bumps the correction epoch — with a P95 target of under five minutes from correction to fresh cells. KQ-OVR-007

deepWhy a dependency-targeted rebuild instead of a full nightly rebuild on every correction?

Re-deriving nine years of cohorts on every correction would blow the freshness budget many times over; ignoring corrections until the next night would serve known-wrong numbers. The dependency index makes each rebuild proportional to the correction rather than to the corpus, which is what lets the system be fresh and correct at the same time instead of trading one for the other.

deepHow does the system behave when the projector falls behind?

Projector lag beyond 30 minutes trips the yellow tier, surfacing a staleness signal before the 24-hour snapshot boundary is even reached. The point is to degrade visibly and early — an operator and a reader both see the warning while the numbers are still only slightly behind, rather than discovering a silent cliff.

deepDoes the whole query fail when one stratum is too thin, or does it degrade gracefully?

Suppression is per cell. A thin stratum returns insufficient_data with its denominator breakdown while well-populated cells in the same response emit normally. The query degrades cell-by-cell, so one sparse corner never takes down the answer for the strata that are perfectly well observed.

deepWhat would you alert on operationally?

Per-cell N and CI width, sign-flip alerts across windows on refresh, snapshot age and projector lag against the tiers, correction-to-fresh latency against the five-minute P95, and any tenant assertion failure. Each alert maps one-to-one to a way a served number could quietly go wrong, so the monitoring covers the failure modes the gates are meant to catch rather than generic host metrics.

deepHow does the system know which cells a given correction touches?

Every derived row records the source-event-seq range and dependency set it was built from, registered in a dependency index; the listener resolves the correction's sequence against that index to find the affected slices. If the mapping were ever incomplete, the safe fallback is a broader rebuild — the recorded dependency set is what keeps the common case narrow rather than the only thing standing between a correction and a stale cell.

adversarialWhat if the deterministic exporter and the live projector disagree — which wins, and how would you even notice?

The snapshot is the system of record for every served number, so the exporter wins by construction. But the honest answer to how you would notice is not the golden replay, and conflating the two is exactly how a divergence survives a green suite. Replay shuffles the spine, injects late arrivals, corrections and supersede chains, exports twice at the same as-of, and requires the same columnar hash. That proves the export path is deterministic — it re-runs one path, so it cannot compare that path against the live projector. A projector that diverged deterministically would replay green every time.

What actually keeps the two from drifting is structural rather than assertive: history and the live stream are two drivers over one projection library, not two implementations, so there is no second copy of the projection logic to disagree with; the ungated observation projection is that same shared code; and projector lag is watched against the freshness tiers. The checks that compare projected state against an outside truth — zero-event-loss reconciliation and as-of spot checks against known-good historical reports — gate the hydration load, not continuous live drift. That residual is a stated limit, not a solved problem, and it is in the honest gaps below.

adversarialIf the 48-hour block trips during an incident, clinicians lose the dashboard entirely — is hard-blocking really safer than a warned stale number?

For population statistics that inform protocol decisions, a silently stale rate is more dangerous than an absent one, and the 24–48h banner already gives a graded warning well before the block. The block is deliberate: past two days the design chooses a visible outage over an invisible error. If a client's risk tolerance differed, the tier boundaries are policy — but the default errs toward not misleading anyone.

Theme 11 Evaluation and acceptance criteria

Acceptance gates, not vibes — proven on seeded cohorts where every gate's correct decision is known.

screeningWhat are the headline acceptance gates?

At least 30 mature patients per cell; SMD ≤ 0.1 before any raw headline; three pre-registered windows; a seeded 2,000-resample bootstrap; snapshot freshness tiers; correction freshness at P95 ≤ 5 minutes; zero tolerance on tenant safety; and a golden regression that fails on more than a one-point drift. Each is a pre-registered target the design was reviewed and built against.

screeningWhat is the golden regression?

Ten pinned cells with seeded fixtures that must reproduce within one percentage point across rebuilds. The fixtures deliberately include late arrivals, corrections, and supersede chains inherited from the shared exporter replay test, so the check exercises the hard paths, not just the happy one.

deepWhy evaluate on seeded synthetic cohorts rather than real outcomes?

Because the thing under test is the gate logic, and on synthetic data the correct decision of every gate — suppress, adjust, emit — is known by construction. You can prove the balance gate routes when it should and the floor suppresses when it should, which real data with unknown ground truth cannot do. Evaluation certifies the honesty of the machinery, not the truth of any particular clinical result.

deepWhy is the golden tolerance a percentage-point band rather than an exact hash?

The cell rates are floating-point statistics, so exact-equality across platforms would be brittle; a one-point band catches a logic regression while tolerating benign numerical noise. The exporter hash test, by contrast, is exact — because the columnar bytes should be identical — so structural determinism is checked precisely even where the statistical band is deliberately loose.

deepWhy do the golden fixtures specifically include corrections and supersede chains?

Those are the paths most likely to break silently: a correction that fails to invalidate, a superseded decision still counted in a cohort. Baking them into the pinned fixtures means every CI run proves the invalidation and supersede semantics rather than assuming them — the failure that would otherwise only appear months later, on a real correction, is forced to appear now.

deepHow do you evaluate "tenant safety" when the target is zero?

It is a page-out, not a percentage. The test deliberately issues a mis-scoped request and asserts that it fails closed rather than returning a foreign cell — so the acceptance bar is "the assertion fired," not "the leakage rate was low." A cross-tenant cell is an incident by definition, and the eval treats it that way.

adversarialPassing on synthetic cohorts proves the code, not the clinical validity — so what does "green" actually mean?

Green means the machinery is correct: gates fire, numbers replay, tenancy holds, corrections propagate. It makes no claim that a given correlation is clinically meaningful — which is exactly why every emitted number is tagged observational. The evaluation is honest about its own scope: it certifies the pipeline's discipline, and explicitly not the truth of any result the pipeline produces.

adversarialA one-point drift tolerance could mask a real regression that happens to land under a point — isn't that a hole?

It is a deliberate trade against floating-point flakiness, backed by the exact exporter-hash test and the seeded bootstrap, so structural regressions surface elsewhere even when a rate moves less than a point. If sub-point precision ever mattered for a particular cell, that cell's fixture can pin a tighter band — the tolerance is a per-fixture policy, not a global blind spot.

Theme 12 Tradeoffs, alternatives, and second thoughts

Where the design spends its budget, what it deliberately gives up, and what would age first.

screeningWhat is the biggest single tradeoff in the whole design?

Freshness for correctness. Snapshot-only analytics is up to a day behind live state, and the design accepts that in exchange for reproducibility, as-of safety, and no per-row graph cost on the request path. The freshness that actually matters — a landed correction — is clawed back through dependency-targeted rebuilds rather than by giving up the snapshot.

deepWhy matching plus IPW rather than a regression adjustment or a full causal model?

Matching and weighting are transparent, deterministic, and easy to audit against a pinned covariate set, which suits a governed clinical surface better than a fitted model whose coefficients drift with the data. A structural causal model would promise more than observational data here can support and would be harder to defend line-by-line to a reviewer. The design prefers a defensible correlation over an impressive-looking causal claim.

deepWhy suppress fragile findings rather than show them with a wide interval?

A wide interval is still a headline number a reader will anchor on. Suppression with a visible denominator refuses the anchor while keeping the underlying data in a drill-down. For a decision-support surface, the cost of a confident-looking fragile number outweighs the information lost by withholding the headline — the reader who wants the raw cells can still open them.

deepWhat would you build first if the shared platform components weren't ready?

Exactly what the design did: wire the query's contract against stubs that returned representative payloads for the then-platform-gated pieces — the tenant wrapper, the shared outcome and bootstrap libraries, the ungated observation projection, the deterministic exporter — so the query's own logic could be built and tested before those dependencies landed, which they since have. The gating dependencies were named per build step so nothing was quietly assumed present.

deepIf you had to relax one gate under delivery pressure, which one, and why?

The window sweep is the most negotiable, because its data survives in the drill-down and its absence degrades to a caveated headline rather than a wrong one. The sample-size floor and the balance gate are not negotiable, because dropping either produces a confidently wrong number. Knowing which gate is load-bearing — and which merely improves an already-honest answer — is the point of the ordering.

adversarialWould a randomised trial or a target-trial emulation make this whole apparatus unnecessary?

A trial would answer the causal question this query deliberately does not, and a target-trial emulation would be a natural next step on the same as-of, matured-outcome substrate. But neither removes the need for the machinery here — the floor, the balance gate, the reproducibility, the censoring — which is exactly what a credible emulation would have to sit on top of. This design is the foundation such an effort would build from, not a competitor to it.

adversarialThe design is "ship-with-changes," not approved — why should a client trust an unapproved design in production?

Because the honest status is itself the trust signal. The hardened v2 shipped after executive sign-off with all sixteen review findings dispositioned, and the one remaining gate is that final design-review approval re-runs now that five shared platform components have landed — approval earned against the running platform rather than declared on paper. A design that names its open dependencies is safer than one that claims a closure it hasn't earned.

adversarialWhat is the single thing most likely to be wrong in this design a year from now?

The pre-registered covariate set and windows. They encode today's understanding of what confounds a nutrition-protocol effect, and a genuinely better set would change the balance gate's verdicts. The risk is bounded by versioning — any change moves a schema hash and re-runs the golden regression — but the choice of what to balance is a modelling judgment, and modelling judgments age faster than infrastructure does.

Honest gaps

What this query cannot answer, and what is still gated

The system is deliberately honest about its own limits, and so is this page.

  • It cannot tell you the protocol worked. The output is a balanced, suppression-gated correlation, never a cause. Matching balances only the covariates we measured; unmeasured confounding is never ruled out, and no statistical step in the pipeline upgrades a correlation to a causal claim.
  • It stays silent where the data is thin or fragile. Cells below the 30-patient floor return insufficient-data rather than a rate, and findings that flip sign or shift materially across the pre-registered windows have their headline withheld. Those answers exist only as drill-down data, by design.
  • Aggregate-reconstruction is mitigated, not solved. The split allowlist and sample floor make differencing a suppressed cell hard, but this query does not claim formal differential-privacy accounting across correlated reads.
  • Balance is gated before adjustment, not re-gated after it. The SMD ≤ 0.1 check decides whether a raw rate may be a headline; a cell that fails it routes to propensity matching + IPW and the adjusted rate is emitted. The pipeline does not re-measure post-adjustment SMD and withhold a cell that matching could not balance. So the SMD-balanced tag on an adjusted cell records that adjustment was applied — not that residual imbalance was verified to sit under the threshold. A cell with poor covariate overlap can be adjusted and still emitted.
  • Exporter-versus-projector agreement is structural, not asserted. One projection library with two drivers means there is no second implementation to diverge from, and the golden replay proves the export path is deterministic — but determinism is not agreement. This query does not claim a standing fixture that re-exports as-of and diffs it against live-projector state; a divergence that survived the shared library would not be caught by the replay hash.
  • The real design-review verdict is ship-with-changes, not approved. The v1 adversarial review returned sixteen findings and an unambiguous "do not implement as written." The hardened v2 absorbed eleven of them locally and promoted five into shared platform components, and shipped to production on executive sign-off — but final design-review approval has not been granted. It re-runs now that those shared components have landed, so the honest status is ship-with-changes. Across the whole programme, only the precedent-search query ever reached approved-with-nits.
  • Five dependencies have since landed at the platform layer. The three-field tenant wrapper, the shared outcome-labelling library, the ungated observation projection, the deterministic exporter replay test, and the four-field event-time schema all now live at the platform layer, and this query binds to the shared version of each. The review also minted two programme-wide requirements — canonical cache-key completeness, now standard across the programme, and a shared seeded bootstrap library, which has since landed.
  • There is no runnable code sample for this query. Within the portfolio, only the precedent-search, override-intelligence, and cross-domain orchestration queries ship runnable examples; this one is a design-and-production case study, not an executable artifact.
