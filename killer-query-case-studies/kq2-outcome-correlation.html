Case study · ML system design · Healthcare

Did adding a nutrition protocol help — or did healthier patients just get it?

For a chronic-care platform covering 80+ conditions, I designed the outcome-correlation system — the cohort-comparison query that five other production analytics queries are built on — and took it to production. Every rate, confidence interval, and suppression decision is computed by deterministic, seeded, replayable code; no language model touches a number, because none is needed. The same discipline applies anywhere you compare outcomes across groups people didn't choose at random: clinical effectiveness, program and policy evaluation, insurance and claims outcomes.

01 The problem

One GROUP BY away from a false conclusion

The naive answer is one GROUP BY away — filter to metformin starts, split by whether a nutrition protocol ran alongside, divide responders by the total. And it would be wrong three ways at once.

  • Confounded comparison. The patients who also get a nutrition protocol are systematically different — often more engaged, earlier-stage, with better baseline control. A raw with-versus-without gap measures who got the protocol as much as what it did.
  • False confidence from small cells. Split finely enough and some cells hold a handful of patients, where two lucky outcomes read as a confident 100%.
  • Future leakage. Country, program phase, and prior adherence all change over time. Compute them from today's graph and you describe a past decision with facts that arrived after it — quietly inflating the apparent effect.

So the design treats correlation-versus-causation as the first-class problem it is. This is also the canonical shape of the whole analytics programme: filter a cohort, reconstruct covariates as-of the decision, label a matured outcome, gate for balance, and report a rate with a seeded confidence interval. Five other production queries are variants of it — so the controls built here are the controls everywhere.

02 The architecture

Snapshot-only analytics, built from a frozen past

Two rules shape everything. First, nothing is computed from live graph state: a nightly snapshot freezes the world at a pinned as-of timestamp, and every number is built from that frozen state plus an as-of replay of the event spine. Second, the graph database is never on the serving path — the cohort is assembled with tenant-scoped columnar joins, not per-row graph traversal.

That second data path is the subtle one. An outcome that matured — a 90-day HbA1c — must be counted even when no follow-up decision was ever attached to it. So a second projection writes lab observations to their own snapshot, ungated by decision attachment; the cohort reads both the decision snapshot and the observation snapshot.

The build reconstructs each cohort member's pre-treatment covariates as-of the decision, labels the 90-day outcome through a shared outcome library, gates for balance, and pre-computes every metric cell to Parquet. Corrections don't trigger a full rebuild — a listener maps each spine correction to the metric slices that depend on it and rebuilds only those, P95 under five minutes.

03 Hydration

Nine years of matured outcomes, replayed into the spine

This system was not switched on against an empty world. Roughly nine years of historical records — every drug start, nutrition protocol, and HbA1c result the platform had ever seen — were hydrated through the append-only spine before a single cohort rate was computed. Hydration is a first-class, quarantine-first, proof-carrying pipeline, not a throwaway backfill, and it is where a 90-day outcome question earns the right to be answered at all.

Five rules keep the replayed history honest — and they are the same rules the live stream obeys:

  • Event time is not ingestion time. Each legacy record carries two real-world moments — event_time_ms, when it actually happened, and observed_at_ms, when its result was observed (the lab-result timestamp this query's 90-day HbA1c window keys off) — while separate received and written stamps record only when the load learned of it. Collapse a real-world moment into a load stamp and nine years of decisions would look as though they all happened on load day, voiding every as-of covariate reconstruction this query leans on. The watermark runs in a distinct hydration mode where a nine-year spread of "late" data is expected, not the small live-mode value that would classify almost the entire corpus as too-late and drop it.
  • Hydrating twice is a no-op. Content-addressed event identities plus conditional writes make the load idempotent and resumable; re-running the whole pipeline over the same corpus yields a byte-identical spine and an identical snapshot-manifest hash. That is an executable proof of determinism, not an assurance.
  • Nothing is silently coerced. Nine years means many legacy payload-schema versions; each gets an explicit upconverter, and any row that cannot be mapped to a known schema — or to a tenant — is quarantined with a reason, never defaulted. A defaulted tenant would be a cross-tenant leak, the one failure this platform treats as unforgivable.
  • The same projection code runs for history and for live. Hydration and the live stream are two drivers over one projection library — history moves in bulk, the live stream through its streaming transport — but both call the exact same projection logic. Were that a separate library rather than a separate driver, the hydrated graph would be a different product from the streaming one and every determinism guarantee would be theatre.
  • The load is proof-carrying. Zero-event-loss reconciliation against the source, per-tenant and per-class counts, as-of spot checks against known-good historical reports, and a golden replay over a historical slice all gate the load before anything downstream is built. A fenced cutover then attaches the live stream with a proof of neither gap nor duplicate at the boundary.

The first rule is the one this query cannot survive without, so it is worth drawing. Every legacy record carries two real-world moments — when a drug start or lab actually happened, and when its result was observed — and the loader keeps both. The two moments it does not trust are its own: the instant it received a row and the instant it wrote one are pinned to a single load-fence constant, not to the wall clock, and survive only as provenance. As-of order is therefore sorted by real event time, with the source event sequence breaking ties — never by the order the loader happened to write things.

An assertion enforces the separation on every historical row: event time must fall strictly before receive time, so a bug that quietly defaulted event time to load time fails the load rather than silently dating nine years of decisions to the day they were imported. Without that guard, every as-of covariate this query reconstructs — baseline HbA1c, program, prior adherence at the decision — would collapse onto load day, and the confounding controls would be measuring nothing.

For this query in particular, what nine years buys is maturity at scale: the 90-day outcomes arrive already settled — hydrated, not awaited — so per-cell counts clear the thirty-patient floor across programmes and segments instead of collapsing into suppression. A confounder-gated comparison needs both arms populated in every cell it reports; without a deep back-catalogue of matured outcomes, most cells would never survive the sample-size gate long enough to be compared at all.

04 The honesty ladder

What a raw rate must survive to become a headline

The heart of the system is a fixed decision procedure a raw rate must pass before it is allowed to be a headline. It reads like a statistician's chain of thought — but it is deterministic code, and every exit is explicit.

The rule is deliberately strict where it matters most. Where covariate imbalance exceeds the gate — measured as a standardised mean difference across eight-plus pre-treatment covariates — the propensity-adjusted rate doesn't sit in a footnote. It becomes the displayed headline, and the raw descriptive rate is demoted to a drill-down carrying an observational tag. Windows are pre-registered before any number is seen; a sign flip or a clinically material shift across them withholds the headline rather than letting the analyst pick the flattering window after the fact.

That last step earns its own picture, because it is the system's defence against the oldest move in observational analysis — trying windows until one flatters the result. The three outcome windows are pre-registered: fixed in the statistical plan before any number is computed. The same matched cohort is then scored through all three, and only a finding that agrees across them is allowed to be a headline.

If the windows disagreed and the code emitted anyway, an analyst could quietly publish whichever window told the better story. The agreement gate makes that impossible by construction: a fragile finding degrades to a suppressed, drill-down-only result rather than a confident claim.

05 The query path

By query time, the honest work is already done

At query time there is almost no statistics left to compute — the cells were built overnight. What remains is a gauntlet of refusals: a complete cache key, a split allowlist, the sample-size floor, the balance gate, and a tenant-scope assertion — account, workspace, and control-unit, resolved from the caller's auth, never a single field — on every cell, even on a cache hit.

The flowchart above is the full decision tree; the common case is far shorter. Because every cell was built overnight, the path a clinician actually waits on is a cache read — and even that cheapest path refuses to skip the tenant check.

This query is where two programme-wide requirements were born. The first is cache-key completeness: a metric cache key that omits the snapshot hash, the source-event-seq high-water mark, the metric-schema hash, or the correction epoch will happily serve a stale or wrong number on a cache hit. The canonical key pins all of them, plus the tenant scope — account, workspace, and control-unit — and the exact split and filters. The second is a shared, seeded bootstrap-CI library — clustered by enrollment, seeded from the snapshot manifest — so every sibling query's intervals reproduce the same way. Both began as findings in this query's design review: cache-key completeness is already standard across the programme, and the shared bootstrap-CI library has since landed at the platform layer every sibling query now shares.

06 Guardrails

Every way this number could mislead has a named control

What could go wrongThe control
Confounded comparison An SMD balance gate over eight-plus pre-treatment covariates — baseline HbA1c, age band, BMI, country, program, phase, prior adherence, prior nutrition exposure — all reconstructed as-of the decision. Any imbalance above the gate routes the cell to a propensity-matched + IPW estimate, and the adjusted rate becomes the displayed headline.
False confidence from small cells Minimum 30 mature patients per cell; below the floor the cell reports "insufficient data" with its full denominator breakdown — never a rate. This is what stops a two-patient cohort reporting a confident 100%.
Window-sensitive findings Every rate is recomputed across three pre-registered outcome windows. If the sign flips or the magnitude shifts materially between them, the cell is marked window-sensitive and its headline is withheld — the analyst cannot pick the flattering window after seeing the data.
Future leakage Every covariate is computed as-of the decision timestamp from the spine's source-event-seq, never from current graph state; each field carries its value, its as-of timestamp, and the event sequence it was read at. Country and program are the values at decision time, not today's.
Outcomes coerced or mis-attributed Outcomes carry explicit statuses — mature, not-mature, missing, dropout, program-switch, censored-by-a-later-decision — under a Kaplan-Meier censoring policy; a missing lab is never counted as a zero. A versioned attribution rule (nearest same-domain decision before the outcome) stops two overlapping decisions both claiming the same HbA1c move.
Stale snapshot behind the numbers Freshness tiers: under 24h serves normally; 24–48h serves with a visible staleness banner; past 48h the service blocks and escalates rather than serve quietly outdated statistics.
Upstream corrections ignored A correction listener maps each spine correction to the metric slices that depend on it and rebuilds just those — P95 under five minutes from correction to fresh tables.
Irreproducible statistics Bootstrap seeded from the snapshot manifest and clustered by enrollment; every response carries the manifest hashes and source-event-seq needed to replay its numbers byte-for-byte.
Tenant leakage Tenant scope wrapped at the call site, partitioned on disk, and asserted at write and on every served cell — even cache hits. A cross-tenant cell or a partition mismatch pages an operator rather than being served.

One row in that table hides a scaling problem worth drawing out. When an upstream correction lands on the spine — a relabelled outcome, a fixed covariate — the numbers that depend on it must change; but re-deriving nine years of cohorts on every correction would blow the freshness budget many times over. A dependency index makes each rebuild proportional to the correction, not to the corpus.

Without that index the only options would be a full nightly rebuild — correct but far too slow to stay fresh — or ignoring corrections until the next night — fast but wrong. The targeted rebuild is what lets the system be both fresh and correct at once.

07 The LLM boundary

No model touches the numbers — because there is no model

This is the portfolio's purest case of knowing when not to use an LLM. There is no language model anywhere in this pipeline. Rates, gates, suppression, matching, censoring, and confidence intervals are all deterministic, seeded code. The platform's self-hosted model seam runs in-VPC for the three queries that actually generate prose — precedent retrieval, causal-path, and cross-domain synthesis — and this one calls into none of it. A correlation carried with its confidence interval is already a finished, auditable statement: handing it to a model to reword would add nothing the interval does not say more honestly, and a single paraphrase is all it takes to turn a hedged number into a confident-sounding one. There is no retrieval-augmented generation surface here either, which is a judgment rather than an omission: an outcome-correlation cell earns its meaning from the balance gate, the suppression floor, and the observational caveat the code already stamped onto it, so there is nothing left for a retrieval step to fetch or a model to narrate.

Observational, not causal

The gate-then-propensity path keeps the headline honest: where covariate imbalance exceeds the gate, the displayed headline is the propensity-adjusted rate, and the raw descriptive rate stays in a drill-down carrying an observational tag. But matching only balances the covariates we measured — unmeasured confounding is never ruled out. Every surface reads the result as a correlation, and no amount of statistical machinery upgrades it to a cause. That honesty is the product.

08 The stack

Boring infrastructure, and a language model left on the shelf

The analytical core is deliberately unexciting — columnar files and an embedded query engine, each doing one job. The AI layer is present on the platform and pointedly unused here; the observability that is used was designed for PHI from the start.

Read the two tables below as a legend; this picture says when each component is touched and where the trust boundary sits. Three lanes run at three cadences. The request path is the only thing a clinician waits on — the metric API, its in-process cache, and, on a miss, a columnar scan of the pre-computed metric table. The nightly lane does the real work offline: project the graph, export the snapshot, build the cohort, label outcomes, gate for balance, and pre-compute every cell. The one-time lane is the historical hydration and pre-seed that filled the spine with nine years of matured outcomes. Everything runs inside the client VPC; the only thing that leaves is a suppressed aggregate travelling to the clinician's browser over TLS. No patient row ever crosses the boundary.

10 The review gauntlet

The review said "do not implement as written"

Before anything shipped, the design went through structured adversarial self-review under a Principal-AI-Engineer lens — and this one drew blood: sixteen findings across correctness, statistics, and scalability, with an unambiguous "do not implement as written."

  1. Design review · round 1 Ship-with-changes, 16 findings — tenant scoping not fail-closed, current-state country leakage, an ad-hoc outcome label that dropped missing labs, a weak and late confounding gate, repeated decisions treated as independent, unhandled censoring, an arbitrary outcome window, an under-specified bootstrap, per-row graph traversal, split-cardinality explosion, and an under-keyed cache.
  2.
