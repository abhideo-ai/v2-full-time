Case study · ML system design · Healthcare

Why is this patient on this treatment — and can you prove every step?

For a chronic-care platform covering 80+ conditions, I designed the decision-chain reconstruction system — the query a regulator subpoenas and a clinician trusts at the bedside — and took it to production. A structured adversarial self-review found the first design could not serve both audiences at once and returned one verdict — redesign; the version that shipped is two products from that verdict: a fast, bounded live view for the clinician and a byte-deterministic, spine-replayed export for the auditor. A language model appears only as an optional narrator, and only for sentences it can cite.

01 The problem

One decision chain, two very different burdens of proof

"Show me the chain that led to this plan" sounds like one question. It is two. A clinician opening the panel mid-visit needs what is true right now, fast enough not to interrupt the consultation, bounded so it shows the decisions that matter and not a decade of history. A regulator asks the same words but means something else entirely: what was true at exactly this instant, reconstructed so precisely that the same query, run again next year, returns the byte-identical chain.

The obvious build is one pipeline — a live walk over the decision graph — serving both. That is the design I brought to review, and it failed, for a reason that is structural rather than fixable with more care: the live graph is an asynchronously projected copy of the event history. It is excellent at "right now" and cannot, on its own, guarantee point-in-time replay of "back then." A single pipeline is forced to under-serve one audience to satisfy the other.

The design also has to survive three failure modes that quietly destroy trust in an audit surface:

  • Tenant leakage — one clinic's decision nodes surfacing in another's chain, a reportable privacy incident rather than a bug.
  • Time leakage — reconstructing a chain against "now" when the question was about a specific moment, so the answer silently includes decisions that had not happened yet.
  • A narrative that outruns its evidence — a fluent summary that asserts more than the cited nodes support, which is exactly the sentence a regulator will pull.
The review's verdict, in one line

The single pipeline serving both the clinician panel and the regulator audit export cannot meet both bars — so the v2 you are reading splits it into two architecturally distinct products that share only a seed-derivation contract and an optional narrative layer.

02 The architecture

Two products from one review verdict

The split is the whole design. Both products start from an identical, deterministic seed contract — how to find the patient's active protocol set and the current medication-plan decision, ordered the same way every time. From there they diverge completely: the clinician product walks the live graph, bounded; the regulator product ignores the live graph and reconstructs the chain from the event spine, deterministically, at the exact moment asked. An optional narrative layer, shared and heavily guarded, hangs off the side of both.

The divergence starts upstream, at the data itself. The clinician endpoint reads the live decision graph, which a projection Lambda maintains off the append-only event spine and stamps with a projection version and a live-lag figure. The regulator endpoint skips the graph and reads the spine directly, by source sequence — because only the spine can be replayed to a precise past state.

03 Hydration

Nine years of decisions, replayed into the spine

None of this is built against an empty world. Roughly nine years of historical records were replayed through the same append-only spine that now carries live traffic, so the system starts with its full past instead of accruing one. That backfill is a first-class, proof-carrying pipeline — not a throwaway load — and for a regulator asking about a decision made years ago, it is the whole reason there is anything to reconstruct.

Event time is not ingestion time. Every legacy record keeps the real-world moment it actually happened, and the moment the source observed or resulted it; separate received and written timestamps record only when the system learned of it on load. Collapse those apart and nine years of decisions would look as though they all occurred on load day, and every as-of reconstruction — the entire regulator product — would be void. Lateness runs in a distinct hydration mode where a nine-year spread of "late" data is expected and kept, not the small operational window live traffic uses.

Hydrating twice is a no-op. Content-addressed event IDs and conditional writes make the load idempotent and resumable: re-running the pipeline over the same corpus yields a byte-identical spine and an identical snapshot manifest hash. That equality is an executable proof of determinism, not a written assurance.

Nothing is silently coerced. Nine years means many legacy payload-schema versions; each gets an explicit upconverter, and any row that cannot be mapped to a known schema — or attributed to exactly one tenant — is quarantined with a reason rather than defaulted. A defaulted tenant would be a cross-tenant leak; a force-fit schema would poison every downstream cell.

The same projection code runs for history and for live. History is replayed through the exact projection logic that serves the live stream, so the hydrated graph is the same product as the streaming one — otherwise every determinism guarantee would be theatre. Physical write order is deliberately irrelevant: no derived artifact is a function of arrival order, which is precisely what lets a bulk load and a live stream converge on the same state.

The load is proof-carrying. Zero-event-loss reconciliation against the source, per-tenant and per-class counts, as-of spot checks against known-good historical reports, and a golden replay over a historical slice all gate the load before anything downstream is built. A fenced cutover then attaches the live stream with a proof of neither gap nor duplicate at the boundary: the new spine is authoritative at or below the fence, and live traffic dual-writes above it.

The stages above guard one axis with obsessive care, because the whole regulator product is a sort over it. Two of the four time fields — when a decision actually happened, and when the source resulted the evidence — come straight from the record. The other two — when the load received and wrote the row — are pinned to a single per-corpus load fence, never to now(). A guard asserts on every row that event time is strictly earlier than received time, which is precisely the check that catches a row accidentally stamped as loaded-today. Reconstructing a plan as of a past instant then means: keep the rows whose event time is at or before that instant, order them by event time and then source sequence, and fold corrections by epoch — the same total order the export cursor paginates on.

Pin those load timestamps to now() instead of the fence and every one of nine years of rows would look as though it arrived today — collapsing the whole history onto load day and voiding every as-of reconstruction the regulator product exists to produce.

What nine years buys this query

KQ9 hands back the complete chain. A regulator's reconstruction of a decision from years ago is only possible because the spine holds those events — with their provenance and correction lineage — across the whole span, not just the recent months. A reconstruction of a plan set years ago reads the same spine, by the same source-sequence cursor, and replays to the byte; without hydration the oldest and most-scrutinised decisions would simply be absent.

04 The clinician's product

A live walk, deliberately bounded

The clinician path is a live traversal, computed at query time and cached only as a finished result. It resolves the as-of instant once, at admission — never silently "now", always echoed back so the answer states the moment it describes. It builds a canonical cache key from the full provenance tuple, and on a miss it checks the projection's freshness before walking anything: if the live graph has fallen behind its SLA, the response comes back marked bounded_view_degraded with a serving-stale banner rather than quietly presenting old data as current.

Two details in that walk carry most of the correctness. First, the causal edge allow-list is filtered by event time before the traversal expands, not after — filtering after expansion fragments chains and was a high-severity finding in the review. Second, attribution — who made the decision — is enriched as a separate sibling block and never folded into the causal path, so the chain cannot imply that a person "led to" a decision the way a piece of evidence or a policy did. The walk is bounded to depth 4 and a 365-day window by default, both configurable per patient, and the response states plainly when older ancestors were omitted.

Seen as an interaction rather than a flowchart, the path has two shapes. A warm request is a single hop: authorize, log, look up the canonical key, assert tenancy, return. A cold request gates freshness, derives the seed, walks the graph bounded, optionally narrates under the claim grammar, and — the part that keeps the panel live — sets the cache under a key that a later decision-write or correction for this patient will invalidate. That subscription is why a clinician who just entered a decision sees it immediately rather than waiting out a TTL.

A concrete chain

Asked "why is this patient on this plan?", the panel returns: the current medication plan, started 2026-02-14, attributed to clinician a_4f2b (a pseudonymous actor id, in its own block); supported by an HbA1c reading of 7.4 (ev_b2); constrained by a first-line-therapy policy (pol_c3); and superseding an earlier diet-only plan (dec_a0). Every step cites the evidence, policy, or decision id that justifies it — no quote, no claim. A warm response returns well inside the sub-500 ms budget; the footer shows the bounds in force and the live projector lag with a green / yellow / red status.

05 The regulator's product

Deterministic replay, not a live query

The regulator export is a different animal. It has its own RBAC role, its own rate and payload limits, its own timeout budgets and audit retention, and it requires an explicit access_reason on every call. It never touches the live graph. Instead it reconstructs the chain from the event spine by source sequence, at the requested as-of instant, applying supersedes and correction-epoch handling so the reconstruction reflects the state that was actually in force then — not today's values. It is deliberately not cacheable: it produces a deterministic stream instead.

Determinism is enforced by the cursor. It sorts over a total order — event time, source sequence, vertex id, edge position — is valid only against the same as-of anchor, and is rejected if the underlying freshness changes without that anchor. It is stable across server restarts, so a paginated export that spans a deploy still stitches together into one byte-identical chain. Every page carries the full provenance a replay needs, and the response returns the replay instructions verbatim. Large exports drop into an async job with a committed export hash and a webhook, so a subpoena-sized chain never blocks the service.

07 The LLM boundary

The language model narrates; it never decides the chain

This is the portfolio's through-line — knowing when not to use an LLM. The seed derivation, the bounded walk, the spine reconstruction, the cursor, the tenant assertions, and the access control are all deterministic code. The only language-model call is an optional narrator: after the chain is built, the model is handed the finished nodes and edges and asked to describe them. For the regulator export it is an optional appendix only — the chain JSON is always the primary evidence.

What keeps that narrator honest is the claim grammar. Every candidate sentence must decompose into an allowed template, and every template must be backed by cited node fields and cited edges. A sentence that cannot be traced to the chain does not get softened — the whole narrative is suppressed and the response falls back to the chain JSON. An uncited sentence is, by construction, unrepresentable in the contract.

When enabled in production the narrator runs on a self-hosted Llama 3.1 model inside the VPC, so clinical text never leaves the network. An external provider is permitted only when the payload has been de-identified first — a contractual no-retention agreement is a privacy control, not a substitute for network residency, so the PHI-bearing path is self-hosted and in-VPC, full stop. The model, system prompt, and temperature (zero) are pinned, and the response emits a chain hash and a narrative hash. Those hashes buy drift detection and reproducible auditing, not generation determinism: temperature zero alone does not make a language model regenerate a chain identically, so the hashes detect drift — they do not prevent it. The determinism in this system lives in the structured chain, not the narrator; the clinician panel does not flicker on a cache rehydrate because the narrative is stored and served from cache keyed by the chain hash, not regenerated on every view. The same guarded contract is reused by the portfolio's per-patient cohort drill-in.

08 The RAG pipeline

Retrieval-augmented, on a short leash

The narrator in the previous section is, strictly, retrieval-augmented generation — so it is worth being exact about what the retrieval is. It is not a vector search over documents. It is a governed query against the context graph: tenant-scoped, reconstructed as of the decision, and gated before a model ever sees it. On the clinician surface that retrieval is the bounded live traversal; on the regulator surface it is the deterministic spine replay. Either way the model's only job is to say, in a brief cited summary, what the retrieved chain already shows — and a validator rejects it if it says anything else.

The pipeline is five stages, and only the first is specific to this query:

  • Retrieve — the query's own governed access path: a bounded graph traversal for the clinician, or a deterministic spine replay by source-sequence cursor for the regulator. The tenant filter lives inside the query, not as a check bolted on after it.
  • Assemble — a typed, as-of context object holding only the fields that existed at the decision moment, each carrying its provenance. Post-decision evidence never enters the context.
  • Generate — a pinned model version and a pinned system prompt at temperature zero, self-hosted inside the VPC so PHI never leaves the network. The prompt forbids computing, re-ranking, or concluding.
  • Validate — beyond the claim grammar already described, every cited node ID must resolve and the edge between two cited nodes must structurally exist, and a leakage guard rejects any post-decision value — the checks that only a graph retrieval can enforce.
  • Serve or suppress — a failed citation re-prompts exactly once; a second failure sets the narrative suppressed with failure metadata. The chain is served either way — the answer never depends on the model.

Three invariants hold across the whole surface. No node, edge, or value in any response is written by a language model — deterministic code reconstructs the chain, and the model only narrates it. Every generated sentence carries a citation that resolves, or it is not served. And hashes pin the inputs and the output — the chain JSON hash, the narrative hash, and the validation version — so any narrative can be re-derived and audited long after the fact. On the regulator export the structured chain JSON is authoritative and the sentence is an optional appendix: a citation that fails validation re-prompts once and then suppresses, and the chain ships regardless of whether the sentence survives.

09 The stack

Boring infrastructure, deliberate framework choices

The traversal and reconstruction core is deliberately unexciting — an append-only spine, a projected graph, and a strict cursor, each doing one job. The AI layer reuses the platform's LangChain seam and LangGraph runtime, with observability designed for PHI from the start.

The picture below places each of those components in the lane where it actually runs, so the two tables underneath read as its legend. Three lanes matter. The request path is everything a clinician or an auditor waits on — the API, the split RBAC and audit, the clinician's cache, freshness gate and bounded live walk, and the regulator's deterministic spine reconstruction and cursor. The off-request continuous lane is the projection that keeps the live graph fresh and the in-VPC tracer, neither of which a request blocks on. The one-time historical lane is the hydration and fenced cutover that pre-seeded the spine. Everything sits inside the client VPC; only the clinician's browser and the regulator's export client are outside it, over TLS.

11 The review gauntlet

The review said redesign — the v2 you just read is the redesign

Before anything shipped, the design went through structured adversarial self-review under a Principal-AI-Engineer lens, and this one did not pass. It came back with a single unambiguous recommendation — redesign — behind two blockers, thirteen high-severity findings, and six mediums.

  1. Design review · round 1 · redesign The two blockers were structural: the audit export was not deterministically reconstructible from the live graph, and its response was missing the provenance fields a replay needs. The verdict: one pipeline cannot serve both the clinician panel and the regulator audit export.
  2. Design revision · v2 · the redesign The review prescribed a four-part split — clinician, regulator, narrative, operations — and the v2 implements it one-to-one: two architecturally distinct products sharing only the seed contract and the guarded narrator. Every blocker and high is dispositioned — most map to a named v2 mechanism (the pre-expansion edge filter, the deterministic cursor, the projector-lag gate, the completed cache key, the claim grammar); the rest are carried as tracked platform dependencies on the ladder below rather than marked closed.
  3. Programme impact The review minted a shared narrative-guardrail requirement, adopted by the per-patient drill-in query as well, and an explicit, week-tagged platform-dependency ladder — tenant isolation, a correction-event backbone, and spine indexing for the regulator path, all since landed, plus a signed narrative-guardrail contract, which remains outstanding — so the synthesis narrator ships off by default until it is signed. The redesigned v2 is the design that shipped.

12 Delivered

What was delivered — and where it transfers

  • A production deployment — the causal-path service live on the platform's self-hosted, in-VPC stack, serving both the clinician's live decision-chain panel and the regulator's deterministic export. Shipped after executive sign-off on the redesign.
  • A complete design packet — the two API envelopes with full provenance pins, the shared seed-derivation contract, the bounded-walk and deterministic-cursor specs, the split RBAC and audit governance, the freshness and degradation SLAs, and the narrative-guardrail contract.
  • A review audit trail — a redesign-grade adversarial review (two blockers, thirteen highs, six mediums) and the two-product split that answered it, every finding tracked to the mechanism that dispositioned it.
  • A reused contract — the deterministic chain artifact and its validation metadata are consumed by the portfolio's cohort-orchestration query for per-patient drill-in, which passes an explicit time window when it calls this service, and shares the same narrative guardrail.

The pattern generalises well beyond healthcare: any system that must reconstruct and defend a decision chain to two very different audiences — one that needs "what is true right now" fast, and one that needs "what was true at exactly this instant" provably. Credit decisioning with a regulatory audit trail, incident forensics beside a live operations view, any workflow where live operations and compliance-replay are the same data seen through two burdens of proof — this is the discipline I bring.
