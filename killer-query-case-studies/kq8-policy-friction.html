Case study · ML system design · Healthcare

Which rules do clinicians keep overriding — and which overrides track better or worse outcomes?

For a chronic-care platform covering 80+ conditions, I designed the policy-friction analytics system — which platform rules generate the most clinician overrides, and which of those overrides are associated with better or worse outcomes — and took it to production. Every number is computed by deterministic, seeded, replayable code; there is no language model anywhere in the pipeline; and the data layer emits only neutral zone codes — never a verdict on a policy or a clinician.

01 The problem

The same GROUP BY, pointed at the rulebook

Count how often each policy is overridden, compare outcomes when it is followed against when it is broken, and plot the result: a 2×2 friction map. It looks like one query. It is one bad label away from a machine that puts rules — and, by proximity, the clinicians who break them — on trial. Three failure modes are built into the naive version, and this one is more dangerous than its sister query on individual overrides, because its output can trigger a policy revision or a model retraining. A wrong signal here rewrites the rulebook.

So the design treats it as three first-class problems, each with a named control:

  • Confounding — clinicians override on their sickest, most atypical patients, so a naive followed-versus-overridden comparison is confounded by design. Every override is compared only against a propensity-matched control on the same policy family. Matching balances only what is measured; unmeasured confounding is never ruled out — which is exactly why every surface stays observational, not causal.
  • False confidence at scale — the map ranks dozens of policy versions at once, so a handful will look damning by chance alone. Under-powered cells, caliper-fragile findings, and multiplicity are all handled explicitly — false-discovery-rate disclosure, sensitivity sweeps, and a power floor — and the ranking is labelled exploratory, never a league table.
  • Verdict misuse — the moment a cell reads "policy too strict" or "clinicians ignoring evidence", a governance action fires off a machine's opinion. The data layer is forbidden from ever producing those words; it emits neutral codes only, and interpretation lives in governed copy a human owner signs off on.
02 The architecture

Snapshot-only analytics, indexed by policy version

The pipeline never touches the live graph at query time. A nightly snapshot feeds a cohort builder that reconstructs every override as of the moment it happened — the alignment, the rationale, and the exact policy version the decision was made under — by replaying the append-only event spine. Everything is grouped by policy_version_id and the immutable policy_text_hash, never by a mutable policy id, so editing a rule tomorrow can never rewrite yesterday's history. Downstream, per-version metrics are pre-computed into columnar tables served by an embedded analytical engine.

03 Hydration

Nine years of policy versions, replayed into the spine

The system was not switched on against an empty world. Roughly nine years of prior decisions — every override, every rule version, and every outcome that followed — were hydrated through the same append-only event spine before the first friction map was ever drawn. Hydration is a first-class, quarantine-first, proof-carrying pipeline, not a throwaway backfill, and it holds five lines it will not cross.

  • Event time is not the day it was loaded. Each legacy record keeps the real-world moment it happened — when a decision was made, when a rule was in force — and carries the source's own observed-or-resulted time with it; only the received and written timestamps record when the new system learned of the event. Collapse event time into load time and nine years of decisions would all appear to have happened on load day, and every as-of reconstruction this query depends on would be void; so the load runs in a distinct hydration mode whose watermark and late-arrival window span the whole corpus, and a nine-year spread of "late" data is expected, not dropped.
  • Hydrating twice changes nothing. Every event carries a content-addressed identifier and is written conditionally, so re-running the load over the same corpus yields a byte-identical spine and an identical snapshot manifest hash. Idempotency here is an executable proof, not a promise.
  • Nothing is silently coerced. Nine years means many generations of legacy payload schema; each gets an explicit up-converter, and any row that cannot be mapped to a known schema — or to a tenant — is quarantined with a reason and counted, never defaulted. A defaulted tenant would be a cross-tenant leak, which on a governance surface is unthinkable.
  • History runs the live code. The replayed history is projected through the exact same code that serves the live stream. Were it not, the hydrated graph would be a different product from the streaming one, and every determinism guarantee would be theatre.
  • The load has to prove itself. Zero-event-loss reconciliation against the source, per-tenant and per-class counts, as-of spot checks against known-good historical reports, and a golden replay over a historical slice all gate the load before anything downstream is built; a fenced cutover then attaches the live stream with a proof of neither gap nor duplicate at the boundary.

What nine years buys this query in particular: a friction signal can be attached to a policy version only if that version — and every override recorded against it — exists across time. Hydration reconstructs each historical decision under the exact rule text in force when it was made, indexed by the immutable policy_version_id and policy_text_hash. Without that depth most policy cells would fall below the thirty-per-arm floor and be suppressed; with it, enough overridden decisions accrue per version — and enough comparable non-overridden controls — for the matched comparison to run at all, rather than returning an empty map.

The load preserves not just each event but its place in time, and that is what makes this particular query defensible. Every legacy record keeps two timestamps that are true of the world — when the decision was made and when its result was observed — while the two timestamps the new system controls, received and written, are pinned to a single load fence rather than to the load clock. A policy cell is then grouped by the rule version that was live at the decision's own event time, reconstructed by sorting the spine on event time and then source sequence and folding corrections by their epoch — never by the order rows happened to load. Collapse event time into load time and every override would appear to have happened on load day, the as-of grouping this whole query is built on would be void, and nine years of distinct policy versions would smear into one.

04 The borrowed control arm

Never a silently different control group

This system does not build its own matched controls. Its sister query — the override-analytics service documented in a companion case study — already pairs each override with a propensity-matched control decision, and ships that cohort with a versioned match manifest: a single hash over the covariate schema, the snapshot manifest, the cohort filters, the caliper, the replacement policy, the seed, and the tie-break rule. This system reuses that control arm verbatim — but only when all seven manifest fields match exactly. On any mismatch it rebuilds the full cohort with the same matcher binary and records why. There is no "fresh fallback" that quietly matches a few unmatched rows a different way — that path was designed out, because two dashboards disagreeing about the same cohort is precisely the bug this removes.

The fixture that started life red

The cross-query parity check began as a failing test. It reproduces a real class of bug — a cross-query sign-flip — on an illustrative cohort: for one policy version with 418 references and 71 overrides, this pipeline reported an outcome delta of −0.05 while its sibling reported +0.04, opposite signs on the same cohort, a contradiction that would have shipped to a governance dashboard. The fixture that reproduces it had to go green before either system was allowed to release. A disagreement about human decisions should fail a test, not a career.

05 The gate ladder

Chain-of-thought as deterministic code

Before any policy cell may show a headline zone code, it climbs a ladder of gates — and the reasoning is not a prompt, it is seeded, replayable code. Fail any rung and the cell is suppressed with a stated reason and full denominators: "insufficient data", "arms not comparable", or a neutral uncertain_zone when the outcome interval crosses zero. The dashboard never shows a confident code built on sand, and never a silent gap.

Drawn as a flow, that ladder is the query's reasoning made literal — each rung is a gate that either passes the cell down to the next test or diverts it to a stated, neutral suppression with its full denominators. There is no rung a model could reach into, and no path that ends in a confident code the evidence did not earn.

Because the branch is deterministic, the same cell suppresses the same way on every rebuild — the audit can replay exactly why a code was, or was not, shown.

Every read is also an audit event and a canonical cache key. Serving is a pre-computed lookup with a per-row tenant assertion on the way out — account, workspace, and control-unit, resolved from the caller's auth, must all match every row; a cache hit still re-checks that every row belongs to the caller before it leaves the process.

06 Guardrails

Every way this map could mislead has a named control

What could go wrongThe control
Confounded comparison Propensity-matched controls on pre-treatment covariates only; any imbalance above the standardised-mean-difference (SMD) gate, or a lack of common support, suppresses the cell's delta and emits a diagnostic instead of a headline.
A silently different control group The sibling system's matched cohort is reused only on exact seven-field manifest equality; any mismatch triggers a full rebuild with the same matcher binary and a recorded rebuild_reason. A cross-query parity fixture must pass before the design can be called approved.
False confidence from small cells Minimum 30 patients in each arm per policy version; below the floor the cell reports "insufficient data" with its full denominator breakdown, unmatched count, and censored counts.
Fragile findings Every comparison is recomputed at threshold tweaks of ±20% and ±50% and at strict, base, and loose matching calipers. Any sign flip or interval shift tags the cell borderline_zone and withholds a conclusive recommendation.
Ranking dozens of policies at once Multiple-testing diagnostics on every response — policies tested, insufficient, borderline, uncertain — plus Benjamini–Hochberg q-values computed at q = 0.10 and returned with every cell. The zone gate itself is unadjusted: a cell earns its code on power, balance, sensitivity and an interval excluding zero, so the q-value is a disclosure the reader must weigh, not a filter applied on their behalf. The ranking is explicitly labelled exploratory. Confidence intervals do not assume policy rows are independent.
A machine issuing a verdict The data layer emits only six neutral zone codes — no "policy too strict", no "who was right". Interpretation lives in governed UI copy owned by the policy-governance team, labelled observational, and versioned separately from the numbers.
Editing a rule rewrites its own history Everything is grouped by immutable policy_version_id and policy_text_hash, reconstructed as-of from the spine — never by a mutable policy id, never from the current graph.
Cross-tenant leakage Tenant filter applied at the storage scan before load; fail-closed row assertions in the builder and on every served row. Cross-tenant pooling is not the default — it requires separate authorization, a per-tenant heterogeneity report, and suppression of small identifying cells.
Stale snapshot or ignored corrections Freshness tiers (green under 24h, yellow banner at 24–48h, red past 48h — at red the service blocks and escalates rather than serve quietly outdated statistics; projector lag over 30 min also flags yellow). A correction listener maps each spine correction to the policy versions it touches and rebuilds just those — P95 under five minutes.
Irreproducible statistics Bootstrap seeded from the snapshot manifest and clustered by enrollment; every response carries the manifests, hashes, and library versions needed to replay its numbers byte-for-byte, and every refresh is recorded in a zone-history trail for quarter-over-quarter audit.

Two of those controls deserve to be seen working, not just listed. The first is fragility. A matched delta that only clears zero at one exact threshold and one exact caliper is not a finding — it is an artifact of where the knobs happened to sit. So every comparison is recomputed across a small grid of deliberately mild perturbations, on the same cohort and the same seed, and a single sign flip or interval crossing anywhere in the grid downgrades the cell.

Without the sweep, the most confident-looking headline would routinely be the most fragile one.

The second is staleness. When a correction lands on the spine — a policy's text is fixed, an edge is repaired, an outcome is amended — the system neither rebuilds the world nor ignores the change. A listener maps the correction to exactly the policy versions that depend on the affected events and rebuilds only those cells, invalidating just their cache keys.

A rebuild-everything policy would either miss the SLA or burn the cluster nightly; a do-nothing policy would keep serving a number the source has already retracted.

07 The friction map

The picture invites a story the gates refuse to tell

The product surface is a quadrant: override rate on one axis, outcome delta on the other. It is the fastest way for a governance team to see where friction lives. It is also where the temptation to conclude is strongest — so the quadrant names on the chart are the literal neutral zone codes the data layer emits, and nothing more evaluative ever reaches the screen.

The detail table is where the discipline shows. A single friction map is a snapshot; governance cares about movement. Every refresh appends to a zone-history trail keyed by the immutable policy version and text hash, so a rule's friction can be tracked quarter over quarter. And because a rule edit mints a new version id rather than mutating the old one, changing a policy tomorrow can never rewrite what its previous wording looked like in the map — the history is appended to, not overwritten.

That is what lets a governance owner honestly ask whether the outcomes moved with last quarter's change, instead of comparing today's rule against a past it has already erased.

08 The LLM boundary

The right number of model calls was zero

This is the portfolio's purest case of knowing when not to use an LLM. There is no language model in this system at all — not in the numbers, not in the labels, not even as an optional narrator. The platform's self-hosted model seam is wired only to the surfaces that produce sentences — the retrieval summary and the traversal and cross-domain narratives — and this one is deliberately not among them, because its output is a governance signal that can revise a rulebook or trigger a retraining. A friction zone is a neutral, non-blaming code on purpose, and the moment a model is allowed to narrate one in prose it editorialises a policy into good or bad — the very verdict the codes exist to withhold. A generated sentence that reads as a judgment is exactly the thing that must never happen on this surface.

By the same logic there is no retrieval-augmented generation surface here, and its absence is a judgment rather than an omission: a governance signal that can revise a rulebook has to be a matched-cohort statistic an auditor can trace back to its own denominators, not a passage retrieved from somewhere and paraphrased into a sentence that merely sounds authoritative.

  • Neutrality is code, not a prompt: the six zone codes are assigned by deterministic classification; the interpretive wording is governed UI copy owned by the policy-governance team and versioned separately. There is no prompt to jailbreak, because no claim ever passes through a model.
  • The self-hosted option exists — and is declined here: the platform can run a Llama 3.1 model inside the VPC so patient data never leaves the network. On this system it is not wired to any path. The correct design was to leave it out.
  • Nothing to fail quiet: because there is no generated text, there is no additive layer to lose. Every figure and every label on the dashboard is deterministic and reproducible.
09 The stack

Boring infrastructure, deliberate framework choices

The analytical core is deliberately unexciting — columnar files and an embedded query engine, each doing one job. The framework layer reuses the platform's shared seams, with observability designed for PHI from the start; the model seam that its sibling queries use for optional narration sits present but untouched here.

Before the component-by-component legend below, here is when each piece is actually touched. The request path — the only lane a clinician ever waits on — is deliberately short: the API, an in-process cache, and the DuckDB tables of pre-computed cells. The decision graph and the model seam are drawn dotted and off to the side, because the request path touches neither — the graph is for audit and drill-down, and no language model sits on any path at all. Everything is inside the client VPC; only the clinician's browser, over TLS, is outside it.

The work that makes those DuckDB tables exist happens off the clock, in two lanes that never touch a live request. A one-time historical lane hydrated roughly nine years of decisions into the event spine; a nightly, offline lane then replays that spine into a snapshot, rebuilds the cohort and its borrowed control arm, runs the seeded statistics, and writes the zone tables the request path reads.

10 Evaluation

The binding metric: cross-query parity

For a governance surface, the costliest failure is disagreeing with yourself — two dashboards telling opposite stories about the same cohort — closely followed by a false emit: a headline zone code that should have been suppressed. Evaluation is built around those asymmetries, on seeded synthetic cohorts where the ground truth of every gate decision is known by construction.

11 The review gauntlet

The review said do not implement as written

Before anything shipped, the design went through structured adversarial self-review under a Principal-AI-Engineer lens, and this one drew blood: seventeen findings across correctness, statistics, and governance, with a verdict of ship-with-changes — an unambiguous "do not implement as written."

The high-severity core was real: the cross-query sign-flip that would have shipped opposite conclusions about the same cohort; a "fresh fallback" that silently built a different control group; reading the current graph for historical policy facts; no policy versioning; ad-hoc outcome labels; zone labels assigned from point estimates while the interval crossed zero; sensitivity tested at only ±20%; a one-arm sample gate; no tenant fields anywhere; and conclusory friction labels baked into the data layer.

  1. Design review · round 1 Ship-with-changes, 17 findings — confounding and control-arm integrity, statistical rigour gaps, and the governance risk of a friction map that reads as a verdict.
  2. Design revision · v2 Fifteen findings absorbed directly into the design body; the remaining findings — the cross-query sign-flip and its manifest contract — promoted into shared platform components (tenant wrapper, outcome library, matcher binary, deterministic exporter, cache-key envelope, clustered bootstrap) and guarded by a parity fixture that must pass before the design can be called approved.
  3. Its own standing gate The design writes its own blocker: it does not get to call itself approved until the sibling system emits the match manifest and the cross-query parity fixture passes for every policy version. The nine platform components have since landed; two conditions remain. The hardened v2 is the design that shipped to production; the design-review approval is held open, on the record, at ship-with-changes until those conditions are met.

12 Delivered

What was delivered — and where it transfers

  • A production deployment — the policy-friction analytics service live on the platform's self-hosted, in-VPC stack, serving governed governance dashboards. Shipped after executive sign-off on the design.
  • A complete design packet — the snapshot-only cohort spec, the borrowed-control-arm contract (seven-field manifest, reuse-or-rebuild, no fresh fallback), the pre-registered statistical plan (balance gate, dual sensitivity sweep, both-arm power floor, seeded clustered bootstrap, FDR multiplicity disclosure — q-values reported, gates unadjusted), API envelopes with full provenance pins, the neutral-code governance spec with a threshold-version registry, the freshness SLAs, and the zone-history audit trail.
  • A review audit trail — a seventeen-finding adversarial design review, its absorption into v2, and a self-imposed platform-closure gate held open before final approval. The programme's hiring-CTO review loop, documented in the precedent-search case study, is the same standard the runnable work samples pass through.

The pattern generalises well beyond healthcare: any system that grades a rulebook by how often it is broken and which breaks track better or worse outcomes — underwriting exception rates, SRE runbook bypasses, fraud-rule overrides, content-policy appeals. When the finding can change the rule, it must never read as a machine's verdict; the statistics must be gated, the control group must be exactly the one you claim, and the access must be governed. That is the discipline I bring.
